## Reading objectives used (verbatim from `learningobjectives.csv`, Module 1: Describing, Reading modality)

| Task Level | Role | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extension-down | Can read a short descriptive text about a familiar object and identify its features, one comparison, and the brief reason given for that comparison, decoding unfamiliar words as needed using known letter-sound patterns. |

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Visual Inquiry hook (5 min):** Project the lesson's photo (`Lesson1_SagradaFamilia_Img_Hook.jpg`, embedded in the student packet) of the Sagrada Família's towers (exterior, showing the contrast
between the older Nativity Facade and the newer construction cranes still visible near the top). Ask: "What do you
notice? What questions do you have? Does this look finished to you?" Take 3-4 call-outs without confirming or
correcting; write every guess on the board under a running heading, **"What We Think We're Looking At."** This
list stays up and gets checked against the text later in Phase 1 (see board-dependent moment below).
Image: `Lesson1_SagradaFamilia_Img_Hook.jpg` - "Sagrada familia (left side)" by Stefano Mortellaro, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sagrada_familia_(left_side).jpg.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing a place by noticing specific features, like a facade
or a column, and comparing them to something more familiar, like a typical cathedral. We're also going to
practice something a little trickier: spotting the exact words a writer uses to show excitement or admiration,
even when the writer never says 'I love this' directly." (This exact skill, feature-plus-comparison for the lowest
task Level and evaluative-word-spotting for the native and extension-up task Levels, is what Day 2's Closing
Transfer Check will ask every student to reproduce.)

**Target vocabulary (6 min):** Pre-teach these 6 words using in-context paragraph matching (show each word's
sentence from the text, cold, and have students guess the meaning from context before confirming):

| Word | Paragraph | Student-friendly definition |
|---|---|---|
| facade | [B] | the front or outer face of a building |
| ornamentation | (theme word, appears throughout as carved decoration) | decorative details added to a building |
| spire | (theme word; the towers of the basilica function as spires) | a tall, narrow, pointed structure on top of a building |
| mosaic | (theme word; visible in the color-arranged glass, Paragraph [C]) | a picture or pattern made from small pieces of colored material |
| organic | [B] | shaped like something found in nature, not straight or geometric |
| structural | [E] | related to how a building is built and holds itself up |

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight (2 min):** The text uses two expressions worth flagging before reading:
- "a running joke" [D] - a transparent chunk: something people keep mentioning, half-seriously, because it
  keeps happening. Gloss: "if something is 'a running joke,' people joke about it again and again because it
  never seems to change."
- "completely blown away" [F] - an opaque idiom: gloss it directly rather than asking students to infer it.
  "If you are 'blown away' by something, you are extremely impressed or surprised by it, so much that it's like a
  strong wind hit you."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min)

**Strategy: Close Reading with Annotation.** Distribute the anchor text below with wide margins. Provide this
annotation key on the board and on a printed strip at the top of the handout:

```
ANNOTATION KEY
__________  underline any unfamiliar word
( )         circle a connector word (however, even so, since, because)
[ ]         bracket the sentence that states the paragraph's main idea
?           write a question mark next to anything that surprises you
!           write an exclamation point next to a word that feels like the writer's opinion
```

Give students 20 minutes to read and annotate individually or in pairs (Close Reading with Annotation is suited
to independent readers at this band). Circulate and coach. With 15 minutes remaining, do a quick partner check:
compare annotations with a neighbor for two minutes, then a whole-class share of 2-3 "!" marks (words that
felt like the writer's opinion) to preview Phase 3's comprehension focus.

**Anchor Text (Level 5, 460 words, 6 paragraphs)**

> **The Basilica That Refuses to Be Finished**
>
> [A] More than a hundred years after construction began, the Basílica de la Sagrada Família still rises above
> Barcelona, unfinished and unmistakable. Antoni Gaudí spent over forty years on the project and never lived to
> see it completed. Visitors arriving from Gothic cathedrals often expect something familiar: grey stone, matching
> towers, a facade built to impress through scale. What they find instead is stranger, and to most eyes, far more
> alive.
>
> [B] The building's three main facades tell three different stories. The Nativity Facade, the oldest, is crowded
> with carved doves and twisting vines, so densely arranged that some visitors call it a stone forest. Gaudí worked
> on this facade himself, choosing organic, flowing shapes over the straight lines of a typical Gothic cathedral.
> The Passion Facade, finished decades after his death, is the opposite: stark, angular figures meant to convey
> suffering rather than joy. The Glory Facade, still unfinished, is planned as the largest of the three.
>
> [C] Inside, stone columns rise like actual trees, branching near the ceiling into narrower supports that spread
> the roof's weight outward. Gaudí based this design on trees he had studied near his childhood home. A forest
> canopy, he reasoned, already solves the problem a roof needs to solve: holding enormous weight using the
> least material. Sunlight enters through stained glass arranged by color, blue and green on one side, orange and
> red on the other, so the light inside shifts through the day.
>
> [D] Construction has always moved slowly, funded mainly by ticket sales and private donations rather than
> government money. For decades, the building's completion date was a running joke* among Barcelona
> residents. Engineers now use computer modeling and stone-cutting robots that Gaudí never had, which has
> sped up the work considerably. Even so, project leaders stay careful about naming a firm finish date, since
> structural challenges have delayed the basilica before and could do so again.
>
> [E] What makes the building remarkable, according to architects who study it, is not just its size but how it
> fuses natural forms with engineering decades ahead of its time. Gaudí spent years building small hanging
> models¹ made of weighted string to calculate his stone arches. This let him design shapes a drafting table
> alone could never have produced. Critics who tour the site often use words like extraordinary and awe-inspiring
> to describe standing beneath the towers as light pours through the glass.
>
> [F] The basilica became a UNESCO World Heritage Site² in 2005, alongside several other Gaudí works in the
> city. Architecture students now travel from around the world to study its structural innovations, and local
> guides say many visitors arrive for a quick photo and end up staying for hours, completely blown away†.
> Whether the building will ever truly be finished, Barcelona seems, for now, content to wonder.
>
> *a running joke: something people joke about again and again because it keeps happening
>
> †blown away: extremely impressed or surprised
>
> ¹ Gaudí calculated his arches using upside-down hanging models: a string weighted at points would naturally
> curve into the exact shape a stone arch needed to stand without extra support, so he built the model, then
> flipped a photo of it right-side up to get his design.
>
> ² UNESCO is the United Nations agency that names and protects sites of major cultural importance around the
> world.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, satisfies Section 0.7):** After reading, return to the "What We Think We're
Looking At" list built live during Visual Inquiry. As a class, check each guess against the text: correct, partly
correct, or not mentioned. This list existed only from student call-outs before reading and gets tested against
the text together, so it cannot be replaced by a handout.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Fact Finders (for the lowest task Level, Level 4, 10 min):** Answer using the text. Point to the paragraph letter
that gives you the answer.
1. Name one feature of the Nativity Facade. (Paragraph ___)
2. What did Gaudí compare the stone columns to inside the basilica? (Paragraph ___)
3. What year did the basilica become a UNESCO World Heritage Site? (Paragraph ___)
4. What do the project leaders avoid doing, and why? (Paragraph ___)

**Cause & Effect set (for the native task Levels, 5 and 6, 10 min):**
1. Why does sunlight inside the basilica shift through the day? Point to the paragraph and quote the reason.
2. Why did Gaudí build small hanging models before cutting any stone? What effect did this have on his design?
3. Construction sped up in recent years. What caused that change?

**Vocabulary context completion (all task Levels, 5 min):** Fill in the blank using a target word from Phase 1.
1. The Nativity Facade uses curved, ______ shapes instead of straight lines.
2. A ______ is the front or outer face of a building.
3. The colored glass windows create a kind of ______ effect with light.
4. The columns solve a ______ problem: how to hold up the roof.

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-Minute Partner Re-Scan:** Pairs re-scan the Sagrada Família text for key details and paragraph markers (no
new reading, just relocating).

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min, unrelated topic, recycles Day 1 vocabulary):**

> **A Neighborhood Bakery Gets a New Look**
>
> When the Ferreira family renovated their bakery last spring, they wanted the new facade to feel different from
> every other shop on the street. Instead of a flat, straight-lined storefront, they chose an organic design with
> curved wooden shelving visible through the window and a small mosaic of colored tile above the door. A local
> artist added light ornamentation around the sign, small carved leaves that customers now stop to look at
> before going in. The renovation wasn't simple. It required real structural changes to the old building, including
> a new support beam that had been a running joke among the family for years before anyone finally paid for it.
> Regulars say the new look completely blew them away.

Task (light-touch, 3-4 min): Circle the 6 target words (facade, ornamentation, spire, mosaic, organic, structural)
wherever they appear, then tell a partner in one sentence what changed about the bakery.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Oral round-robin: each student picks one target word and uses it as
either a noun or in a comparison ("The new shelving looks organic, kind of like tree branches.").

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Form groups of 4 (one student per task Level: 4, 5, 6, 7). Each student completes their own task Level below as a
prep tool (10 min), then the group combines findings into a shared Collaborative Evidence Matrix on chart paper
or a shared doc (10 min), then reports out as the board-dependent moment (10 min, see below).

**Task Level 4 (extension-down, Level 4 ceiling: familiar-object description, one feature, one comparison, one
stated reason):**
Using Paragraph [B] only: (1) Name one feature of the Nativity Facade. (2) The text compares the Nativity Facade
to something. What is it compared to? (3) What reason does the text give for why Gaudí chose organic shapes
instead of straight lines? Word bank provided: *stone forest, straight lines, doves, vines.* Sentence starter: "One
feature of the Nativity Facade is ___. The text compares it to ___ because ___."

This task also carries a genuine, simplified interpretive component (Respectful Tiers check): (4) Which facade
sounds more interesting to you, the Nativity Facade or the Passion Facade? Circle one and complete: "I think the
___ Facade sounds more interesting because the text describes it as ___."

**Task Level 5 (native, anchor level ceiling: summarize organization, identify one evaluative word):**
(1) In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the article is organized. (Hint: does it move facade by facade, inside vs.
outside, or something else?) (2) Find one word in Paragraph [E] that clearly signals the writer's positive attitude
toward the building, even though the writer never says "I love this building." Explain what that word suggests.

**Task Level 6 (native, Level 6 ceiling: separate stated fact from evaluative language, locate a register shift):**
(1) List two facts stated directly in Paragraph [D] (no opinion words). (2) List one clearly evaluative phrase from
Paragraph [E] that shows judgment rather than fact. (3) Locate the place in Paragraph [D] where the writer's tone
shifts from a confident claim ("has sped up the work considerably") to a hedge ("Even so, project leaders stay
careful..."). Quote both halves of the shift.

**Task Level 7 (extension-up, Level 7 ceiling: compare two texts, identify an unstated interest):** See the second
text below. (1) List one detail the magazine article includes that the tourism-board text leaves out, and one
detail the tourism-board text includes that the magazine article leaves out. (2) Both texts describe the same
basilica but create a different impression. In 2-3 sentences, describe that difference. (3) Identify one unstated
commercial interest behind the tourism-board text's framing: what does the tourism board gain by describing the
basilica the way it does, and where in the text can you see that interest working even though it's never stated
directly?

**Second comparison text for Task Level 7 only (Level 7 ceiling, official/promotional framing, ~180 words):**

> **Visit Casa Gaudí: Plan Your Sagrada Família Experience**
>
> No trip to Barcelona is complete without the Sagrada Família, the city's most beloved landmark and a
> must-see for every visitor. Skip the line with a Priority Access ticket and step straight into one of the most
> breathtaking interiors in the world, where soaring stone columns and rainbow-colored light create a truly
> unforgettable atmosphere for the whole family. Our expert-led tours reveal secrets most visitors miss:
> Gaudí's ingenious hanging-model technique, the hidden symbolism carved into every facade, and the
> best photo spots for capturing the towers at golden hour. Combine your visit with a Park Güell ticket and
> save 15%. Tickets sell out weeks in advance during peak season, so book today to guarantee your spot at
> Barcelona's crown jewel. Groups, students, and skip-the-line audio guides available in 12 languages.

**New in v2.1 board-dependent moment (Phase 2, preferred anchor slot):** As each group reports out, build a
shared comparison T-chart on the board live: **"Feature/Description"** on the left, **"Comparison"** on the right,
matching Module 1's Describing skill. No single student's own task-Level sheet contains the full picture; the
board only fills in once every task Level in every group has reported.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min)

**Protocol: Panel Round.** Five or six students speak in turn from their seats while the rest listen; the speaking
group swaps every 5-6 minutes so multiple task Levels get a turn. Work through three prompts as the group turns
over, roughly one per changeover:
1. "Is the Sagrada Família more impressive as a feat of engineering or as a work of art?"
2. "Which facade, the Nativity Facade or the Passion Facade, does a better job of matching its mood to its subject?"
3. "Should the Sagrada Família ever be declared finished, or does it work better as a project that never quite ends?"

**Listener task (Section 0.10):** every student not currently speaking keeps a running tally sheet marking which
side (or, for prompts 2-3, which position) each speaker argues for, plus one target vocabulary word they
hear used well. This is the default active task for every listener, separate from the Level 4 and
Foundation Support roles below, which layer additional support on top of it for those specific students.

**Sentence stems by task Level (register capped at each Level's own ceiling):**
- Level 4: "I think it's more ___ because the text says ___."
- Level 5: "One reason I'd argue for [engineering/art] is that the writer describes ___, which shows ___."
- Level 6: "The writer's language shifts when describing ___, which suggests ___ rather than just stating fact."
- Level 7: "Comparing the two texts, I noticed the [tourism-board version/magazine article] frames this as ___,
  which serves the unstated interest of ___."

**Differentiated participation (Section 0.8, Part B):** The Level 4 student does not take an on-demand speaking
turn alongside the higher task Levels. Instead: (1) Rehearsed pair-share first, 2 minutes before the Panel Round
begins, where the Level 4 student says their stem once, quietly, to a partner and gets a thumbs-up; their first
public attempt is then already a repetition, not a cold performance. Alternatively, if the teacher
judges the student needs more support that day: (2) a listening/tracking role, marking a tally sheet for which
side (engineering or art) each speaker argues, with one fully-prepared sentence to contribute at a
natural pause.

**Foundation Support (for a student functioning below Level 4's own floor):** Provide a picture card set showing
the Nativity Facade, the Passion Facade, and the columns. The student's Phase 3 role is physical, not spoken:
they hold up the picture card that matches whatever feature is currently being discussed in the Panel Round, and,
during the K-W-L-style board check from Day 1, they can point to the paragraph letter a partner names rather
than reading it aloud. Pair this student with a peer buddy throughout Phase 2's matrix work as well.

**Closing Transfer Check (5 min, ends Phase 3):** In pairs, every student produces one new instance of the Day
1 Skill Spotlight skill, applied to a piece of clothing someone in the room is wearing, not the basilica. Sentence
frame: "This [clothing item] has [feature], which is different from [something familiar] because [reason]," or for
Levels 5-7: "Notice how I just used the word ___ to show I think this is [good/interesting], without saying it
directly." Say it out loud to a partner. The teacher cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share aloud. Nothing is collected or
graded.
