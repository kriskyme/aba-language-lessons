### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Mystery Quote hook (board-dependent, 5 min):** Write this quote on the board with no source given: "It sounds
warm, like it's alive." Ask: "What could this be describing? A person? A song? Something else?" Collect 3-4
student guesses on the board exactly as students say them (do not pre-write any guesses). After reading, return
to the board and check which guesses come closest to what the writer actually means.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing something by comparing it to something else and
giving a clear reason for our opinion, the way this writer compares listening to vinyl records to streaming music
and explains exactly why they prefer one. We're also going to practice spotting the exact words a writer uses to
reveal their stance, even when they never say 'I believe' directly."

**Target vocabulary (6 min):** Pre-teach these 6 words using in-context paragraph matching (show each word's
sentence from the text, cold, and have students guess the meaning from context before confirming):

| Word | Paragraph | Student-friendly definition |
|---|---|---|
| turntable | [B] | the machine used to play a vinyl record |
| needle | [B] | the small pointed part of a turntable that touches the record to play sound |
| groove | [B] | the thin spiral line cut into a record that stores the sound |
| crackle | [B] | a soft popping or hissing sound a record makes as it plays |
| pressing | [B] | one printed copy, or a batch, of a vinyl record made from a mold |
| warmth | [C] | a full, rich quality of sound that some listeners say vinyl has |

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight (2 min):** The text uses two expressions worth flagging before reading:
- "a dying breed" [E] - an opaque idiom: gloss it directly rather than asking students to infer it. "A 'dying
  breed' is something or someone that is becoming rare and may disappear soon."
- "get hooked on" [E] - a transparent chunk: gloss it lightly. "If you 'get hooked on' something, you become very
  interested in it, almost unable to stop."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min)

**Strategy: Think-Aloud Modeling.** Read the text aloud to the class. At each `[THINK-ALOUD: ...]` marker, stop
and verbalize your own reasoning process out loud, modeling how a skilled reader thinks while reading, rather
than checking student comprehension at that point. Students listen and, after the modeled thought, jot one word
or phrase capturing your reasoning in the margin next to that marker.

**Anchor Text (Level 5, 434 words, 6 paragraphs)**

> **The Vinyl Comeback Is Real**
>
> [A] Every Saturday morning, a small crowd gathers outside Deadwax Records before the shop even opens. They are
> waiting for first pick of the crates stacked along the back wall. [THINK-ALOUD: I notice the writer opens with
> a scene, not a statistic. I'm guessing that's a choice: showing the crowd first makes the argument feel real
> before any claim gets made.] Ten years ago, a line like this would have seemed unlikely, since vinyl was a
> format for collectors and grandparents back then, not for anyone under thirty. Today, independent record shops
> are reopening in cities that had not seen one in decades, and most of the customers are young.
>
> [B] Inside, the appeal is not hard to understand. A staff member drops a new arrival onto the turntable, and
> the needle settles into the outer groove. A soft crackle rises before the first note plays, and customers spend
> an hour flipping through crates and reading liner notes. They ask staff for recommendations, the way people
> once asked a trusted friend rather than an app. Once a year, the shop's busiest day is Record Store Day¹, when
> a line stretches around the block for pressings that will never be printed again.
>
> [C] Ask a regular customer why they bother, when the same album is one tap away on a phone. [THINK-ALOUD:
> I'm going to keep reading and watch for the specific words that follow, since "why they bother" is exactly
> the question I want the writer to answer next.] Most give some version of the same answer: streaming is
> convenient, but it isn't the same. They talk about the warmth of a record's sound, a fullness a compressed
> digital file smooths away. They also talk about the small ritual of choosing an album, not just a song, and
> living with that choice for forty minutes.
>
> [D] The numbers back up what the shop floor suggests, since industry trackers report that vinyl sales have
> climbed for well over a decade. In several recent years, records have even outsold CDs in the United States, a
> shift almost no one in the music business predicted. For some listeners, at least, this is not simple
> nostalgia. Younger buyers, many too young to have owned a turntable the first time vinyl was popular, are
> choosing the format on its own terms.
>
> [E] Independent record shops were once assumed to be a dying breed*, squeezed out first by big chain stores
> and then by streaming services. [THINK-ALOUD: the writer just called shops a "dying breed," and I want to
> notice how that word choice makes the shops sound almost like an animal facing extinction, a stronger, more
> emotional image than just saying "closing down."] Instead, customers keep getting hooked on† the ritual these
> shops offer. That ritual includes the crate-digging, the staff recommendations, and the chance to hold a
> pressing in your hands before you have even heard it.
>
> [F] None of this means streaming will disappear, and no one serious argues that it should. But the record
> shop's return says something worth taking seriously, since convenience was never the only thing listeners
> wanted from music. A generation raised on instant, frictionless access is now paying extra, on purpose, for a
> little friction back.
>
> *a dying breed: something or someone that is becoming rare and may disappear soon
>
> †get hooked on: to become very interested in something, almost unable to stop
>
> ¹ Record Store Day is an annual event, started in the United States in 2007, when independent record shops
> release special limited-edition pressings and hold in-store events to celebrate vinyl and support local shops.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, satisfies Section 0.7):** After reading, return to the Mystery Quote guesses
collected on the board during the hook. As a class, check each guess against Paragraph [C]: which guesses come
closest to the writer's actual point about vinyl's "warmth"? This only exists once the class's own guesses are
on the board to check.

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-Minute Partner Re-Scan:** Pairs re-scan "The Vinyl Comeback Is Real" for key details and paragraph markers
(no new reading, just relocating).

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min, unrelated topic, recycles Day 1 vocabulary):**

> **My Grandfather's Turntable**
>
> Last summer, my grandfather pulled an old turntable out of his garage and asked me to help him fix it. Dust
> covered the needle, and the drive belt underneath had cracked with age. Neither of us had ever opened a machine
> like this before, so we looked up a repair guide and worked through it step by step, checking each part
> against the pictures. We cleaned each part slowly, and once it finally powered on, he set his favorite record
> on top and lowered the needle into the first groove himself. A soft crackle came through the speaker before the
> music started, and he smiled like he hadn't heard that sound in twenty years. He told me the record was an
> early pressing, one of the first copies ever printed, and that its warmth was something a new copy could never
> quite match. We spent the rest of the afternoon listening to his old records together, and for the first time,
> I understood why he had kept that machine all these years.

Task (light-touch, 3-4 min): Circle the 6 target words (turntable, needle, groove, crackle, pressing, warmth)
wherever they appear, then tell a partner in one sentence what the grandfather and the shop from Day 1 have in
common.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Oral round-robin: each student picks one target word and uses it as
either a noun or a verb where possible ("She presses new records at the factory" vs. "This pressing is rare.").

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Form groups of 4 (one student per task Level: 4, 5, 6, 7). Each student completes their own task Level below as a
prep tool (10 min), then the group combines findings into a shared Collaborative Evidence Matrix on chart paper
or a shared doc (10 min), then reports out in Phase 3's board-dependent moment (see below).

**Task Level 4 (extension-down, Level 4 ceiling: familiar-object description, one feature, one comparison, one
stated reason):**
Using Paragraphs [B]-[C] only: (1) Name one feature of the shop experience. (2) The text compares listening to
vinyl to something. What is it compared to? (3) What reason does the text give for why customers prefer vinyl?
Word bank provided: *streaming, one tap away, warmth, the ritual of choosing an album.* Sentence starter: "One
feature of the shop is ___. The text compares vinyl to ___ because ___."

This task also carries a genuine, simplified interpretive component (Respectful Tiers check): (4) Which part of
the shop experience sounds more interesting to you, flipping through crates or hearing the crackle? Circle one
and complete: "I think ___ sounds more interesting because the text describes it as ___."

**Task Level 5 (native, anchor level ceiling: summarize organization, identify one evaluative word):**
(1) In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the editorial is organized. (Hint: does it move from a scene, to a
comparison, to facts, to a closing point, or some other way?) (2) Find one word in Paragraph [E] that clearly
signals the writer's attitude toward record shops, even though the writer never says "I like record shops"
directly. Explain what that word suggests.

**Task Level 6 (native, Level 6 ceiling: separate stated fact from evaluative language, locate a register shift):**
(1) List two facts stated directly in Paragraph [D] (no opinion words). (2) List one clearly evaluative phrase from
Paragraph [E] that shows judgment rather than fact. (3) Locate the place in Paragraph [D] where the writer's tone
shifts from a confident claim ("vinyl sales have climbed for well over a decade") to something closer to hedging
("For some listeners, at least"). Quote both halves of the shift.

**Task Level 7 (extension-up, Level 7 ceiling: compare two texts, identify an unstated interest):** See the second
text below. (1) List one detail the editorial includes that the industry report leaves out, and one detail the
report includes that the editorial leaves out. (2) Both texts describe the same trend but create a different
impression. In 2-3 sentences, describe that difference. (3) Identify one unstated commercial interest behind the
industry report's framing: what does the report's publisher gain by describing the trend the way it does, and
where in the text can you see that interest working even though it's never stated directly?

**Second comparison text for Task Level 7 only (Level 7 ceiling, industry-report framing, ~139 words):**

> **Vinyl Manufacturing: A Growth Sector Ready for Investment**
>
> Demand for vinyl records has outpaced pressing capacity for the fourth consecutive year, creating a rare
> opportunity for investors entering the analog audio manufacturing space. Independent pressing plants currently
> report order backlogs averaging six to nine months, a bottleneck that has only increased as consumer interest
> continues its steady, well-documented climb. Industry analysis shows this demand is broad-based, spanning
> multiple age groups and genres, and shows no signs of slowing. New pressing equipment, largely unchanged in
> basic design for decades, remains scarce and expensive to manufacture, limiting how quickly existing plants can
> expand. Investors who enter this space now, before larger manufacturers move to close the capacity gap, are
> well positioned to benefit from a market that combines strong consumer loyalty with limited supply. Our firm
> offers detailed capacity and demand projections for qualified investors interested in this sector.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 3, see below):** this lesson's Day 2 board-dependent moment lives in Phase 3,
matched to the Town Hall protocol, rather than in Phase 2 - see the Board-Dependent Moment Rotation note at the
end of this lesson.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Town Hall Role-Play

Set up the room as a town hall with several discussion tables, each working the same rotation of prompts below.
Groups stay together for the full window; every seat is already active inside a small group at all times, so no
outer-circle scaffolding is needed. Advance each table to the next prompt at each changeover (roughly every 8-10
minutes across the 25-30 minute window) rather than discussing one prompt the whole time:

1. Describe the shop experience from Day 1's text. How does browsing a record shop compare to browsing a
   streaming app? Use one specific detail from the text to support your description.
2. Describe how listeners talk about the sound of vinyl compared to digital music. What specific words does the
   writer use to show this, and what impression do those words create?
3. Describe what is happening with vinyl sales right now. What is one reason the text gives for why this is
   happening?

**Sentence stems by task Level (register capped at each Level's own ceiling):**
- Level 4: "One difference between a record shop and a streaming app is ___, because the text says ___."
- Level 5: "The writer's word choice, like ___, shows their stance because ___."
- Level 6: "The writer states a fact when they say ___, but shifts to a more careful claim when they say ___."
- Level 7: "Comparing the two texts, I noticed the [industry report/editorial] frames this as ___, which serves
  the unstated interest of ___."

**Differentiated participation (Section 0.8, Part B):** The Level 4 student does not take an on-demand turn at
their table alongside the higher task Levels. Instead: before their table's first prompt, they rehearse their
stem once, quietly, with a same-table partner, and get a thumbs-up; their first contribution at the table is then
already a repetition, not a cold performance. Alternatively, if the teacher judges the student needs more support
that day: for the first prompt, they take a listening/tracking role - a tally sheet marking how many times their
table mentions "sound" versus "experience" as a reason for preferring vinyl - with one fully-prepared sentence to
contribute at a natural pause.

**Foundation Support (for a student functioning below Level 4's own floor):** Provide a picture card set showing
a turntable, a vinyl record, and a phone screen with a streaming app. The student's Phase 3 role is physical, not
spoken: they hold up the picture card that matches whatever is currently being discussed at their table, and,
during the Mystery Quote board check from Day 1, they can point to the paragraph letter a partner names rather
than reading it aloud. Pair this student with a peer buddy throughout Phase 2's matrix work as well.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 3, satisfies Section 0.7):** Once each table's window on the first prompt ends,
one member from each table reports one notable claim or detail back to the board, building a shared list live:
"What listeners say they get from vinyl that streaming doesn't give them." No single table's own notes contain
the complete list; it exists only once every table has reported in.

**Closing Transfer Check (5 min, ends Phase 3):** In pairs, every student produces one new instance of the Day 1
Skill Spotlight skill, applied to something other than records. Sentence frame: "I would describe [something in
this room] by comparing it to ___, because ___," or for Levels 5-7: "Notice how I just used the word ___ to show
I think this is [good/well-made], without saying it directly." Say it out loud to a partner. The teacher
cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share aloud. Nothing is collected or graded.

**Board-Dependent Moment Rotation note:** this lesson places its two board-dependent moments in Phase 1 (Day 1,
the Mystery Quote guess-and-check) and Phase 3 (Day 2, the Town Hall report-back), rather than Phase 1 + Phase 2
as Lesson 2 did, keeping the required slot genuinely varied across this Set's cycles rather than settling into
its own template.
