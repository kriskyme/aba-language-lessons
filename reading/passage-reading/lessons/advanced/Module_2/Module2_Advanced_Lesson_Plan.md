# Module 2 (Narrating), Advanced Band - Lesson Plan

**Module:** 2, Narrating | **Band:** Advanced | **Task Levels:** 4, 5, 6, 7 | **Set:** 1 (Lessons 1-4)
**Planned:** 2026-09-14, against `Generate_Module_Lesson_Plan_Prompt_v2.2.md`

This plan covers **Set 1** of Module 2 for the Advanced Band: 4 lessons, 8 instructional days. Set numbering
restarts at 1 for a new Module and lesson numbering restarts with it, since lesson numbers are global within a
Module/Band (`shared/Program_Conventions.md` §C). Version codes therefore run `2.1.1.0` to `2.1.4.0` (§G).

No anchor text, item, vocabulary list, or answer key is written here. That is Step 2's job, two lessons per pass.

---

## Band calibration

The anchor text is calibrated to the band's **lower Level, Level 5** (Conventions §A): 400-460 words, 5-7
paragraphs, average sentence length 17-24 words, mixed past/present with one subordinate clause per sentence,
everyday abstract plus 4-6 pre-taught topic words (Lesson prompt 0.2). Task Levels are 4, 5, 6, 7 (Conventions
§B): one extension-down, two native, one extension-up.

**Level 7 needs a second text in every lesson.** The Level 7 Reading row for this Module requires *two* extended
narratives of the same or related events, so each lesson carries a short second narrative for its extension-up
task only, embedded inline in the packet - the same treatment Module 1 Set 1's Lessons 2-4 used for their Level 7
tasks. It is not a second anchor: Levels 4, 5, and 6 work the main text alone.

### Task-Level basis (Reading rows, `learningobjectives.csv`, Module 2: Narrating, quoted verbatim)

| Task Level | Role | Reading Can-do (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Extension-down (1 band below) | "Can read a short narrative text and identify the sequence of events, one outcome, and the brief reason given for that outcome, decoding unfamiliar words as needed." |
| 5 | Native lower (anchor Level) | "Can read an extended narration, summarize the sequence of events and why they mattered, and identify at least one clearly telling action or detail that signals how someone felt, even though the feeling is never named directly, decoding unfamiliar words as needed." |
| 6 | Native upper | "Can read an extended narrative and identify specific pacing choices as heightening or minimizing significance, separating stated events from clearly-signaled reactions, and locate at least one place where the narrative's tone visibly shifts partway through (e.g. from neutral to noticeably more sympathetic language)." |
| 7 | Extension-up (1 band above) | "Can read two extended narratives covering the same or related events, critically assess how each writer's structural choices shape what the reader is meant to conclude, and identify at least one unstated motive or interest behind one writer's structural choices, using sophisticated inference and register awareness." |

What each Level's task does, in every lesson of this Set:

- **Level 4** traces the order of events and names the one outcome plus the reason the text states for it. The
  reason is always directly stated, never implied (0.2's Level 4 row).
- **Level 5** summarizes the sequence and why it mattered, and names one concrete action or detail that signals a
  feeling the text never labels. The signalling detail is a countable, physical action, not an adjective.
- **Level 6** separates stated events from clearly-signaled reactions, names one pacing choice (a stretched
  moment, a compressed span) and what it makes the reader weigh more heavily, and locates the paragraph where
  the tone visibly shifts. Motive for the shift is out of scope here; that is Level 7's.
- **Level 7** compares the main text against the lesson's second narrative of the same events, assesses what each
  writer's structure is built to make the reader conclude, and names one unstated interest behind one of them.
  Quality Standards §C9 governs the comparison: both texts carry at least three verbatim excerpts on the same
  shared subjects, written into the lesson `.md` itself, never a one-line summary of what each covers.

**Module verb check.** Every item in this Set exercises Narrating: sequence, outcome, pacing, tone shift across
a told sequence of events. Cause-and-effect item sets belong to Module 3 (Explaining) and are not used here;
Day 1 Phase 3's native-Level set is a **Sequence & Significance** set, not a Cause & Effect set (see the note
under Known risks below).

## Lesson 1 - The Laugavegur Trail

- **Topic / anchor idea:** an invented hiker's field journal of four days walking the Laugavegur trail in
  Iceland, hut to hut from Landmannalaugar to Thorsmork. The trail, the huts, the river crossings, and the
  terrain are real and verifiable; the hiker and the journal are invented. No real person is quoted.
- **Genre:** travel / field-journal narrative. Dated entry headings, first-person voice, one entry per day.
- **Reading strategy:** Say Something. Short chunks separated by `[PAUSE: say something to your partner]` at
  entry boundaries, so partners react before the next day's entry.
- **Phase 1 hook:** Mystery Quote. One line lifted from the last entry printed with no context; students guess
  on the board what has happened and to whom, checked off against the text after reading.
- **Phase 3 protocol:** Jigsaw Expert Panels. The 2-3 rotated prompts are split across groups so simultaneous
  conversations are not identical (Lesson prompt 0.10).
- **Vocabulary theme:** hiking and volcanic terrain (4-6 pre-taught words; no overlap with any other lesson in
  this Set and none with Module 1 Set 1's architecture, metalworking, music, or lighthouse fields).
- **Level 7 second text:** the trail association's own short route description of the same four stages, written
  to recruit walkers. Its unstated interest is filling huts; the journal's is being believed.
- **Board-dependent moment:** Day 1 Phase 1 (the Mystery Quote guess board, checked off after reading) and Day 2
  Phase 2 (a live sequence-and-significance timeline built as each group reports its Level's findings).
- **Closing Transfer Check:** narrate, in one sentence, how you got to class today, including the one moment
  that mattered most.
- **Interactivity note:** partner talk carries the reading itself (Say Something); Day 2's investigation is
  group report-back onto a shared timeline. No written task outside the matrix cells.
- **Image:** none. The hook is a printed quotation.

## Lesson 2 - Six Days in the Suez Canal

- **Topic / anchor idea:** an invented maritime-logistics account's post thread narrating the six days in March
  2021 when the container ship Ever Given ran aground and blocked the Suez Canal. The ship, the grounding, the
  dredging and tug work, the queue of waiting vessels, and the refloating are real and verifiable; the account,
  its handle, and every reply are invented. No real person or company spokesperson is quoted.
- **Genre:** social media post thread. Labeled original post, then labeled numbered replies in sequence, casual
  register, one hashtag where natural.
- **Reading strategy:** Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops. `[STOP & CHECK]` points between posts, each
  bracket carrying the question only, with the expected answer on an `Answer note:` line beneath.
- **Phase 1 hook:** Visual Inquiry, on a real embedded photo of the grounded ship (see Image below).
- **Phase 3 protocol:** Panel Round. 4-6 speakers in turn from their seats; every listener keeps a running list
  of the three moments a speaker treats as the turning point (the listener task Narrating takes under 0.10), and
  the panel and listeners swap at each prompt changeover.
- **Vocabulary theme:** shipping and supply chains.
- **Level 7 second text:** a short shipping-line statement thread covering the same six days, structured to open
  on the refloating and treat the grounding as weather. Its unstated interest is liability.
- **Board-dependent moment:** Day 1 Phase 3 (a class timeline of the six days assembled from what each task
  Level's set surfaced) and Day 2 Phase 3 (the Panel Round's running record of turning-point claims as spoken).
  This rotates the Day 1 slot away from Lesson 1's Phase 1.
- **Closing Transfer Check:** narrate, in one sentence, the last thing you ate and the one moment in getting it
  that mattered.
- **Interactivity note:** whole-class read-aloud with stops on Day 1; oral panel with a listener tally on Day 2.
- **Image:** one, for the Visual Inquiry hook. Fetch from Wikimedia Commons at generation time per Conventions
  §I (a photo of the Ever Given aground in the canal, 2021), save as
  `Lesson2_EverGiven_Img_Hook.jpg`, cite at the point of use in the `.md`, and open
  `Set1_Advanced_Image_Credits.md` in `Module_2/Set_1/` with its row. No packet credit (Style Guide §F).

## Lesson 3 - The Night the Repair Cafe Ran Long

- **Topic / anchor idea:** an invented volunteer's letter to an advice column narrating an evening at a Repair
  Cafe where a repair went wrong and a visitor left angry, plus the columnist's reply. The Repair Cafe movement
  (volunteer-run free repair sessions, begun in Amsterdam in 2009, now several thousand worldwide) is real and
  verifiable; the volunteer, the visitor, the columnist, and the evening are invented.
- **Genre:** advice-column letter with response. The letter carries its own salutation and sign-off, then the
  columnist's reply with a distinct signature, the two voices visually separated.
- **Reading strategy:** Partner / Shared Reading. Partner A reads the letter's paragraphs, Partner B the reply's,
  with turn-checking prompts at the handover; paragraph letters stay bracketed and partner roles spelled out
  (0.9 Part A).
- **Phase 1 hook:** K-W-L Chart. K and W columns printed in the packet, filled in individually, compared with a
  neighbor, and built live on the board from what students report; the L column returns in Day 2 Phase 1.
- **Phase 3 protocol:** Town Hall Role-Play, run from students' seats, 2-3 prompts split across groups.
- **Vocabulary theme:** repair and secondhand goods.
- **Level 7 second text:** the same evening as the visitor would tell it, posted as a short review of the
  session. Its unstated interest is being owed something; the letter's is being told it was not their fault.
- **Board-dependent moment:** Day 1 Phase 1 (the K and W columns) and Day 2 Phase 1 (the L column added to the
  same chart, so the board persists across the two days - the D4 "built on in Day 2" property).
- **Closing Transfer Check:** narrate, in one sentence, a time something you own stopped working, and what
  happened next.
- **Interactivity note:** paired reading with role handover, then a seated town hall; the K-W-L is the only
  written individual moment before Day 2's matrix.
- **Image:** none.

## Lesson 4 - The Mosaic Under the Wheat

- **Topic / anchor idea:** a feature-style article reconstructing how a Roman mosaic showing scenes from the
  Trojan War came to be found under a wheat field in Rutland, England, in 2020 and excavated in 2021: the
  pottery noticed at the field edge, the first trench, the aerial survey, the full dig, the scheduling of the
  site. The find, the site, and the sequence are real and verifiable. Per the plan prompt's guardrail, the
  people in the article are composites and unnamed by real name; no real finder, archaeologist, or official is
  named or quoted.
- **Genre:** feature-style newspaper article. Headline, dateline, short paragraphs, a quoted composite site
  supervisor.
- **Reading strategy:** Close Reading with Annotation. Guided annotation key plus margin space for individual
  marking before discussion.
- **Phase 1 hook:** Take a Side. A printed four-option choice about what should happen to a find like this,
  marked in the packet, then discussed with someone who marked it differently; a show of hands gives the board
  tally.
- **Phase 3 protocol:** Rotating Partners. Students find a new partner for each of the 2-3 prompts, so no
  listener task is needed (0.10).
- **Vocabulary theme:** archaeology and excavation.
- **Level 7 second text:** a short county heritage-service account of the same excavation, structured to open on
  protection and permissions rather than on the discovery. Its unstated interest is deterring unauthorized
  digging.
- **Board-dependent moment:** Day 1 Phase 1 (the Take a Side tally) and Day 2 Phase 2 (a live two-column board
  chart of stated events against signalled reactions, built as groups report).
- **Closing Transfer Check:** narrate, in one sentence, what you did in the five minutes before you sat down
  today, and which part was the real beginning of it.
- **Interactivity note:** individual annotation, then group synthesis, then pair rotation - the Set's only
  lesson whose reading step is silent and individual, deliberately placed last.
- **Image:** none. The hook is a printed choice, not a picture.

## Self-check

- **Genres:** travel/field-journal, social media thread, advice-column letter with response, feature-style
  article. Four distinct, none repeated in the Set, all from the Advanced bank (Lesson prompt 0.6). Lesson 1's
  travel journal differs from Module 1 Set 1's final lesson (interview-profile), satisfying the cross-Module
  adjacency rule (Conventions §F).
- **Strategies:** Say Something, Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops, Partner / Shared Reading, Close
  Reading with Annotation. Four distinct, no consecutive repeat, well above the 3-distinct floor. Lesson 1
  differs from Module 1's final lesson (Reader's Theater).
- **Hooks:** Mystery Quote, Visual Inquiry, K-W-L Chart, Take a Side - each used exactly once. Lesson 1 differs
  from Module 1's final lesson (Take a Side), which is why Take a Side is held to Lesson 4.
- **Protocols:** Jigsaw Expert Panels, Panel Round, Town Hall Role-Play, Rotating Partners - each once, and
  Lesson 1 differs from Module 1's final lesson (Rotating Partners), which is likewise held to Lesson 4. The one
  Panel Round lesson (2) carries an explicit listener task; every lesson carries 2-3 rotated prompts.
- **Vocabulary themes:** hiking and volcanic terrain / shipping and supply chains / repair and secondhand goods
  / archaeology and excavation. No overlap inside the Set; no echo of Module 1 Set 1's four themes
  (architecture and ornamentation, metalworking and forging, music and analog sound, coastal-lighthouse life).
- **Topics:** four distinct real, verifiable referents, as the band requires; no fabricated quote is attributed
  to any real named individual in any lesson (Lessons 2 and 4, the two with real events and real organizations
  behind them, use invented accounts and unnamed composites by design). No echo of Module 1's topics.
- **Task Levels:** exactly 4, the Advanced band's row, each traced to a quoted Reading CSV row above, each held
  to its own Level's ceiling and not the Level beyond.
- **Module verb:** Narrating across all four lessons - sequence, outcome, pacing, tone shift. No lesson's
  graded work is cause-and-effect, comparison-of-features, or verdict work.
- **Fixed-output Levels:** none. Advanced's Levels are 4-7, so 0.11 and Quality Standards §D10 do not apply to
  this Set.
- **Closing Transfer Checks:** the journey to class / the last thing you ate / a thing of yours that broke /
  the five minutes before sitting down. Four distinct scenarios, none reused from Module 1 Set 1 (clothing,
  furniture, a sound, the view out the window).
- **Board slots:** Lesson 1 Day1 P1 + Day2 P2; Lesson 2 Day1 P3 + Day2 P3; Lesson 3 Day1 P1 + Day2 P1; Lesson 4
  Day1 P1 + Day2 P2. The carrying slot varies across the Set, and Lesson 3's persists from Day 1 into Day 2.
- **Lesson numbering:** 1-4. Module 2 is a new Module for this Band, so numbering restarts with the Module
  (Conventions §C), and Set numbering restarts at 1 with it.

## Known risks to watch in Step 2

- **Day 1 Phase 3's native-Level set name.** `Generate_Lesson_Prompt_v3.5.md` names that set a "Cause & Effect
  set" as its default. Cause and effect is Module 3's verb; following the line literally here would drift a
  Narrating lesson into Explaining, which Quality Standards §A4 forbids. This Set uses a **Sequence &
  Significance** set for Levels 5 and 6 instead. Passage Reading's `Index.md` already logs the prompt fix
  (v3.6) as pending; these four lessons are to be generated with the corrected reading of that line, and their
  self-checks should say so explicitly.
- **§D12 has no Reading expression yet.** The commit / verify / return-unsupported arc is logged in `Index.md`
  as unbuilt for Passage Reading. These lessons will be generated against v3.5 as it stands, so they will carry
  no §D12 beat; that remains one backlog item, not four new ones.
- **Level 5 sentence-length calibration by genre.** Two of this Set's genres have strong native rhythms that
  pull against the Level 5 band (17-24 words average): a social media thread runs short, a field journal runs
  long and coordinated. Count average sentence length, not only total words, before finalizing either - the
  same failure Proficient Module 1 Lessons 3-4 hit and had to be rewritten for.
