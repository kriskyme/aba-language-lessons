The Floor Under the Wheat
Rutland, England
Mark It Up
underline a word you don't know
( circle ) a connector that shows time or order: then, after, later, by the time
[ bracket ] the sentence with the paragraph's main idea
? next to anything that surprises you
! next to a word that feels like the writer's opinion

[A] The field looks like every other field on this side of the county, and for most of the last seventy years it has grown wheat. In the summer of 2020 a man walking the edge of it picked a piece of pottery out of the topsoil, turned it over, and put it in his pocket. He went back to the same corner of that field four times that week before he telephoned anybody.

[B] What lay under the crop, about a metre down, was the floor of a Roman dining room, and set into it a mosaic eleven metres long showing Achilles dragging the body of Hector1 around the walls of Troy. No floor telling that story had been recorded anywhere in Britain before. The room was part of a large villa, and the mosaic was its floor, walked on at dinner.

[C] He reported it to the county archaeologist in the end, and a fortnight later somebody came out to look. The first trench went in that autumn, two metres by one, on the strength of the pottery and an aerial survey that had shown a pale rectangle in the standing crop. It reached coloured stone almost at once. The team filled the trench in again, left the field to the winter, and told hardly anybody.

[D] The full dig ran for ten days the following August, in heat, with a line of people on their knees excavating by hand and lifting fragments of burnt plaster into trays. The room had been damaged by fire some time after it was built, and somebody had later cut a grave down through the floor. Photographs were taken every evening, and the trays went up to the farm track at dusk. "Nothing about this place is tidy," one of the site supervisors said. "People went on using the room long after it stopped being grand, and that is the part I cannot stop thinking about."

[E] Word got out in November, when the site was given legal protection2, which makes digging there without permission a criminal offence and has a great deal to do with people who do not dig above board. Two questions came back more than any others: can it be seen, and who owns it? It cannot be seen. It was recorded, photographed, and buried again, which is what usually happens, because a mosaic is safer under a field than under a roof nobody has yet paid for.

[F] The family still farm the field. They plough around a rectangle now, and the wheat above the dining room comes up exactly the same colour as the wheat either side of it, which is how nobody noticed for seventeen hundred years in the first place.

1. Achilles and Hector: two warriors on opposite sides in the Trojan War, a story told in Greek poetry and known all over the Roman world. Achilles kills Hector and drags his body behind a chariot.
2. Legal protection: in Britain, an important site can be put on a national list that makes it a crime to dig or build there without permission. The land can still be farmed.

[G] A nationally important Roman site in this county was added to the protected list in November following a programme of survey and controlled excavation carried out under licence by a professional team.
