## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  Take a Side,          Reader's Theater             Decode and Match
  Spotlight, Sort         The Fruit Stand              Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Take a Side hook (4 min):** the packet prints three fruits as a printed choice. Every student circles the
one they would buy, then a show of hands is counted for each option.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, this lesson's Day 1 slot):** tally the show of hands on the board, one
column per fruit, and write beside each column the one describing word students call out for it. The class's
full picture of what they picked and why exists only in that board tally; no handout carries it.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing a thing by its size and its shape, the way
Nina describes one fruit as small and round and another as long." Say the same sentence to the whole class;
do not narrate who is doing which version. (This is the exact skill Day 2's Closing Transfer Check will ask
you to reproduce.)

**Pre-teach the 6 target words (5 min), with in-context paragraph matching:** apple, banana, big, small,
round, long. Say each word, sound it out, and show the paragraph letter where it appears: apple [B],
round [B], small [B], banana [C], long [C], big [D].

**Sort first (4 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot, and a whole-class pre-teach activity rather than a
differentiated task: everyone sorts the same bank before any frame sentence is read. The bank is padded with
two already-familiar near-miss words (`shared/Generation_Quality_Standards.md` §C4).

Word bank: apple, banana, egg, cup, big, small, round, long.

Sort the eight words into two groups: **things you can eat**, and **words that tell you about a thing**.
**Answer note:** things you can eat = apple, banana, egg; describing words = big, small, round, long. *Cup*
belongs in neither group and is there to be left out.

Then read the frame aloud once, as a class, with one word from the describing group: "It is ___." This is the
only time the frame is printed on Day 1; every later task on Day 1 refers to it rather than reprinting it.

**No Idiom/Slang Spotlight in this lesson.** Section 0.4 starts the idiom requirement at Level 2 and up; this
anchor is calibrated to Level 1.

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Reader's Theater

Three speaking roles: **Nina**, **Raj**, and the **Seller**. Cast three students, then swap the cast twice so
nine students read in all; the rest follow the script with a finger on the paragraph letters. Rehearsal cue:
each cast reads their own lines once quietly to a neighbour before performing them. The stage directions in
brackets are spoken by nobody; they tell the readers what to do.

---

**The Fruit Stand**

**[A]** *[Nina and Raj stop at a fruit stand. The Seller stands behind the fruit.]*

**[B]** **Nina:** Look at this apple on the fruit stand. It is small and round in my hand. I like small
fruit better than big fruit.

**[C]** **Raj:** I want a banana from the same stand. It is long and it bends in the middle. It is small, so
it fits in my bag.

**[D]** **Seller:** Then look at my big pineapple over here. It is not small at all. One pineapple feeds a
whole family at home.

**[E]** **Nina:** *[laughs]* It is much too big for my school bag! I take the small apple, please.

**[F]** **Raj:** And I take the long banana, please. We can eat them at school today.

---

No contextual footnotes are needed (Section 0.9 Part B): the text names no real person, place, or cultural
reference that the sentence does not already explain.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Decode and match (15 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot, and each Level's one production for Day 1.
Every item points at Picture 1, Picture 2, and Picture 3, printed in the packet.

- **Level 1 (★), four items.** Sound out the printed word or phrase, then circle the picture it describes.
  1. **apple** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  2. **banana** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
  3. **a small apple** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  4. **a long banana** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
- **Level 2 (★★), three items.** Read the frame sentence and circle the picture it describes.
  1. It is round. (Picture 1 / Picture 2)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  2. It is long. (Picture 1 / Picture 2)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
  3. It is big. (Picture 1 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3.
- **Level 3 (★★★), two items.** Read the two-slot frame and circle the picture it describes. Both open-slot
  words have to fit, not just one.
  1. It is small and round. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1. Paragraph [B] gives the apple both words; Picture 2 is small but long, and
     Picture 3 is not small.
  2. It is small and long. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2. Paragraph [C] gives the banana both words; Picture 1 is small but round.

Item counts are tiered for balanced time on task: the lowest Level gets more, shorter items, the highest
fewer and deeper ones.

**Vocabulary context completion (10 min)** - use the 6 target words from Phase 1, one word per blank. Each
sentence names its own subject rather than reusing the "It is ___." frame, so the frame is still produced
only once per Level per day (Quality Standards §D10).

Nina picks up an ______. The apple is ______ and ______. Raj wants a ______. The banana is ______. The
pineapple is too ______ for a bag.

**Answer note:** apple, small, round, banana, long, big.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher      Read to a Partner Matrix     Panel Round + Closing
    Text, Vocab Drill        (Board T-Chart)              Transfer Check
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**Partner re-scan (3 min):** in pairs, find the paragraph letter where each fruit is first named: the apple,
the banana, the pineapple.
**Answer note:** [B], [C], [D].

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated everyday scene, recycling 5 of the 6 target words:

*The Lunch Box*

Raj opens his lunch box at school. A banana sits on top. It is long, and it bends in the middle. Under it he
finds a small round egg and a big roll of bread.

*Task (3-4 min):* find and circle the five words you learned yesterday as you read: banana, long, small,
round, big. Then tell a partner in one sentence what is in the lunch box.
**Answer note:** a banana, an egg, and a roll of bread.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** oral opposites drill. The teacher says a word, the class says the
opposite: big / small, long / short. Then each student says one spoken sentence using the frame "It is ___."
about a fruit they ate this week.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability triads, one student per task Level (three task Levels at this Band, so triads rather than
groups of four). Each student does their own Level's cell first as rehearsal, then the triad shares out loud
and puts one answer on the board.

**Read to a partner (the 0.11 shape for this slot).** Every cell has the student read a completed line out
loud to the partner beside them, who says which picture it describes or names the paragraph letter it came
from. The reading is the task; the partner's answer is how it is checked.

**Level 1 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 1 Reading objective ("Can decode an unfamiliar simple
word or short adjective-noun phrase... and match it to the object or image it describes, with visual
support.")
- Task: read the phrase **a long banana** out loud to your partner. Your partner says which picture it
  describes.
  **Answer note:** Picture 2.
- Respectful Tiers add-on (a genuine, scaffolded interpretive step, not fact-copying alone): say which fruit
  is easiest to carry to school, and circle the printed word that tells you why: *small* / *big*.
  **Answer note:** either fruit is accepted if the student circles the size word that fits the one they
  chose.

**Level 2 (native)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... to identify the matching
image, with visual support.")
- Task: read **It is big.** out loud to your partner. Your partner says which picture it describes, and then
  which paragraph letter says so.
  **Answer note:** Picture 3, Paragraph [D].

**Level 3 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
two-slot frame describing a familiar object, decoding both open-slot words... to identify the matching
image.")
- Task: read **It is small and round.** out loud to your partner. Your partner says which picture it
  describes, and then says which word rules out Picture 2.
  **Answer note:** Picture 1; *round* rules out Picture 2, which is long.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, this lesson's Day 2 slot and the preferred anchor slot):** as each triad
reports, build a two-column T-chart on the board, **Round** and **Long**, and write in the fruit plus the
describing word each triad actually says. The full chart exists only once every triad has reported; no one
handout has it.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Panel Round

Four to six students of a typical 8-12 speak in turn from their seats; nobody moves. The panel and the
listeners swap at each changeover, roughly every 8-10 minutes across the window, and a new speaker brings
their listening notes as live material.

**The listeners are never just listening.** While the panel talks, every student not currently speaking keeps
a running list of three describing words a speaker used well. A student taking a turn may read from that
list.

1. Which fruit would you buy at this stand? Say what it is like.
2. Nina says the pineapple is too big for her bag. What fruit fits in your bag? Say why.
3. Think of a fruit that is not on this stand. Say two things about it.

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own ceiling):
- **Level 1 (★):** "A small ___ ." or "A long ___ ."
- **Level 2 (★★):** "It is ___ ."
- **Level 3 (★★★):** "It is ___ and ___ ."

**Differentiated participation (Level 1):** before their first turn on the panel, the Level 1 student
rehearses their one phrase quietly with the person next to them and gets a thumbs-up, so their first public
turn is a repetition rather than an on-demand production. Their version of the listener's running list is
that same differentiated task, not a second requirement.

**Foundation Support (if a student is working below Level 1):** matching a printed word in the packet to the
same word in the script, and a two-choice circle instead of an open completion. Nothing here needs a card set
the teacher has to make, and nothing needs an object to be present in the room.

**Closing Transfer Check:** in pairs, out loud, every student describes **their own jacket** by its size and
shape, using the frame at their star level ("a long jacket" / "It is long." / "It is long and big."). They
describe it from what they know of it; nothing is taken out, held up, or pointed at. Teacher cold-calls two
or three pairs to share. Nothing is collected or graded. This is the demonstration that the Skill Spotlight's
skill transferred to a thing the lesson never described.

## Self-check (Section 0.3)

Quality Standards §F ran first; the Reading-specific items follow.

1. **Word count:** the anchor is 101 spoken words, inside Level 1's 60-120 range and in its middle part
   (115 counted with the speaker labels and the stage direction). Counted, not estimated.
2. **Paragraph count and average sentence length:** 6 lettered parts (one stage direction and five speaking
   turns), 13 sentences, average 7.8 words per sentence - inside Level 1's 6-10 band. The 2-4 line exchange
   is one of the three forms Level 1 names; six short turns keeps it inside that shape while giving the two
   days enough text.
3. **Clause complexity:** every sentence is simple present, each with a single clause except two that join
   two clauses with one coordinator ("It is long and it bends in the middle"; "It is small, so it fits in my
   bag"). Neither uses two coordinators. No subordination and no passive.
4. **Vocabulary scan:** every content word is concrete and high-frequency (fruit, stand, apple, banana, bag,
   hand, books, family, school, lunch, egg, bread). Zero abstract nouns. Nothing that belongs two bands higher. *Pineapple* is the one
   longer word; it is never decoded as a task and is carried by its picture.
5. **Fact density:** two invented first names, no dates, no numbers, no named places.
6. **Idioms (0.4):** none, and none required - the idiom rule begins at Level 2 and this anchor is calibrated
   to Level 1. Recorded here so the packet prompt does not look for a Phrase Spotlight.
7. **Refresher text (0.5):** 35 words against the anchor's 101, inside the one-third-to-one-half band;
   unrelated scene (a lunch box at school, not a fruit stand); recycles 5 of the 6 target words (all but
   *apple*); introduces no new grammar and no new content word beyond ordinary connective language.
8. **Genre (0.6):** simple 2-4 line dialogue, straight from the Beginner bank, and a real rotation from
   Lesson 2's postcard, with speaker labels and turn-based formatting per the genre's own convention.
9. **Paragraph lettering and footnotes (0.9):** every part is lettered [A] to [F] and referred to by letter
   in the pre-teach, the re-scan, and two Answer notes. No footnotes, and none needed.
10. **Phase 3 protocol (0.10):** Panel Round with three rotated prompts advanced at each changeover, and an
    explicit listener task (a running list of three describing words) for every student not speaking. All
    from students' seats.
11. **Reading strategy:** Reader's Theater, with three named roles, bracketed stage directions, a rehearsal
    cue, and two cast swaps - a real rotation from Lesson 2's Echo Reading.
12. **Fixed-output Levels (0.11):** three shapes, each in its own slot - Sort first (Day 1 Phase 1), Decode
    and match (Day 1 Phase 3), Read to a partner (Day 2 Phase 2) - plus Transfer at Day 2 Phase 3. No shape
    appears twice, and none sits in the same slot as Lesson 2's: Lesson 2 had nothing at Day 1 Phase 1
    (Sort first here), Choose what fits at Day 1 Phase 3 (Decode and match here), Fix the wrong word at
    Day 2 Phase 1 (nothing here), and Decode and match at Day 2 Phase 2 (Read to a partner here). Each Level
    produces its own form once per day.
13. **Answer notes:** every expected answer sits on an **Answer note:** line under its item. No stem or
    parenthetical states what its item is asking for.
14. **Module verb:** every task stays inside Describing - a thing, its size, and its shape. No
    cause-and-effect set appears, because inference is outside Level 1's ceiling (0.2) and Module 1's
    comprehension work centres on descriptive features (0.1).
15. **Board-dependent moment:** one genuine instance per day, in different slots - the Take a Side tally on
    Day 1 Phase 1, the Round/Long T-chart on Day 2 Phase 2.
16. **Task Levels:** exactly three (1, 2, 3), each traced to its own verbatim Reading CSV row. No fourth
    Level and no star beyond three anywhere.
17. **Self-contained (§D8, §D9):** the only materials are this document and the packet. All three pictures
    are embedded. The Closing Transfer Check uses a belonging the student describes from memory, with no
    instruction to look at, hold, or take out anything, and no task depends on an earlier lesson.
18. **Room-neutral (§D11):** nothing moves furniture, forms a circle, or posts anything on a wall. Take a
    Side is a printed circle plus a show of hands, and the Panel Round is spoken from seats.
19. **Style:** no em-dashes; ASCII pacing diagram present for each day; metadata line under the H1 carrying

20. **Adjacency and overlap across the Set (§D6, §D3):** genre, reading strategy, Phase 1 hook, and Phase 3
