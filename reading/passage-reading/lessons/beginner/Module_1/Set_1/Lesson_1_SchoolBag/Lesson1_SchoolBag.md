## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  Visual Inquiry,      Read-Aloud w/ Stops          Decode and Match
  Spotlight, Sort         Bag Check labels           Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Visual Inquiry hook (4 min):** Display Picture 1, the school bag (`Lesson1_SchoolBag_Img_Bag.jpg`,
embedded in the student packet). Ask: "What is in this bag?" Take six or seven guesses.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, this lesson's Day 1 slot):** write every guess on the board as it is
called out, in one running list. After the read-aloud in Phase 2, go back to the list and tick the guesses
the labels actually confirm. The list only exists because the whole class contributed to it, and it is not
reproduced on any handout.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing a thing by its size, the way the labels
describe Sam's bag as big and his pen as small and long." Say the same sentence to the whole class; do not
narrate who is doing which version. (This is the exact skill Day 2's Closing Transfer Check will ask you to
reproduce.)

**Pre-teach the 6 target words (5 min), with in-context paragraph matching:** bag, book, pen, big, small,
long. Say each word, sound it out, and show the paragraph letter where it appears: bag [B], book [C],
pen [D], big [B], small [C], long [D].

**Sort first (4 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot, and a whole-class pre-teach activity rather than a
differentiated task: everyone sorts the same bank before any frame sentence is read. The bank is padded
with two already-familiar near-miss words (`shared/Generation_Quality_Standards.md` §C4).

Word bank: bag, book, pen, cup, big, small, long, old.

Sort the eight words into two groups: **things**, and **words that tell you about a thing**.
**Answer note:** things = bag, book, pen, cup; describing words = big, small, long, old.

Then read the frame aloud once, as a class, with one word from the describing group: "It is ___." This is
the only time the frame is printed on Day 1; every later task on Day 1 refers to it rather than reprinting
it.

**No Idiom/Slang Spotlight in this lesson.** Section 0.4 starts the idiom requirement at Level 2 and up;
this anchor is calibrated to Level 1.

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops

Read the label set aloud, slowly, once through. Stop at each bracketed point and put the question to the
class. Then read it a second time and have the class follow the paragraph letters with a finger. The
bracket carries the question only; the expected answer is on the Answer note line beneath it and is never
read out as part of the question.

---

**Bag Check**

**[A]** It is Bag Check Day at school today. Look in your school bag and find three things. Read the label
on each one.

*[STOP & CHECK 1: Ask "How many things do you find?"]*
**Answer note:** three.

**[B]** **The bag.** This is Sam's school bag. It is big and long. Sam puts a book and a pen in it. The bag
goes to school with Sam every day.

*[STOP & CHECK 2: Ask "What does Sam put in the bag?"]*
**Answer note:** a book and a pen.

**[C]** **The book.** This is Sam's reading book. It is not big. It is small. The book sits at the back of
the bag.

*[STOP & CHECK 3: Ask "Where does the book sit?"]*
**Answer note:** at the back of the bag.

**[D]** **The pen.** This is Sam's pen. It is small and long. Sam writes with this pen in class every day.
The pen sits in the front of the bag.

---

No contextual footnotes are needed (Section 0.9 Part B): the text names no real person, place, or cultural
reference that the sentence does not already explain.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Decode and match (15 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot, and each Level's one production for Day 1.
Every item points at Picture 1, Picture 2, and Picture 3, printed in the packet.

- **Level 1 (★), four items.** Sound out the printed word or phrase, then circle the picture it describes.
  1. **pen** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3.
  2. **bag** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  3. **book** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
  4. **a big bag** (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
- **Level 2 (★★), three items.** Read the frame sentence and circle the picture it describes.
  1. It is big. (Picture 1 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  2. It is small. (Picture 1 / Picture 2)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
  3. It is long. (Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3.
- **Level 3 (★★★), two items.** Read the two-slot frame and circle the picture it describes. Both open-slot
  words have to fit, not just one.
  1. It is small and long. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3. Picture 2 is small but not long; Picture 1 is neither.
  2. It is big and long. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1. Paragraph [B] gives the bag both words; Picture 3 is long but not big.

Item counts are tiered for balanced time on task: the lowest Level gets more, shorter items, the highest
fewer and deeper ones.

**Vocabulary context completion (10 min)** - use the 6 target words from Phase 1, one word per blank:

Each sentence names its own subject rather than reusing the "It is ___." frame, so the frame is still
produced only once per Level per day (Quality Standards §D10).

Sam has a school ______. The bag is ______. A ______ sits at the back. The book is not big; the book is
______. A ______ sits at the front. The pen is ______.

**Answer note:** bag, big, book, small, pen, long.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher      Better of Two Matrix       Rotating Partners + Closing
    Text, Vocab Drill        (Board T-Chart)              Transfer Check
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**Partner re-scan (3 min):** in pairs, find the paragraph letter for each of the three things: the bag, the
book, the pen.
**Answer note:** [B], [C], [D].

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated everyday scene, recycling all 6 target words:

*At the Shop*

Mum goes to the shop on Saturday. She gets a big bag for the food. She gets a pen for Sam. The pen is small
and long. She gets a new book for Sam too.

*Task (3-4 min):* find and circle the six words you learned yesterday as you read: bag, book, pen, big,
small, long. Then tell a partner in one sentence what Mum gets for Sam.
**Answer note:** a pen and a book.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** oral opposites drill. The teacher says a word, the class says the
opposite: big / small, long / short. Then each student says one spoken sentence using the frame "It is
___." about anything in the refresher text.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability triads, one student per task Level (three task Levels at this Band, so triads rather than
groups of four). Each student does their own Level's cell first as rehearsal, then the triad shares out
loud and puts one answer on the board.

**Better of two (the 0.11 shape for this slot).** Every cell prints two frame sentences about one of the
three pictures; the student circles the one that fits and says the word that makes it fit.

**Level 1 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 1 Reading objective ("Can decode an unfamiliar simple
word or short adjective-noun phrase... and match it to the object or image it describes, with visual
support.")
- Task: for Picture 3, circle the phrase that fits: **a long pen** / **a big pen**. Then say the word that
  made you pick it.
  **Answer note:** a long pen; the word is *long*.
- Respectful Tiers add-on (a genuine, scaffolded interpretive step, not fact-copying alone): say which of
  the three things would be hardest to lose in a school bag, and circle the printed word that tells you
  why: *big* / *small*.
  **Answer note:** the bag, because it is big. Either answer is accepted if the student names a size word
  that fits the picture they chose.

**Level 2 (native)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... to identify the matching
image, with visual support.")
- Task: for Picture 2, circle the sentence that fits: **It is small.** / **It is big.** Then say the
  open-slot word out loud.
  **Answer note:** It is small.

**Level 3 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
two-slot frame describing a familiar object, decoding both open-slot words... to identify the matching
image.")
- Task: for Picture 3, circle the sentence where **both** words fit: **It is small and long.** / **It is
  big and long.** Then say which picture the sentence you crossed out would fit instead.
  **Answer note:** It is small and long. The crossed-out sentence fits Picture 1.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, this lesson's Day 2 slot and the preferred anchor slot):** as each triad
reports, build a two-column T-chart on the board, **Big** and **Small**, and write in the thing plus the
describing word each triad actually says. The full chart exists only once every triad has reported; no one
handout has it.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Rotating Partners

Everyone talks at once, in pairs, from where they sit. Students find a new partner for each prompt, so
every partner works a fresh angle. Because everyone is speaking at the same time, no listener task is
needed. When a prompt's window ends, each pair reports one thing to the board.

1. What is in your school bag? Tell your partner two things.
2. Which thing in your bag is big? Which one is small?
3. Which thing do you use every day? Say what it is like.

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own ceiling):
- **Level 1 (★):** "A ___ ." or "A big ___ ."
- **Level 2 (★★):** "It is ___ ."
- **Level 3 (★★★):** "It is ___ and ___ ."

**Differentiated participation (Level 1):** before the first rotation, the Level 1 student rehearses their
one phrase quietly with the partner beside them and gets a thumbs-up, so their first real turn is a
repetition rather than an on-demand production.

**Foundation Support (if a student is working below Level 1):** matching a printed word in the packet to
the same word in the label set, and a two-choice circle instead of an open completion. Nothing here needs a
card set the teacher has to make, and nothing needs an object to be present in the room.

**Closing Transfer Check:** in pairs, out loud, every student describes **their own pencil case** by its
size, using the frame at their star level ("a small case" / "It is small." / "It is small and long."). They
describe it from what they know of it; nothing is taken out, held up, or pointed at. Teacher cold-calls two
or three pairs to share. Nothing is collected or graded. This is the demonstration that the Skill
Spotlight's skill transferred to a thing the lesson never described.

## Self-check (Section 0.3)

Quality Standards §F ran first; the Reading-specific items follow.

1. **Word count:** the anchor is 105 words counted with its three bold label headings, 99 without, inside
   Level 1's 60-120 range and in its middle-to-upper part. Counted, not estimated.
2. **Paragraph count and average sentence length:** 4 lettered paragraphs, 15 sentences, average 6.6 words
   per sentence - inside Level 1's 6-10 band. The label-set form is one of the three forms Level 1 names.
3. **Clause complexity:** every sentence is simple present with a single clause. No subordination, no
   passive, no second coordinator.
4. **Vocabulary scan:** every content word is concrete and high-frequency (bag, book, pen, school, class,
   day, shop, food). Zero abstract nouns. Nothing that belongs two bands higher.
5. **Fact density:** one invented first name and no dates, numbers beyond "three," or named places.
6. **Idioms (0.4):** none, and none required - the idiom rule begins at Level 2 and this anchor is
   calibrated to Level 1. Recorded here so the packet prompt does not look for a Phrase Spotlight.
7. **Refresher text (0.5):** 35 words against the anchor's 99, inside the one-third-to-one-half band;
   unrelated scene (a shop, not a school bag); recycles all 6 target words; introduces no new grammar and
   no new content word.
8. **Genre (0.6):** label set with its images embedded in the packet, straight from the Beginner bank, and
   the first lesson in this Band, so there is no previous cycle to repeat.
9. **Paragraph lettering and footnotes (0.9):** every anchor paragraph is lettered [A] to [D] and referred
   to by letter in the re-scan and the pre-teach. No footnotes, and none needed.
10. **Phase 3 protocol (0.10):** Rotating Partners with three rotated prompts and a new partner for each;
    no listener task needed, since everyone speaks at once. All from students' seats.
11. **Reading strategy:** Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops, with three stops embedded between
    paragraphs and the second pass tracking the paragraph letters.
12. **Fixed-output Levels (0.11):** four slots, four different shapes - Sort first (Day 1 Phase 1), Decode

13. **Answer notes:** every expected answer sits on an **Answer note:** line under its item. No stop

14. **Module verb:** every task stays inside Describing - a thing, its size, and the word that names that
    size. No cause-and-effect set appears, because inference is outside Level 1's ceiling (0.2) and
    Module 1's comprehension work centres on descriptive features (0.1); the native and extension-up sets
    are literal descriptive-feature sets instead.
15. **Board-dependent moment:** one genuine instance per day, in different slots - the live guess list on
    Day 1 Phase 1, the Big/Small T-chart on Day 2 Phase 2.
16. **Task Levels:** exactly three (1, 2, 3), each traced to its own verbatim Reading CSV row. No fourth
    Level and no star beyond three anywhere.
17. **Self-contained (§D8, §D9):** the only materials are this document and the packet. All three pictures
    are embedded. The Closing Transfer Check uses a belonging the student describes from memory, with no
    instruction to look at, hold, or take out anything, and no task depends on work from an earlier lesson.
18. **Room-neutral (§D11):** nothing moves furniture, forms a circle, or posts anything on a wall.
19. **Style:** no em-dashes; ASCII pacing diagram present for each day; metadata line under the H1 carrying
    the version code 1.1.1.0.
20. **Closing Transfer Check object:** the student's own pencil case, distinct from the objects planned for
    Lessons 2 to 4 (water bottle, jacket, phone).

**Topic-grounding check:** the topic is a generic familiar scene (a school bag), which the Module
Lesson-Plan prompt's topic rule permits at Beginner and which the approved Set plan chose deliberately, so
the anchor could stay inside Level 1's decodable-vocabulary ceiling. No statement is attributed to a real,
named individual; Sam is a fictional pupil.
