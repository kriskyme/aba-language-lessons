## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  K-W-L Chart,         Echo Reading the postcard     Choose What Fits
  Spotlight, Vocab        Ana's postcard              Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**K-W-L Chart hook (5 min):** draw three columns on the board, **K**, **W**, and **L**. Ask the class what
they know about a hot day at the sea and write every call-out into K. Then ask what they want to know about
Ana's day and write those into W. Nobody leaves their seat; the chart is built entirely from call-outs.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, this lesson's Day 1 slot):** the K-W-L chart itself. The L column is
filled in at the end of Phase 2, from what the postcard actually says, and the class checks which W
questions the postcard answered. The completed chart exists only on the board.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing a place by how it feels, the way Ana
describes the sand as hot and dry and the sea as wet and cold." Say the same sentence to the whole class;
do not narrate who is doing which version. (This is the exact skill Day 2's Closing Transfer Check will ask
you to reproduce.)

**Pre-teach the 6 target words (8 min), with in-context paragraph matching:** sand, hat, hot, wet, cold,
dry. Say each word, sound it out, and show the paragraph letter where it appears: sand [B], hot [B],
dry [B], hat [C], wet [D], cold [D]. Then hold each word against the picture it fits, so the word is tied
to something visible before any frame sentence is read: sand and hot and dry against Picture 1, hat against
Picture 2, wet and cold against Picture 3.

**No Idiom/Slang Spotlight in this lesson.** Section 0.4 starts the idiom requirement at Level 2 and up;
this anchor is calibrated to Level 1.

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Echo Reading

Read one phrase at a time, at normal speed and with real expression; the class echoes it back before you
move on. The slash marks below are the echo breaks, not punctuation the students read out. Run the whole
postcard twice: once phrase by phrase, once with the class echoing whole sentences. Then fill in the K-W-L
chart's L column together.

---

**A Postcard from the Sea**

**[A]** Dear Ben, /

**[B]** I am at the sea / with Mum. / It is a hot day. / The sun is up / all day. / The sand is hot / and
dry. / I sit on it / and eat my lunch. /

**[C]** I have a big hat / for the sun. / The hat is big / and dry. / Mum has a hat too. /

**[D]** The sea is wet / and cold. / I put my feet in it. / The water is cold / on my feet. / Then I run
back / to my hat. /

**[E]** I see you on Monday. / From Ana

---

No contextual footnotes are needed (Section 0.9 Part B): the text names no real person, place, or cultural
reference that the sentence does not already explain.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Choose what fits (15 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot. From the printed word list, students circle
the words the postcard supports for a given thing and cross out the ones it does not. The list is padded
with two already-familiar near-miss words (`shared/Generation_Quality_Standards.md` §C4).

Word list for every Level: **hot, wet, cold, dry, big, sad**

- **Level 1 (★), three items.** Sound out each word in the list, then circle the ones the postcard uses for
  the thing named and cross out the rest.
  1. the sand
     **Answer note:** circle hot and dry; cross out wet, cold, big, sad.
  2. the hat
     **Answer note:** circle big and dry; cross out hot, wet, cold, sad.
  3. the sea
     **Answer note:** circle wet and cold; cross out hot, dry, big, sad.
- **Level 2 (★★), three items.** Read the frame sentence, then circle the picture it describes.
  1. It is hot. (Picture 1 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
  2. It is wet. (Picture 1 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3.
  3. It is dry. (Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
- **Level 3 (★★★), two items.** Read the two-slot frame and circle the picture it describes. Both open-slot
  words have to fit, not just one.
  1. It is wet and cold. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3. Picture 1 is dry, and Picture 2 is dry too.
  2. It is hot and dry. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1. Paragraph [B] gives the sand both words; Picture 2 is dry but not hot.

Item counts are tiered for balanced time on task: the lowest Level gets more, shorter items, the highest
fewer and deeper ones.

**Vocabulary context completion (10 min)** - use the 6 target words from Phase 1, one word per blank:

Each sentence names its own subject rather than reusing the "It is ___." frame, so the frame is still
produced only once per Level per day (Quality Standards §D10).

Ana sits on the ______. The sand is ______ and ______ in the sun. She has a big ______ on her head. The sea
is ______ and ______.

**Answer note:** sand, hot, dry, hat, wet, cold.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher      Decode and Match Matrix     Small Groups + Closing
   Text, Fix the Word         (Board T-Chart)             Transfer Check
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**Partner re-scan (3 min):** in pairs, find the paragraph letter for the sand, for the hat, and for the sea.
**Answer note:** [B], [C], [D].

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated everyday scene, recycling 5 of the 6 target words:

*At the Bus Stop*

Tom waits at the bus stop. The seat is wet from the rain. His hands are cold. He has a hat, and the hat is
dry. Then the sun comes out, and the day gets hot.

*Task (3-4 min) - Fix the wrong word,* the 0.11 shape for this slot. Each sentence below is about the
refresher text and has one word that does not fit. Cross it out and write the right word above it.

- **Level 1 (★):** the dry seat
  **Answer note:** cross out *dry*, write *wet*.
- **Level 2 (★★):** It is cold at the end of the story.
  **Answer note:** cross out *cold*, write *hot*.
- **Level 3 (★★★):** The seat is hot and cold.
  **Answer note:** cross out *hot*, write *wet*. The second word was already right.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** oral opposites drill. The teacher says a word, the class says the
opposite: hot / cold, wet / dry. Then each student says one spoken sentence using the frame "It is ___."
about the weather outside the window today.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability triads, one student per task Level (three task Levels at this Band, so triads rather than
groups of four). Each student does their own Level's cell first as rehearsal, then the triad shares out
loud and puts one answer on the board.

**Decode and match (the 0.11 shape for this slot),** and each Level's one production for Day 2. Every cell
points at the three printed pictures.

**Level 1 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 1 Reading objective ("Can decode an unfamiliar simple
word or short adjective-noun phrase... and match it to the object or image it describes, with visual
support.")
- Task: sound out **hot sand** and circle the picture it describes (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3).
  Then say the word that made you pick it.
  **Answer note:** Picture 1; the word is *hot* (or *sand*).
- Respectful Tiers add-on (a genuine, scaffolded interpretive step, not fact-copying alone): say which
  picture you would rather be in, and circle the printed word that tells you why: *hot* / *cold*.
  **Answer note:** either picture is accepted if the student names a weather word that fits the picture
  they chose.

**Level 2 (native)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... to identify the matching
image, with visual support.")
- Task: read **It is cold.** and circle the picture it describes (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3). Then
  say the open-slot word out loud.
  **Answer note:** Picture 3.

**Level 3 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
two-slot frame describing a familiar object, decoding both open-slot words... to identify the matching
image.")
- Task: read **It is hot and dry.** and circle the picture where **both** words fit (Picture 1 / Picture 2 /
  Picture 3). Then say which word rules out the picture you nearly chose.
  **Answer note:** Picture 1. Picture 2 is ruled out by *hot*.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, this lesson's Day 2 slot and the preferred anchor slot):** as each triad
reports, build a two-column T-chart on the board, **Hot** and **Cold**, and write in the thing plus the
weather word each triad actually says. The full chart exists only once every triad has reported; no one
handout has it.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Small-group discussion

Groups of three or four, formed where students already sit. Every group is talking at the same time, so
every student is active throughout. Split the three prompts across the groups so no two neighbouring groups
are working the same one, then swap prompts halfway through the window. When a prompt's window ends, each
group reports one thing to the board.

1. What is the weather like today? Tell your group two things about it.
2. Ana likes a hot day at the sea. What day do you like: a hot day or a wet day? Say why.
3. What do you wear on a hot day? What do you wear on a cold day?

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own ceiling):
- **Level 1 (★):** "A ___ day." or "A hot ___ ."
- **Level 2 (★★):** "It is ___ ."
- **Level 3 (★★★):** "It is ___ and ___ ."

**Differentiated participation (Level 1):** before their group's first round, the Level 1 student rehearses
their one phrase quietly with the person next to them and gets a thumbs-up, so their first real turn is a
repetition rather than an on-demand production.

**Foundation Support (if a student is working below Level 1):** matching a printed word in the packet to
the same word in the postcard, and a two-choice circle instead of an open completion. Nothing here needs a
card set the teacher has to make, and nothing needs an object to be present in the room.

**Closing Transfer Check:** in pairs, out loud, every student describes **their own water bottle** by how
it feels, using the frame at their star level ("a cold bottle" / "It is cold." / "It is cold and wet."). They
describe it from what they know of it; nothing is taken out, held up, or pointed at. Teacher cold-calls two
or three pairs to share. Nothing is collected or graded. This is the demonstration that the Skill
Spotlight's skill transferred to a thing the lesson never described.
