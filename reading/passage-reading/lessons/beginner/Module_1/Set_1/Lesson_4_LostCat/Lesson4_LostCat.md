## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  Mystery Quote,       Partner / Shared Reading      Choose What Fits
  Spotlight, Vocab        The Lost Cat notice          Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Mystery Quote hook (5 min):** before anyone sees the notice, show one line from it on its own:

> Her fur is very soft to touch.

Ask: "Who is *her*? Where would you read a line like this?" Take six or seven guesses.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 1, this lesson's Day 1 slot):** write every guess on the board as it is
called out. After the shared reading in Phase 2, go back down the list and tick the guesses the notice
actually confirms and cross out the ones it rules out. The checked list exists only on the board.

**Skill Spotlight (2 min):** "Today we're practicing describing an animal by its markings, the way the notice
tells you Pip is black and white and has a long tail." Say the same sentence to the whole class; do not
narrate who is doing which version. (This is the exact skill Day 2's Closing Transfer Check will ask you to
reproduce.)

**Pre-teach the 6 target words (8 min), with in-context paragraph matching:** cat, tail, black, white, soft,
long. Say each word, sound it out, and show the paragraph letter where it appears: cat [A], black [B],
white [B], soft [B], tail [C], long [C]. Then hold each word against the picture it fits, so the word is tied
to something visible before any frame sentence is read: black against Picture 2, white against Picture 3, and
black, white, soft and long all against Picture 1.

**No Idiom/Slang Spotlight in this lesson.** Section 0.4 starts the idiom requirement at Level 2 and up; this
anchor is calibrated to Level 1.

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Partner / Shared Reading

Students read in pairs, taking turns by paragraph, then swap so both partners read every part once.

- **Partner A reads Paragraphs [A]-[B].** Partner B follows with a finger and then says, out loud, one thing
  Partner A just read about Pip.
- **Partner B reads Paragraphs [C]-[D].** Partner A follows and then says one thing Partner B just read.
- **Swap and read the whole notice again,** this time with Partner B starting.

Keep the paragraph letters and the partner roles apart: paragraph letters are always bracketed, partner roles
are always spelled out in full.

---

**LOST CAT**

**[A]** Our little cat Pip is lost since Monday. She ran out of the house at night. We live on Mill Street
near the park.

**[B]** Pip is black and white all over. Her face is white and her back is black. Her fur is very soft to
touch.

**[C]** Her tail is long and black at the end. She has one white ear and one black ear.

**[D]** Pip is small and she is still young. Please call us at home if you see her. Thank you for your help.

---

No contextual footnotes are needed (Section 0.9 Part B): the text names no real person, place, or cultural
reference that the sentence does not already explain.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Choose what fits (15 min)** - the 0.11 shape for this slot. From the printed word list, students circle the
words the notice uses for the thing named and cross out the ones it does not. The list is padded with two
already-familiar near-miss words (`shared/Generation_Quality_Standards.md` §C4).

Word list for every Level: **black, white, soft, long, big, sad**

- **Level 1 (★), three items.** Sound out each word in the list, then circle the ones the notice uses for the
  thing named and cross out the rest.
  1. Pip's fur
     **Answer note:** circle black, white, soft; cross out long, big, sad.
  2. Pip's tail
     **Answer note:** circle long, black; cross out white, soft, big, sad.
  3. Pip's ears
     **Answer note:** circle white, black; cross out soft, long, big, sad.
- **Level 2 (★★), three items.** Read the frame sentence and circle the picture it describes.
  1. It is black. (Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 2.
  2. It is white. (Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3.
  3. It is soft. (Picture 1 / Picture 2)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1.
- **Level 3 (★★★), two items.** Read the two-slot frame and circle the picture it describes. Both open-slot
  words have to fit, not just one.
  1. It is black and white. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 1. This is Pip. Picture 2 is black only and Picture 3 is white only, so neither
     one has both words.
  2. It is white and soft. (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3)
     **Answer note:** Picture 3, and this cat is not Pip. Picture 1 is black **and** white, so a cat
     described only as white is a different cat; Picture 2 is not white at all.

Item counts are tiered for balanced time on task: the lowest Level gets more, shorter items, the highest
fewer and deeper ones.

**Vocabulary context completion (10 min)** - use the 6 target words from Phase 1, one word per blank. Each
sentence names its own subject rather than reusing the "It is ___." frame, so the frame is still produced
only once per Level per day (Quality Standards §D10).

A ______ called Pip is lost. Pip is ______ and ______ all over. Her fur is very ______. Her ______ is
______ at the end.

**Answer note:** cat, black, white, soft, tail, long.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher      Decode and Match Matrix     Jigsaw Panels + Closing
   Text, Fix the Word      (Board: what Pip looks         Transfer Check
                                   like)
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**Partner re-scan (3 min):** in pairs, find the paragraph letter that tells you about each one: Pip's fur,
Pip's tail, where the family lives.
**Answer note:** [B], [C], [A].

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated everyday scene, recycling 5 of the 6 target words:

*The Dog Next Door*

A big dog lives next door to us. His name is Bruno. His coat is black and white like a cow. His tail is long
and it never stops. His ears feel very soft.

*Task (3-4 min) - Fix the wrong word,* the 0.11 shape for this slot. Each line below is about the refresher
text and has one word that does not fit. Cross it out and write the right word above it.

- **Level 1 (★):** the short tail
  **Answer note:** cross out *short*, write *long*.
- **Level 2 (★★):** It is a small dog.
  **Answer note:** cross out *small*, write *big*.
- **Level 3 (★★★):** His coat is black and soft.
  **Answer note:** cross out *soft*, write *white*. The first word was already right; it is his ears that are
  soft, not his coat.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** oral opposites drill. The teacher says a word, the class says the
opposite: black / white, long / short, soft / hard. Then each student says one spoken sentence using the
frame "It is ___." about an animal they know.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability triads, one student per task Level (three task Levels at this Band, so triads rather than
groups of four). Each student does their own Level's cell first as rehearsal, then the triad shares out loud
and puts one answer on the board.

**Decode and match (the 0.11 shape for this slot),** and each Level's one production for Day 2. Every cell
points at the three printed pictures.

**Level 1 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 1 Reading objective ("Can decode an unfamiliar simple
word or short adjective-noun phrase... and match it to the object or image it describes, with visual
support.")
- Task: sound out **a long tail** and circle the picture it describes (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3).
  Then say the word that made you pick it.
  **Answer note:** Picture 1; the word is *long*.
- Respectful Tiers add-on (a genuine, scaffolded interpretive step, not fact-copying alone): say which cat
  would be hardest to see at night, and circle the printed word that tells you why: *black* / *white*.
  **Answer note:** either cat is accepted if the student circles the word that fits the one they chose.

**Level 2 (native)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... to identify the matching
image, with visual support.")
- Task: read **It is white.** and circle the picture it describes (Picture 1 / Picture 2 / Picture 3). Then
  say the open-slot word out loud.
  **Answer note:** Picture 3.

**Level 3 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
two-slot frame describing a familiar object, decoding both open-slot words... to identify the matching
image.")
- Task: read **It is black and white.** and circle the picture where **both** words fit (Picture 1 /
  Picture 2 / Picture 3). Then say which word rules out Picture 2.
  **Answer note:** Picture 1. *White* rules out Picture 2.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, this lesson's Day 2 slot and the preferred anchor slot):** as each triad
reports, build a single labelled list on the board headed **What Pip looks like**, one line per feature
(fur, tail, ears, size), filled in only from what triads actually say. The complete description of Pip exists
only there; no one handout has it.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Jigsaw Expert Panels

Everyone works from where they sit; no group moves furniture or leaves its seats.

**Round 1, expert groups (12 min).** Three groups, each becoming the expert on one part of the notice:

- **Group 1, the markings:** what the notice says about Pip's fur and her face.
- **Group 2, the tail:** what the notice says about Pip's tail.
- **Group 3, the ears:** what the notice says about Pip's two ears.

Each group agrees one spoken sentence about their feature, at each member's own star level.

**Round 2, mixed groups (12 min).** Re-form into groups of three, one expert from each round-1 group, so
every new group holds all three features. Each expert says their sentence; the group then works one prompt.
Split the prompts across the groups so neighbouring groups are not working the same one, then swap for a
second prompt.

1. Someone tells you they found a cat. Say one thing about Pip they should check.
2. Think of an animal you know. Say two things about how it looks.
3. Which is easier to find outside at night, a black cat or a white cat? Say why.

Each group reports one thing to the board when its window ends.

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own ceiling):
- **Level 1 (★):** "A black ___ ." or "A long ___ ."
- **Level 2 (★★):** "It is ___ ."
- **Level 3 (★★★):** "It is ___ and ___ ."

**Differentiated participation (Level 1):** in Round 1 the Level 1 student rehearses their one phrase quietly
with a partner in their expert group and gets a thumbs-up, so their turn in the Round 2 mixed group is a
repetition rather than an on-demand production.

**Foundation Support (if a student is working below Level 1):** matching a printed word in the packet to the
same word in the notice, and a two-choice circle instead of an open completion. Nothing here needs a card set
the teacher has to make, and nothing needs an object to be present in the room.

**Closing Transfer Check:** in pairs, out loud, every student describes **their own phone** by its markings,
using the frame at their star level ("a black phone" / "It is black." / "It is black and soft."). They
describe it from what they know of it; nothing is taken out, held up, or pointed at. Teacher cold-calls two
or three pairs to share. Nothing is collected or graded. This is the demonstration that the Skill Spotlight's
skill transferred to a thing the lesson never described.
