# Set 1 Intermediate Study Guide — Task Level 4 (★★★)

Companion to `Set1_Intermediate_Assessment.md`. Review this guide for about 30 minutes right before the test.
This guide does not use the test's new passages or repeat any test item — it reviews the words and skill you
will need, using different examples.

## Vocabulary & Idiom Review

**Lesson 1: A Grandmother's Kitchen**
- **traditional** — done the old, established way, passed down over time. Example: *"She makes each candle
  the traditional way, using melted wax and small clay pots."*
*(Lesson 1: A Grandmother's Kitchen)*

**Lesson 2: New Corner of Yoyogi Park Opens to Visitors**
- **welcoming** — friendly and comfortable; makes people want to stay. Example: *"Library staff say the entrance
  already feels more welcoming to visitors."*
*(Lesson 2: New Corner of Yoyogi Park Opens to Visitors)*

**Lesson 3: How to Choose Running Shoes That Feel Comfortable**
- **comfortable** — feels good to wear or use, not painful. Example: *"Not every backpack feels comfortable
  after a long day."*
- **"in the end"** — finally, after everything else is considered. Example: use it to state the most important
  point after listing several smaller details, e.g., *"You can compare many backpacks, but in the end, comfort
  is what matters most."*
*(Lesson 3: How to Choose Running Shoes That Feel Comfortable)*

**Lesson 4: Painting Wynwood Walls: An Interview**
- **neighborhood** — the area and streets around a place, where people live and walk. Example: *"She used
  simple shapes... to build a picture of the neighborhood around the school."*
*(Lesson 4: Painting Wynwood Walls: An Interview)*

## Skill Review

The skill every lesson in this Set practiced: **identifying a specific comparison in the text and the exact
reason the writer gives for it — not just noticing that a comparison exists.**

- **Lesson 1:** the text states the wood-fired oven cooks slowly and evenly, and that is *why* the crust turns
  gold while the inside stays soft — the reason is written directly in the paragraph, not left for the reader to
  guess.
- **Lesson 2:** the text states the new stone path stays dry, which is *why* it solves the old muddy-path
  problem.
- **Lesson 3:** the text states getting fitted at a store prevents blisters and pain later, which is *why* it
  recommends skipping online-only shopping.
- **Lesson 4:** the artist states she wanted the mural to feel like motion, not calm, which is *why* she chose
  sharp shapes and bright colors instead of the wave mural's soft, round style.

## Sample Items (worked examples — not on the test)

**Comparison-and-reason example:** *Find the sentence in "A New Look for the Library Entrance" that compares
the new path to the old one. What reason does the text give?* Strong answer: the text says the new path "stays
dry after rain," and gives the reason that the old path's muddiness "was a common complaint before" — so the
new path was built to fix a problem people already noticed.

## Self-Check

- Can I find the exact sentence in a paragraph that makes a comparison?
- Can I state, in my own words, the specific reason the text gives for that comparison — not just that a
  comparison exists?
- Can I explain what "in the end" means and use it correctly in a new sentence?
- Do I know the meaning of "traditional," "welcoming," "comfortable," and "neighborhood" well enough to use
  each one in my own sentence?
