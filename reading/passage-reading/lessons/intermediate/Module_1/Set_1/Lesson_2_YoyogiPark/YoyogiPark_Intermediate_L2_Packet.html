[A] A new section of Yoyogi Park¹ opened to the public this month, and visitors are already walking its wide paths. The renovated area sits near the park's east entrance, close to the rows of cherry trees the park is famous for. City officials announced the opening on Monday, and dozens of visitors arrived within the first hour.

[B] The new section includes a long stone path, several wooden benches, and a small open lawn. Workers finished the path last week, and the benches arrived a few days later. The city also installed small lamps along the path for evening walks. City workers say the area is now ready for both quiet walks and small family picnics.

[C] Before the renovation, this part of the park had only a narrow dirt path and no benches at all. Visitors had to stand or sit on the grass, and the path often turned muddy after rain. Parents often kept small children away from the muddy path in the past. The new stone path stays dry, and the wooden benches give visitors a comfortable place to rest.

[D] In spring, the cherry trees near this section fill with pink blossoms, and many visitors come just to see them. Local resident Kenji Sato said the new benches make the area feel more welcoming than before. "Now families can sit together and enjoy the trees," he said. Before long, the new benches were full of families enjoying the view.

[E] Park officials plan to add more benches to other sections of Yoyogi Park next year. Workers will also plant new grass on the open lawn this autumn. Many visitors say they now plan to visit this corner of the park every week. For now, this renovated corner gives visitors a comfortable new place to enjoy the park's cherry blossoms and open space.

¹ Yoyogi Park is a large public park in Tokyo, Japan, known for its wide paths and cherry blossom trees in spring.

Focus on the Objective

Feature and comparison. In Paragraph [C], the article describes the new path's feature: it "stays dry." The comparison is to the old path, which "often turned muddy after rain." Naming a feature and comparing it to what came before is exactly what makes this a description, not just a list of facts.

Evaluative word choice. In Paragraph [D], the article says the new benches make the area feel more "welcoming" than before. The writer never says "I like this park more now" - the word "welcoming" does that work instead, showing the writer's positive attitude without stating an opinion directly.

Unit 2B: New Corner of Yoyogi Park

Warm-Up

Find the 5 words to know from before in the following short piece. Then tell a partner, in one sentence, how the library entrance is like the new corner of Yoyogi Park.

A New Look for the Library Entrance

The city library finished a small renovation of its front entrance last week. Workers replaced the old dirt path with a wide stone path that leads right to the door. Two new wooden benches now sit under the windows, and a small lawn was added beside the steps. Library staff say the entrance already feels more welcoming to visitors. In spring, a row of flowering trees nearby will fill the area with blossoms. Staff say the new path also stays dry after rain, which was a common complaint before. Local families say they now stop by the entrance more often just to sit for a few minutes before going inside.

With a partner, say one example each using the words "renovation" and "renovated" about a place you know (for example: "The park had a renovation" and "The park is renovated.")

Discuss It

Get into a small group. Your group will get one question below. Take turns discussing it out loud, using a starter phrase from the list.

1. What is the single best change the city made to this park, and why?
2. Which detail from the article best shows that this corner of the park now feels more welcoming than before? Point to the sentence and explain your choice.
3. If you visited this new corner of Yoyogi Park, what would you notice first: the path, the benches, or the lawn? Describe it and say why you'd notice it first.

Starter phrases:

  • ★ "It is ___."
  • If you have this line, practice it quietly with a partner first. While your group talks, keep a tally of which feature - path, bench, or lawn - each person mentions, then use your line when there's a good pause.
  • ★★ "It is ___ and ___."
  • ★★★ "I think the best change was ___ because ___."
  • ★★★★ "The writer organizes the brief by ___, and the word '___' shows that the writer feels ___ about the renovation."
Wrap It Up

With a partner, pick one thing in the classroom or school. Describe how it looks now and compare it to how it used to look, or how it could look different.
