### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Visual Inquiry hook:** Display the lesson's photo of bread baking in a wood-fired oven in Altamura, Puglia (`Lesson1_Kitchen_Img_Hook.jpg`,
embedded in the student packet). Ask: "What do you think people cook here? How is this kitchen different from your own?"
Take 3-4 quick guesses before reading.
Image: `Lesson1_Kitchen_Img_Hook.jpg` - "Forno a legna (Altamura)" by Francesco Paolo Fumarola, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Forno_a_legna_(Altamura).jpg.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing describing a place by comparing it to somewhere more familiar, and
noticing small sensory details, the way our writer compares her grandmother's wood-fired kitchen to her own
kitchen at home." (This is the exact skill Day 2's Closing Transfer Check will ask you to reproduce.)

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** aroma, clay pot, herbs, warmth, traditional,
wood-fired (oven). Show each word next to the paragraph letter where it appears and match it to the photo.

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "in no time" (transparent chunk, tied to topic) = very quickly. Gloss it before reading:
"If something happens in no time, it happens very fast."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops

Read the anchor text aloud, pausing at each bracketed STOP & CHECK. Paragraphs are lettered for reference.

---

**A Grandmother's Kitchen**

**[A]** Every summer, Elena visits her grandmother's house in Puglia, a sunny region in southern Italy. The house
is called a trullo¹, and it has a round white roof made of stone. Inside, the kitchen still uses an old wood-fired
oven instead of a modern stove.

*[STOP & CHECK 1: Ask "What kind of oven does the grandmother's kitchen have?"]*

**[B]** When Elena walks into the kitchen, the aroma of baking bread fills the room. Her grandmother keeps fresh
herbs in a row of clay pots near the window, and their smell mixes with the smoke from the fire. She grows basil,
rosemary, and oregano herself in the garden behind the house. The wood-fired oven gives the whole kitchen a
steady warmth, even on a cool morning.

*[STOP & CHECK 2: Ask "Where does the grandmother keep her herbs?"]*

**[C]** Elena's grandmother cooks the traditional way, and she never uses a timer. She checks the bread by
tapping it and listening for a hollow sound. She says a modern electric oven heats food fast, but it never gives
bread the same smoky flavor. The wood fire, she explains, cooks slowly and evenly. That is why the crust turns
dark gold in no time, but the inside stays soft.

*[STOP & CHECK 3: Ask "Why does the grandmother prefer the wood fire over an electric oven?"]*

**[D]** The kitchen table is old and covered in small burn marks from years of cooking. Elena's grandmother says
every mark tells a story about a meal the family shared. Elena likes to touch each mark and guess which meal
made it. Above the table, dried herbs hang from the ceiling, ready to be added to the next pot of soup.

**[E]** Elena loves this kitchen because it feels different from her own kitchen at home. At home, the stove is
electric and the room stays quiet. Here, the fire crackles and the herbs smell strong. Her grandmother hums
while she cooks. For Elena, this kitchen is not just a room for cooking. It is the warmest part of the whole
house, and she always asks her grandmother to teach her one new recipe before summer ends.

---

¹A trullo is a traditional stone house from Puglia, in southern Italy, with a round white roof and no cement
between the stones.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, if not used in Phase 2 of Day 2 instead):** as students share guesses during
STOP & CHECK 3, build a two-column board list live: "Wood fire" vs. "Electric oven," filling it in only from what
students say, not a pre-written list. (This lesson anchors its main board moment in Day 2 Phase 2 instead - see
below - so use this Day 1 moment lightly or skip it if Day 2's is stronger; every lesson only needs one genuine
instance per day.)

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Fact Finder set (Level 2, lowest task Level):**
1. What color is the roof of the trullo? (Point to Paragraph A.)
2. What does Elena smell when she walks into the kitchen? (Point to Paragraph B.)
3. Where does the grandmother keep her herbs? (Point to Paragraph B.)

**Cause & Effect set (Levels 3-4, native Levels):**
1. Why does the bread's crust turn dark gold "in no time"? (Because the wood fire cooks _____.)
2. Why doesn't the grandmother use a timer? (Because she checks the bread by _____.)
3. What is one reason the electric oven is faster but the wood-fired oven still wins for the grandmother?

**Vocabulary context completion** (use the 6 target words from Phase 1):
Elena smelled the ______ of baking bread. Her grandmother's herbs sat in small ______s by the window. Even
on a cool day, the kitchen felt full of ______. She cooked the ______ way, the way her own mother taught her,
using a real ______ oven.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher      Collaborative Matrix          Fishbowl + Closing
    Text, Vocab Drill        (Board T-Chart)              Transfer Check
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** Partners quickly scan the Day 1 text for the paragraph letters and the six target
words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic, recycles 5 of the 6 target words:

*At the Market*

At the Saturday market, Maria sells candles at a small stall. She makes each candle the traditional way, using
melted wax and small clay pots. Maria adds dried herbs, like lavender and rosemary, to the wax before it cools.
The herbs give every candle a soft, natural aroma. Customers often stop at her stall just to smell the candles
before they buy one. Maria says her candles bring warmth to any room, even without a flame. Some customers say the smell
reminds them of their own grandmother's kitchen. Maria smiles every time someone tells her this. Her
grandmother taught her this method years ago, and Maria still uses the same steps today.

*Task (3-4 min):* Circle the 5 recycled words (traditional, clay pot, herbs, aroma, warmth) as you find them.
Then tell a partner in one sentence: how are Maria's candles like Elena's grandmother's kitchen?

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Word-forms drill - "warmth" (noun) vs. "warm" (adjective): "The kitchen
has warmth" vs. "The kitchen is warm." Students give one oral example of each using a classroom object.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each completes their own task, then shares orally.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
fixed frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... with visual support.")
- Task: Using the photo from Phase 1, complete: "It is ___." (e.g., "It is warm.")
- Respectful Tiers add-on (genuine, simplified interpretive task, not fact-copying alone): circle the word that
  best shows how Elena feels in this kitchen - *happy* / *bored* - using the picture and Paragraph E as a clue.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("...two-slot frame... decoding both
open-slot words... to identify the matching image.")
- Task: Complete the two-slot frame naming two features of the kitchen: "It is ___ and ___." (e.g., "It is old and
  warm.")

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading objective ("...identify its features, one comparison, and the
brief reason given for that comparison...")
- Task: Find the sentence that compares the wood-fired oven to a modern electric oven (Paragraph C). What
  reason does the grandmother give for preferring the wood fire? Write one sentence with your answer.

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading objective ("...summarize how the writer organizes
details, and identify at least one clearly evaluative word choice that signals the writer's attitude...")
- Task: In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the text moves from describing the house, to the oven, to how Elena
  feels about it. Then find one phrase that shows the writer likes this kitchen without saying "I love this kitchen"
  directly (hint: Paragraph E).

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, primary anchor slot for this lesson):** as each group reports back, build a
live two-column T-chart on the board: "Wood-Fired Oven" vs. "Modern Electric Oven," filled in only from what
groups actually say (features, one comparison, one reason) - this is where the class's full comparison exists,
not on any single group's handout.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Fishbowl (scaffolded per v2.7, Section 0.10)

Inner circle (roughly half the class, 4-6 of a typical 8-12) discusses. The outer circle is never just listening: while
the inner circle talks, every outer-circle student keeps a running tally of which kitchen (wood-fired or electric)
each inner-circle speaker prefers, plus one describing word or phrase they use for it. Rotate through the three
prompts below at each inner-circle changeover (roughly every 8-10 minutes across the 25-30 minute window),
swapping which students sit in the circle each time; a student rotating in may draw on the notes they took as an
outer-circle listener.

1. Which kitchen would you rather cook in: Elena's grandmother's, or a modern kitchen? Why?
2. Which detail in the article makes the kitchen feel most real to you: the smell of the bread, the burn marks on
   the table, or something else?
3. Is a kitchen like this better for cooking, or is a modern kitchen better? What would you miss if you traded one
   for the other?

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own vocabulary ceiling):
- **Level 2:** "It is ___." (differentiated participation - see below)
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___."
- **Level 4:** "I think the [wood-fired oven / electric oven] is better because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The writer organizes the text by ___, and I think the phrase '___' shows that Elena feels ___
  about the kitchen."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** before their first rotation into the circle, Level 2's student rehearses
their "It is ___." stem once, quietly, with a partner, and gets a thumbs-up. Before that first rotation, they take
the same listening/tracking role described above for the outer circle generally, with one fully-prepared line
ready to contribute at a natural pause once they rotate in, rather than an on-demand turn.

**Closing Transfer Check:** In pairs, every student describes one object or place in the classroom by comparing
it to something else, out loud (e.g., "This desk is like my kitchen table at home because..."). Teacher cold-calls
2-3 pairs to share. No self-report, no confidence vote - this is the evidence that the Skill Spotlight's skill
actually transferred.
