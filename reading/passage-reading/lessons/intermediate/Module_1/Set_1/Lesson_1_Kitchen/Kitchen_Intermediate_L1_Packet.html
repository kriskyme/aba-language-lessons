[A] Every summer, Elena visits her grandmother's house in Puglia, a sunny region in southern Italy. The house is called a trullo¹, and it has a round white roof made of stone. Inside, the kitchen still uses an old wood-fired oven instead of a modern stove.

[B] When Elena walks into the kitchen, the aroma of baking bread fills the room. Her grandmother keeps fresh herbs in a row of clay pots near the window, and their smell mixes with the smoke from the fire. She grows basil, rosemary, and oregano herself in the garden behind the house. The wood-fired oven gives the whole kitchen a steady warmth, even on a cool morning.

[C] Elena's grandmother cooks the traditional way, and she never uses a timer. She checks the bread by tapping it and listening for a hollow sound. She says a modern electric oven heats food fast, but it never gives bread the same smoky flavor. The wood fire, she explains, cooks slowly and evenly. That is why the crust turns dark gold in no time, but the inside stays soft.

[D] The kitchen table is old and covered in small burn marks from years of cooking. Elena's grandmother says every mark tells a story about a meal the family shared. Elena likes to touch each mark and guess which meal made it. Above the table, dried herbs hang from the ceiling, ready to be added to the next pot of soup.

[E] Elena loves this kitchen because it feels different from her own kitchen at home. At home, the stove is electric and the room stays quiet. Here, the fire crackles and the herbs smell strong. Her grandmother hums while she cooks. For Elena, this kitchen is not just a room for cooking. It is the warmest part of the whole house, and she always asks her grandmother to teach her one new recipe before summer ends.

¹ A trullo is a traditional stone house from Puglia, in southern Italy, with a round white roof and no cement between the stones.

Focus on the Objective

Feature and comparison. In Paragraph [C], the article describes the wood-fired oven's feature: it "cooks slowly and evenly." The comparison is to the electric oven, which "heats food fast" but "never gives bread the same smoky flavor." Naming a feature and comparing it to something else is exactly what makes this a description, not just a list of facts.

Sensory detail. In Paragraph [E], the article says "the fire crackles and the herbs smell strong." These are small sensory details, sound and smell, not just what the kitchen looks like. Details like this are why the writer can say the kitchen "feels different" without ever using the word "different" again.

Unit 1B: A Grandmother's Kitchen

Warm-Up

Find the 5 words to know from before in the following short piece. Then tell a partner, in one sentence, how Maria's candles are like Elena's grandmother's kitchen.

At the Market

At the Saturday market, Maria sells candles at a small stall. She makes each candle the traditional way, using melted wax and small clay pots. Maria adds dried herbs, like lavender and rosemary, to the wax before it cools. The herbs give every candle a soft, natural aroma. Customers often stop at her stall just to smell the candles before they buy one. Maria says her candles bring warmth to any room, even without a flame. Some customers say the smell reminds them of their own grandmother's kitchen. Maria smiles every time someone tells her this. Her grandmother taught her this method years ago, and Maria still uses the same steps today.
