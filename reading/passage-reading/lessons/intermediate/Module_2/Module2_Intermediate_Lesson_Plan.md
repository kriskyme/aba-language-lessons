## Task Levels for this plan (Intermediate Band)

Per Conventions §B, Intermediate's task Levels are **2, 3, 4, 5**: one extension down to 2, native 3 and 4, one
extension up to 5. Four differentiated sections per lesson; packets print ★ / ★★ / ★★★ / ★★★★. The anchor text
is calibrated to **Level 3**, the band's lower Level (Conventions §A), which per Lesson prompt §0.2 means 4-5
paragraphs of connected prose, 280-350 words, simple past as the backbone with one coordinating connector per
sentence, 4-6 pre-taught concrete target words, 12-18 word average sentences, and light factual grounding with
no dense multi-date timeline.

Quoted verbatim from `learningobjectives.csv`, Module 2: Narrating, **Reading modality only**:

| Level | Reading Can-do |
|---|---|
| 2 | "Can read one short sentence using the fixed frame (\"I ___-ed.\") narrating a single past action, decoding the open-slot word to identify the matching image, with visual support." |
| 3 | "Can read one short sentence using the two-slot frame narrating two sequential past actions, decoding both open-slot words to identify the matching picture pair." |
| 4 | "Can read a short narrative text and identify the sequence of events, one outcome, and the brief reason given for that outcome, decoding unfamiliar words as needed." |
| 5 | "Can read an extended narration, summarize the sequence of events and why they mattered, and identify at least one clearly telling action or detail that signals how someone felt, even though the feeling is never named directly, decoding unfamiliar words as needed." |

**Note for Step 2: two fixed-output Levels here, not one.** At Module 1 only Level 2 was frame-and-picture work.
In Module 2 **both Level 2 and Level 3** are fixed-output rows: Level 2 decodes a one-slot past-tense frame
("I ___-ed.") to a single image, and Level 3 decodes a two-slot frame naming two sequential past actions to a
matching **picture pair**. So Quality Standards §D10 and Lesson prompt §0.11 govern two Levels per lesson, not
one, and the slot-to-shape table below applies to both. The anchor text still has to be a real 280-350 word
narrative (§0.2's Level 3 row governs the text's difficulty; the CSV row governs what skill the task exercises),
and Levels 4 and 5 work that prose directly.

**Note for Step 2: every lesson needs real images, and they must survive grayscale.** Both fixed-output rows end
in matching a decoded frame to an image, and Quality Standards §D8 forbids anything the teacher prepares. Its
order of preference lands on option (3) here, as it did for the Beginner Band, because the student must choose
between two or more printed candidates. Each lesson therefore needs Wikimedia Commons photos fetched at
generation time under Conventions §I, embedded in the packet as base64 data-URIs, named at the point of use in
the lesson `.md`, saved per §H, and rowed into `Set_1/Set1_Intermediate_Image_Credits.md` (created lazily on
this Set's first image; the existing Module 1 register is a different Set's file and is not reused). No packet
is generated before its images exist, and no placeholder is ever printed.

Two constraints specific to Narrating pictures:

- **The action has to be readable from the photo,** because the open slot is a past-tense verb. Choose photos
  where a person is visibly mid-action (carrying, digging, queuing, splashing, falling) rather than posed, and
  write the frame to the photo rather than hunting for a photo to fit a frame already written (§I).
- **Style Guide §A.2:** no item is answered by reading a chromatic colour off a printed picture. Build the
  distinctions on the action itself, plus size, quantity, setting, or time of day. Black, white, and grey are
  fine.

Task formats favor variety across oral, collaborative, and visual formats; writing is one format among several,
never the default, and no task below is grounded in a Writing CSV row even where the student writes a word.

## Topic grounding

The prompt requires **Intermediate and up** to use a real, verifiable referent, with the surrounding frame (a
diarist, an emailer, a reporter) free to be invented. All four topics below are real, documented events or
places a student could look up: a sea-turtle hatchling release at Tortuguero National Park in Costa Rica,
Songkran in Chiang Mai, the Koningsdag vrijmarkt in Amsterdam, and the Cooper's Hill cheese-rolling race in
Gloucestershire. Every narrator is invented or composite. **Guardrail applied:** no quote or statement is
attributed to a real, named individual anywhere in this Set. Lesson 4 is a news brief, the genre most likely to
want a real person's quote, so its quoted participant is explicitly an invented runner, and the lesson `.md`
will say so in its fictional-subject note. Step 2 verifies each referent's concrete details by live search
before writing; the anchor text is original writing to this program's ceiling, never copied or closely
paraphrased source text.

## Self-check

- **Genre variety.** Four distinct genres from the Intermediate bank (§0.6), one per lesson, no repeats and no
  two consecutive lessons alike: field-journal/diary entries, short narrative/personal-story article, email/
  message exchange, simple news brief. Two of these (personal-story article, news brief) also appear in Module 1
  Set 1, which the rules permit: Conventions §F blocks adjacency only, and neither is Lesson 1. Text-message/chat
  threads and product listings are held back for a later Set, the listing because it is the bank's weakest fit
  for the Narrating verb; the chat thread was kept out of this Set because it sits too close to Lesson 3's email
  exchange to read as a genuine rotation.
- **Reading-strategy variety.** Four distinct strategies, one per lesson (Say Something, Reciprocal Teaching,
  Partner/Shared Reading, Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops), past the "at least 3 distinct" floor with
  no consecutive repeat. Lesson 1's Say Something clears the cross-Module adjacency check against Module 1's
  Reader's Theater. Close Reading with Annotation is not used: §0.6 reserves it for Advanced and Proficient
  independent readers.
- **Hook rotation.** Mystery Quote, Visual Inquiry, Take a Side, K-W-L Chart: each used exactly once. Lesson 1's
  Mystery Quote differs from Module 1 Lesson 4's Take a Side.
- **Protocol rotation.** Panel Round, Jigsaw Expert Panels, small-group discussion, Town Hall Role-Play: four of
  the five §0.10 protocols, each once, and Lesson 1's Panel Round differs from Module 1 Lesson 4's Rotating
  Partners. Rotating Partners is left for a later Set precisely because it was the last thing this Band ran.
  Lesson 1's Panel Round carries an explicit listener task; every lesson runs 2-3 rotated prompts advanced at
  each changeover, never one static question.
- **Room-neutral (Quality Standards §D11).** Every hook and protocol runs from where students sit. No Fishbowl,
  Concentric Circles, Four-Corner Debate, K-W-L Walk, carousel, gallery walk, corners, or stations appears
  anywhere in this plan, and nothing is posted around the room. Lesson 3's Take a Side is the printed-and-marked
  form, and Lesson 4's K-W-L is the printed-and-boarded form.
- **Topic and vocabulary overlap.** All four topics and all four vocabulary themes are distinct within the Set,
  and none echoes Module 1 Set 1's themes (cooking tools, park features, footwear, art and creative process) or
  its topics. The four themes are also verb-led rather than noun-led, which is what keeps them inside Narrating.
- **Topic grounding.** All four referents are real and verifiable at Intermediate, with invented narrators, and
  no quote is attributed to a real, named individual. Lesson 4's quoted runner is explicitly invented.
- **Task-Level grounding.** Every entry traces to the verbatim **Reading** row quoted above: L2's one-slot
  past-tense frame plus image identification, L3's two-slot sequential frame plus picture pair, L4's
  sequence-plus-outcome-plus-stated-reason, L5's summary plus one unnamed-feeling signal. Exactly four task
  Levels per lesson, matching the Intermediate Band. No Writing or Listening/Speaking row grounds any task, and
  L5 is held at its own ceiling: a clearly telling action, not Level 6's pacing and tone-shift work.
- **Fixed-output shapes.** The slot table assigns a shape per slot per lesson with no shape in the same slot in
  consecutive lessons, and exactly one written production per Level per lesson day.
- **Module-verb consistency.** Every task stays inside Narrating: what happened, in what order, how it turned
  out, and the reason given. None drifts into Describing (features of a place), Explaining (why a phenomenon
  works), or Evaluating (a verdict). Note for Step 2: `Generate_Lesson_Prompt_v3.5.md`'s Day 1 Phase 3 bullet
  names a "Cause & Effect set" for the native Levels, which is Module 3's verb. Per `Index.md`'s open item on
  that line, this Set's native-Level Day 1 set takes the Narrating shape instead (a **Sequence and Outcome
  set**: order of events, the outcome, the reason stated for it), and each lesson's self-check says so.
- **Lesson numbering.** Module 2 is a new Module for this Band, so Set numbering restarts at 1 and lesson
  numbering starts at 1 within this Module: Lessons 1-4, version codes `2.1.1.0` through `2.1.4.0`
  (Conventions §C, §G).
- **Cross-Module adjacency.** Run against Intermediate Module 1 Set 1 Lesson 4, above; all four rotated columns
  differ, and no theme or topic flag was raised.
- **§D12 (Quality Standards v1.17).** Reading has no expression of the commit/verify/return arc yet: building it
