### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**K-W-L Chart hook (board-dependent, room-neutral form):** the packet prints a three-column chart. Students fill
the K column ("what I know about races down hills") and the W column ("what I want to know about this one")
individually, compare with a neighbour, and then the class builds both columns live on the board from what
students report. The L column is left empty until Day 2 Phase 1. Nothing is posted on a wall and nobody leaves
their seat.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing telling what happened in the order it happened, and giving the one
reason things turned out the way they did."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** slope, chase, slip, rescue, prize, cheer.
Show each word beside the paragraph letter where it appears ([A] slope, chase; [B] slip, rescue; [C] prize,
cheer).

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "I just went" (transparent chunk, from the quoted runner) = I kept going without
stopping to think. Gloss it before reading, because the words alone do not show the speed: "If someone says 'I
just went,' they mean they started and did not stop."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Teacher Read-Aloud with Interactive Stops

Read the brief aloud twice: once straight through, then again stopping at each bracketed point. The bracket
carries the question only; the expected answer is on the `Answer note:` line beneath it, for the teacher.

---

**Nobody Caught the Cheese Again This Year**

*BROCKWORTH, Gloucestershire -*

**[A]** Thousands of people stood on the side of Cooper's Hill¹ on Monday afternoon. They came to watch twenty
runners chase a round of cheese down a very steep slope. The cheese went over the edge first, as it always does.
It reached the bottom long before any of the runners. The first runner across the line took it home. Two of the
twenty had travelled from Canada for the day.

`[STOP & CHECK 1: Ask "What reached the bottom of the hill first?"]`
**Answer note:** the cheese, not any runner.

**[B]** The hill is about 180 metres long. In places it is so steep that runners cannot stay on their feet. Most
of them slipped in the first few seconds and rolled the rest of the way down. A rescue team in red overalls
waited at the bottom. They walked two runners across to the medical tent.

`[STOP & CHECK 2: Ask "What did most of the runners do in the first few seconds?"]`
**Answer note:** they slipped and then rolled down the hill.

**[C]** "I slipped after four steps, and then I just went," said Tom Hale, the winner of the second men's race.
He held the prize above his head with both hands. The crowd cheered from both sides of the hill. The cheese
weighs between three and four kilos, and the winner keeps it.

`[STOP & CHECK 3: Ask "What happened to Tom Hale after four steps, and what did he do after that?"]`
**Answer note:** he slipped, and then he kept going to the bottom and won the race.

**[D]** The races happen every year on the spring bank holiday at the end of May. Nobody organizes the day in an
official way. There is no entry list: runners walk up the hill and line up at the top. Uphill races for local
people and for under-eighteens run between the downhill races. The oldest written record of the day is from
1836².

`[STOP & CHECK 4: Ask "How does a runner enter this race?"]`
**Answer note:** they do not enter; they walk up the hill and line up at the top.

**[E]** The rain stayed away until five o'clock this year. By then the last cheese was gone. The crowd was
walking slowly back down the lane, and the field at the bottom was full of empty boxes and folded chairs. The
first cars left the narrow lane at half past five. A man at the gate said he had come to this hill every year
since he was a boy.

---

¹Cooper's Hill is a steep grass hill at Brockworth, near Gloucester, in the west of England.

²The cheese race has been written about since at least 1836, and local people believe it is older than that.

**Anchor text count:** 309 words, 5 paragraphs, 25 sentences, average 12.4 words per sentence.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2, Phase 1):** bring back yesterday's K-W-L chart, still on the board, and fill
the L column from what students now know. Cross out any W question the brief did not answer and leave it

**3-minute partner re-scan:** partners scan the brief for the paragraph letters, the numbers (180 metres, three
to four kilos, 1836), and the six target words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic (a mountain path, not a race), recycling five of the
six target words:

*The Path Above the Village*

**[A]** The path above our village climbs a long grass slope and then turns into loose stone. My aunt walked it
every Sunday for thirty years. Last spring she slipped near the top and sat down hard, and a walker behind her
stopped to help.

**[B]** A rescue team came up from the road in under an hour, and they walked her down slowly between them. She
was back on the path six weeks later. The village gave her a small prize at the summer fair, and everybody
cheered when she stood up to take it.

*(107 words, about one third of the anchor text.)*

*Task (3-4 min), Levels 2 and 3 -* §0.11 shape **Fix the wrong word**: one word in each frame sentence below is
wrong for the refresher text. Cross it out and write the right one above it.

- **I climbed and then I slipped.**
- **The rescue team carried her up the hill.**

**Answer note:** the first frame is right as printed; the wrong word is in the second: *up* becomes *down* (they
walked her down to the road). If a student changes the first frame, send them back to Paragraph [A].

Everyone else: circle the five words from Day 1 as you find them (slope, slip, rescue, prize, cheer).

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** word forms out loud - *slip / slipped / slippery*, *cheer / cheered / a
cheer*. Each student says one sentence about a time they watched a race or a game, using the past form.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Town Hall Role-Play

Groups of four, formed where students sit; each group takes one role for the whole window: a runner, someone who
lives in the lane, a member of the rescue team, a visitor who came to watch. Split the three prompts across the
groups so neighbouring groups are working different questions, then take one line from each group.

**Prompts:**

1. Tell Monday from your role's point of view, in order, from the morning to five o'clock.
2. The brief says nobody organizes the day. From your role, say what that means for you, and give one reason.
3. Should the race happen again next year exactly as it is? Say what happened this year that supports your
   answer.

**Sentence stems by task Level:**

- **Level 2:** "I ___ed." (one action from the brief, prepared in advance)
- **Level 3:** "First I ___ed, and then I ___ed."
- **Level 4:** "At ___ we ___ed, and that happened because ___."
- **Level 5:** "For my role the day mattered because ___, and the line '___' shows it without saying it."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** the Level 2 student rehearses their sentence once with one group
member before the first prompt, and carries a tracking task through the window (a tally of how many speakers
mention the hill, the cheese, and the crowd), contributing their prepared line at a natural pause.

**Foundation Support (below Level 2):** match a printed word from the packet (*watched, chased, slipped,
lifted*) to the printed photo it goes with, and in the Town Hall hold up the word they wrote on their own paper
when a speaker says it, instead of taking a spoken turn.

**Board record:** when each group's window ends, one member reports a single claim or event to the board, so the
finished board holds all four roles' accounts of the same afternoon.

**Closing Transfer Check (oral, in pairs, 5 min):** tell your partner about **the last meal you cooked or
ordered**: two things you did, in order, and one reason it turned out the way it did. Teacher cold-calls two or
three pairs. Nothing is collected, and nobody reports how confident they feel.
