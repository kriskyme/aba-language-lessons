### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Mystery Quote hook (board-dependent):** Write one line on the board before anyone sees the text:

> "The last hatchling reached the waves at 11:40, and after that the beach was empty again."

Ask three questions and collect the answers on the board as students call them out: Who wrote this? Where were
they standing? What happened in the hour before this line? Do not correct anything yet. After Phase 2, come back
to the board and tick each guess that the text supports and cross out each one it does not.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing telling what happened in the order it happened, and giving the one
reason things turned out the way they did."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** guide, shore, tracks, tide, nest,
hatchling. Show each word beside the paragraph letter where it appears ([A] guide, shore; [B] tracks; [C] tide,
nest; [D] hatchling).

**Levels 2 and 3, Sort first (§0.11 shape for this slot):** before any frame sentence is read, students sort
this printed word bank into the three headings below. The bank carries two extra taught words beyond what the
headings need, so the last word cannot be placed by elimination.

Word bank: *shore, tide, tracks, nest, hatchling, guide, boat, notebook*

| THE BEACH | THE TURTLES | THE PEOPLE AND THEIR THINGS |
|---|---|---|

**Answer note:** THE BEACH: shore, tide. THE TURTLES: nest, hatchling, tracks. THE PEOPLE AND THEIR THINGS:
guide, boat, notebook.

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "little by little" (transparent chunk, tied to the topic) = slowly, a small amount at
a time. Gloss it before reading: "If something changes little by little, it changes slowly, in small steps you
can watch."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Say Something

Partners read one lettered entry at a time and stop at each `[PAUSE: say something to your partner]` marker.
Either partner speaks first, and one of these three moves counts as "something": react to what just happened,
ask a question about it, or connect it to the entry before. No fixed roles, no turn order, and no written
answer: the talk is the work. Circulate and listen for pairs who only summarize, and ask them for a question
instead.

**Five Nights at Tortuguero**
*From the notebook of a beach patrol volunteer*

**[A]** **Monday, 9 August.** I arrived at the village by boat in the afternoon. A guide named Marisol met me at
the dock and walked me down to the beach. The sand was dark and warm, and the waves were louder than I
expected. She told me that green turtles¹ come to this shore every year from July to October. Our first patrol
starts tonight at eight o'clock, and I am already nervous about staying awake.

`[PAUSE: say something to your partner]`

**[B]** **Tuesday, 10 August.** We walked four kilometres along the shore in the dark. We carried no bright
lights, because a bright light frightens a turtle before she finishes her nest. Marisol used a small red lamp
instead. I carried the notebook and a water bottle in a dry bag. We found fresh tracks near the trees, but the
turtle had already gone back into the sea. I counted eleven sets of tracks and wrote the number in my notebook.

`[PAUSE: say something to your partner]`

**[C]** **Wednesday, 11 August.** The tide was high last night, so the strip of dry sand was narrow. We waited
beside one old nest for almost an hour, and nobody spoke. My legs hurt, and my eyes kept closing. Little by
little, the sand at the top of the nest began to move. Marisol smiled at me and put one finger to her lips.

`[PAUSE: say something to your partner]`

**[D]** **Thursday, 12 August.** Tonight the nest finally opened in front of us. Dozens of hatchlings pushed
out of the sand together, and they crawled straight down towards the water. Each one was smaller than my hand. We stood well
back. We kept the red lamp low, because a bright light sends them the wrong way. The last hatchling reached the
waves at 11:40, and after that the beach was empty again.

`[PAUSE: say something to your partner]`

**[E]** **Friday, 13 August.** I slept until noon and woke up with sand still in my hair. In the afternoon I
copied four nights of numbers into the station record. Marisol says that very few of those hatchlings will reach
their first birthday. I know that now. I still put my boots on at eight o'clock tonight, because eleven sets of
tracks mean eleven more nests.

---

¹A green turtle is a large sea turtle. The females come ashore at night to lay their eggs in the sand.

²Tortuguero is a national park on the Caribbean coast of Costa Rica. Its beaches are one of the largest green
turtle nesting sites in the world, and visitors walk them at night only with a licensed guide.

*(Footnote 2 is marked at the title.)*

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** partners scan the five entries for the paragraph letters, the dates, and the six
target words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic (a harbour visit, not a beach patrol), recycling five
of the six target words plus the idiom:

*A Morning at the Old Harbour*

**[A]** Our class visited the old fishing harbour on Saturday morning, and a guide from the museum met us at
the gate. The tide was out, so the boats sat low in the mud below the wall. The guide showed us two iron tracks
in the stone, where carts once carried fish up to the road. Gulls had built a nest on the roof of the grey
warehouse, and one bird watched us the whole time.

**[B]** We walked along the shore for twenty minutes and collected pieces of blue and white china. Little by
little the water came back, and by eleven o'clock the boats were floating again. Our guide said that the tide
turns twice every day, in winter and in summer.

*(122 words, about one third of the anchor text.)*

*Task (3-4 min):* circle the five words from Tuesday's list as you find them (guide, tide, tracks, nest, shore),
then tell your partner in one sentence what happened first at the harbour and what happened after that.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** word forms out loud - *count / counted / a count*, *walk / walked / a
walk*. Each student says one sentence about last weekend using the past form.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each student completes their own cell first as rehearsal,
then the group shares out loud.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading row ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('I ___-ed.') narrating a single past action, decoding the open-slot word to identify the matching image,
with visual support."). This slot's §0.11 shape is **Better of two**; the form is not produced again today, it
was produced on Day 1.

- Task: look at the photo of the person carrying a bucket (`Lesson1_Tortuguero_Img_Carried.jpg`). Two frame
  sentences are printed under it. Circle the one that fits the photo, and say the word that makes it fit.
  **I carried.** / **I counted.**
  **Answer note:** "I carried." The word is *carried*: the person is holding a bucket and walking with it.
- Respectful Tiers add-on: which night on the beach would you rather have worked, Tuesday or Thursday? Circle
  one, then say one word from the word bank that shows why: *quiet, dark, long, busy*.
  **Answer note:** either night is a correct choice; the word has to come from the bank and fit the entry the
  student picked.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading row ("...the two-slot frame narrating two
sequential past actions, decoding both open-slot words to identify the matching picture pair."). §0.11 shape:
**Better of two** at the two-slot frame.

- Task: two frame sentences are printed under the same picture pair (the walking photo, then the counting
  photo). Circle the one that matches the order of the pair, and say the word that makes it fit.
  **I walked and then I counted.** / **I counted and then I walked.**
  **Answer note:** "I walked and then I counted." The order word is *then*: the walking photo comes first.

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading row ("...identify the sequence of events, one outcome, and the
brief reason given for that outcome...").

- Task: list the four nights of the patrol in order, in four short phrases, then write one sentence naming what
  finally happened on Thursday and one sentence giving the reason the journal states for keeping the lamp low.
  **Answer note:** Monday arrive, Tuesday walk and count tracks, Wednesday wait at the nest, Thursday the nest
  opens; outcome: the hatchlings reached the sea, the last at 11:40; reason: a bright light sends them the wrong
  way.

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading row ("...summarize the sequence of events and why they
mattered, and identify at least one clearly telling action or detail that signals how someone felt, even though
the feeling is never named directly...").

- Task: in 2-3 sentences, summarize the five nights and say why the week mattered to the writer. Then find one
  action in Paragraph [E] that shows how the writer feels about the work, and copy the phrase. The journal never
  names the feeling.
  **Answer note:** the summary runs arrival, patrol and counting, waiting, the nest opening, the record. The
  telling action is "I still put my boots on at eight o'clock tonight" (accept also "I copied four nights of
  numbers into the station record"), directly after the line about how few hatchlings survive.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2, Phase 2):** build one sequence chain across the board as each group reports -
Monday to Friday along the top, and under each night what the patrol did, what happened, and (for Thursday) the
reason the journal gives. No group's own cell holds the whole chain; it exists only once all four Levels have
reported.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Panel Round

Four to six students speak in turn from their seats as the panel; everyone else listens with the task below.
Panel and listeners swap at each prompt changeover, roughly every 9 minutes.

**Listener task (never passive, Narrating form per §0.10):** keep a running list of three phrases a speaker uses
that tell you *when* something happened or *what happened next* ("after that," "by the time," "the next
morning"). Bring one of them into your own turn when the panel swaps.

**Prompts, advanced at each changeover:**

1. Which night of the five was the hardest night of work, and what in the journal shows it?
2. The writer finishes the week knowing very few hatchlings survive, and still goes back out. Tell that part of
   the story in your own words, in order.
3. If you kept this journal, which night would you have written the most about, and what would you have put in?

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each inside its own Level's ceiling):

- **Level 2:** "I ___ed." (one past action from the journal, prepared in advance - see below)
- **Level 3:** "First the writer ___ed, and then the writer ___ed."
- **Level 4:** "On ___ the writer ___ed, and it ended that way because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The hardest night was ___, and the line that shows it is '___', because it tells us how the
  writer felt without saying it."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** the Level 2 student rehearses their one sentence quietly with a
partner immediately before the first changeover, and spends the round before their turn on the listener task
above (that list is their participation, not an extra requirement). They contribute their prepared line at a
natural pause rather than on demand.

**Foundation Support (where a student is working below Level 2):** they match a printed word from the packet
(*walked, waited, counted, carried*) to the printed photo it goes with, and in the Panel Round they hold up the
word they wrote on their own paper when a speaker says it, instead of taking a spoken turn.

**Closing Transfer Check (oral, in pairs, 5 min):** tell your partner about **the last time you bought something
from a shop**: two things you did, in order, and one reason it ended the way it did. Teacher cold-calls two or
three pairs. Nothing is collected, and nobody reports how confident they feel.
