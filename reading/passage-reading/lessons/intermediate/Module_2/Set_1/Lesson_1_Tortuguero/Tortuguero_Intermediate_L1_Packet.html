Five Nights at Tortuguero
From the notebook of a beach patrol volunteer

[A] Monday, 9 August. I arrived at the village by boat in the afternoon. A guide named Marisol met me at the dock and walked me down to the beach. The sand was dark and warm, and the waves were louder than I expected. She told me that green turtles1 come to this shore every year from July to October. Our first patrol starts tonight at eight o'clock, and I am already nervous about staying awake.

[B] Tuesday, 10 August. We walked four kilometres along the shore in the dark. We carried no bright lights, because a bright light frightens a turtle before she finishes her nest. Marisol used a small red lamp instead. I carried the notebook and a water bottle in a dry bag. We found fresh tracks near the trees, but the turtle had already gone back into the sea. I counted eleven sets of tracks and wrote the number in my notebook.

[C] Wednesday, 11 August. The tide was high last night, so the strip of dry sand was narrow. We waited beside one old nest for almost an hour, and nobody spoke. My legs hurt, and my eyes kept closing. Little by little, the sand at the top of the nest began to move. Marisol smiled at me and put one finger to her lips.

[D] Thursday, 12 August. Tonight the nest finally opened in front of us. Dozens of hatchlings pushed out of the sand together, and they crawled straight down towards the water. Each one was smaller than my hand. We stood well back. We kept the red lamp low, because a bright light sends them the wrong way. The last hatchling reached the waves at 11:40, and after that the beach was empty again.

[E] Friday, 13 August. I slept until noon and woke up with sand still in my hair. In the afternoon I copied four nights of numbers into the station record. Marisol says that very few of those hatchlings will reach their first birthday. I know that now. I still put my boots on at eight o'clock tonight, because eleven sets of tracks mean eleven more nests.

1. A green turtle is a large sea turtle. The females come ashore at night to lay their eggs in the sand.
2. Tortuguero is a national park on the Caribbean coast of Costa Rica. Its beaches are one of the largest green turtle nesting sites in the world, and visitors walk them at night only with a licensed guide.

A Morning at the Old Harbour

[A] Our class visited the old fishing harbour on Saturday morning, and a guide from the museum met us at the gate. The tide was out, so the boats sat low in the mud below the wall. The guide showed us two iron tracks in the stone, where carts once carried fish up to the road. Gulls had built a nest on the roof of the grey warehouse, and one bird watched us the whole time.

[B] We walked along the shore for twenty minutes and collected pieces of blue and white china. Little by little the water came back, and by eleven o'clock the boats were floating again. Our guide said that the tide turns twice every day, in winter and in summer.
