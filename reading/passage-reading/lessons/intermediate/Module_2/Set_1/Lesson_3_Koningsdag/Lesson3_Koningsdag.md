### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Take a Side hook (board-dependent):** the packet prints the choice below. Every student circles one, then
turns to someone who circled the other and gives one reason. A show of hands gives the board tally, which stays
up for the rest of the lesson.

> You have one box of things you do not use any more. On market day you:
> (a) sell it all cheaply and go home early
> (b) hold your prices and stay until the evening

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing telling what happened in the order it happened, and giving the one
reason things turned out the way they did."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** pavement, crate, blanket, price, coins,
swap. Show each word beside the paragraph letter where it appears ([A] pavement, crate, blanket; [B] price,
coins, swap).

**Levels 2 and 3, Sort first (§0.11 shape for this slot):** before any frame sentence is read, sort this printed
word bank under the three headings. The bank carries two extra taught words, so the last word cannot be placed
by elimination.

Word bank: *crate, blanket, pavement, price, coins, swap, coffee, stairs*

| WHAT YOU SELL FROM | WHAT MONEY DOES | WHERE YOU STAND |
|---|---|---|

**Answer note:** WHAT YOU SELL FROM: crate, blanket. WHAT MONEY DOES: price, coins, swap. WHERE YOU STAND:
pavement, stairs. (*coffee* belongs to none of the three.)

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "a good deal" (transparent chunk, tied to the topic) = a good result for the money.
Gloss it before reading: "If you got a good deal, you are happy with what you paid or what you were paid."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Partner / Shared Reading

Partner A reads Paragraphs [A]-[C] (Sanne's email) out loud while Partner B follows with a finger and stops the
reader at any word they want repeated. Partner B then reads Paragraphs [D]-[E] (Daan's reply) under the same
rule. After each email, the listening partner says one sentence back: what the writer did, in order. Note that
the paragraph letters are bracketed and the partner roles are spelled out, so the two never blur.

---

**From:** Sanne
**To:** Daan
**Subject:** We survived the vrijmarkt¹

**[A]** Daan, we got home twenty minutes ago, and my hands still smell of old books. We left the flat at half
past six in the morning, because the good places on the pavement go early. Mila carried two crates of toys down
the stairs, and I took a blanket with all my winter clothes on it. The pavement was still wet from the night. By
seven o'clock the whole street was full, and a man on the corner was already selling coffee from a box.

**[B]** The first hour was slow. A man asked the price of my grey coat, looked at it for a long time, and then
walked away without a word. After that a woman bought six mugs, and she paid in small coins from a jar. Mila
sold her old bicycle helmet to a boy who wanted a swap instead, so she came back with two comics and one euro.
A girl offered me four euros for the grey coat at eleven o'clock, and I took it.

**[C]** We packed up at four o'clock, because it started to rain hard. We came home with an empty crate,
forty-one euros, and somebody else's plant. Mila counted the coins twice on the kitchen table. Next year we are
leaving the flat at six.

---

**From:** Daan
**To:** Sanne
**Subject:** RE: We survived the vrijmarkt

**[D]** Forty-one euros is a good deal for a wet Monday. I stayed here in Utrecht and sold nothing at all. I
forgot that our market runs until eight in the evening, so I arrived at two with half the street already packed
up. I sat on my blanket for three hours beside a man selling forty identical lamps.

**[E]** Next year I am coming to your street at six with coffee and a folding chair. Please keep the plant,
though. There is no room for it in this flat, and I would only give it back to you in May.

---

¹The *vrijmarkt* ("free market") is the street market held across the Netherlands on King's Day, 27 April.
For that one day anyone may sell used things on the pavement without a permit, from six in the morning until
eight at night.

**Anchor text count:** 312 words, 5 paragraphs, 21 sentences, average 14.9 words per sentence.

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** partners scan the two emails for the paragraph letters, the clock times, and the
six target words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic (a house move, not a market), recycling five of the
six target words:

*Moving Day on a Narrow Street*

**[A]** The van arrived at eight, and the driver parked half of it on the pavement because the street was too
narrow. We carried fourteen crates down two flights of stairs before the coffee was even cold. My brother
wrapped the mirror in a blanket and held it on his knees for the whole drive.

**[B]** The price of the van included two hours of the driver's time, and we used every minute of it. At the new
flat we found a jar of old coins behind the kitchen door, left by somebody who lived there before us. We put it
on the window sill, and it is still there.

*(121 words, about one third of the anchor text.)*

*Task (3-4 min):* circle the five words from Day 1 as you find them (pavement, crate, blanket, price, coins),
then tell your partner in one sentence what happened first on moving day and what happened after that.

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** word forms out loud - *sell / sold / a sale*, *pack / packed / packing
up*. Each student says one sentence about something they sold or gave away, using the past form.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each student completes their own cell first as rehearsal,
then the group shares out loud.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading row ("Can read one short sentence using the fixed
frame ('I ___-ed.') narrating a single past action, decoding the open-slot word to identify the matching image,
with visual support."). §0.11 shape **Read to a partner**; the form is not produced again today, it was produced
on Day 1.

- Task: read this frame sentence out loud to your partner. Your partner says which paragraph letter it belongs
  to, and you check the letter together against the page.
  **I packed.**
  **Answer note:** Paragraph [C] ("We packed up at four o'clock"). Accept [D] if the student reads it as Daan's
  street packing up, as long as they can point to the sentence.
- Respectful Tiers add-on: whose day would you rather have had, Sanne's or Daan's? Circle one, then say one word
  from the bank that shows why: *busy, quiet, wet, long*.
  **Answer note:** either choice is correct; the word must come from the bank and fit the email chosen.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading row ("...the two-slot frame narrating two
sequential past actions, decoding both open-slot words to identify the matching picture pair."). §0.11 shape
**Read to a partner** at the two-slot frame.

- Task: read this frame sentence out loud to your partner. Your partner says which two paragraph letters it
  covers and which word tells the order, and you check together against the page.
  **I sold and then I packed.**
  **Answer note:** Paragraphs [B] then [C]; the order word is *then*.

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading row ("...identify the sequence of events, one outcome, and the
brief reason given for that outcome...").

- Task: write the day as five short phrases from half past six to four o'clock, then one sentence naming what
  Sanne came home with and one sentence giving the reason the email states for going home when they did.
  **Answer note:** leave the flat, carry the crates, the slow first hour, the mugs and the helmet swap, packing
  up at four; home with an empty crate, forty-one euros, and a plant; reason: "because it started to rain hard."

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading row ("...summarize the sequence of events and why they
mattered, and identify at least one clearly telling action or detail that signals how someone felt, even though
the feeling is never named directly...").

- Task: in 2-3 sentences, summarize both emails and say why the day mattered to each writer. Then find one
  action, one in Sanne's email and one in Daan's, that shows how each of them feels about the day, and copy the
  phrases. Neither email names a feeling.
  **Answer note:** Sanne: "Mila counted the coins twice on the kitchen table" or "Next year we are leaving the
  flat at six." Daan: "I sat on my blanket for three hours beside a man selling forty identical lamps" or "Next
  year I am coming to your street at six."

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2, Phase 2):** build one chain across the board as groups report - Sanne's day on
the top row, Daan's on the bottom row, hour by hour - so the class can see the two days against each other. No
group's own cell holds both rows.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Small-group discussion

Groups of four, formed where students sit. Split the three prompts across the groups so neighbouring groups are
not answering the same one; take one line from each group at the end.

**Prompts:**

1. Which of the two writers had the better day, and what in their own email shows it?
2. Tell the story of Sanne's morning in order, from the flat to the first sale.
3. Daan says he will come at six next year. Say what went wrong this year, in order, and what he is changing.

**Sentence stems by task Level:**

- **Level 2:** "I ___ed." (one action from the emails, prepared in advance)
- **Level 3:** "First they ___ed, and then they ___ed."
- **Level 4:** "At ___ they ___ed, and that happened because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The day mattered to ___ because ___, and the line '___' shows it without saying it."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** the Level 2 student rehearses their sentence once with one group
member before the group round starts, and carries a tracking task during it (a tally of how many times each
writer's name is said), contributing their prepared line at a natural pause.

**Foundation Support (below Level 2):** match a printed word from the packet (*searched, paid, pulled, packed*)
to the printed photo it goes with, and in the group round hold up the word they wrote on their own paper when a
speaker says it, instead of taking a spoken turn.

**Closing Transfer Check (oral, in pairs, 5 min):** tell your partner about **the last time you had to wait
longer than you expected**: two things you did, in order, and one reason it ended the way it did. Teacher
cold-calls two or three pairs. Nothing is collected, and nobody reports how confident they feel.
