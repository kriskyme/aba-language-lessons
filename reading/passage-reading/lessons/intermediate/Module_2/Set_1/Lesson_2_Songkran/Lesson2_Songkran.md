### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Reciprocal Teaching

Groups of four take one role each and work paragraph by paragraph, with a four-box organizer per paragraph:
**Summarizer** (one sentence saying what happened in this paragraph), **Questioner** (one question about it),
**Clarifier** (one word or phrase the group found unclear, and what the group decides it means), **Predictor**
(what the next paragraph will say, before reading it). Roles move one seat to the left after Paragraph [C], so
nobody predicts for the whole text.

**My First Songkran**
*A newcomer's account of one day in Chiang Mai*

**[A]** My neighbour told me to buy a water gun before April, and I thought she was joking. I moved to Chiang
Mai in March, and I knew almost nothing about the holiday. On 13 April I walked out of my building at ten in the
morning. A boy on the corner emptied a small bucket over my shoes before I reached the gate. That was the first
minute of my first Songkran¹.

**[B]** Songkran is the Thai new year, and the old city of Chiang Mai holds the biggest water fight in the
country. The old city sits inside a square moat, and the road beside the water fills with people for three or
four days. Families park their pickup trucks along the road and fill blue barrels from the moat. Children stand
in the back of the trucks with buckets and wait for someone to walk past.

**[C]** I walked towards Tha Phae Gate² with my neighbour, and we were soaked to the skin before the second
corner. People poured cold water down my back and said the words for happy new year. An old woman rubbed soft
white powder on my cheek, and she laughed when I jumped. Nobody looked angry, and nobody ran away.

**[D]** By noon the crowd near the gate was too thick to walk through. We stopped under a shop roof and ate
mango with sticky rice. A slow parade passed us at two o'clock, and the drums were louder than the water. My
shirt stayed wet all afternoon, because the buckets never stopped for long.

**[E]** In the evening my neighbour took me to her family's house instead of the street. We poured a little
scented water over her grandmother's hands, and the grandmother said a short blessing. That quiet minute
surprised me more than the buckets had. The next morning I went back to the moat at nine o'clock. This time I
carried my own bucket.

---

¹Songkran is the Thai new year festival, held in the middle of April. People pour and throw water over each
other in the street for several days.

²Tha Phae Gate is one of the old brick gates of Chiang Mai's walled city, and the main gathering point during
the festival.

**Anchor text count:** 320 words, 5 paragraphs, 22 sentences, average 14.5 words per sentence.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic (a rowing race, not a festival), recycling five of the

*A Wet Day at the Boat Race*

**[A]** The rowing club held its summer race at the reservoir on Sunday, and a crowd stood along the bank in the
rain. Two old barrels marked the finish line, and a boy in a yellow coat counted the boats as they passed. The
rain soaked every jacket in the first ten minutes, but nobody went home.

**[B]** One boat took on water near the last turn, and the crew used a plastic bucket to empty it. They still
finished third, and the crowd on the bank counted the last ten metres out loud. After the race the club held a
short parade of boats along the bank, and the crowd clapped from under their umbrellas.

*(114 words, about one third of the anchor text.)*

*Task (3-4 min), Levels 2 and 3 -* §0.11 shape **Fix the wrong word**: one word in each frame sentence below is

- **I counted and then I clapped.** (about the crowd on the bank)

**Answer note:** the first frame is right as printed for the crowd; the wrong word is in the second: *dried*

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Jigsaw Expert Panels

First round: students sit with their own task Level as an Expert Panel and compare cells. Second round: regroup
into mixed fours, one member from each panel. Split the three prompts across the mixed groups so no two
neighbouring groups are answering the same one, then take one line from each group at the end.

**Prompts:**

1. Which minute of this day would you most want to have been there for, and what in the article shows why?
2. The writer's first minute of Songkran was a bucket over the shoes. Tell the story of the day from that minute
   to the evening, in order.
3. The evening at the family's house is quieter than everything before it. Say what happened there and why the
   writer puts it last.

**Sentence stems by task Level:**

- **Level 2:** "I ___ed." (one action from the article, prepared in advance)
- **Level 3:** "First the writer ___ed, and then the writer ___ed."
- **Level 4:** "At ___ the writer ___ed, and that happened because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The day mattered because ___, and the line '___' shows how the writer feels without saying it."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** the Level 2 student rehearses their sentence once with their Expert
Panel partner before the mixed round, and carries a tracking task through the mixed round (a tally of which part
of the day each speaker talks about: morning, noon, evening), contributing their prepared line at a natural
pause.

**Foundation Support (below Level 2):** match a printed word from the packet (*filled, sprayed, poured, walked*)
to the printed photo it goes with, and in the Jigsaw round hold up the word they wrote on their own paper when a
speaker says it, instead of taking a spoken turn.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2, Phase 3):** as each mixed group finishes, one member reports a single event and
the reason the article gives for it; these are written on the board in the order the day happened, so the
finished board is the class's own version of the day and no single group's work.

**Closing Transfer Check (oral, in pairs, 5 min):** tell your partner about **this morning, from waking up to
leaving home**: two things you did, in order, and one reason it went the way it did. Teacher cold-calls two or
three pairs. Nothing is collected, and nobody reports how confident they feel.
