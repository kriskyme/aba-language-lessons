# Set 1 Intermediate Study Guide — Task Level 2 (★, lowest)

Companion to `Set1_Intermediate_Assessment.md`. Review this guide for about 30 minutes right before the test.
This guide does not use the test's new passages or repeat any test item — it reviews the words and skill you
will need, using different examples.

## Vocabulary & Idiom Review

**Lesson 1: A Grandmother's Kitchen**
- **aroma** — a pleasant smell. Example: *"The herbs give every candle a soft, natural aroma."*
- **warmth** — a comfortable heat. Example: *"Maria says her candles bring warmth to any room, even without a
  flame."*
- **herbs** — plants used to add flavor or scent to food. Example: *"Maria adds dried herbs, like lavender and
  rosemary, to the wax before it cools."*
*(Lesson 1: A Grandmother's Kitchen)*

**Lesson 2: New Corner of Yoyogi Park Opens to Visitors**
- **bench** — a place to sit outside. Example: *"Two new wooden benches now sit under the windows."*
- **blossom** — a flower that grows on a tree, usually in spring. Example: *"A row of flowering trees nearby will
  fill the area with blossoms."*
- **lawn** — an area of short grass. Example: *"A small lawn was added beside the steps."*
*(Lesson 2: New Corner of Yoyogi Park Opens to Visitors)*

**Lesson 3: How to Choose Running Shoes That Feel Comfortable**
- **comfortable** — feels good to wear or use, not painful. Example: *"Not every backpack feels comfortable
  after a long day."*
- **snug** — fits closely but not too tightly. Example: *"The straps should fit snugly on your shoulders, not
  loosely."*
- **arch** — the curved part on the bottom of your foot. Example: *"Stand on a hard floor and look at your foot's
  arch."*
*(Lesson 3: How to Choose Running Shoes That Feel Comfortable)*

**Lesson 4: Painting Wynwood Walls: An Interview**
- **mural** — a large painting on a wall. Example: *"One student painted a bright mural on a large piece of
  cardboard instead of using a canvas."*
- **gallery** — a room or building where people go to look at art. Example: *"Every spring, the school hallway
  becomes a small gallery for one week."*
- **bright** — a color that stands out and is easy to notice. Example: *"Many students say the bright colors are
  the first thing they notice."*
*(Lesson 4: Painting Wynwood Walls: An Interview)*

## Skill Review

The skill every lesson in this Set practiced: **describing something by comparing it to something else, and
noticing specific details.**

- **Lesson 1:** Elena compares her grandmother's wood-fired kitchen to her own electric kitchen at home — the
  smell of baking bread, herbs in clay pots, and the warmth of the fire are the specific details that make the
  comparison work.
- **Lesson 2:** The article compares the park's new stone path and benches to the old dirt path, which had no
  benches at all and turned muddy after rain.
- **Lesson 3:** The guide compares getting fitted for shoes in a real store to buying shoes online, and compares
  a shoe that feels snug at the heel to one that is tight everywhere.
- **Lesson 4:** The artist compares her new bird mural (bright, sharp shapes) to last year's wave mural (soft
  blues and greens, round shapes).

## Sample Items (worked examples — not on the test)

**Fact-retrieval example (pointing to the text):** *Where does Maria sell her candles?* Answer: at a small stall
at the Saturday market (Paragraph A of "At the Market"). This is the kind of question the test will ask about the
new passage you have not seen yet — find the exact detail the paragraph states, and point to it.

**Interpretive example (circle the word):** *Circle the word that best shows how Elena feels about her
grandmother's kitchen: bored / warm.* A strong answer circles "warm" and can point to Paragraph E, where
Elena calls the kitchen "the warmest part of the whole house." Any reasonable answer with a text-based reason
earns credit — there is no single "trick" word.

## Self-Check

- Can I match each of the 12 target words above to its meaning?
- Can I find a specific detail in a short passage when I'm told which paragraph to look in?
- Can I circle a word that shows how someone feels about a place or object, and explain why using the text?
- Can I describe something by comparing it to something else, the way each of our four lessons did?
