**[A]** Winter boots should feel comfortable from the very first day, but many shoppers pick the wrong pair. A
podiatrist and boot-shop staff often repeat the same advice, and most shoppers never hear it.

**[B]** First, know your arch type. A flat arch needs more support, while a high arch needs more cushioning.

**[C]** Second, try boots on with the same socks you plan to wear in winter. A boot that fits well with thin socks
may feel too tight once thick wool socks are added.

**[D]** Third, check the fit at the heel and the toes together. A good winter boot should feel snug at the heel but
leave room to wiggle your toes, since tight toes lose warmth faster in the cold. A boot that pinches in the store
will still pinch on a cold walk.

**[E]** Finally, do not expect a tight boot to loosen with time. If a boot feels wrong on the first try, it will likely
still feel wrong after a season of wear. Comfort, more than style, should decide which pair goes home with you.

## SCORING GUIDE (teacher-facing)

Task Levels are not meant to produce comparable raw scores. Grade using **task-Level-internal percentage**
(a student's score divided by that Level's own total), never by comparing raw points across Levels — a Level 2
student scoring 20/24 and a Level 5 student scoring 45/57 are both at roughly 83-84%, and neither score is
"better" than the other.

**Rubric — comparison-and-reason items (29, 31, 33, 35):**
- **Not yet (0-1 pt):** identifies neither the comparison nor the reason, or misquotes the passage.

**Rubric — extended-reasoning comprehension items (40-47):**
- **Not yet (0-2 pts):** summary or explanation is missing, inaccurate, or does not reference the passage.
- **Developing (3-4 pts):** summary/explanation is present and mostly accurate but generic, or the evaluative
  word/phrase identified is not genuinely evaluative, or reasoning is asserted without textual evidence.
- **Meets (5 pts):** summary accurately traces the passage's organization (or the explanation is grounded in
  specific paragraph evidence), and — where the item asks for it — the evaluative word/phrase choice is
  genuinely evaluative and clearly explained.

**Rubric — cross-text synthesis item (48):**
- **Not yet (0-2 pts):** compares only one passage, or the comparison does not accurately represent either
  passage's content.
- **Developing (3-5 pts):** compares both passages accurately but the "which change had a bigger effect"
  judgment is unsupported, generic, or not clearly tied to evidence from both texts.
- **Meets (6-8 pts):** compares both passages accurately, states a clear judgment about which change had a
  bigger effect, and supports that judgment with specific evidence drawn from both passages.

Item 39's vocabulary identification and Item 37-38's are credited on the same Developing/Meets logic as the
comprehension rubric above (any well-reasoned, text-grounded explanation earns full credit — there is no
single "correct" instance to identify).
