### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Mystery Quote hook (board-dependent):** Write on the board: *"The best shoe is one you forget you're
wearing."* Ask: "Who do you think said this? What do you think it means?" Collect 3-4 guesses on the board
before reading, without confirming any of them. Check the guesses against the text at the end of Phase 3.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing describing an object by comparing two versions of it and giving the
reason one works better, the way this guide compares a well-fitted shoe to a poorly-fitted one."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** comfortable, cushioning, support, fit,
snug, arch. Show each word next to the paragraph letter where it appears.

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "in the end" (transparent chunk, tied to topic) = finally, after everything else is
considered. Gloss it before reading: "If you say something is true in the end, you mean it is the most important
point after all the details."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Whisper Reading

Students read the guide individually in a whisper voice, at their own pace. Provide these self-paced focus
questions to answer as they read:
- What should you check first about your own foot?
- Why does Step 2 recommend a real store instead of buying online only?
- What should a running shoe feel like at the heel? At the toes?

**Teacher Circulation Checklist** (targeted oral coaching prompts for individual check-ins while students read):
- "Show me where the text tells you what a flat arch needs."
- "Read this sentence to me quietly - what does 'snug' mean here?"
- "Can you find the word that means the shoe's padding?"

**How to Choose Running Shoes That Feel Comfortable**

**[A]** Every runner wants shoes that feel comfortable, but many people choose the wrong pair. A foot doctor
and running-shop staff at real shoe stores give the same basic advice again and again, and most runners never
hear it. This guide collects that advice in one place so you do not have to guess. Follow these steps before you
buy your next pair.

**[B]** Step 1: Know your foot type. Stand on a hard floor and look at your foot's arch. A flat arch needs more
support. A high arch needs more cushioning. You can also look at an old pair of shoes. Uneven wear on the
sole often shows your arch type. Most people never check this before buying shoes.

**[C]** Step 2: Get fitted at a real store, not online only. A shoe-store worker can measure your foot and watch
how you walk. This step takes a few extra minutes, but it prevents blisters and pain later. Many stores offer this fitting
service for free, even if you do not buy shoes that day. Buying online is faster, but it skips this important
check.

**[D]** Step 3: Check the cushioning and the fit together. Walk around the store in both shoes. A good running
shoe should feel snug at the heel but roomy near the toes, never tight from the very first step. If a shoe feels
tight in the store, it will not "stretch out" later the way some people believe. A shoe that pinches your toes in
the store will still pinch after a long run.

**[E]** Step 4: Replace your shoes after enough miles, even if they still look new. Most running shoes lose their
cushioning after several hundred miles, and old cushioning cannot protect your feet the way new cushioning
can. Write the date of purchase inside the shoe so you remember when it is time to check again. In the end,
comfort matters more than style.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Fact Finder set (Level 2, lowest task Level):**
1. What does a flat arch need? (Point to Paragraph B.)
2. Where should the shoe feel snug, and where should it feel roomy? (Point to Paragraph D.)
3. How many miles do most running shoes last before losing their cushioning? (Point to Paragraph E.)

**Cause & Effect set (Levels 3-4, native Levels):**
1. Why does the guide recommend getting fitted in a real store? (Because it prevents _____.)
2. Why should you replace shoes even if they still look new?
3. What is one reason a tight shoe in the store is a problem, even though "it might stretch out later"?

**Vocabulary context completion** (use the 6 target words from Phase 1):
A good running shoe should feel ______ from the very first step. Check the ______ under your heel before you

Check the Mystery Quote guesses now against the text: does "the best shoe is one you forget you're wearing"

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** Partners quickly scan the Day 1 text for the paragraph letters and the six target
words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic, recycles 5 of the 6 target words:

*Choosing a Backpack for School*

Not every backpack feels comfortable after a long day. A good backpack needs enough support for your back,
especially if you carry heavy books. Look for padding, or cushioning, on the straps and along your back. The
straps should fit snugly on your shoulders, not loosely. Before you buy, put some books inside and wear it
around the store for a few minutes. A backpack that feels fine empty can still feel wrong once it is full, so
always test it with real weight inside. Some students also check the zippers and the material before buying,
since a weak zipper can break within a few months.

*Task (3-4 min):* Circle the 5 recycled words (comfortable, support, cushioning, snug, fit) as you find them. Then
tell a partner in one sentence: how is choosing a backpack like choosing running shoes?

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Word-forms drill - "comfort" (noun) vs. "comfortable" (adjective): "This
shoe has comfort" vs. "This shoe is comfortable." Students give one oral example of each about something they
own.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each completes their own task, then shares orally.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
fixed frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... with visual support.")
- Task: Using a picture of a running shoe, complete: "It is ___." (e.g., "It is soft.")
- Respectful Tiers add-on: circle the word that best shows how a well-fitted shoe should feel - *comfortable* /
  *painful* - using the picture and Paragraph D as a clue.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("...two-slot frame... decoding both
open-slot words... to identify the matching image.")
- Task: Complete the two-slot frame naming two features of a good running shoe: "It is ___ and ___." (e.g., "It is
  snug and soft.")

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading objective ("...identify its features, one comparison, and the
brief reason given for that comparison...")
- Task: Find the sentence that compares buying shoes online to getting fitted in a store (Paragraph C). What
  reason does the guide give for preferring the store? Write one sentence with your answer.

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading objective ("...summarize how the writer organizes
details, and identify at least one clearly evaluative word choice that signals the writer's attitude...")
- Task: In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the guide is organized (the order of the four steps and why that order
  makes sense). Then find one evaluative phrase that reveals the writer's preference without stating an opinion
  directly (hint: "comfort matters more than style," Paragraph E).

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, primary anchor slot for this lesson):** as each group reports back, build a
live two-column T-chart on the board: "Buying Online" vs. "Getting Fitted in a Store," filled in only from what
groups actually say - this is where the class's full comparison exists, not on any single group's handout.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Town Hall

Set up a Town Hall: one student moderates while classmates take on roles (a runner, a shoe-store worker, a foot
doctor) to discuss: "Which step in this guide matters the most, and why?"

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own vocabulary ceiling):
- **Level 2:** "It is ___." (differentiated participation - see below)
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___."
- **Level 4:** "I think Step ___ matters most because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The guide is organized by ___, and the phrase '___' shows that the writer thinks ___ matters
  most."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** before the Town Hall opens, Level 2's student rehearses their "It is
___." stem once, quietly, with a partner, and gets a thumbs-up. During the Town Hall, they take a
listening/tracking role - a tally sheet marking which step (1, 2, 3, or 4) each speaker argues is most important -
with one fully-prepared line ready to contribute at a natural pause, rather than an on-demand turn.

**Closing Transfer Check:** In pairs, every student describes one everyday object by comparing a good version
of it to a bad version, and gives one reason, out loud (e.g., "A good pencil feels light, but a bad one feels
heavy, because..."). Teacher cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share. No self-report, no confidence vote.
