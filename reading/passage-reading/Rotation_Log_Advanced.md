**Revisions, 2026-09-14 (all four lessons).** Day 2's Investigate the Text stopped being a group project. Its lead had read "Work in a group of four, one person per Task... and build your group's chart together," and every lesson's Task A then said to write the days or events "in the left column of your group's chart" - a chart that exists only on the teacher's board and that no packet in the repo prints. A survey of all 84 packets found none renders the Collaborative Evidence Matrix as a grid, and nothing required or caught it: the term appears nowhere in either shared file and neither packet prompt has a rendering section for Day 2 Phase 2 at all. The lead now says choose one Task, do it on your own, then get together with people who did a different Task and share, lowest stars first; Task A names no chart. The `.md` board moments are unchanged - the class chart is still built live as groups report, which is the day's board-dependent moment - it is simply no longer named in a student's instructions. The rule behind it is new: Style Guide §F, see `shared/Changelog.md` v2.41. The lead then dropped its closing "lowest stars first" as well, so the packet sets no order on the sharing. The `.md`'s Phase 2 keeps "combines them orally in Level order" as teacher direction, because the order is a real design point - the Level 4 contribution supplies the sequence the Level 5 and 6 contributions attach to - but it is the teacher's to run, not an instruction printed at the student. Task A then lost its ordering half in all four lessons, leaving only the choice and its reason ("Choose the day you would least like to have walked and say one reason from the text"). The Level 4 Reading row's sequence component is not lost with it: every lesson's Day 1 Phase 3 already opens with a Number-these-events item, and each Level 4 answer note now records that, so a later regeneration does not restore the ordering half to satisfy the row. Lessons 1 and 2 needed their board moments reworded to match, since both had said the Level 4 row supplies the order or spine the other rows attach to; the day and post columns now go up from the order the class fixed on Day 1, and the Level rows attach to those. Lesson 3's K-W-L board moment and Lesson 4's two-column one never depended on it. Lesson 1's Level 7 cell was then rewritten to match its three siblings. It had read "Take one stretch from the route notes [G] to [J] and rewrite its sentence...", which was unreadable three ways: "stretch" reads as a stretch of trail in a hiking text, "one stretch... its sentence" names no sentence, and "say which word you had to move" presupposed the technique. The paragraph letters were the worse half: [G] to [J] are printed back in Unit 1A, while the only lettered text on the Unit 1B pages where Task D sits is the Old Quarry refresher, whose letters restart at [A]. Lessons 2, 3 and 4 never had the problem - all three name their second text in the stem ("from the Meridian update," "from the heritage note") and use no letters - so this was Lesson 1 alone, not a Set-wide pattern, and the fix was to adopt the siblings' shape: "Take one sentence from the route notes and rewrite it so it keeps every fact but leaves the reader expecting a hard day. Then say what the original was buying by putting it the other way round." The answer note gained what the original buys and a second acceptable sentence. The Closing Transfer Check was then fixed in all four lessons. Each had said "In one sentence, tell your partner..." and then asked for the events in order with one moment made to matter, in a Module whose verb is Narrating and whose Level 5 row is an extended account: the cap and the demand contradicted each other, and Lessons 2 and 3 had gone grammatically adrift trying to hold both ("with one moment in it made the moment that mattered"). All four now say three or four sentences, and the two broken sentences are repaired. The rule behind it is new: Quality Standards §D2 extended, see `shared/Changelog.md` v1.30; a check of every Closing Transfer Check in the repo found no other one-sentence cap on a sequence. In the same pass Lesson 1's first discussion prompt dropped "Say what you mean by honest," leaving "The journal and the route notes are both true. Is one of them more honest?" Hooks rewritten to Quality Standards §D13, which requires the hook
to name the topic before it asks anything: Lesson 1's Mystery Quote now orients, asks a question from the
student's own experience, and only then prints a line from Paragraph [E], chosen because the diary's closing
line is the answer to its own Task C item 3 (§C9). In the same pass the Task A ordering item in every lesson
dropped its "write the paragraph letter beside each" half, keeping the order alone, and Lesson 1's Task A item 2
was rewritten because its stem ("the writer stops telling people the trail is easy") stated the very thing it
asked the student to find (§C9). Lesson 1's Task B item 4 also lost a dangling "other" (no action in [C] had
been identified by any earlier item, so there was nothing for it to be other than) and had its answer note widened
to accept both qualifying quotations. Lesson 1 carries eighteen revisions and sits at 2.1.1.18; the others at .11. The last fixed its Day 2 partner re-scan, which asked for "the paragraph where they are lowest" in a text that states no elevation: [E] was inferable from the gorge and the tree line, [A] equally defensible as the start of the climb, and the answer note named only [E]. It now asks for the paragraph where they reach the trees, which is stated and lands on the same paragraph (§C12). The same note had also claimed [B] was "stated outright" as the highest; it is not. The text calls the hut at Hrafntinnusker "the highest and coldest on the trail," a fact about the hut, and [B] is reached by inference from that plus [C]'s "We came down off the snow by ten." The note now says so and tells the teacher not to present it as stated. The re-scan was then cut altogether, leaving Day 2's warm-up as the refresher text plus its two items, and the spoken round dropped both its "answer out loud, in a full sentence" lead and its third question, "Where is the nearest ridge to this building?", which asked for a geographic fact the student has no way of knowing rather than a use of the word; the other three lessons' equivalent questions all stay inside the student's own experience and were left alone, as was their shared "answered aloud in a full sentence" lead, no shared rule forbidding it. The last gave every printed text a byline naming its source and made each Task D lead name the text it is about, instead of "Read the second text, then answer." In Lesson 1 the second text is headed "The Laugavegur: Four Days, Four Huts," which says nothing about who wrote it, so a student had to infer which block the items meant by "the route notes." Lesson 3's anchor gained a byline too, the only text in the Set whose `.md` never carried a source line. The cause was in the stylesheet: Style Guide §B scoped `.byline` to `.article` while §2.10 puts a second text in a `.refresher` block, so there was no class for a second text's source line and the packet had silently dropped the one the `.md` supplied (see `shared/Changelog.md`, Style Guide v2.40). The last dropped "Name both with their paragraph letters" from Task C item 1, and rebuilt item 2's sort as a three-way one. The two-way stated-event / signalled-reaction sort was ill-formed for this Module: in a narrative an action routinely reports what happened and shows how somebody took it in the same clause, so lines like "she sat down on a rock and did not get up for a long time" had two defensible homes and no correct answer. The sorts now carry an E / R / B key, with the pure categories reserved for lines that do only one thing; keys are E,B,R,E,B / E,B,R,R / E,E,B,E,B / E,R,E,B,E,E, still differing in length, split and order across the Set (§C11). The rule behind it is new: Quality Standards §C12, see `shared/Changelog.md` v1.28. The last trimmed Task D to two items: the compare item now asks for two shared subjects rather than three, and the standalone unstated-interest question was dropped, its objective coverage carried by the remaining item, which asks what a text needs its version for or whether it could still do its job. Lesson 1's remaining item also lost the framing sentence that named Paragraph [F] and its move, since locating that is the student's work (§C9). Each answer note now records where the Level 7 row's unstated-motive component is met, so a later regeneration does not restore the deleted question to satisfy it. The last of them rebuilt Task D item 1 in all four lessons: it had printed a table naming the three shared subjects and pairing both texts' quotations, which performs the locating and matching the Level 7 row assigns to the student. Both texts are printed in full in a Reading packet, so the student can do that work; the item now says how many shared subjects to find and gives ruled space for each side's wording. The rule behind it changed too: Quality Standards §C9's three-excerpts-per-source floor turns out to be conditional on the source not being re-inspectable, which is why it reads correctly for a Listening/Speaking clip and backwards for a printed second text (see `shared/Changelog.md`, v1.27 / Style Guide v2.39). The find-one-more-of-each-kind half that the Task C rebuild had added to item 2 was removed again from all four lessons on review: the rebuilt sort carries the difficulty on its own, through its varied length, split and order and its one uncued member per category, so the extra half was depth the item did not need. The printed sort stays, which is what Day 2's matrix depends on having been modelled first (§D5). Its fifth dropped "and how you know" from the rebuilt Task C item 1: with the student already naming both stretches and their paragraph letters on the page, that clause asked them to justify an identification they had just made. The other three lessons keep their own second clause, which in each case asks something new rather than a justification (how the timestamps help it work, what it costs the writer, why an article about a Roman floor might want that).

**Task C rebuilt across all four lessons, same pass.** Its first item had been naming the pacing contrast in its own stem ("Day 3 takes one paragraph, the few minutes take most of [C]"), which performs the identifying the Level 6 CSS row assigns to the student and leaves an answer that is close to tautological, since the longer treatment is self-evidently the weightier one; it now asks the student to find both stretches. Its second item was the same four-line sort with the same E, R, E, R key in all four lessons, an even split open to elimination, with every reaction line carrying a word of absence ("nobody," "without," "never," "hardly") that a student could sort on without doing the judgement. The four sorts now differ in length (5, 4, 5, 6), in split (2E/3R, 1E/3R, 3E/2R, 4E/2R) and in answer order, each includes one reaction line with no absence marker, and each adds a find-one-more-of-each-kind half. Both rules are new: Quality Standards §C9 extended and §C11 added, see `shared/Changelog.md` v1.26.

**Protocol and hook names made room-neutral (2026-09-10).** Quality Standards §D11 retired every hook and
protocol that required rearranging the room. The table rows above carry the new names, and all four affected
lessons were rewritten to match in the same pass: Lesson 1's Fishbowl became a **Panel Round** (speakers take
turns from their seats, listeners keep the same tally task), Lesson 4's Concentric Circles became **Rotating
Partners** (find a new partner per prompt) and its Four-Corner Debate became **Take a Side** (the four options
printed in the packet and circled, with a show of hands for the board tally), and Lesson 2's hook was renamed
K-W-L Walk -> **K-W-L Chart**, which is a rename only: it already built the chart on the board with nobody
leaving their seat. The old-to-new mapping is in Listening/Speaking's `Rotation_Log.md`.
