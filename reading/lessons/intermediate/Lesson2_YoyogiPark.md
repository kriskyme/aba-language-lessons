## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  K-W-L Walk, Skill      Reciprocal Teaching         Fact Finders / C&E
  Spotlight, Vocab          Anchor Text                Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**K-W-L Walk hook (board-dependent):** Build a K-W-L chart live on the board. Ask: "What do you already know
about city parks?" (fill the K column from student call-outs). Then: "What do you want to know about this park?"
(fill the W column). Do not pre-fill either column - the content only exists once students supply it. The L column
is completed after reading, at the end of Phase 3 or the start of Day 2 Phase 1.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing describing a place by comparing how it looks now to how it looked
before, and noticing the small details that make a space feel welcoming, the way this news brief describes the
park's new corner."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** path, bench, renovated, blossom,
welcoming, lawn. Show each word next to the paragraph letter where it appears.

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "before long" (transparent chunk, tied to topic) = soon, after a short time. Gloss it
before reading: "If something happens before long, it happens after only a short wait."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Reciprocal Teaching

Groups of 4 take one role each: **Summarizer** (states the main point of each paragraph in one sentence),
**Questioner** (writes one question about each paragraph), **Clarifier** (finds any word or phrase the group
found confusing and explains it), **Predictor** (guesses what the next paragraph will say before reading it).
Provide each role its own graphic-organizer box (four boxes per paragraph, one per role) to fill in as the group
reads together.

**New Corner of Yoyogi Park Opens to Visitors**

*TOKYO -*

**[A]** A new section of Yoyogi Park¹ opened to the public this month, and visitors are already walking its wide
paths. The renovated area sits near the park's east entrance, close to the rows of cherry trees the park is
famous for. City officials announced the opening on Monday, and dozens of visitors arrived within the first hour.

**[B]** The new section includes a long stone path, several wooden benches, and a small open lawn. Workers
finished the path last week, and the benches arrived a few days later. The city also installed small lamps along
the path for evening walks. City workers say the area is now ready for both quiet walks and small family
picnics.

**[C]** Before the renovation, this part of the park had only a narrow dirt path and no benches at all. Visitors had
to stand or sit on the grass, and the path often turned muddy after rain. Parents often kept small children away
from the muddy path in the past. The new stone path stays dry, and the wooden benches give visitors a
comfortable place to rest.

**[D]** In spring, the cherry trees near this section fill with pink blossoms, and many visitors come just to see
them. Local resident Kenji Sato said the new benches make the area feel more welcoming than before. "Now
families can sit together and enjoy the trees," he said. Before long, the new benches were full of families
enjoying the view.

**[E]** Park officials plan to add more benches to other sections of Yoyogi Park next year. Workers will also plant
new grass on the open lawn this autumn. Many visitors say they now plan to visit this corner of the park every
week. For now, this renovated corner gives visitors a comfortable new place to enjoy the park's cherry blossoms
and open space.

---

¹Yoyogi Park is a large public park in Tokyo, Japan, known for its wide paths and cherry blossom trees in
spring.

**Note on the quoted resident:** "Kenji Sato" is a fictional, composite local resident used to give the news-brief
genre its expected quote structure; the quote is not attributed to a real, identifiable individual.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Fact Finder set (Level 2, lowest task Level):**
1. What did workers finish building first: the path or the benches? (Point to Paragraph B.)
2. What color are the cherry blossoms? (Point to Paragraph D.)
3. What will workers plant on the lawn next autumn? (Point to Paragraph E.)

**Cause & Effect set (Levels 3-4, native Levels):**
1. Why did visitors used to avoid this part of the park after rain? (Because the path was _____.)
2. Why does Kenji Sato say the area feels more welcoming now?
3. What is one reason the city added benches instead of only fixing the path?

**Vocabulary context completion** (use the 6 target words from Phase 1):
The new ______ was made of stone, so it never got muddy. Visitors sat on the wooden ______s to rest. The
park felt more ______ once families could sit together. In spring, pink ______s cover the trees, and children
run across the open ______, all part of the park's ______ area.

Complete the K-W-L chart's L column now: what did we learn that we did not know before reading?

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher        Collaborative Matrix         Jigsaw Expert Panels
    Text, Vocab Drill                                     + Closing Transfer
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** Partners quickly scan the Day 1 text for the paragraph letters and the six target
words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic, recycles 5 of the 6 target words:

*A New Look for the Library Entrance*

The city library finished a small renovation of its front entrance last week. Workers replaced the old dirt path
with a wide stone path that leads right to the door. Two new wooden benches now sit under the windows, and a
small lawn was added beside the steps. Library staff say the entrance already feels more welcoming to
visitors. In spring, a row of flowering trees nearby will fill the area with blossoms. Staff say the new path also
stays dry after rain, which was a common complaint before. Local families say they now stop by the entrance
more often just to sit for a few minutes before going inside.

*Task (3-4 min):* Circle the 5 recycled words (path, bench, renovated/renovation, welcoming, blossom, lawn -
find at least 5) as you find them. Then tell a partner in one sentence: how is the library entrance like the new
part of Yoyogi Park?

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Word-forms drill - "renovation" (noun) vs. "renovated" (adjective): "The
park had a renovation" vs. "The park is renovated." Students give one oral example of each about a place they
know.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each completes their own task, then shares orally.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
fixed frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... with visual support.")
- Task: Using a picture of the park's new corner, complete: "It is ___." (e.g., "It is new.")
- Respectful Tiers add-on: circle the word that best shows how the park feels now - *welcoming* / *empty* -
  using the picture and Paragraph D as a clue.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("...two-slot frame... decoding both
open-slot words... to identify the matching image.")
- Task: Complete the two-slot frame naming two features of the new park corner: "It is ___ and ___." (e.g., "It is
  new and big.")

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading objective ("...identify its features, one comparison, and the
brief reason given for that comparison...")
- Task: Find the sentence that compares the new stone path to the old dirt path (Paragraph C). What reason
  does the article give for why the new path is better? Write one sentence with your answer.

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading objective ("...summarize how the writer organizes
details, and identify at least one clearly evaluative word choice that signals the writer's attitude...")
- Task: In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the brief moves from the announcement, to what was built, to how
  visitors feel about it. Then find one evaluative word choice (hint: "welcoming," Paragraph D) that shows the
  writer's approval without the writer stating an opinion directly.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, primary anchor slot for this lesson):** as each group reports back, build a
live two-column T-chart on the board: "Before the Renovation" vs. "After the Renovation," filled in only from
what groups actually say - this is where the class's full comparison exists, not on any single group's handout.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Jigsaw Expert Panels (scaffolded per v2.7, Section 0.10)

Regroup students into four Expert Panels, one per task Level, so each panel has only same-Level members first
(to compare notes), then reshuffle into mixed groups of 4 (one member from each original panel) to share
findings. Split the mixed groups across the three questions below - not every group answering the same one -
so groups compare different answers when the class synthesizes afterward:

1. What is the single best change the city made to this park, and why?
2. Which detail from the article best shows that this corner of the park now feels more welcoming than before?
   Point to the sentence and explain.
3. If you visited this new corner of Yoyogi Park, what would you notice first: the path, the benches, or the
   lawn? Describe it and say why.

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own vocabulary ceiling):
- **Level 2:** "It is ___." (differentiated participation - see below)
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___."
- **Level 4:** "I think the best change was ___ because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The writer organizes the brief by ___, and the word '___' shows that the writer feels ___ about
  the renovation."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** before the mixed-group round, Level 2's student rehearses their "It is
___." stem once, quietly, with their same-Level Expert Panel partner, and gets a thumbs-up. During the mixed
group round, they take a listening/tracking role - a tally sheet marking which feature (path, bench, or lawn)
each speaker mentions - with one fully-prepared line ready to contribute at a natural pause, rather than an
on-demand turn.

**Closing Transfer Check:** In pairs, every student describes one thing in the classroom or school by comparing
how it looks now to how it used to look, or how it could look different, out loud (e.g., "This wall used to be
empty, but now it has..."). Teacher cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share. No self-report, no confidence vote.
