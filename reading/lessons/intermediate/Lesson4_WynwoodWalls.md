## DAY 1: COMPREHENSION & TEXT DECODING (75 MIN)

```
|--Phase 1 (15m)--|--------Phase 2 (35m)--------|------Phase 3 (25m)------|
  Four-Corner Debate      Reader's Theater            Fact Finders / C&E
  Skill Spotlight, Vocab     Anchor Text                Vocab in Context
```

### Phase 1: Pre-Reading & Activation (15 min)

**Four-Corner Debate hook:** Label the room's four corners "Color matters most," "Shape matters most," "Both
equally," "Neither, something else matters more." Students move to the corner that matches their opinion about
art, then 2-3 students from each corner explain why in one sentence.

**Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing describing a piece of art by comparing it to another piece and giving
the reason for the difference, the way this artist compares her new mural to one she painted last year."

**Target vocabulary (pre-teach with in-context paragraph matching):** mural, bright, shape, gallery,
neighborhood, spray paint. Show each word next to the paragraph letter where it appears.

**Idiom/Slang Spotlight:** "jump out" (transparent chunk, tied to topic) = to stand out or be very easy to notice.
Gloss it before reading: "If a color jumps out at you, it is the first thing you notice."

### Phase 2: Text Engagement & Strategy Execution (35 min) - Reader's Theater

Assign two speaking roles: **Reporter** and **Artist**. Format the interview as a script with brief stage directions
in parentheses. Partners rehearse their part twice quietly before performing the exchange for the class or a
small group.

---

**Painting Wynwood Walls: An Interview**

**[A]** REPORTER: Thanks for talking with me today. Can you tell me about the mural behind us?
*(gestures toward the wall)*
ARTIST (JADE RIVERA): Of course. This wall is in Wynwood Walls¹, and I painted it last spring using spray paint
and a few brushes for the fine lines. It shows a large bird made of bright orange and blue shapes.

**[B]** REPORTER: Why did you choose those two colors?
ARTIST: Orange and blue sit across from each other on the color wheel, so they make the bird jump out from the
gray wall behind it. I always start with color before I think about the exact shape. Sometimes I test a color on a scrap piece of
wood before I ever touch the wall. The color decides the mood first.

**[C]** REPORTER: How is this mural different from the one you painted last year, the one with the ocean waves?
*(points to a photo on her phone)*
ARTIST: The wave mural used soft blues and greens, and the shapes felt slow and round. This bird uses sharp
shapes and bright colors, so it feels fast and alive. I wanted this wall to feel like motion, not calm.

**[D]** REPORTER: What do you like most about painting outdoors, in a place like this?
ARTIST: Anyone can walk by and see the work for free. In a gallery, only some people come inside and pay for a
ticket. Here, the whole neighborhood becomes part of the art, in a way, and that changes how I paint. Kids often stop
and ask me questions while I paint, which never happens in a quiet gallery.

**[E]** REPORTER: What do you hope people feel when they see this mural?
ARTIST: I hope they slow down for a second and really look, not just walk past it. Even one bright wall can
change how a street feels, and that is worth more to me than any prize.

---

¹Wynwood Walls is an outdoor area in Miami, Florida, known for large, colorful murals painted by street artists
from around the world.

### Phase 3: Day 1 Literal Comprehension & Vocab Context Check (25 min)

**Fact Finder set (Level 2, lowest task Level):**
1. What two colors did the artist use for the bird mural? (Point to Paragraph A.)
2. What tools did she use to paint it? (Point to Paragraph A.)
3. What did last year's mural show? (Point to Paragraph C.)

**Cause & Effect set (Levels 3-4, native Levels):**
1. Why does the artist say orange and blue make the bird "jump out"? (Because the colors _____.)
2. Why does the artist prefer painting outdoors instead of in a gallery?
3. What is one reason the bird mural feels different from the wave mural?

**Vocabulary context completion** (use the 6 target words from Phase 1):
The artist painted a large ______ using ______ and a few brushes. She chose ______ colors so the bird would
stand out. Anyone walking through the ______ can see it for free, since it is not inside a ______.

## DAY 2: DEEP ANALYSIS, EVALUATION & PRODUCTION (75 MIN)

```
|---Phase 1 (15m)---|-------Phase 2 (30m)-------|--------Phase 3 (30m)--------|
  Re-scan, Refresher        Collaborative Matrix         Concentric Circles
    Text, Vocab Drill                                     + Closing Transfer
```

### Phase 1: Text Re-Engagement & Vocab Warm-Up (15 min)

**3-minute partner re-scan:** Partners quickly scan the Day 1 text for the paragraph letters and the six target
words.

**Vocabulary Refresher Text (8-9 min)** - unrelated topic, recycles 5 of the 6 target words:

*A School Art Show*

Every spring, the school hallway becomes a small gallery for one week. Students hang their paintings and
drawings along the walls, and teachers add small cards next to each one. This year, one student painted a
bright mural on a large piece of cardboard instead of using a canvas. She used simple shapes, mostly circles
and triangles, to build a picture of the neighborhood around the school. Visitors from other classes walk
through the hallway during lunch to look at the work. Many students say the bright colors are the first thing they
notice. A few visitors even take photos of their favorite pieces before leaving. Teachers say the show helps
students feel proud of their work.

*Task (3-4 min):* Circle the 5 recycled words (gallery, bright, mural, shape, neighborhood) as you find them.
Then tell a partner in one sentence: how is the school hallway like Wynwood Walls?

**Spoken Vocabulary Mastery (3-4 min):** Word-forms drill - "brightness" (noun) vs. "bright" (adjective): "The
mural has brightness" vs. "The mural is bright." Students give one oral example of each about something they
have seen.

### Phase 2: Collaborative Multi-Level Investigation (30 min)

Mixed-ability groups of 4, one student per task Level. Each completes their own task, then shares orally.

**Level 2 (extension-down)** - grounding: Level 2 Reading objective ("Can read one short sentence using the
fixed frame ('It is ___.') describing a familiar object, decoding the open-slot word... with visual support.")
- Task: Using a picture of the bird mural, complete: "It is ___." (e.g., "It is orange.")
- Respectful Tiers add-on: circle the word that best shows how the mural makes the street feel - *alive* /
  *empty* - using the picture and Paragraph C as a clue.

**Level 3 (native, anchor Level)** - grounding: Level 3 Reading objective ("...two-slot frame... decoding both
open-slot words... to identify the matching image.")
- Task: Complete the two-slot frame naming two features of the bird mural: "It is ___ and ___." (e.g., "It is
  orange and blue.")

**Level 4 (native)** - grounding: Level 4 Reading objective ("...identify its features, one comparison, and the
brief reason given for that comparison...")
- Task: Find the sentence that compares the bird mural to last year's wave mural (Paragraph C). What reason
  does the artist give for wanting this mural to feel different? Write one sentence with your answer.

**Level 5 (extension-up)** - grounding: Level 5 Reading objective ("...summarize how the writer organizes
details, and identify at least one clearly evaluative word choice that signals the writer's attitude...")
- Task: In 2-3 sentences, summarize how the interview moves from describing the mural, to the artist's color
  choice, to comparing it with past work, to why outdoor art matters to her. Then find one evaluative word (hint:
  "alive," Paragraph C) that shows the artist's attitude without her stating an opinion directly.

**Board-dependent moment (Phase 2, primary anchor slot for this lesson):** as each group reports back, build a
live two-column T-chart on the board: "Bird Mural (this year)" vs. "Wave Mural (last year)," filled in only from
what groups actually say - this is where the class's full comparison exists, not on any single group's handout.

### Phase 3: Structured Oral Output Debate & Synthesis (30 min) - Concentric Circles

Form two circles, an inner circle facing an outer circle. Ask: "Which matters more when describing art: color or
shape? Use the mural to support your answer." Partners discuss for 1 minute, then the outer circle rotates to a
new partner and repeats with a new prompt: "Would you rather see this mural in a gallery or on this outdoor
wall? Why?"

**Sentence stems by task Level** (each capped at that Level's own vocabulary ceiling):
- **Level 2:** "It is ___." (differentiated participation - see below)
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___."
- **Level 4:** "I think [color / shape] matters more because ___."
- **Level 5:** "The artist organizes her answers by ___, and the word '___' shows that she feels ___ about
  painting outdoors."

**Differentiated participation (Level 2):** before the circles begin rotating, Level 2's student rehearses their "It
is ___." stem once, quietly, with a partner, and gets a thumbs-up. During Concentric Circles, they take a
listening/tracking role for the first rotation - a tally sheet marking how many partners choose "color" versus
"shape" - with one fully-prepared line ready to contribute once they rotate to a partner, rather than an
on-demand turn with a stranger from the very first round.

**Closing Transfer Check:** In pairs, every student describes one object in the classroom by comparing its color
to its shape and saying which one they notice first, out loud (e.g., "I notice the color of this poster before its
shape because..."). Teacher cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share. No self-report, no confidence vote.
