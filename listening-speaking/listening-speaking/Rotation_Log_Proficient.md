**Lesson 3 generated (2026-09-10):** two real sources. Primary: "Remembering Ernest Hemingway Biographer
A. E. Hotchner," Fresh Air, NPR, Terry Gross interviewing the writer A. E. Hotchner about Ernest Hemingway,
whom Hotchner knew closely from 1948 onward; originally broadcast July 21, 1999 on the centennial of
Hemingway's birth, rebroadcast February 18, 2020 after Hotchner's death at 102; runtime 16:01, stated on
NPR's own page. Secondary (Level 7 only): the Nobel Prize's own Hemingway biographical note, written at the
time of the 1954 award, about 370 words. The pairing is a friend's account of a man against the awarding
institution's permanent record of him; neither was written in response to the other, and no connection
between them is invented. Generated against `Generate_Lesson_Prompt_v2.5.md` and
`Generate_Student_Packet_Prompt_v2.6.md`. Version `1.1.3.0`. Three artifacts saved in
`lessons/proficient/Module_1/Set_1/Lesson_3_PapaHemingway/`: `PapaHemingway_Transcript.md` (NPR's full
published transcript, teacher-only, carrying its own content warning), `Lesson3_PapaHemingway.md`, and
`PapaHemingway_Proficient_L3_Packet.html`. No image, so the Set's image register is unchanged.

**The source is taught as two cued ranges, not in full, and this is the lesson's defining constraint.** The
interview's first two minutes are about Mary Hemingway's lawsuit to stop publication of Hotchner's memoir,
and in answering the opening question Hotchner describes Hemingway's suicide directly and graphically;
Terry Gross's framing narration names it too. A later stretch about the Buick sponsorship of the television
adaptations ends in a crude anatomical joke. Both are excluded, along with two short passages just before
Range A carrying "horse's ass" and "sex-for-the-single-girl magazine." Range A begins at Hotchner's "They
wanted to run a series on the future of everything" and ends at "when are you leaving?"; Range B begins at
"But the funniest thing that happened was when we did 'For Whom The Bell Tolls'" and runs to the end. Every
quotation, task, vocabulary item, and hook draws only on those two ranges, and nothing about the exclusion
is printed in the student packet. A teacher should cue the two ranges rather than press play at 0:00. The
one thing the cut would otherwise break, an unexplained reference to a television broadcast whose setup
sits in the excluded stretch, is supplied as a Background Note rather than left as a hole.

An earlier plan for this lesson built the hook, the Level 7 compare set, and the Level 8 task on the
lawsuit passage. That plan was abandoned once the passage was read in full, and the lesson was rebuilt on
the clean material: the hook is now a Mystery Quote from Segment 5 ("I picked the verys like you pick
berries"), Level 7 compares on his writing, what kind of man he was, and where the work came from, and
Level 8 targets the Faulkner moment, where Hotchner breaks off at "That was a...", lets Gross supply
"Jealous?", and answers "Oh, that was a real warfare there" without ever naming what was wrong. That target
is subtler than the abandoned one and rewards closer listening.

**Lesson 4 generated (2026-09-10), completing Set 1:** two real sources. Primary: "A History of the World in
100 Objects," Episode 77, "Benin plaque: the oba with Europeans," BBC Radio 4 with the British Museum,
written and presented by Neil MacGregor, then director of the British Museum, first broadcast 21 September
2010. The BBC's own published transcript was retrieved complete via the Wayback Machine, since the live BBC
path no longer resolves; it runs 1,921 words, which puts the episode at roughly 14 minutes, inside the Level
7 range. Secondary (Level 7 only): "Smithsonian agrees to return its collection of Benin Bronzes to
Nigeria," NPR via DCist/WAMU, 9 March 2022, about 700 words. Version `1.1.4.0`. Three artifacts saved in
`lessons/proficient/Module_1/Set_1/Lesson_4_BeninPlaque/`. No image, so the Set's image register is
unchanged.

**This is the purest fit the row could have had.** The row asked for an institution's own framing of a
collection, in a lecture or docent walkthrough, where that framing plays down a known shortcoming. The
presenter is the museum's director, describing for a quarter of an hour a single object his own museum holds
because of the 1897 British raid on Benin City. He is more forthright than expected, saying "booty" and
letting Wole Soyinka call the objects "looted," which makes the hedging better material rather than worse
because it is subtle: "in ostensible revenge," "the details of what actually happened are still far from
clear... but whatever the real facts," the agentless "The arrival and reception of these completely unknown
sculptures," and "the removal of some of Benin's great art works... may have spread knowledge of Benin's
culture to the world, but." Level 8's task centres on the one thing he never says anywhere in the episode:
that the plaque he has been describing is in his own building. Level 7 sets his words against the 2022
article's for the same act, "the removal of some of" against "confiscating."

**Built to the six-phase Day 1 (Lesson prompt v2.6 / packet v2.7), the first Proficient lesson with the
§D12 beats.** Both prompts were released while this Set was being generated. Lesson 4's Day 1 was
restructured to them before it was logged: an audio-only first listen, answers committed in writing before
any replay, a second audio-only listen of Segments 4 to 6 under a narrower purpose stated per Level, then a
new Phase 5 printing two verification windows and ending on a covered replay. Two windows because Proficient
allows a second and the two carry different features: Window 1 (68 words, Segment 5) is the stretch Task D
turns on and is about who is named as the actor in a sentence; Window 2 (41 words, Segment 3) is the
sentence Task B item 3 turns on and is dense with weak forms and lost consonants ("every bit as," "not
least," "able to"). Proficient Lessons 2 and 3 were finished against v2.5/v2.6 and stay on the §D12 backlog
in `Index.md`.

**Adjacency check:** no bank value repeats Lesson 3's, and across the complete Set all four lessons differ
from one another in every rotated column. Lesson 3 ran a long-form interview, Predict from Context Before
Confirming, Turn-Taking and Interrupting Politely, Mystery Quote, Rotating Partners, and rising and falling

across the Set are contrastive stress, reduced forms, intonation, and thought-group pausing, all distinct.

**Open items, flagged (not silently resolved):** (1) the episode quotes the British Museum's own 1897
