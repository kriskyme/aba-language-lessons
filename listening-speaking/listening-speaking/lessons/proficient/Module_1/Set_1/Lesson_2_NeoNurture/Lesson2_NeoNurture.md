**Idioms to gloss (Proficient band, opaque, explicit gloss):**

- **"a booby prize"** (transcript 03:01) - a joke prize given for coming last or doing badly, so calling an
  award a booby prize means it felt like a consolation for having failed at the real thing
- **"have all your bases covered"** (transcript 03:43) - to have thought of and prepared for every part of
  a problem, so that nothing has been left out

**Background Note 1:** newborn jaundice is a yellowing of a baby's skin and eyes caused by a substance
called bilirubin building up in the blood. It is common and usually harmless, but when it is severe it is
treated by shining blue light on as much of the baby's skin as possible, which is what the talk means by
phototherapy.

**Background Note 2:** the Peace Corps, named at 09:40, is a United States government program that sends
American volunteers to work on development projects in other countries, usually for about two years.

**Background Note 3:** at 07:46 and 07:55 the speaker uses two American cultural references as shorthand.
"E.R." was a long-running American television drama set in a hospital emergency room, so its reruns are
where many people formed a picture of what medical equipment looks like. "Buck Rogers" is a science-fiction
hero from 1920s and 1930s American comics and film serials, and his name is used to mean a sleek,
futuristic, high-technology look.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

  2. In two or three sentences, summarize how the talk is organized, from how it opens to how it closes.
  3. Find one word the speaker uses about a device that tells you what he thinks of it, even though he

  4. The speaker says the NeoNurture won lots of awards. From his words alone, does he sound proud of that

  moves to the problem of newborns dying of cold and the incubator his team built for it, then to who

  and closes on three questions about what designers should be designing for. For 3, available words
  include "junk" for the donated equipment, "crummy" for cheap-looking devices, "amazing" for his partner's
  technology, "rugged," and "goofy." For 4, disappointed: "But it felt like a booby prize," or "I don't
  think this kid was even in it long enough to get warm."

- **Level 6 (two stars):**
  1. Write two things the speaker states as facts about newborn health or about the devices.
  2. Write two comments where he judges something rather than states it, and next to each, write the words

  3. Find one place where he grants something good about a design immediately before turning against it.

  **Answer note:** facts available include "Four million babies around the world, mostly in developing

  03:01, where "recognition is fantastic" and "It won lots of awards" are granted before "But it felt like

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Panel Round (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing hedging an opinion, softening a judgement so that it is offered
  rather than pronounced." This is the exact skill today's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask
  students to reproduce.
- The speaker hedges constantly, and the language is quoted here from his own real lines at cited
  timestamps, so nothing in this spotlight is invented:
  - 01:41: "Probably what happened is a hospital in Japan upgraded their equipment and donated their old
    stuff to Nepal."
  - 03:18: "I guess what I would say is, for us, for what I want to do, it's either too slow or it just
    doesn't work, it's ineffective."
  - 05:45: "It's very technological, it's very complex, a little daunting."
  - 07:02: "From a phototherapy standpoint, maybe not the best behavior."
- Teach the frames these lines give: "Probably what happened is ___." / "I guess what I'd say is ___." /
  "It's a little ___." / "From a ___ standpoint, maybe not ___." / "It's either ___ or ___."
- Point out what each frame buys the speaker: room to be wrong, and room for the listener to disagree
  without a fight.

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level.

- **Level 5 (one star):** give an extended spoken description of one of the four devices from the talk,
  organized so a listener can follow it, and include one word choice that signals what you think of the
  device without you stating your opinion outright.
- **Level 6 (two stars):** describe one design decision in the Firefly device, moving between what the
  feature is and why it matters, and explaining explicitly why that one feature matters. When your partner
  reacts, adjust how you are speaking once in response to their reaction.
- **Level 7 (three stars):** describe the NeoNurture twice to the same partner, using only details that are
  true. The first time, choose and emphasize the details that leave the impression the magazine article
  leaves. The second time, choose and emphasize the details that leave the impression the talk leaves.
  Somewhere in the two deliveries, catch a word of your own that is not quite accurate and repair it out
  loud as you go.
- **Level 8 (four stars):** describe, without preparing first, an object you own that is well made or badly
  made. Your partner interrupts three times with a specific reaction (looking unconvinced, asking for a
  number, saying they own the same thing). Each time, shift how you are speaking in response: more formal
  or less, more general or more specific. Correct yourself at least once when a word you have chosen turns
  out to be inaccurate.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Teach reduced forms in fast speech, using the speaker's own audio as the model. The talk is full of them,
  and the reductions cluster exactly where the hedges are, because hedges are unstressed:
  - 00:16, "that's going to change the world" - "going to" runs together as "gonna"
  - 02:30, "we want to marry something beautiful" - "want to" runs together as "wanna"
  - 05:51, "You've got to shine blue light on the kid" - "got to" runs together as "gotta"
  - 06:51, "so that's already kind of a bummer" - "kind of" runs together as "kinda"
- Students mark, on the four printed lines, where they expect the speaker to run words together, then
  check their marking by watching those four moments again, then rehearse their own Phase 2 language in
  pairs with the reductions in place, especially in the hedging frames: "I guess what I'd say is" reduces
  almost to a single word before the real opinion arrives.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Panel Round (20 min)**

- Four to six students speak in turn from their seats on each prompt while everyone else works the
  listener's task. Run the prompts in sequence, with a different set of speakers each time.
  1. A hospital a long way from where a machine was built is offered a donated one. Describe what the
     hospital should find out before saying yes.
  2. Should a design prize go to the object that looks best, or to the object that most people end up
     using? Describe what each kind of prize would reward.
  3. Describe something you own that works well but looks cheap, or looks good but works badly.
- **Listener's task, for every student not currently speaking:** write down one hedging phrase each speaker
  actually uses, and one detail that speaker chooses to emphasize.
- **Differentiated participation:** one-star students prepare and rehearse their opening turn with a
  partner before the first prompt begins, rather than speaking spontaneously from the first turn.
  **Foundation Support (below Level 5):** a student below the lowest task Level's floor takes the
  listener's task for the whole round, writing down the hedging phrases as they are said, and contributes
  one rehearsed line when called on.
- **Board-dependent moment:** a running board record of the hedging phrases as they are actually spoken,
  built from what listeners report between prompts, feeding Phase 5.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- Every student, in pairs and out loud, produces one new hedged opinion on a topic unrelated to the talk:
  an opinion about a rule they have to follow, a place they go often, or a service they use, hedged with
  one of Phase 1's frames. Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, did the one-star Level get a genuine interpretive
  step on Day 1 (item 4, judging whether the speaker sounds proud or disappointed and proving it from his
  words) and a real production role on Day 2 (a rehearsed opening turn inside the Panel Round), rather than
  fact-retrieval and silent listening?
- **Reflection (the one bounded question Quality Standards §E6 allows at this band, tied to a named
  sentence of the student's own):** "Look at the hedged sentence you just said to your partner. If you took
  the hedge out of it, what would the sentence be claiming that you are not ready to claim?"
