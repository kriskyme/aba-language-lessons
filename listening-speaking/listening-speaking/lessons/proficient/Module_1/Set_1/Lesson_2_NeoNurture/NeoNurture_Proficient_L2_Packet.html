Good to Know

Newborn jaundice is a yellowing of a baby's skin and eyes caused by a substance called bilirubin building up in the blood. It is common and usually harmless, but when it is severe it is treated by shining blue light on as much of the baby's skin as possible. That treatment is called phototherapy.

The Peace Corps is a United States government program that sends American volunteers to work on development projects in other countries, usually for about two years.

Two American references come up near the end. "E.R." was a long-running American television drama set in a hospital emergency room, so its reruns are where many people formed a picture of what medical equipment looks like. "Buck Rogers" is a science-fiction hero from 1920s and 1930s American comics and film serials, and his name is used to mean a sleek, futuristic, high-technology look.

Words to Know

1. prototype an early working version of a product, built to test an idea before anything is manufactured for sale
2. rugged built strongly enough to survive rough handling, heat, dust, or hard daily use
3. state-of-the-art as advanced as anything currently available
4. trustworthy looking or behaving in a way that makes people willing to rely on it
5. effective actually producing the result it is supposed to produce
6. distribution the work of getting a finished product from where it is made to the places and people who need it
Phrase Spotlight
a booby prize: a joke prize given for coming last or doing badly, so calling an award a booby prize means it felt like a consolation for having failed at the real thing.
have all your bases covered: to have thought of and prepared for every part of a problem, so that nothing has been left out.

Listening & Speaking · Describing Proficient 1.1.2.0

Objective: I can soften an opinion so that I am offering it rather than announcing it.

Learn the Phrase
Softening an Opinion

The speaker almost never states a judgement flat. Four of his own lines, and what each one buys him:

"Probably what happened is a hospital in Japan upgraded their equipment and donated their old stuff to Nepal." He is guessing at a story he cannot know, and says so.

"I guess what I would say is, for us, for what I want to do, it's either too slow or it just doesn't work, it's ineffective." He narrows a judgement about everyone down to a judgement about himself.

"It's very technological, it's very complex, a little daunting." He calls something difficult without calling it too difficult.

"From a phototherapy standpoint, maybe not the best behavior." He criticizes a mother's action and leaves her a way out of it.

Frames to use: Probably what happened is ___. / I guess what I'd say is ___. / It's a little ___. / From a ___ standpoint, maybe not ___. / It's either ___ or ___.

Say It Like a Native Speaker

In fast speech, small unstressed words run into each other. The speaker does this constantly, and he does it most where he is softening an opinion, because a softener is never the stressed part of the sentence.

"that's going to change the world" sounds like "gonna."

"we want to marry something beautiful" sounds like "wanna."

"You've got to shine blue light on the kid" sounds like "gotta."

"so that's already kind of a bummer" sounds like "kinda."

1.
Watch those four moments again, then say your own Practice It language to your partner with the same reductions, especially in the frames: "I guess what I'd say is" runs almost into a single word before the real opinion arrives. Write the frame you used.
