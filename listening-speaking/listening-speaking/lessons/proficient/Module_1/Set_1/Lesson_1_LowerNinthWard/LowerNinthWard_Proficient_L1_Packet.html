Unit 1A: Mixed Blessing: The Lower Ninth Ward

Listening & Speaking · Describing Proficient 1.1.1.2

Objective: notice when a speaker's word choice reveals an opinion they never state directly, and compare two real accounts of the same neighborhood's recovery.

Good to Know

Hurricane Katrina struck the U.S. Gulf Coast in August 2005; the levee failures that followed flooded roughly 80 percent of New Orleans, and the Lower Ninth Ward, a historically Black, working-class neighborhood, was among the hardest-hit and slowest-to-recover areas in the city.

The Make It Right Foundation was a nonprofit founded in 2007 by actor Brad Pitt to build affordable, storm-resistant homes for displaced Lower Ninth Ward families. It is the organization behind the "Make It Right" homes you'll hear named in today's segment.

Words to Know

1. displacementthe forcing of people to leave their home or community, often because of money or development pressure
2. redevelopmentthe process of rebuilding or reorganizing an area, often after damage or decline
3. investmentmoney put into something, with the goal of improving or growing it
4. gentrificationa process in which a lower-income neighborhood changes as wealthier residents move in and prices rise
5. newcomera person who has recently arrived or moved to a place
6. incentivesomething, often money or a tax benefit, offered to encourage a particular action
Phrase Spotlight
mixed blessing: something that has both good and bad results at the same time, so it's hard to call it simply good or bad.

Unit 1B: Mixed Blessing: The Lower Ninth Ward

Listening & Speaking · Describing Proficient 1.1.1.2

Objective: compare two things, time periods, or perspectives clearly, saying how they're alike or different.

Learn the Phrase
Making Comparisons

The segment itself sets up a real comparison: the neighborhood's population today is roughly half of what it was before Katrina, even though more than $500 million in public and private investment has reached it since. That same kind of gap sits behind Keisha Henry's point that her family has been there ninety years, while others are only just arriving.

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll practice making comparisons using frames like these:

  • • "Unlike ___, ___ actually ___."
  • • "Compared to how it was, ___ is now more ___, but ___."
  • • "On the surface, ___ looks like ___, but underneath, ___."

Say It Like a Native Speaker

Fluent speakers stress the exact word being contrasted, and say the rest of the sentence more quickly and lightly. Practice this real line, stressing the capitalized words: "Developers market these to people NOT FROM this community, primarily WHITE people."

Discuss It

Town Hall: each person takes on one real stakeholder role from the segment (a longtime resident, a newer resident, a community organizer, a city official). Prompt: "What should come next for a neighborhood recovering from disaster?" Take turns speaking, using today's comparison frames and contrastive stress.

1.
As your role, compare the neighborhood before and after the disaster. What's better now, and what still needs work?
2.
Compare your role's view of "progress" to another role's view. Where do they agree, and where do they disagree?
3.
