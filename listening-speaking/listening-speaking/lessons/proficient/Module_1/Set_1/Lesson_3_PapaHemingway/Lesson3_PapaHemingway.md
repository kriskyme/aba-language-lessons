## Target vocabulary (all six occur in the taught ranges of the real transcript)

awe (n) - a feeling of respect mixed with slight fear, strong enough that you hesitate to approach the
person who causes it (Segment 1: "I'd been in awe of him since high school")
considerate (adj) - careful to think about how your actions affect other people, and to act on that thought
(Segment 3: "He was very considerate that way")
generous (adj) - willing to give freely, of money, time, or praise (Segment 3: "He really was very generous
other writers")
warfare (n) - sustained fighting between two sides, used here of a personal quarrel rather than an actual
war (Segment 3: "that was a real warfare there")
transference (n) - the carrying over of a feeling from one person or situation to another (Segment 6: "it
was an easy transference")
prize (v) - to value something highly, especially over a long time (Segment 6: "almost everything that I
came to prize later on")

Context sentences (paraphrased from what the source says, not quoted verbatim):

**Background Note 1:** Ernest Hemingway (1899-1961) was an American novelist and short-story writer, known
for very short, plain sentences and for novels including "A Farewell To Arms," "For Whom The Bell Tolls,"
and "The Old Man And The Sea." He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1954.

**Background Note 2:** in the 1950s Hotchner adapted several of Hemingway's novels for American television.
One of them, "For Whom The Bell Tolls," was broadcast in two parts, with a cast that included Jason
Robards, Maria Schell, Eli Wallach, and Maureen Stapleton. That broadcast is the one Hotchner describes
watching in a motel room.

**Background Note 3:** Gertrude Stein (1874-1946) was an American writer who lived in Paris, where her home
was a gathering place for young writers and artists in the 1920s. She read and gave advice on the early
work of several of them, Hemingway among them.

  available words include "considerate," "generous," "terrific," "warfare," "in awe," and "flea-bitten"

  2. Write two comments where he judges Hemingway rather than states something, and next to each, write the

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing taking a turn in a conversation, coming in without shutting the
  other person down." This is the exact skill today's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask students to
  reproduce.
- The interview is full of real examples, quoted here from the two speakers' own words at cited segments,
  so nothing in this spotlight is invented. Three moves, each with the real moment it comes from:
  - **Offer the word they are reaching for, as a question.** Segment 3: Hotchner says "That was a...",
    Gross says "Jealous?", and Hotchner carries straight on. One word, offered rather than asserted, and
    the turn goes back immediately.
  - **Say almost nothing, and keep the turn with them.** Segment 3: Hotchner says "I nearly fell over
    because I didn't think he was keeping...", Gross says "Right.", and Hotchner finishes "...Any track."
    The interruption is real, but it is designed to be handed back.
  - **Put two words of judgement in, and let them take it up.** Segment 3: Hotchner lists the cast and
    trails off with "whatever.", Gross says "Good cast.", and Hotchner opens his next turn by repeating
    "Good cast."
- Teach the frames these moves give: "___?" (one word, offered, with a rising tone) / "Right." / "Yeah." /
  a two-word judgement the speaker can pick up and repeat.
- Point out what all three have in common: none of them takes the floor. Each one is short enough that the
  first speaker keeps going, which is what makes it polite rather than an interruption.

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level.

- **Level 5 (one star):** give an extended spoken description of one person in the interview, Hemingway or
  Hotchner, organized so a listener can follow it, and include one word choice that signals what you think
  of that person without you stating your opinion outright.
- **Level 6 (two stars):** describe one thing Hotchner says Hemingway did, moving between what he did and
  why it matters, and explaining out loud why that one thing matters to the picture of him. When your
  partner reacts, adjust how you are speaking once in response to their reaction.
- **Level 7 (three stars):** describe Hemingway twice to the same partner, using only details that are
  true. The first time, choose and emphasize the details that leave the impression the interview leaves.
  The second time, choose and emphasize the details that leave the impression the biographical note leaves.
  Somewhere in the two deliveries, catch a word of your own that is not quite accurate and repair it out
  loud as you go.
- **Level 8 (four stars):** describe, without preparing first, a person you know well. Your partner
  interrupts three times using the three moves from Phase 1. Each time, shift how you are speaking in
  response: more formal or less, more general or more specific. Correct yourself at least once when a word
  you have chosen turns out to be inaccurate or misleading.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Teach rising and falling intonation for questions against statements, using the real audio as the model.
  The interview gives a clean set of contrasts, and they matter directly to the day's skill: a falling tone
  hands the turn over, a rising tone asks the other person for more.
  - Segment 3, "Jealous?" - one word, rising, and it is a question
  - Segment 3, "Good cast." - two words, falling, and it is a statement
  - Segment 6, "Did you call him Papa?" - a yes-or-no question, rising at the end
  - Segment 6, "How did that feel?" - a question that begins with a question word, falling at the end
- Students mark the four printed lines for rising or falling, then check their marking by listening to
  those four moments again, then rehearse their own Phase 2 language in pairs, deliberately using a falling
  tone where they intend to hand the turn over and a rising one where they want the other person to
  continue.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Rotating Partners (20 min)**

- Three prompts. Students answer one prompt with a partner, then find a new partner for the next, from
  wherever they are sitting.
  1. Describe someone you know well. What are they like, and what is the one story you would tell to show
     it?
  2. Describe someone whose reputation does not match what they are actually like.
  3. Describe one thing you learned from a person who taught you something.
- **The listener's task, in every pair:** use at least one of the three moves from Phase 1 while your
  partner is speaking, and afterwards write down which one you used and what your partner did next.
- **Differentiated participation:** one-star students rehearse their answer to prompt 1 with a partner
  before the rotation begins, so their first spoken turn is not a cold one. **Foundation Support (below
  Level 5):** a student below the lowest task Level's floor takes the listener's task for all three
  rounds, using the printed moves and contributing one rehearsed line about a person of their own when
  called on.
- **Board-dependent moment:** a running board record of the turn-taking moves as students report them
  between rounds, with what happened next in each case, feeding Phase 5.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
