Unit 3A: Papa

Listening & Speaking · Describing Proficient 1.1.3.0

Objective: I can use what I have already heard to guess what is coming next, and then listen to find out whether I was right.

Good to Know

Ernest Hemingway (1899-1961) was an American novelist and short-story writer, known for very short, plain sentences and for novels including "A Farewell To Arms," "For Whom The Bell Tolls," and "The Old Man And The Sea." He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1954.

The television broadcast you will hear about: in the 1950s A. E. Hotchner adapted several of Hemingway's novels for American television. One of them, "For Whom The Bell Tolls," was shown in two parts, with a cast that included Jason Robards, Maria Schell, Eli Wallach, and Maureen Stapleton.

Gertrude Stein (1874-1946) was an American writer who lived in Paris, where her home was a gathering place for young writers and artists in the 1920s. She read and gave advice on the early work of several of them, Hemingway among them.

Words to Know

1. awe a feeling of respect mixed with slight fear, strong enough that you hesitate to approach the person who causes it
2. considerate careful to think about how your actions affect other people, and to act on that thought
3. generous willing to give freely, of money, time, or praise
4. warfare sustained fighting between two sides, used here of a personal quarrel rather than an actual war
5. transference the carrying over of a feeling from one person or situation to another
6. prize to value something highly, especially over a long time
Phrase Spotlight
hit it off: to like each other immediately on first meeting, without either person having to work at it.
deliver the goods: to actually produce what you promised, rather than only promising it.

Check What You Heard

This is printed upside-down on purpose. Don't turn the page around until you've said and written your answer above.

My old neighbor ran the corner hardware shop for forty years. Customers walked in braced for a lecture, because everyone knew about the long-running warfare between him and the discount chain on the ring road. What they got instead was a man so considerate that he sent you next door if the other shop was cheaper. I was in awe of him. He was generous with his time in a way I came to prize, and something like a transference happened: he taught me what my father never had.

Unit 3B: Papa

Listening & Speaking · Describing Proficient 1.1.3.0

Objective: I can take a turn in a conversation without shutting the other person down.

Learn the Phrase
Coming In Without Taking Over

Terry Gross interrupts her guest three times in the part you heard, and each time he keeps going. Here is what she actually does.

Offer the word he is reaching for, as a question. Hotchner says "That was a...", Gross says "Jealous?", and Hotchner carries straight on. One word, offered rather than asserted, and the turn goes back at once.

Say almost nothing, and keep the turn with him. Hotchner says "I nearly fell over because I didn't think he was keeping...", Gross says "Right.", and Hotchner finishes "...Any track."

Put two words of judgement in, and let him take it up. Hotchner lists the cast and trails off with "whatever.", Gross says "Good cast.", and Hotchner opens his next turn by repeating "Good cast."

Frames to use: ___? (one word, offered, with your voice rising) / Right. / Yeah. / a two-word judgement they can pick up and repeat.

What all three have in common: none of them takes the floor. Each is short enough that the first speaker keeps going, which is what makes it polite rather than an interruption.

Say It Like a Native Speaker

A falling voice at the end hands the turn over. A rising voice asks the other person for more. That is why these four lines from the interview sound so different from each other.

"Jealous?"

"Good cast."

"Did you call him Papa?"

"How did that feel?"

1.
Next to each of the four lines above, write "rising" or "falling." Then listen to those four moments again and check what you wrote.
2.
Say your own Practice It language to your partner again, using a falling voice where you mean to hand the turn over and a rising one where you want them to keep going. Write which one you used more.
