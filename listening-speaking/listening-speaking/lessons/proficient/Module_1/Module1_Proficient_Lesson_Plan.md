## Task Levels for this plan (Proficient band)

Per the Lesson prompt's Section 0.1 Task Levels by Band table, Proficient's task Levels are **5, 6, 7, 8**
(extension-down: 5, 6, both borrowed from Advanced, per the Proficient-only two-Levels-down exception in
`shared/Program_Conventions.md` §B; native: 7, 8). The real source is calibrated to Level 7 (Proficient's
lower/native level): 10-18 minute runtime, full natural or fast pace, idiomatic and some academic register - a
full unedited TED Talk, a long-form (NPR Fresh Air-style) interview, or a university lecture excerpt, per
Section 0.2.

Quoted verbatim from `learningobjectives.csv` (Module 1: Describing, Listening/Speaking modality):

| Level | Listening (Day 1 basis) | Speaking (Day 2 basis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 (extension-down) | "Can listen to an extended spoken description of a familiar or semi-familiar place or object, summarize how it's organized, and identify one clearly evaluative word choice that signals the speaker's opinion even though the speaker never states the opinion directly." | "Can give an extended description, organizing details logically and including at least one clearly evaluative word choice that signals an opinion without stating it outright, working independently." |
| 6 (extension-down) | "Can listen to an extended spoken description and distinguish the speaker's stated facts from their clearly-signaled evaluative comments (e.g. 'the real upside is,' 'what's nice about it is')." | "Can deliver a fluent, integrated extended description that moves between features and their significance without a fixed template, explicitly explaining why at least one feature matters, and adjusting register or approach at least once in response to a specific listener reaction, using complex sentence structures with sustained control." |
| 7 (native, anchor level) | "Can listen to two extended spoken descriptions of the same subject, critically assess how each speaker's rhetorical choices shape a different impression, and identify at least one unstated interest or assumption behind one speaker's framing that neither speaker states outright." | "Can deliver a well-structured spoken description that deliberately selects and emphasizes detail to shape listener perception, self-monitoring and smoothly repairing at least one imprecise word choice mid-delivery, using idiomatic vocabulary and flexible register control." |
| 8 (native) | "Can listen to a dense, digressive, or intentionally evasive description (e.g., someone describing a person or situation while avoiding a sensitive detail) and reconstruct both the surface content and the specific thing being withheld or downplayed, articulating how word choice and hedging conceal it." | "Can deliver an extended, unrehearsed description under real-time pressure - self-correcting at least once when a chosen word proves inaccurate or misleading - while deliberately shifting tone, register, and level of abstraction at least twice in response to specific, observable listener reactions." |

**Sourcing flags for Step 2 (lesson generation):**

- **Level 7 needs TWO real sources on the same subject** for every lesson, same requirement Advanced hit for
  its own Level 7 row - not solvable from the single shared main source. A suggested angle is in the table below.
- **Level 8 needs the primary (or a secondary) source to genuinely hedge, downplay, or withhold something** -
  new relative to Levels 5-7, and the reason every topic direction below was chosen to naturally contain a
  sensitive or withheld angle rather than being a neutral description. A suggested angle is noted per lesson
  under the table.

## Topic grounding

All four topics lean into real, specific, findable subjects with a genuine framing tension built in (a
promotional vs. resident account, a creator vs. critic account, a profiler visibly guarding a sensitive detail,
an institution downplaying a shortcoming) - both to satisfy Level 7's two-framings requirement and Level 8's
withheld-content requirement from the start, rather than retrofitting either later.

minimizing a real limitation of the design; Lesson 3 - this lesson's whole premise (a profiler visibly
circumspect about a sensitive detail) *is* the Level 8 target, built into the topic itself; Lesson 4 - the
institutional narrator's own hedging around the known shortcoming.
