Unit 1A: The Stripped Screw

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Advanced 2.1.1.0

Objective: I can follow a spoken story by tracking the words that mark the order of events, so I can say what happened first, next, and last even when a lot is going on.

Good to Know

The Hubble Space Telescope orbits about 350 miles above the Earth. The instrument in this story, the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph, splits incoming light apart so scientists can work out what distant planets and stars are made of.

STS-125 was the last space shuttle mission sent to repair Hubble. Its crew carried out five spacewalks in 2009. The one in this story was the fourth.

Before You Listen

Here is one real line from the story you are about to hear. Read it, then answer with a partner.

"And my tool is moving, but the screw is not."

  1. 1. Who do you think is speaking?
  2. 2. What job do you think they are in the middle of?

Words to Know

1. stripped (of a screw) worn smooth so a tool can no longer grip it and turn it.
2. access panel a removable cover over the working parts of a machine, so someone can reach them.
3. tether a line clipping a person to a fixed point so they cannot drift away.
4. treacherous dangerous in a way that is easy to underestimate.
5. swap out take one part off and put a replacement in its place.
6. buttoned up closed and sealed so tightly that nothing is meant to come apart.

Phrase Spotlight

hang in there keep going through something hard; do not give up yet.

a house call someone coming out to where you are in order to help you.

running out of ideas close to having nothing left to try.

Last spring our stage crew had ninety minutes to fix a light before the doors opened. Rosa clipped her tether to the rail and went up. The access panel was buttoned up tight, and the third screw was already stripped, so she could not swap out the bulb. The catwalk was narrow and treacherous. Unit 1B: The Stripped Screw

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Advanced 2.1.1.0

Objective: I can tell a true story of my own in an order a listener can follow, using words that mark what came first, what came next, and how much time went by.

Learn the Phrase

Sequencing Language

Here are three real moments from the story you heard: "And the first thing I had to do was to pull off or remove a handrail from the telescope that was blocking the access panel." "And for the next hour or so, we tried all kinds of things." "A few days later, we get back."

His ordering words do two different jobs. Some open a step, like the first thing I had to do was. Others move time in a jump, like for the next hour or so and a few days later, skipping what does not matter. A story a listener can follow needs both.

  • •"The first thing I had to do was ___."
  • •"And then ___."
  • •"For the next ___, ___."
  • •"By the time ___, ___."
  • •"A few ___ later, ___."

Practice It

Do one task below. Work in pairs, and take turns: one of you tells the story while the other listens. When you are done, find someone who worked on a different task and take turns sharing what you told.

Task A ★

Tell a true story of a time you were fixing, building, or setting up something and it did not work the first time.

  1. 1.Tell the sequence in four or five sentences, choosing your own ordering words.
  2. 2.Say how it turned out.
  3. 3.Give one brief reason it turned out that way.

Task B ★★

Tell a true story of a time you were fixing, building, or setting up something and it did not work the first time.

  1. 1.Tell it as a longer account, six to eight sentences.
  2. 2.Say why it mattered to you.
  3. 3.Include one action or detail that shows how you felt at the worst moment, without naming the feeling.
  4. 4.When you finish, your partner says what they think you felt and which detail told them.

Task C ★★★

Tell a true story of a time you were fixing, building, or setting up something and it did not work the first time.

  1. 1.Tell it without a fixed pattern, moving between what happened and what it meant.
  2. 2.Quote at least one thing somebody actually said to you.
  3. 3.Keep an eye on your partner. When they look lost or look ahead of you, change your pace or your wording once, and keep going.

Task D ★★★★

Tell a true story of a time you were fixing, building, or setting up something and it did not work the first time.

  1. 1.Tell it twice, to two different partners. The first time, shape it so the listener finds it funny. The second time, shape it so the listener finds it tense.
  2. 2.Keep the same events in the same order both times.
  3. 3.If your order comes out unclear at any point, catch it and fix it out loud as you go, for example "sorry, that was after the part where ___."

Say It Like a Native Speaker

In fast speech, whole words get squeezed. Listen again to a short stretch of the story and notice the gap between what is written and what you hear: going to comes out as "gonna," kind of as "kinda," want to as "wanna," and and flattens almost to nothing at the front of a sentence.

Mark five places where you hear a squeezed form. Then read the same stretch aloud twice, once with every word fully pronounced and once squeezed, and listen to the difference. Now say one of your own sentences from the task above the squeezed way.

Discuss It

Your class will talk through these questions together, one at a time, from where you sit. While someone else is speaking, keep a tally of which question each speaker is actually answering and one phrase they used to order their points. You will read your tally out at the end of each question.

1. The storyteller spends more words on a few seconds at a window than on an hour of failed attempts. Was that the right call for a story? Would it be the right call for a report?

2. NASA's record and his telling describe the same day. If you only ever read one, what would you not know?

3. Think of a story you have heard someone tell about their own worst day at work. What did they leave out, and why do you think they left it out?

Starter phrases:

  • ★ "First he ___, and then ___."
  • ★★ "The part he spends longest on is ___, and I think that is because ___."
  • ★★★ "His version and NASA's version disagree less about what happened than about ___."
  • ★★★★ "The effect of putting ___ at that point in the story is ___, which serves ___."

Wrap It Up

With a partner, take turns telling the story of the last meal you cooked or put together that did not go to plan. Use three or four sentences, at least two ordering words, and one jump in time. Then say which ordering word you leaned on most.

  • ★ "First I ___, and then ___."
  • ★★ "By the time ___, ___."
  • ★★★ "For the next ___, ___, which meant ___."
  • ★★★★ "What I skipped over there was ___, because ___."
