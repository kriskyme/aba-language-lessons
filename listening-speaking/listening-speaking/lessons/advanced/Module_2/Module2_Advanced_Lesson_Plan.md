## Topic grounding

All four topic directions name real, findable first-person accounts, leaning into real-world specificity per the
band-conditioned grounding rule. Advanced's Levels 6 and 7 shape the choice as much as the Module verb does: Level
6 asks students to spot a clearly missing piece of information that makes an account one-sided, and Level 7 asks
them to compare two tellings of the same events and find an unstated motive. Both work best on accounts where a
genuine competing or corroborating version exists in the record, which is why every direction below sits in a
domain that produces one.

None repeats a Module 1 Advanced topic - Module 1's eight directions are all place, object, or craft
*descriptions* (kitchen, restoration, habitat, studio, waterfront, exhibit, observatory, atelier), a different
verb entirely.

**Cross-Band note (flag, not a block).** This Band's Module 2 Set 1 was planned the same day as Intermediate's
(`lessons/intermediate/Module_2/Module2_Intermediate_Lesson_Plan.md`), and Conventions §F scopes rotation to one
Module/Band pair, so cross-Band repeats are not blocked - but a teacher sourcing both Bands in the same term
would be hunting near-identical material, so the directions below were deliberately moved off Intermediate's.
Two light echoes remain and are flagged rather than engineered away: Lesson 3 (an expedition or survival account)
sits in a domain adjacent to Intermediate's Lesson 2 (a rescue or emergency), and Lesson 4 (an oral history of a
documented historical event) is adjacent to Intermediate's Lesson 4 (an older person's one remembered day). In
both cases the Advanced version turns on a second, checkable account of the same events, which the Intermediate
version does not have and does not need.

## Amendment, 2026-09-10 (before any lesson was generated)

**Lesson 1's topic direction, vocabulary theme, and Level 7 second-source angle were changed.** The approved
direction was "an athlete's, performer's, or competitor's own account of a defining contest or performance." It
could not be met: the source families that carry stage-told athlete narratives (TED, NPR, StoryCorps) publish
their transcripts in a form that cannot be machine-verified, so no candidate could clear the Lesson prompt's
0.3 requirement that a transcript be confirmed before any task is built; and the families that can be verified
(PBS, The Moth) carry no athlete contest narration with a second account of the same events for Level 7. One
PBS candidate, an interview with Lindsey Vonn about her 2026 Olympic crash, was retrieved in full and rejected
on 0.1 grounds: it is weighted to present-tense recovery and future plans rather than a narrated sequence, which
is Module drift.

The replacement keeps the row's format and all four rotated columns unchanged, so every adjacency clearance in
the self-check above still holds. Only the topic direction, the vocabulary theme, and the second-source angle
moved. Decided with the user 2026-09-10 rather than silently substituted.

## Amendment 2, 2026-09-10 (before Lesson 2 was generated)

**Lesson 2's Content Format cell was changed** from "Long-form investigative interview clip" to "Investigative news
feature with insider interview." The approved wording could not be met with a verifiable source: the long-form
investigative interviews that carry an insider's own account are either unavailable as confirmable transcripts or
sit at documentary length. The source used instead is a PBS NewsHour report built around exactly the insider
interview the row is after (Dr. Mona Hanna-Attisha on finding the lead in Flint's children), and it runs close to
Level 5's runtime target. Topic direction, vocabulary theme, listening strategy, speaking skill, hook, and
protocol are all unchanged, so every adjacency clearance in the self-check above still holds.

Level 7's second source is the CDC's own published report on the same finding, not the state's own statement.
Michigan's Flint Water Advisory Task Force report would have matched the plan's suggested angle more closely, but
it is served as a PDF that returns HTTP 403 and could not be verified, so it was not used. The CDC report is
"an investigator's published findings," which the approved angle also names.
