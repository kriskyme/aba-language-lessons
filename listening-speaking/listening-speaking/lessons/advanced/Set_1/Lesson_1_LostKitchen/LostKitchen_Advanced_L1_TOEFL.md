## PART A: TOEFL LISTENING PRACTICE

### Listen to an Academic Talk

**Passage note (per Section 1.1):** the passage below is an original account, written to be
faithful to the real facts and sequence of the PBS segment (Section 0.3 sourcing basis: The Lost
Kitchen, Freedom, Maine, [Segments 1-6]). It reuses only the short quotations the base lesson
itself already established - "quiet little cafe in the middle of nowhere, with really good coffee,
some homemade English muffins, and free Wi-Fi" and "the magic will be lost in a heartbeat" - and is
not a verbatim transcript excerpt.

> Erin French runs The Lost Kitchen, a forty-eight-seat restaurant built inside a restored 1834
> grist mill in Freedom, Maine, a town of about seven hundred people. French never planned for the
> restaurant to become famous. In her own words, she once imagined it would be "a quiet little
> cafe in the middle of nowhere, with really good coffee, some homemade English muffins, and free
> Wi-Fi." Instead, thousands of people from across the country and dozens of other countries now
> apply every year for a reservation, hoping their name gets drawn for one of the restaurant's
> limited tables.
>
> French calls herself a cook rather than a chef, since she never trained formally and instead
> relies on intuition, working each day with whatever local ingredients are freshest. The
> restaurant is seasonal, closing for part of the year, and its growing popularity has forced
> French to confront a tension she did not expect: how to keep growing without losing what made
> the place special in the first place. As she put it, if the restaurant changes too much, "the
> magic will be lost in a heartbeat." She has tried to keep her feet on the ground even as demand
> for a table keeps climbing.

(211 words.)

### Questions

1. **[Main Idea]** What is the talk mainly about?
2. **[Factual]** According to the talk, how does a guest get a table at The Lost Kitchen?
3. **[Inference]** What can be inferred about Erin French's approach to cooking?
4. **[Attitude]** What is French's attitude toward her restaurant's growing popularity?

*(Question-type labels shown here for the instructor only; the student packet does not label
them, per Section 0.3/2.2.)*

### Answer key and explanations

**1. Main Idea - Correct answer: (B) How a small-town restaurant became famous, and how its owner
has tried to keep it feeling the same despite that growth.**
The passage opens by establishing that French "never planned for the restaurant to become famous"
and closes on her effort to "keep her feet on the ground even as demand... keeps climbing" - the
whole passage is organized around that before/after tension, not just a description of the
restaurant.
- (A) "The history of a small town in Maine" is incorrect: Freedom, Maine is mentioned only as a
  setting, not the passage's focus.
- (C) "A specific recipe French is known for" is incorrect: no specific dish is described.
- (D) "How to start a small restaurant" is incorrect: the passage describes what happened to one
  restaurant, not general advice for starting one.

**2. Factual - Correct answer: (A) Applicants submit their name for a reservation and are chosen at
random.**
The passage states people "apply every year for a reservation, hoping their name gets drawn."
- (B) "Tables are given on a first-come, first-served basis" is incorrect: the passage describes a
  drawing, not a line.
- (C) "Only local residents may reserve a table" is incorrect: applicants come "from across the
  country and dozens of other countries."
- (D) "Reservations open once a year for the whole season" is incorrect: this detail is not stated.

**3. Inference - Correct answer: (C) She prefers deciding what to cook based on what ingredients
are available that day, rather than planning meals in advance.**
The passage says she "relies on intuition, working each day with whatever local ingredients are
freshest" - this supports, without directly stating, that her menu is not planned far ahead.
- (A) "She follows a fixed weekly menu" is incorrect: this directly contradicts working with
  whatever is freshest each day.
- (B) "She imports ingredients from other countries" is incorrect: not supported; the passage
  emphasizes local ingredients.
- (D) "She rarely changes her recipes" is incorrect: not supported by anything in the passage.

**4. Attitude - Correct answer: (D) Cautious: she wants the restaurant to succeed but worries about
losing what makes it special.**
Her own words ("the magic will be lost in a heartbeat") and the framing of "a tension she did not
expect" both signal worry, not simple enthusiasm.
- (A) "Entirely comfortable with rapid growth" is incorrect: contradicts the stated tension and the
  "magic will be lost" quote.
- (B) "Indifferent to the restaurant's popularity" is incorrect: her quoted words show she has
  clearly thought hard about this, not that she's indifferent.
- (C) "Regretful that the restaurant became popular" is incorrect: overstated; nothing suggests
  regret, only caution.

### Take an Interview

**Scenario (read aloud once):** You are taking part in a short online interview about food and
community for a local magazine. The interviewer will ask you some questions. You will have 45
seconds to respond to each question, with no preparation time.

1. **[Personal Experience]** Can you describe a meal you've cooked using only ingredients you
   already had at home, without following a specific recipe?
2. **[Personal Preferences/Interests]** Do you prefer eating at a small, lesser-known restaurant or
   a well-known one? Why?
3. **[Opinions on Current Topics/Trends]** Some people think a small local business should stay
   small to protect what makes it special. Others think it should grow to reach more people. What
   do you think?
4. **[Speculate About the Future]** Imagine a small restaurant you love suddenly became famous.
   What do you think would change about it, and would that change be good or bad?

### Scoring guides (for the teacher, a peer, or self-assessment)

**Listen and Repeat - holistic 0-5 scale**

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 5 | An exact, fully intelligible repetition of the sentence. |
| 4 | Captures the full meaning; minor word/grammar changes that don't change the meaning (a missing function word, a transposed pair of words, a mispronounced word the speaker self-corrects). |
| 3 | Mostly complete but not fully accurate; several function words changed or missing, or one content word missing/changed; still a full sentence; occasional intelligibility trouble. |
| 2 | A significant part of the sentence is missing or wrong; not a complete, standalone sentence; hard to understand without already knowing the prompt. |
| 1 | Only a few recognizable words remain; mostly unintelligible as a repetition. |
| 0 | No response, entirely unintelligible, no English, or completely unconnected to the prompt. |

**Take an Interview - holistic 0-5 scale, scored on Relevance, Elaboration, Delivery, and Language
Use**

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 5 | Fully addresses the question with clear, specific elaboration; natural pace and pausing; intelligible with effective rhythm/intonation; accurate and varied grammar/vocabulary. |
| 4 | Addresses the question and is reasonably clear; elaborated but may lack strong connectors; generally good pace; occasional words need minor effort to understand; grammar/vocabulary adequate for general meaning. |
| 3 | Generally on topic but with limited elaboration; frequent pauses or filler words create a choppy pace; some pronunciation-related intelligibility trouble; limited grammar/vocabulary range restricts precision. |
| 2 | Minimally connected to the question, with little relevant elaboration (or mostly repeats the question's own words); intelligibility limited; very limited grammar/vocabulary range. |
| 1 | Only vaguely connected to the question; mostly unintelligible; consists mainly of isolated words or phrases. |
| 0 | No response, entirely unintelligible, no English, or completely unconnected to the prompt. |
