Listening Test: Module 1 Set 1 Assessment

You'll watch: "Artists with disabilities let their creativity soar at this Utah studio," a PBS News Weekend video about an art studio in Utah that builds custom tools to help artists with disabilities make their own art.

Words to Know

1. contraptionan unusual or elaborate machine or gadget, often homemade
2. havena place of safety, comfort, or refuge
3. reckon withto deal with or come to terms with something difficult
4. banea persistent source of annoyance or trouble
5. underdogsomeone expected to lose, or seen as at a disadvantage
Listening Notes

While you watch, fill in what you learn about Jump the Moon below.

  Your Notes
What the studio does
A real example (a tool, or something a person made)
An opinion or feeling someone expressed (and who said it)
Listening Test: ★

Answer each question below.

  1. 1.
    In 2-3 sentences, describe what Jump the Moon is and who it helps.
  2. 2.
    Name one tool used at Jump the Moon and explain, in your own words, why it was made that way.
  3. 3.
    How is the painting pendulum different from a regular paintbrush? Write one difference, using a word like "unlike" or "instead of."
  4. 4.
    Complete: "When Bingham's art room got too crowded, he decided to ___."
  5. 5.
    Do you think Bingham enjoys his work? Circle: yes / no. Give one reason.
Listening Test: ★★

Answer each question below.

  1. 1.
    Summarize how the video is organized (Bingham's approach, the tools, two artists' stories, Bingham's own background, the ending). Name at least two words or phrases that show when the video moves to a new part.
  2. 2.
    Find one word or phrase Lori Jenson or Paul Prendergast uses that shows they feel good about Jump the Moon, without saying "I like it." Write it, and explain what feeling it shows.
  3. 3.
    Give one real example of a tool from the video and explain why it helps the artist who uses it.
  4. 4.
    Explain how Bingham's own childhood experience connects to why he started Jump the Moon.
Listening Test: ★★★

Answer each question below.

  1. 1.
    List two facts about Jump the Moon (things that are just true) and two opinions someone expresses about it (things that show a feeling). Write the exact words used for each opinion.
  2. 2.
    Explain how Bingham's own experience as a child connects to how he teaches today. Write the exact words that show the connection.
  3. 3.
    Find one place where the video shifts from one artist's story to another. Write the words that show the shift, and explain what changes.
Listening Test: ★★★★
You'll also read: a short written page about Jump the Moon from the Utah Division of Arts & Museums, a state government arts office.

Answer each question below.

  1. 1.
    Compare how the video and the written page each describe Michael Bingham. What does each one focus on? How does that change your impression of him?
  2. 2.
    What is one thing either source might want you to think, even though it never says so directly? Explain your reasoning.
  3. 3.
    Find one place in the video where a word or phrase signals a shift between ideas, and one place where the video explains that one thing caused another. Explain how noticing both helped you follow the video.

Speaking Task: Module 1 Set 1 Assessment

Topic: describe a workspace or creative space that matters to you (your own, or one you know well because someone described it to you), comparing it to a more ordinary version of that kind of space, and giving your honest opinion of it without stating that opinion outright.

Present this live in class on the date your teacher gives you. If your teacher assigns you the recorded version instead, record yourself using Microsoft Teams Speaking Progress and submit by the date given. You may re-record as many times as you want before you submit.

Speaking Task ★

Target length: 45-60 seconds recorded, about 60-75 seconds live.

  1. 1.
    Describe a workspace or creative space you know well, in your own words.
  2. 2.
    Compare it to a more typical version of that kind of space. Give a short reason for the comparison.
