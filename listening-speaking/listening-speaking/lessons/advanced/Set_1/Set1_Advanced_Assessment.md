**Tiered Items (20 min)**

Students work at their own assigned task Level (item counts balanced for time-on-task, more/shorter at Level 4,
fewer/deeper at Level 7, matching this Set's own lessons):

- **Level 4 (5 items):**
  1. In 2-3 sentences, describe what Jump the Moon is and who it serves.
  2. Name one adaptive tool mentioned in the source (the pendulum or the power chair) and, in your own words,
     explain the reason it was built that way.
  3. State one comparison: how is the painting pendulum different from a regular paintbrush? Use your own
     connector (e.g. "unlike," "instead of") to join the two ideas.
  4. Complete, in your own words: "When Bingham's art room got too crowded, he decided to ___." [Answer: narrow
     his scope and expand his space / start a nonprofit studio]
  5. Do you think Bingham enjoys his work? Circle: yes / no. Give one reason from something he said. [genuine
     interpretive step, Respectful Tiers]
- **Level 5 (4 items):**
  1. Summarize how the piece is organized (Bingham's approach, the tools, two artists' stories, Bingham's own
     background, the closing line) and name at least two signposting words/phrases the reporter or Bingham uses
     to move between these parts (e.g. "But," "It's also a haven for," "Since it opened in 2017").
  2. Identify one clearly evaluative word or phrase Lori Jenson or Paul Prendergast uses that signals a positive
     opinion without stating it directly (e.g. "kind of cool," "Mike has the ability to help others"), and explain
     what opinion it signals.
  3. Give one real example of an adaptive tool from the source and explain, in your own words, why it helps the
     artist who uses it.
  4. Explain, using the source's own cause-and-effect language, how Bingham's childhood experience connects to
     why he started Jump the Moon.
- **Level 6 (3 items):**
  1. List two stated facts about Jump the Moon (e.g. founded in 2017, located in Logan, Utah) and two
     clearly-signaled evaluative comments (opinions), naming the exact signal phrase used for each evaluative
     comment.
  2. Explain how the source uses cause-and-effect language to connect Bingham's own undiagnosed learning
     disability as a child to his approach as a teacher and studio founder today. Quote the connecting phrase you
     used as evidence.
  3. Identify one place the source uses a discourse marker or signpost to shift from one artist's story to the
     next (e.g. "That was the case with...," "It's also a haven for..."), and explain what topic shift it
     signals.
- **Level 7 (3 items, using both sources):**

  *Introducing the second source: alongside the PBS News Weekend segment above, this item also draws on a short
  written profile of Jump the Moon published by the Utah Division of Arts & Museums (a state government arts
  office), at https://artsandmuseums.utah.gov/jump-the-moon/. That page describes Bingham as "a former artist at
  Hallmark Cards and graduate of California's prestigious Art Center College of Design" with "more than 40 years
  of experience as a professional artist, educator, designer, sculptor, and illustrator," and frames Jump the
  Moon as a program serving people "with disabilities and diverse abilities."*

  1. Compare how the two sources describe Michael Bingham: the PBS piece centers his personal history (his own
     undiagnosed learning disability, having to "reckon with his own youth") while the Utah Division of Arts &
     Museums page centers his professional credentials (Hallmark, Art Center College of Design, "40 years of
     experience"). Explain how each choice shapes a different impression of who Bingham is.
  2. Identify at least one unstated interest or assumption behind one source's framing that neither source states
     outright (e.g. the government page's emphasis on Bingham's formal credentials and "diverse abilities"
     language may reflect an unstated interest in establishing institutional legitimacy for a state-recognized
     program, something the page never says directly).
  3. Point to one place in the PBS transcript where a discourse marker signals a shift between ideas, and one
     place where cause-and-effect language connects two events. Explain, in a few sentences, how noticing both
     kinds of language helped you follow the piece's structure.

## PART B: SPEAKING ASSESSMENT

No new source is used here; the prompt below is invented, grounded in Module 1 (Describing)'s own topic range,
not tied to any of the Set's own 4 lessons' sources or to Part A's source.

### B.1 Mechanism

**Advanced band: a live presentation (solo) is the default and formally scored Speaking assessment for the
Set.** Delivery defaults to solo per Section B.2's own rule, since a Describing-module task doesn't naturally
call for a small-group presentation the way a Socializing-module task might. **A Teams-recording version of the
exact same task is also generated below**, as a standing scored alternative a student can be moved to (an
absence, illness, or any teacher-approved reason) - not homework, and not a separate lighter assessment. Both
versions are scored against the same rubric (B.4).

**Live time budget:** roughly 2-3 minutes per student, so a class of 20-25 students needs approximately 50-75
minutes total - plan this as its own dedicated period, or spread it across two class days, rather than folding
it into a normal 75-minute lesson-length slot the way Part A's Listening assessment is.

### B.2 Topic and emphasized skills

**Topic:** describe a workspace or creative space that matters to you (your own, or one you know well because
someone described it to you), comparing it to a more ordinary version of that kind of space, and giving your
honest opinion of it without stating that opinion outright.

This topic is invented for the assessment and grounds two of the Set's four taught speaking skills at once:
**Making Comparisons** (Lesson 4) and **Hedging an Opinion** (Lesson 1). Each Level's task below states which it
emphasizes most; Level 7 also draws lightly on **Summarizing What Someone Said** (Lesson 2) for a student
describing a space secondhand.

### B.3 Prompts by task Level

- **Level 4 (target length: 45-60 sec Teams-recording / approx. 60-75 sec live).** *Emphasizes: Making
  Comparisons, with a brief stated reason.* Describe a workspace or creative space you know well, in your own
  words. Include one comparison between it and a more typical version of that kind of space, and give a brief
  reason for the comparison. Choose your own connectors (don't rely on a memorized frame). Present live on
  [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress, teacher-approved.
- **Level 5 (target length: 60-90 sec Teams-recording / approx. 90 sec live).** *Emphasizes: Making Comparisons
  and Hedging an Opinion equally.* Give an extended description of a workspace or creative space, organizing
  your details logically (what it's for, what's inside, who uses it). Include at least one clearly evaluative
  word choice that signals your opinion of the space without stating that opinion directly. Present live on
  [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress.
- **Level 6 (target length: 90-120 sec Teams-recording / approx. 2 min live).** *Emphasizes: Hedging an Opinion,
  with Making Comparisons as support.* Deliver a fluent, integrated description of a workspace or creative space
  that moves between its features and why they matter, explicitly explaining why at least one feature matters
  rather than just listing it. Imagine a listener reacts with mild skepticism partway through; describe how you
  would adjust your register or approach in response, and use hedging language ("seems to," "tends to," "in a
  way") rather than flat statements of opinion. Present live on [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by
  [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress.
- **Level 7 (target length: 120-150 sec Teams-recording / approx. 2-3 min live).** *Emphasizes: Making
  Comparisons and Hedging an Opinion, with Summarizing What Someone Said woven in if applicable.* Deliver a
  well-structured description of a workspace or creative space that deliberately selects and emphasizes certain
  details to shape your listener's impression of it (e.g. emphasize its originality, or emphasize its impact on
  the people who use it). Include one moment where you catch yourself using an imprecise word and repair it
  mid-delivery. If you're describing a space you haven't personally visited, include a moment summarizing, in
  your own words, what someone else told you about it. Present live on [teacher-set date], or submit a recording
  by [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress.

### B.4 Scoring rubric

3-point scale per criterion: Not yet / Developing / Meets. Same rubric for both the live and Teams-recording
versions of a Level's task.

**Level 4**

| Criterion | Not yet | Developing | Meets |
