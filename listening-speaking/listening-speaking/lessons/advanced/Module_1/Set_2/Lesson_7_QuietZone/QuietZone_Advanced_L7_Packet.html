Good to Know

The Green Bank Telescope is a radio telescope in Green Bank, West Virginia. It sits at the centre of the National Radio Quiet Zone, a region of about 13,000 square miles where new radio transmitters have to be coordinated with the observatory so that the telescope can work.

Ham radio is a licensed hobby in which people use two-way radios to talk directly to one another over long distances, without going through a phone network or the internet.

This report was broadcast in 2013, and it calls the site the National Radio Astronomy Observatory. The site is now called the Green Bank Observatory, and the observatory's own description of the quiet zone is the second source in Task D.

Unit 7B: Inside the Quiet Zone

Listening & Speaking · Describing Advanced 1.2.7.1

Objective: I can ask a short question that checks or narrows what someone has just said.

Learn the Phrase
Asking for Clarification

Twice in the report the reporter stops and asks a short question instead of moving on. When her phone loses signal she asks her colleague, "How many bars do you have?" and gets "Zero. Searching."

Later, when residents mention their ham radios, she asks two words, "For fun?" That question is what turns a passing mention into Pat Schaffner's account of the storm that took out the phone lines. A clarification question does not have to be long. It has to be aimed at one thing.

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll ask for clarification using frames like these:

  • • "Sorry, do you mean ___ or ___?"
  • • "When you say ___, what exactly do you mean?"
  • • "Can you say more about ___?"
  • • "So is that ___?"
