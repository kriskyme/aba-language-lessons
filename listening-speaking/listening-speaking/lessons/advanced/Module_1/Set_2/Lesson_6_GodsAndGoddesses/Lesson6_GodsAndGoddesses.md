## Governing CSV objective (Level 5, Listening/Speaking, Module 1: Describing)

> Listening: Can listen to an extended spoken description of a familiar or semi-familiar place or
> object, summarize how it's organized, and identify one clearly evaluative word choice that
> signals the speaker's opinion even though the speaker never states the opinion directly.
> Speaking: Can give an extended description, organizing details logically and including at least
> one clearly evaluative word choice that signals an opinion without stating it outright, working
> independently.

Listening half governs Day 1's task-Level basis column; Speaking half governs Day 2's.

## Target vocabulary (grounded in the real transcript)

unveil (v) - to show something to the public for the first time
reimagine (v) - to rethink something completely and rebuild it in a new form
inscription (n) - words cut or written into the surface of an object
replica (n) - a copy of an object, made to look like the original
pigment (n) - the coloring material in paint, ink, or dye
garish (adj) - too bright or too colorful, in a way that feels tasteless

**Figurative chunks to gloss (Advanced band):**

- **"within reach"** - close enough to touch, and so, figuratively, no longer distant or unavailable
  (Bowen: "The gods and goddesses are within reach here")
- **"front-and-center"** - in the most prominent position, where nobody can miss you
  (Bowen: "Zeus might still be the front-and-center king")
- **"got down to business"** - stopped with the ceremony and did the actual work
  (Bowen: "it's the flanking gods who got down to business")
- **"part of the deal"** - one of the conditions you accept along with everything else
  (Kondoleon: "So this was part of the deal.")

**Background Note 1:** the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston reopened five rebuilt galleries of ancient
Greek, Roman, and Byzantine art in late 2021, after years of planning. The segment was filmed in
those galleries.

**Background Note 2:** Epidaurus was a healing sanctuary in ancient Greece where sick people travelled
to ask for a cure. Asclepius, whom Kondoleon names, was the god of medicine, and Hygeia was the
goddess of health.

**Background Note 3:** the museum's Athena Parthenos is a small Roman copy of a colossal statue of
Athena that once stood in the Parthenon in Athens and has been lost for centuries.

**Phase 2: First Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- **No wording on the page or the screen.** Captions off and the screen's own subtitles turned off, no transcript on the page or the board, and the Phase 5 windows not yet visible. This is the students' one

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach "listen for sequence markers" directly, modeling on real content from **[Segment 3]**.
  Replay Bowen's line: "She descended from the sky into the museum 10 years ago, after standing
  anonymously in a suburban Boston estate for more than a century." Put the three moments on the
  board in the order they happened rather than the order they are said, and name the markers that
  make that possible: "10 years ago," "after," "for more than a century."
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt, from **[Segment 6]**. Play Marest's "Originally, it was
  very brightly painted" and then Bowen's "To determine how the statue originally looked, the museum
  employed a host of methods from old-school scrutiny," followed by "To special lighting,
  photographic techniques and chemical analysis." Students write what "originally" is being contrasted
  with, and what the "from... to..." pattern tells them about the order of the methods.
- Name the two kinds this lesson is after: markers that order a route through a place, and markers
  that order events in time. A tour uses both, often in the same sentence.

**Phase 4: Second Watch & Differentiated Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

This phase runs in three beats inside its own twenty minutes.

1. **Commit (about 10 min).** Students answer their chosen Level's task below in writing, from the first

2. **Second watch (about 5 min).** Play the source a second time, still with no wording on the page, under

3. **Revise (about 5 min).** Students change whatever they now hear differently, and mark which of their
   answers moved. What moved is what Phase 5 checks.

Each student picks one task Level and completes it.

- **Level 4:**
  1. In 2-3 sentences, describe what a visitor sees in the gallery of the gods and how the room is
     set up, choosing your own connectors.
  2. Write down one comparison a speaker makes, and the reason given for it.

  3. Add one row to the class tour-route chart, with the words that told you where you are.
  4. The museum decided to put a screen beside the statue showing what it looked like in color.

     - **Answer note:** either choice is correct. Look for a reason that names something from the
       segment (the traces of pigment you can still see, the shock of the color, what the ancients
       actually saw) rather than a general preference.

- **Level 5:**
  1. Summarize the route this tour takes, in order, in 3-4 sentences.
     - **Answer note:** it opens in the gallery of the gods with Kondoleon on idealized bodies, turns

       where she came from, follows the galleries out to everyday objects and one small inscribed

  2. Identify one evaluative word choice by which a speaker signals an opinion of the galleries

     - **Answer note:** Bowen's "a moment to experience the divine," "within reach," "a formidable

       out loud as an opinion.
  3. Note the route flow on the class tour-route chart.

- **Level 6:**
  1. List two stated facts and two clearly-signaled evaluative comments from the segment, keeping the
     two lists separate.
     - **Answer note:** facts: the Juno is 13 feet tall and 13,000 pounds and is the largest
       classical sculpture in America; she came to the museum 10 years ago after more than a century
       in a suburban Boston estate; the Athena is a Roman replica of a statue at the Acropolis;
       Egyptian blue was found on the aegis and the helmet. Evaluative comments: "What really touches
       me, it's the human connection"; "It's almost garish, sometimes, we think today"; "We really
       need her in the world today"; "a digital revelation all its own."
  2. Locate one clear tonal shift in the segment and describe what changes in the language at that
     point.
     - **Answer note:** the shift out of Kondoleon's warm present-tense enthusiasm about wine, parties
       and odes into the plain technical register of firing experiments, multispectral light and
       chemical analysis; or the shift from the reverent opening, "a moment to experience the divine,"
       to the joke about Technicolor that closes the piece.
  3. Take one of the evaluative comments you listed and rewrite it as a plain stated fact. Then write
     what is lost in the rewrite.
     - **Answer note:** "It's almost garish, sometimes, we think today" becomes something like "the
       original paint was brighter than modern taste expects." The fact survives; what goes is the
       curator's own recoil, and with it the signal that she is telling you how to feel about the
       color rather than only what color it was.

- **Level 7 (uses both sources):** both accounts' own words are printed below, paired subject by

    - Segment **[Segment 6]**: Marest, "Originally, it was very brightly painted. It's almost garish,

  - *What the galleries are for.*
    - Segment **[Segment 4]**: Marest, "What really touches me, it's the human connection. It's this

    - Review: "The MFA is quite explicit about its intention to refute the propaganda from white

  1. Working only from the words printed above, write what impression of these galleries each account
     leaves, and name the choices in their wording that build it.
  2. Name one interest or assumption behind one account's framing that neither states outright, and
     point to the words that led you to it.
     - **Answer note:** the two agree closely on the room and on the color, and the review is if

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Panel Round (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing sequencing language: the words that tell a listener what
  came first, what came next, and what the thing is now." This is the exact skill today's Speaking
  Closing Transfer Check will ask students to reproduce.
- Model directly from the real source. Bowen, **[Segment 3]**: "She descended from the sky into the
  museum 10 years ago, after standing anonymously in a suburban Boston estate for more than a
  century." Point out that the sentence runs backwards through time and the markers are what keep it
  followable.
- Second model, Marest and Bowen, **[Segment 6]**: "Originally, it was very brightly painted." and
  "To determine how the statue originally looked, the museum employed a host of methods from
  old-school scrutiny," then "To special lighting, photographic techniques and chemical analysis."
  One marker sets a "then" against a "now"; the "from... to..." puts the methods in order.
- Teach the frames: "Originally, ___." / "___ years later, ___." / "After ___, it ___." / "From ___
  to ___, ___." / "It ended up ___."

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level. Every Level works on the same thing: an object the student already
owns and knows well, given the treatment the museum gives its objects. Choose it from memory; nothing
has to be brought in or held up.

- **Level 4:** Give your object a spoken wall label: say, in order, where it came from, what it is
  made of, and where it lives now, choosing your own connectors. Include one comparison ("It's like
  ___") with a brief reason for it, and use one frame from Phase 1.
- **Level 5:** Give an extended wall label for your object in a clear order, using at least two
  frames from Phase 1, and include one evaluative word choice that lets a listener hear your opinion
  of the object without your stating it.
- **Level 6:** Describe your object fluently, moving between what it is and why it matters, and
  explain explicitly why one part of its history matters. Watch your partner, and when they react,
  adjust your register or your approach once in response.
- **Level 7:** Give your object two wall labels for the same partner: one as the museum that owns it
  would write it, one as a critic who doubts it is worth showing. Every fact stays true in both; only
  the selection and the emphasis change. Somewhere in the second label, catch one imprecise word of
  your own and repair it aloud as you go.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Teach content-word stress: English speakers lean on the words that carry the meaning (nouns, main
  verbs, adjectives, and the order markers themselves) and hurry through the small grammar words
  between them.
- Model from the real audio, **[Segment 6]**: replay Marest's "Originally, it was very brightly
  painted. It's almost garish, sometimes, we think today." Play it twice and have students underline,
  on the printed line, the words she leans on.
- Students mark a second printed line the same way, **[Segment 3]**: "She descended from the sky into
  the museum 10 years ago, after standing anonymously in a suburban Boston estate for more than a
  century." Then check both markings against the real audio.
- Apply it: in pairs, students say one sentence of their own Phase 2 wall label twice, once with every
  word given equal weight and once leaning on the content words and the order markers, and tell each
  other which one was easier to follow.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Panel Round (20 min)**

- Students form small groups of four or five from where they sit. In each round, two students at a
  time answer the prompt as a panel while the others in the group listen with a task; then the panel
  seats change within the group for the next prompt. No formation, no movement beyond turning a chair.
- **Three rotated prompts,** roughly six minutes each:
  1. Should a museum show visitors what an ancient statue originally looked like in color, or leave
     the white marble to speak for itself?
  2. The 13-foot statue stood in a private garden outside Boston for more than a century before the
     museum got her, and the rest of her group is still in Italy. Does it matter where an object like
     that ends up?
  3. If you were designing the route through these galleries, what would you put first, and what
     would you save for last?
- **Active task for every non-speaker:** keep a running list in the packet of the order markers each
  panelist actually uses, with the name of who used it. Anyone may be asked to read one back, so the
  list has to be kept as the round goes.
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 4 students pair up and rehearse one full contribution with
  a partner, out loud, before the first panel begins, so their first turn is a line they have already
  said once.
- **Foundation Support:** for a student working below Level 4, the role is the order-marker list
