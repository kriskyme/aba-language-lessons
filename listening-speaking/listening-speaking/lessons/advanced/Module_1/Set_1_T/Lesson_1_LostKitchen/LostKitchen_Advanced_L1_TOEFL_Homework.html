The Lost Kitchen: TOEFL Homework Recording Script

Listening & Speaking Advanced 1.1T.1.4

Teacher use only: this is the recording script and scoring guide for Task D (TOEFL)'s take-home Speaking practice. Record (or otherwise produce) real audio of each item below, matching its response-time window, and post it to the class's Teams. Students listen to each item once and record their own spoken response in Teams, submitted by an assigned date. Score each submission using the guide below it.

Teacher use only — recording script and scoring guide. Do not print or share this page with students.

Listen and Repeat

Scenario

You are training a new server at a small countryside restaurant that only takes reservations by request. Listen to your manager and repeat what she says. Repeat only once.

  • 1. "The old grist mill now houses our kitchen."8 sec
  • 2. "Every table needs a reservation."8 sec
  • 3. "We trust the cook's intuition more than a printed recipe."10 sec
  • 4. "Guests travel a long way for a seasonal dinner here."10 sec
  • 5. "The chef calls this small restaurant a real phenomenon."10 sec
  • 6. "Tell visitors the restaurant's reinvention began with a small traveling kitchen."12 sec
  • 7. "If a guest asks, explain that reservation names are drawn at random each year."12 sec

Score each response 0-5 (Intelligibility and Accuracy): 5 = an exact, fully intelligible repetition; 4 = captures the full meaning with only minor word/grammar changes; 3 = mostly complete but not fully accurate, occasional intelligibility trouble; 2 = a significant part missing or wrong, hard to understand without the prompt; 1 = only a few recognizable words remain; 0 = no response, unintelligible, or unconnected to the prompt.

Take an Interview

Scenario

You are taking part in a short online interview about food and community for a local magazine.

  • 1. Can you describe a meal you've cooked using only ingredients you already had at home, without following a specific recipe?45 sec
  • 2. Do you prefer eating at a small, lesser-known restaurant or a well-known one? Why?45 sec
  • 3. Some people think a small local business should stay small to protect what makes it special. Others think it should grow to reach more people. What do you think?45 sec
  • 4. Imagine a small restaurant you love suddenly became famous. What do you think would change about it, and would that change be good or bad?45 sec

Score each response 0-5 (Relevance, Elaboration, Delivery, Language Use): 5 = fully addresses the question with clear elaboration, natural pace, intelligible delivery, accurate and varied grammar/vocabulary; 4 = addresses the question, reasonably clear, adequate grammar/ vocabulary; 3 = generally on topic but limited elaboration, choppy pace, limited grammar/vocabulary range; 2 = minimally connected, little elaboration, limited intelligibility; 1 = only vaguely connected, mostly unintelligible; 0 = no response, unintelligible, or unconnected to the prompt.
