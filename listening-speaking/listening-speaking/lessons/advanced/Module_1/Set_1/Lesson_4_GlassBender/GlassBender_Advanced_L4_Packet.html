An old-fashioned jukebox restorer has worked in her trade for over fifteen years. You would think the hardest part is polishing the wooden case, but actually, it's rebuilding the wiring inside. She replaces the transformer that has weakened with age, and reconnects every wire into one working circuit. Before that, she strips away a cracked coating of old varnish, careful not to let the cleaning solvent vaporize too quickly in the summer heat. Any leftover impurity in a single solder joint can stop the whole machine from lighting up again.

Unit 4B: The Last Glass Bender

Objective: compare two things clearly, saying how they're alike or different and which one you prefer, and why.

Learn the Phrase
Making Comparisons

In the video, Wil Kirkman compares neon signs to LEDs: "When I first started, illuminated signs were all neon. And now it's LEDs, and they don't have that warm glow that reaches out to you and sucks you in. I'm not dissing LEDs, they have their place. But neon is my heart."

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll practice making comparisons using frames like these:

  • • "Unlike ___, ___ actually ___."
  • • "___ has its place, but ___ is what I really ___."
  • • "Compared to ___, ___ is more ___ because ___."

Say It Like a Native Speaker

Fluent speakers stress the exact word being contrasted, and say the rest of the sentence more quickly and lightly. Practice this real comparison line, stressing the capitalized words: "I'm not DISSING LEDs, they have their place. But NEON is my HEART."

Discuss It

Form two circles, an inner circle facing out and an outer circle facing in, with one partner across from you. Compare Kirkman's neon craft to a modern technology that could replace it, then rotate to a new partner and try a new comparison.

1.
Compare neon signs to LEDs or digital signage. Which would you prefer, and why?
2.
Compare old, handmade craft skills in general to fast, mass-produced work. Which matters more to you, and why?
3.
Compare Kirkman's slow, careful process to a faster way the same sign could be made today. What would be gained, and what would be lost?
