## PART B: SPEAKING ASSESSMENT

No new source is used here; the prompt below is invented, grounded in Module 1 (Describing)'s own topic range,
not tied to any of the Set's own 4 lessons' sources or to Part A's sources.

### B.1 Mechanism

**Advanced band: a live presentation (solo) is the default and formally scored Speaking assessment for the
Set.** Delivery defaults to solo per Section B.2's own rule, since a Describing-module task doesn't naturally
call for a small-group presentation the way a Socializing-module task might. **A Teams-recording version of the
exact same task is also generated below**, as a standing scored alternative a student can be moved to (an
absence, illness, or any teacher-approved reason) - not homework, and not a separate lighter assessment. Both
versions are scored against the same rubric (B.4).

**Live time budget:** roughly 2-3 minutes per student, so a class of 20-25 students needs approximately 50-75
minutes total - plan this as its own dedicated period, or spread it across two class days, rather than folding
it into a normal 75-minute lesson-length slot the way Part A's Listening assessment is.

### B.2 Topic and emphasized skills

**Topic:** describe a workspace or creative space that matters to you (your own, or one you know well because
someone described it to you), comparing it to a more ordinary version of that kind of space, and giving your
honest opinion of it without stating that opinion outright.

This topic is invented for the assessment and grounds two of the Set's four taught speaking skills at once:
**Making Comparisons** (Lesson 4) and **Hedging an Opinion** (Lesson 1). Each Level's task below states which it
emphasizes most; Level 7 also draws lightly on **Summarizing What Someone Said** (Lesson 2) for a student
describing a space secondhand.

### B.3 Prompts by task Level

- **Level 4 (target length: 45-60 sec Teams-recording / approx. 60-75 sec live).** *Emphasizes: Making
  Comparisons, with a brief stated reason.* Content requirements (concrete, countable): (1) describe the
  workspace/creative space using at least 2 original features, in your own words; (2) include 1 comparison
  between it and a more typical version of that kind of space; (3) give 1 brief, stated reason for the
  comparison, using your own connector (not a memorized frame). Present live on [teacher-set date], or submit a
  recording by [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress, teacher-approved.
- **Level 5 (target length: 60-90 sec Teams-recording / approx. 90 sec live).** *Emphasizes: Making Comparisons
  and Hedging an Opinion equally.* Content requirements: (1) organize an extended description around at least 3
  details (what it's for, what's inside, who uses it); (2) include at least 1 comparison to a more typical
  version of that kind of space; (3) use at least 1 clearly evaluative word choice that signals your opinion of
  the space without stating that opinion directly. Present live on [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by
  [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress.
- **Level 6 (target length: 90-120 sec Teams-recording / approx. 2 min live).** *Emphasizes: Hedging an Opinion,
  with Making Comparisons as support.* Content requirements: (1) describe at least 3 features of the space and
  explicitly explain why at least 2 of them matter, rather than just listing them; (2) imagine a listener reacts
  with mild skepticism partway through, and describe how you would adjust your register or approach in response;
  (3) use at least 2 different hedging phrases ("seems to," "tends to," "in a way") rather than flat statements
  of opinion. Present live on [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by [teacher-set date] using Teams
  Speaking Progress.
- **Level 7 (target length: 120-150 sec Teams-recording / approx. 2-3 min live).** *Emphasizes: Making
  Comparisons and Hedging an Opinion, with Summarizing What Someone Said woven in if applicable.* Content
  requirements: (1) deliberately select and emphasize at least 3 details to shape your listener's specific
  impression of the space (e.g. its originality, or its impact on the people who use it); (2) include at least 1
  moment where you catch yourself using an imprecise word and repair it mid-delivery; (3) use at least 1 piece of
  idiomatic vocabulary naturally in your description; (4) if you're describing a space you haven't personally
  visited, include a moment summarizing, in your own words, what someone else told you about it. Present live on
  [teacher-set date], or submit a recording by [teacher-set date] using Teams Speaking Progress.

### B.4 Scoring rubric

3-point scale per criterion: Not yet / Developing / Meets. Same rubric for both the live and Teams-recording
versions of a Level's task.
