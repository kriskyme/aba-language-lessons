Unit 1A: The Lost Kitchen

Listening & Speaking Advanced 1.1.1.0

Objective: tell a stated fact apart from an opinion or feeling someone signals without saying it outright, and compare what a place became to what it was originally planned to be.

Good to Know

The James Beard Foundation is a well-known U.S. organization that recognizes chefs and restaurants. Being noticed by "James Beard...taste-makers" is a real marker of culinary reputation.

A grist mill is a historic type of building, once common in small New England towns, originally built to grind local farmers' grain into flour, which is why a restored one now houses a restaurant built around local ingredients.

Unit 1B: The Lost Kitchen

Objective: hedge an opinion, showing you're not completely certain, instead of stating something as flat fact.

Learn the Phrase
Hedging an Opinion

In the video, Erin French is asked about growing her restaurant. She doesn't give a flat answer - she qualifies it, admits uncertainty, and asks her own question back:

Her real words: "Yes. And I continue to struggle with that growth of, where is that fine line? Where does it get to be too much?"

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll practice hedging using frames like these:

  • • "I think..., but I'm not totally sure."
  • • "It seems like..., though I could be wrong."
  • • "In a way, ___, but then again, ___."

Say It Like a Native Speaker

Contrast makes a sentence land. Practice this real line from the video: "I thought this would be a quiet little cafe... but it turned into a culinary phenomenon." Stress "quiet little cafe" and "culinary phenomenon" harder than the rest of the sentence, since the contrast between them is the whole point.
