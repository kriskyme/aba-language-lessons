## Task Levels for this plan (Intermediate band)

Intermediate's task Levels are **2, 3, 4, 5** (Conventions §B: extension-down 2; native 3, 4; extension-up
5) - 4 differentiated task Levels per lesson. The real source is calibrated to **Level 3**, Intermediate's
lower native Level: 2-4 minute runtime, near-natural but clear pace, a simple past/present backbone with
light subordination (Lesson prompt §0.2). Because this Module is Narrating, the source should be a
first-person or closely-reported account of events that actually happened, not a description or an
explainer (Lesson prompt §0.1).

Quoted verbatim from `learningobjectives.csv` (Module 2: Narrating, Listening/Speaking modality), split
into the Listening half (governs Day 1 tasks) and the Speaking half (governs Day 2 tasks):

| Level | Listening (Day 1 basis) | Speaking (Day 2 basis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 (extension-down) | "Can listen to one short spoken sentence narrating a single past action using a fixed frame ("I \_\_\_-ed.") supported by visuals, and identify the matching picture." | "Can produce one sentence narrating a single past action using the memorized frame with one open slot, prompted by the teacher." |
| 3 (native, anchor level) | "Can listen to one short spoken sentence narrating two sequential past actions using a two-slot frame and identify the matching picture pair, with a model available for reference." | "Can narrate two sequential past actions using the memorized frame with two open slots, with a model available rather than being prompted step-by-step." |
| 4 (native) | "Can listen to a short spoken narration of a sequence of past events, choosing their own time connectors, and identify the order, one outcome, and the brief reason given for that outcome." | "Can narrate a short original sequence of past events including one outcome and a brief reason for it, choosing their own connectors, working independently." |
| 5 (extension-up) | "Can listen to an extended spoken narration of a personal event, summarize the sequence and why it mattered, and identify one clearly telling action or detail that signals how the speaker felt, even though the speaker never names the feeling directly." | "Can narrate an extended account of a personal event, stating why it mattered and including at least one clearly telling action or detail that signals a feeling without naming it outright, working independently." |

**Note for Step 2 (lesson generation).** Levels 2-3's fixed frames ("I \_\_\_-ed.", the two-slot version)
are a real mismatch with authentic speech, which never narrates in memorized frames. As in Module 1, the
lowest task Levels *extract* into the frame from what the real speaker actually said ("Complete: First I
\_\_\_-ed, then I \_\_\_-ed," using the clip's own past-tense verbs), rather than expecting the source
itself to speak in frames. Level 2's own rotation is further constrained by §0.6 - see the slot-to-shape
table below.

## Topic grounding

Every topic direction below names a real, findable category of first-person account (an arrival story, an
eyewitness rescue, how someone started something, a remembered day) rather than a generic invented scene.
These are search directions, not confirmed sources: no title, channel, or speaker is named here. All four
are distinct from Module 1's eight topic directions for this Band (bakery, market, gear, family elder,
animal habitat, craft workshop, museum artifact, natural landmark) and from Passage Reading's Module 1
Intermediate topics, since a student may be in both tracks at once. Passage Reading has no Module 2
content for any Band yet, so no cross-track check against it is possible.

**Standing content bound for this Set (added 2026-09-10, per user direction).** No refugee,
migration-status, or displacement content in any of these four sources. An arrival story here is an
everyday one: a first day at a new school, a new job, or in a new city. This narrowed Lesson 1's topic
direction above from "a new country or city" to the everyday form, and it applies to Lessons 2-4's
sourcing too.

**Note-taking organizer for the whole Set:** the **sequence chain** (Lesson prompt §0.4), the structure
matched to Narrating. It does not rotate within the Set; the Module governs it.

### Level 2 slot-to-shape assignment (Lesson prompt §0.6, Quality Standards §D10)

Level 2 is a fixed-output Level, so the plan names which §0.6 shape fills each slot per lesson, keeping the
same shape out of the same slot in consecutive lessons. The frame is printed once per day, in the first
task that uses it; later tasks say "the frame."

| Lesson | Day 1 Phase 2 (listen-for purpose) | Day 1 Phase 4 | Day 2 Phase 2 | Day 2 Phase 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Choose what was heard | **Point or name** (the day's production) + Fix the wrong word | Better of two | **A partner's object** (the day's production) |
| 2 | Fix the wrong word | **Point or name** + Better of two | Say from sound | **A partner's object** |
| 3 | Choose what was heard | **Point or name** + Say from sound | Fix the wrong word | **A partner's object** |
| 4 | Fix the wrong word | **Point or name** + Better of two | Choose what was heard | **A partner's object** |

Day 1 Phase 5 and Day 2 Phase 5 use the `Transfer` shape in every lesson: the form once, on that lesson's
fresh prompt. "A partner's object" at Level 2 means a partner's own past action ("What did you do
yesterday?"), the Narrating equivalent of the bank's Describing wording.

**Phase 4 protocol note.** Town Hall, Jigsaw, Panel Round, and Rotating Partners are all room-neutral
(Quality Standards §D11) and all require, at generation time, 2-3 rotated prompts and an explicit active
task for every student not currently speaking (§D7). Panel Round and Rotating Partners in particular are
valid at a realistic class size (8-12) only on those terms - apply this from the start rather than as a
later patch.

  lesson, and no non-production shape sits in the same slot in consecutive lessons (see the table above).
- **Lesson numbering:** Lessons 1-4, restarting for this Module per Conventions §C; version codes
  `2.1.1.0`-`2.1.4.0`.

### Flagged deviations (not silently resolved)

1. **The two production shapes repeat their slots.** `Point or name` (Day 1 Phase 4) and `A partner's
   object` (Day 2 Phase 4) sit in the same slot in all four lessons. §0.6's bank offers exactly one
   production shape per day while requiring one production per day, so the "no shape in the same slot in
   consecutive lessons" rule is unsatisfiable for those two. It is applied in full to the non-production
   shapes, which rotate every lesson. Worth revisiting if the §0.6 bank ever grows a second production
   shape per day.
2. **Lesson 4 mildly echoes Module 1 Set 1 Lesson 4** (a family elder or mentor). It is not the immediately
   preceding Set, so Conventions §F asks for a flag rather than a block. The two differ in what the lesson
   does with the person: Module 1's described a person's traits; this one narrates one remembered event and
   what changed because of it, and its strategy, skill, hook, protocol, and vocabulary theme are all
   different. At generation time, prefer a source about a day or an event, not a portrait of a person.
