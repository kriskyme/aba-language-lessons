Unit 2A: The Firefighter and the Girl in the Bathtub

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.2.0

Objective: I can use what a speaker has already told me to say what is coming next, and then listen to find out whether I was right.

Good to Know

StoryCorps records everyday people talking about their lives. The recordings are kept at the Library of Congress in Washington, D.C.

Los Angeles County, California, has one of the largest fire departments in the United States. Derek Bart worked there when the 1993 fire happened.

Unit 2B: Asking What You Want to Know

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.2.0

Objective: I can ask a question that gets a speaker to explain or repeat the part I did not follow.

Learn the Phrase
Asking for Clarification

In the story, Myeshia finally asks the question she has held for thirty years: "I was just curious to know, like, why me?"

The firefighter asks permission before he explains himself to a stranger: "I say, if I get too personal, please stop me."

Use these when it is your turn: I was just curious to know, why ___? · Sorry, could you say that part again? · When you said ___, did you mean ___? · Can I ask what happened after ___?

Say It Like a Native Speaker
Up at the End, or Down

A speaker's voice usually rises at the end of a question and falls at the end of a statement. Listen for it in these two lines from the story.

  1. 1.
    Listen twice, and circle up or down for each line.
    "I was just curious to know, like, why me?" (up / down)
    "In the fire service, you see a lot of tragedy." (up / down)
  2. 2.
    Say one question from Learn the Phrase to your partner with your voice rising at the end. Then swap.

Discuss It

Get into a group of three or four. Your group starts with one question below, then passes it on and takes a new one, until your group has worked all three. While someone else is talking, ask them one question from Learn the Phrase and write down the answer on the lines.

1.
The firefighter says he always wants to know whether the people he helped are OK. Tell your group about somebody you would like to hear from again, and what you would ask them.
2.

3.

4.
Write the answer you got each round.

Wrap It Up

With a partner, think of instructions somebody gave you recently that you did not fully follow, at work, on the phone, in an app, or on a form. Say the instruction as you heard it, then ask your partner one question about it. Then swap.
