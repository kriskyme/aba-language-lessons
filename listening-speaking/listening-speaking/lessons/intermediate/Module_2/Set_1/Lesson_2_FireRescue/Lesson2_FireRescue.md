**Teacher note on content (not student-facing).** The clip describes a 1993 house fire: a firefighter finds
an 8-year-old girl hiding in a bathtub with burns on her face and hands and carries her out. He also says
that if anyone had been in the first room, they would not have survived. The host mentions that the
firefighter received a cancer diagnosis in 2020 that later turned out to be a misdiagnosis. Nobody in the
story dies, and the second half is the two of them meeting again as adults. Preview it before class and
decide whether any student needs a heads-up.

**Listening Skill Spotlight (2 min)** (Quality Standards §D1). Say, in these words, to the whole class:

comes next, and then listening to find out whether we were right."

### Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)

**Teach (6 min).** Predicting from context means using what the speaker has already said to guess what is
coming, then checking. Model with these real lines, read aloud from the transcript:

- Segment 2: "Kids like to hide in the closet. They hide under the beds. They hide in the bathtub."
  Ask: what is he about to tell us he did?
- Segment 2: "But for years, I always wondered how Myeshia was."
  Ask: what does *for years* tell us about how much time the next part of the story covers?

Write the two clue phrases on the board and mark what each one lets a listener expect.

**Guided practice (4 min).** Read this second real line aloud twice: "I considered going to the hospital,
but it was too emotional." Ask students to say what they now expect the next sentence to be about, and what
in the line told them.

### Phase 4: Second Listen & Differentiated Task, Choose One Level (20 min)

Three beats, in this order.

**Beat 1 - Commit (about 10 min).** Students write their predictions and answer their own Level's task from
the first listen and their Phase 2 organizer. Nothing is replayed yet and no wording is on the page.

**Beat 2 - Second listen (about 5 min), still audio only.** Play the whole clip, start to finish, including
the part held back in Phase 2. Each Level listens under a narrower purpose than it had in Phase 2, stated
before the audio starts:

- ★ (Level 2): count how many hiding places he names before he finds her. **Answer note:** three (the
  closet, under the beds, the bathtub).
- ★★ (Level 3): listen for how many separate actions he does between seeing her and reaching the stairs.
  **Answer note:** three (ran, grabbed her, put her over his shoulders).
- ★★★ (Level 4): listen for the one detail in the store that told him who she was. **Answer note:** the
  name tag reading Myeshia.
- ★★★★ (Level 5): listen for what Myeshia says she was, in her own word, when he remembered her name.
  **Answer note:** "shocked."

**Beat 3 - Revise (about 5 min).** Students change what they now hear differently and tick the answers that
moved. What moved is what Phase 5 checks.

1. Number these four events 1 to 4 in the order they happened.

   \_\_\_ The two of them sat down to record a conversation.

2. He decided not to do one thing on the night of the fire. Write what it was and the reason he gives, in

   **Answer note:** he considered going to the hospital but did not go, because it was too emotional.
3. Write the prediction you made before the end, then write in one sentence what actually happened.

4. What does Myeshia want to know when they finally talk, and what answer does he give? Answer in two

   **Answer note:** she asks why he remembered her out of so many people; he says that in the fire service

#### ★★★★ Task D (Level 5)

1. Summarize what happened, in order, in three or four sentences. Finish with one sentence saying why that
   1993 night still matters to both of them.
   **Answer note:** the storm, the candles, the fire, the search of the second bedroom, the bathtub, the
   carry downstairs, the years of wondering, the store, the reunion; it matters because each one carried
   the other through a hard time.
2. Find one action or detail the firefighter reports that shows how he felt that night, in a place where he
   never names the feeling. Write the words from the story, then write what they show.
   **Answer note:** any of "I considered going to the hospital, but it was too emotional. So I didn't go"

   starts crying and I'm crying" (what the meeting meant). One is enough.
3. Myeshia never says outright that the meeting changed anything for her. Write one sentence saying what
   her own words show about it, and quote the words you are using.
   **Answer note:** "It amazes me just to know that the memory from 30 years ago is still with you, and
   that means a lot," or "I cherish this": being remembered by name mattered more than the rescue itself.

### Phase 5: Check It Against the Source (7 min)

§D12's Verify and Return beats. Run this only after every Level has committed and revised its answers in
Phase 4.

**One verification window** (Intermediate takes one, not two). It is the stretch Task A item 2 and Task B
item 2 both turn on, and it carries three word boundaries this Band predictably mishears in fast speech:
*ran there*, *grab her*, and *over my shoulders*.

> **Segment 2: The night of the fire** (NPR publishes no timestamps for this segment; the teacher marks the
> elapsed time on the first playthrough)
>
> "So I looked across the hallway, and in a bathtub, I see this young girl. Her name's Myeshia and I notice
> that her face and hands are burned. So I ran there, I grab her and throw her over my shoulders, go
> downstairs."

Students read the window against what they actually wrote in Phase 4 and mark, in one line, the one place

the three boundaries they just saw, with nothing to read.

**Answer note:** the usual divergences are hearing "grab her" as one word, losing "there" after "ran," and
hearing "over my shoulders" as "over his shoulder."

### Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**The skill, in these words:** "Today we are practicing asking for clarification: asking a question that
gets the speaker to explain or repeat the part you did not follow."

**Grounded in the real audio.** Two places in this source model it:

- Segment 4, Myeshia asking the question she has waited thirty years to ask: "I was just curious to know,
  like, why me?"
- Segment 3, the firefighter checking before he explains himself to a stranger: "I say, if I get too
  personal, please stop me."

Play both moments. Point out that one asks the other person to say more, and one asks permission before
saying more, and that both keep the conversation going instead of ending it.

**Frames to teach (these go in the packet's Learn the Phrase box):**

- I was just curious to know, why \_\_\_?
- Sorry, could you say that part again?
- When you said \_\_\_, did you mean \_\_\_?
- Can I ask what happened after \_\_\_?

### Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)

Everyone works from the same prompt: **a time somebody helped you, or you helped somebody.**

#### ★ Task A (Level 2)

*Say from sound.* Your partner says one word from the list out loud. You say it back, then write it. Check
the list together after all four.
List: checked, shopped, called, helped.
**Answer note:** the point is the past-tense ending, not spelling speed; accept any correct rewrite.

Then say one sentence about the story using the frame, out loud, to your partner.

#### ★★ Task B (Level 3)

The two-slot frame is on the page as your model: **First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed.**
Tell your partner two things that happened, one after the other, on a day somebody helped you. Say the
frame twice, about two different days.

#### ★★★ Task C (Level 4)

Tell your partner about a time somebody helped you: three or four things that happened, in order, using
your own time connectors, and finish with what came of it and why. Your partner asks you one clarification
question from Learn the Phrase, and you answer it.

#### ★★★★ Task D (Level 5)

Tell your partner a fuller account of a time somebody helped you: what happened in order, why it mattered,
and one action or detail that shows how you felt without naming the feeling. Your partner asks two
clarification questions, and you answer both.

### Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)

**Feature: rising and falling intonation for questions.** Audible in Segment 4, where Myeshia's voice rises
at the end of her question: "I was just curious to know, like, why me?"

1. Play the question twice. Students mark on the printed line whether the voice goes up or down at the end.
   **Answer note:** up, on *me*.
2. Play one statement from Segment 4 for contrast: "In the fire service, you see a lot of tragedy."
   Students mark up or down.
   **Answer note:** down.
3. Students say one clarification question from Learn the Phrase to a partner with the voice rising at the
   end, then swap.

### Phase 4: Structured Oral Output (20 min)

**Jigsaw, run from where students sit** (Quality Standards §D11). Groups of three or four. Each group takes
one question first, then passes its question to the next group and takes a new one, so every group works
all three over the window.

1. The firefighter says he always wants to know whether the people he helped are OK. Tell your group about
   somebody you would like to hear from again, and what you would ask them.
2. Myeshia waited a long time to ask her question. Tell your group about a question you once wanted to ask
   somebody, and say what stopped you or what happened when you asked.
3. He asked permission before telling her who he was. Tell your group about a time you had to say something
   difficult to somebody, and how you started.

**Active task for every student not speaking** (§D7): ask the speaker one clarification question from
Learn the Phrase, and write down the answer you get in one line.

**Per-Level stems:**

- ★: "I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★: "First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★★: "At first \_\_\_, and after that \_\_\_, because \_\_\_."
- ★★★★: "What I would ask is \_\_\_, and the reason I never did is \_\_\_."

**Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): ★ students rehearse their one line with a partner
immediately before the first round, then say it in their turn, and their standing job in each group is to
ask the printed clarification question. **Foundation Support:** a student below the band's floor circles
the picture in their packet that matches a classmate's turn, says the single verb, and reads back one
written answer line at the end of each round.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2).** As each round ends, one clarification question and the answer it got go
on the board. By the end the board holds the class's own set of questions that worked, and Phase 5 uses it.

### Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)

**Fresh prompt, not the source's topic and not Day 2's practice topic** (Quality Standards §D3): a set of
instructions somebody gave you recently that you did not fully follow - at work, on the phone, in an app,
or on a form.

In pairs, every student says the instruction as they heard it, then asks their partner one clarification
question about it, out loud. Cold-call two or three pairs. ★ students use "Sorry, could you say that part
again?" with the words they remember.

**Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** ★ had genuine interpretive work on Day 1 (Task A item 4) and a
genuine spoken role on Day 2 (Phase 2's partner round, a turn in Phase 4, and this check), not
fact-retrieval and silence.

**Wrap (3 min).** Look at the board's list of clarification questions. Ask two students to say which
question got the fullest answer, and why.
