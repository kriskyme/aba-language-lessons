- ★★★ (Level 4) - Listen for what was different for Darrell after that day at lunch, and for the reason he

- ★★★★ (Level 5) - Listen for one thing Darrell says or does that shows how he felt, in a moment where he

**Board-dependent moment (Day 1).** After the listen, the sequence chain is built on the board from what

### Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)

**Teach (6 min).** Sequence markers are the words that tell a listener when something happened. Model with
these real lines, read aloud from the transcript:

- Segment 2: "And then I heard a voice."
- Segment 3: "And after Reuben and his friends left, Marcus looked at me and said, come on."
- Segment 3: "At the end of the school year, my dad got orders for the Presidio in San Francisco."

Underline *and then*, *after*, *at the end of the school year* on the board and ask what each one tells us
about time.

**Guided practice (4 min).** Read this second real line aloud twice: "Back then, there was no internet." Ask
students to say what *back then* points to, and where it sits in the story's order.

### Phase 4: Second Listen & Differentiated Task, Choose One Level (20 min)

Three beats, in this order.

**Beat 1 - Commit (about 10 min).** Students answer their own Level's task from the first listen and their
Phase 2 organizer, in writing. Nothing is replayed yet and no wording is on the page.

**Beat 2 - Second listen (about 5 min), still audio only.** Play the whole clip again. Each Level listens
under a narrower purpose than it had in Phase 2, stated before the audio starts:

- ★ (Level 2): count how many times Marcus speaks in the whole story. **Answer note:** twice ("leave him
  alone" and "come on").
- ★★ (Level 3): listen for where the two of them went together and what they did there. **Answer note:**
  they walked to the cafeteria and had lunch together.
- ★★★ (Level 4): listen for what Darrell started doing at his next school. **Answer note:** he started
  noticing the loners, getting to know them, and having lunch with them.
- ★★★★ (Level 5): listen for the last thing he says to Marcus. **Answer note:** "I don't think I ever
  thanked you for that. So thank you."

**Beat 3 - Revise (about 5 min).** Students change what they now hear differently and tick the answers that
moved. What moved is what Phase 5 checks.

#### ★★ Task B (Level 3)

The frame, with the model printed once: **First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed.**
Model, from the story: *First I moved. Then I started at a new school.*

1. Listen to the sentence your teacher reads. Circle the picture pair it matches.
   Teacher reads once: "First I walked to the cafeteria. Then I ate lunch."
   Pairs printed: A. moving truck, then hallway / B. hallway, then cafeteria / C. cafeteria, then moving

   **Answer note:** B.
2. Two things happened in the hallway. Number them 1 and 2 in the order you heard them.
   \_\_\_ Reuben let go of Darrell. \_\_\_ A calm voice said, "Leave him alone."
   **Answer note:** 1 the voice, 2 Reuben let go.
3. Circle the words in each line that tell you *when*.
   a. "And after Reuben and his friends left, Marcus looked at me and said, come on."
   b. "At the end of the school year, my dad got orders for the Presidio in San Francisco."
   **Answer note:** a. after; b. at the end of the school year.
4. Complete the frame about the story: First Marcus \_\_\_-ed. Then Marcus \_\_\_-ed.
   **Answer note:** first spoke (or stopped Reuben), then walked to the cafeteria with Darrell (or had
   lunch with him).
5. Darrell says he and Marcus "really didn't talk a lot." Circle what that tells you about Darrell that

   **Answer note:** still shy; "I was still pretty socially awkward."

#### ★★★ Task C (Level 4)

1. Number these four events 1 to 4 in the order they happened.
   \_\_\_ Darrell's father got orders for San Francisco.
   \_\_\_ A group of boys stopped Darrell on his way to the cafeteria.
   \_\_\_ Marcus and Darrell had lunch together.
   \_\_\_ Darrell started noticing the loners at his next school.
   **Answer note:** 2, 1, 3, 4.
2. What happened with Reuben after that day at lunch? Answer in one sentence, using a time connector you
   choose.
   **Answer note:** after that day, Reuben never bothered Darrell again.
3. Darrell explains why students in his situation usually lost their friends. Write the reason he gives, in
   one sentence.
   **Answer note:** there was no internet then, so when a family got new orders and moved, contact was
   usually lost.
4. Retell the end of the school year in two sentences, using two different time connectors of your own.
   **Answer note:** any accurate retelling using connectors such as *at the end of*, *after that*,

#### ★★★★ Task D (Level 5)

1. Summarize what happened, in order, in three or four sentences, and finish with one sentence saying why
   that day still matters to Darrell.
   **Answer note:** the hallway, the voice, Marcus, lunch, the rest of the year, the move; it matters
   because it changed how he treated other people, and he says it changed his life.
2. Find one action or detail Darrell reports that shows how he felt at that moment, in a place where he
   never names the feeling. Write the words from the story, then write what they show.
   **Answer note:** any of "I just kind of braced myself for that" (fear, expecting to be hit); "we really
   didn't talk a lot. We just kind of hung out" (shyness); "I don't think I ever thanked you for that"
   (regret). One is enough.
3. Marcus never explains himself in this story. Write one sentence saying what his actions, not his words,
   tell you about him, and quote the action you are using.
   **Answer note:** he stopped it without raising his voice and then stayed for lunch: quiet, steady, not
   looking for credit.

> marks the elapsed time on the first playthrough)
>

Students read the window against what they actually wrote in Phase 4 and mark, in one line, the one place

### Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**The skill, in these words:** "Today we are practicing summarizing what someone said: putting another
person's words into one short sentence of your own."

**Grounded in the real audio.** Two places in this source model it:

- Segment 1, the host, summarizing Darrell's whole childhood in one sentence: "The family moved every few
  years, and being the new kid often meant he was lonely and a target for bullies."
- Segment 2 and Segment 3, Darrell reporting what Marcus said: "And the voice said, leave him alone," and
  "Marcus looked at me and said, come on."

Play both moments. Point out that the host compresses years into one sentence, while Darrell keeps the
exact words because they were short enough to keep.

**Frames to teach (these go in the packet's Learn the Phrase box):**

- He said, "\_\_\_."
- What she said was that \_\_\_.
- In one sentence: \_\_\_.
- Basically, he told me to \_\_\_.

### Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)

Everyone works from the same prompt: **a time you were new somewhere** (a new class, a new job, a new
neighborhood, a new team).

#### ★ Task A (Level 2)

*Better of two.* For each pair of lines, circle the one that fits the story, and say the word that makes it
fit.

1. "I moved." / "I cooked."
2. "I walked." / "I swam."
3. "I waited." / "I flew."
   **Answer note:** moved, walked, waited; each is a past action the story actually reports.

Then, with a partner, take turns saying one line from the pairs you circled out loud.

#### ★★ Task B (Level 3)

The two-slot frame is on the page as your model: **First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed.**
Tell your partner two things that happened, one after the other, on a day you were somewhere new. Say the
frame twice, about two different days.

#### ★★★ Task C (Level 4)

Tell your partner about a day you were somewhere new: three or four things that happened, in order, using
your own time connectors, and finish with what came of it and why. Your partner writes down the connectors
you used.

#### ★★★★ Task D (Level 5)

Tell your partner a fuller account of a day you were somewhere new: what happened in order, why the day
mattered to you, and one action or detail that shows how you felt without naming the feeling. Your partner
writes down the detail and what they think it showed, then reads it back to you.

### Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)

**Feature: contrastive stress.** Audible in Segment 2, where Darrell describes the voice: "It wasn't loud.
It was calm but firm."

1. Play the line twice. Students mark the two words the speaker pushes hardest on the printed line.
   **Answer note:** *loud* in the first sentence and *calm* in the second carry the contrast.
2. Students say the line to a partner with the same stress, then swap.
3. Students say one sentence of their own Phase 2 language with a contrast in it, stressing the contrasting
   word.

### Phase 4: Structured Oral Output (20 min)

**Town Hall, run from where students sit** (Quality Standards §D11). Three rotated prompts, roughly six
minutes each:

1. Darrell says Marcus "looked at me and said, come on." Tell the group about one time somebody did
   something small for you that you still remember.
2. Tell the group one thing you did the first time you were in a new place.
3. Darrell says that at his next school he started noticing the loners. Tell the group about one time you
   noticed somebody who was on their own, or somebody noticed you.

**Active task for every student not speaking** (§D7): write the speaker's turn as one sentence, using one
of the Phase 1 frames. Two of those sentences go on the board after each prompt.

**Per-Level stems:**

- ★: "I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★: "First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★★: "At first \_\_\_, and after that \_\_\_, because \_\_\_."
- ★★★★: "What I remember most is \_\_\_, and it mattered because \_\_\_."

**Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): ★ students rehearse their one line with a partner
immediately before the round starts, then say it in their turn. Their line is the frame about a partner's
own past action, from prompt 2. **Foundation Support:** a student below the band's floor circles the
picture in their packet that matches a classmate's turn and says the single verb, and keeps the board list
by reading back the two sentences chosen after each prompt.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2).** The board fills with one-sentence summaries as they are spoken, and
Phase 5 works from that list.

### Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)

**Fresh prompt, not the source's topic and not Day 2's practice topic** (Quality Standards §D3): something
a person said to you outside class this week - at home, at work, in a shop, on the phone.

In pairs, every student summarizes that person's words in one sentence, using a frame from Learn the
Phrase. Cold-call two or three pairs. ★ students use "He said, '\_\_\_'" with the words they remember.

**Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** ★ had genuine interpretive work on Day 1 (Task A item 4) and a
genuine spoken turn on Day 2 (Phase 4 and this check), not fact-retrieval and silence.

**Wrap (3 min).** Look back at the sequence chain on the board from Day 1. Ask two students to say what
changed for Darrell, in one sentence each.
