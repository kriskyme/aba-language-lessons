2. Circle the line in each pair that is true about that day, and say the word that makes it true.
   a. "I moved." / "I cooked."
   b. "I walked." / "I swam."
   c. "I waited." / "I flew."
   **Answer note:** no fixed key, since this is now the student's own day. All three of *moved*, *walked* and
   *waited* fit almost any arrival and are the expected choices; the thing to check is the past-tense form,
   not which of the pair was picked. A student who circles *cooked*, *swam* or *flew* because it is genuinely
   true is right.
3. With a partner, take turns. Say where you were, then say the three lines you circled out loud.
   **Answer note:** the production is three frame sentences with the slot filled, prefaced by the place.

**Why this changed at `2.1.1.8`, recorded rather than hidden.** The scaffolding line was added on user
direction: "this one needs more scaffolding it should have a blank for where they can write where they were."
Adding it exposed a second defect that had to be fixed in the same pass, because the two could not both stand.
**Task A was not on the shared prompt.** Practice It's lead-in tells the class "everyone is talking about the
same thing: a time you were somewhere new," and Tasks B, C and D all do; ★ alone was circling lines that fit
**Darrell's** story and saying those aloud. A blank asking where *you* were, bolted onto items about someone
else's day, would have made the task incoherent rather than better scaffolded, so the three pairs now ask what
was true of the student's own day. This is what Quality Standards §B means by a shared core: a Level whose
items contradict the core is not a variation on it.

**What this costs and what it does not.** The old items had a checkable key (*moved, walked, waited* are the
actions the clip reports); the new ones do not, which is the real trade. Level 2 keeps its own CSV row either
way, and arguably sits on it better now: "Can produce one sentence narrating a single past action using the
memorized frame with one open slot" is a statement about the student's own production, not about recalling the
source. Day 1 is where this lesson checks comprehension of the clip; Day 2 is speaking practice, and the key
was doing comprehension work in a speaking phase. The *Better of two* shape the Set plan assigns (Section 0.6)
is unchanged: it is still two printed options with one circled.

### Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)

**Feature: contrastive stress.** Audible in Segment 2, where Darrell describes the voice: "It wasn't loud.
It was calm but firm."

1. Play the line twice. Students mark the two words the speaker pushes hardest on the printed line.
   **Answer note:** *loud* in the first sentence and *calm* in the second carry the contrast.
2. Students say the line to a partner with the same stress, then swap.
3. Students say one sentence of their own Phase 2 language with a contrast in it, stressing the contrasting
   word.

### Phase 4: Structured Oral Output (20 min)

**Town Hall, run from where students sit** (Quality Standards §D11). Three rotated prompts, roughly six
minutes each:

1. Darrell says Marcus "looked at me and said, come on." Tell the group about one time somebody did
   something small for you that you still remember.
2. Tell the group one thing you did the first time you were in a new place.
3. Darrell says that at his next school he started noticing the loners. Tell the group about one time you
   noticed somebody who was on their own, or somebody noticed you.

**Quality Standards §D7 is breached here as of `2.1.1.9`, deliberately, and it is stated rather than left to
be found.** §D7 requires any live discussion protocol to supply "an explicit active task for every student
not currently speaking," and this lesson's was: write the speaker's turn as one sentence using one of the
Phase 1 frames, with two of those sentences going on the board after each prompt. It was removed from the
packet on instruction ("Write one sentence for each speaker in your group"). **What follows:** in a Town Hall
of a dozen students, everyone not speaking now has no task, which is the failure §D7 exists to prevent, and
the per-Level stems below are now used only by the speaker rather than by every listener writing along. The
prompts, the stems, the rotation and the speaking turns are all unchanged. The smallest way back is the one
sentence, printed once under the prompts rather than as a numbered item.

**Per-Level stems:**

- ★: "I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★: "First I \_\_\_-ed. Then I \_\_\_-ed."
- ★★★: "At first \_\_\_, and after that \_\_\_, because \_\_\_."
- ★★★★: "What I remember most is \_\_\_, and it mattered because \_\_\_."

**Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): ★ students rehearse their one line with a partner
immediately before the round starts, then say it in their turn. Their line is the frame about a partner's
own past action, from prompt 2. **Foundation Support:** a student below the band's floor says the single
past-tense verb they heard in a classmate's turn, and keeps the board list by reading back the two sentences
the teacher writes up after each prompt.

**Corrected at `2.1.1.9`:** Foundation Support used to say the student "circles the picture in their packet
that matches a classmate's turn." No picture has been in the packet since `2.1.1.6`, when the two
picture-identification items were deleted, so that instruction pointed at nothing. This was missed in that
pass and is fixed here.

**Board-dependent moment (Day 2).** The teacher writes up two one-sentence summaries after each prompt,
taken from what was actually said, since students no longer write them; the board fills as the round runs, and
Phase 5 works from that list.

### Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)

**Fresh prompt, not the source's topic and not Day 2's practice topic** (Quality Standards §D3): something
a person said to you outside class this week - at home, at work, in a shop, on the phone.

In pairs, every student summarizes that person's words in one sentence, using a frame from Learn the
Phrase. Cold-call two or three pairs. ★ students use "He said, '\_\_\_'" with the words they remember.

**Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** ★ had genuine interpretive work on Day 1 (Task A item 3) and a
genuine spoken turn on Day 2 (Phase 4 and this check), not fact-retrieval and silence.

**Wrap (3 min).** Look back at the sequence chain on the board from Day 1. Ask two students to say what
changed for Darrell, in one sentence each.
