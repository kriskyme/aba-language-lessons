Unit 1A: The New Kid and the Voice in the Hallway

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.1.9

Objective: I can follow a story about the past and use the speaker's own time words to say what happened first, next, and last.

Good to Know

In the United States, a soldier is sent to a new base every few years. The whole family moves, so the children change schools several times while they are growing up.

The Presidio of San Francisco was a U.S. Army post for more than 200 years. It closed as a base in 1994 and is now a public park.

Words to Know

1. braceto get your body or mind ready for something bad
2. ordersofficial instructions telling a soldier where to go next
3. lonera person who is often alone
4. outgoingcomfortable talking to people you do not know well
5. protectivewanting to keep someone safe
6. lose contactto stop being in touch with someone
Phrase Spotlight

hang out (with someone) to spend time with someone without doing anything special. In the story: "We just kind of hung out."

You'll listen to: "A schoolmate saved him from a bully and changed his life," an audio story from NPR in which Darrell Barber tells about the year he was the new kid at school and the day another student stepped in.

Unit 1B: Telling What Someone Said

Listening & Speaking · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.1.9

Objective: I can put what another person said into one short sentence of my own.

Learn the Phrase
Saying What Someone Said

In the story, Darrell keeps the exact words because they were short: "And the voice said, leave him alone," and "Marcus looked at me and said, come on."

The person who introduces the story puts years into one sentence instead: "The family moved every few years, and being the new kid often meant he was lonely."

Use these when it is your turn: He said, "___." · What she said was that ___. · In one sentence: ___. · Basically, he told me to ___.

Say It Like a Native Speaker
Two Words, Pushed Hard

When a speaker puts two things against each other, the voice pushes harder on the word that carries the difference. Darrell does it here: "It wasn't loud. It was calm but firm."

1.
Listen to the line twice and underline the word your teacher pushes hardest in each sentence. Then say both sentences to your partner the same way.
"It wasn't loud. It was calm but firm."
Practice It

Pick one task below. Everyone is talking about the same thing: a time you were somewhere new. When you're done, find someone who worked on a different task and take turns sharing.

Task A ★

I ___-ed.

Think of one time you were somewhere new.

  1. 1.
    Where were you? Write it here.
    Some ideas: a new school · a new class · a new job · a new city · a new team
  2. 2.
    Circle the line in each pair that is true about that day, and say the word that makes it true.
    a. "I moved." / "I cooked."
    b. "I walked." / "I swam."
    c. "I waited." / "I flew."
  3. 3.
    With a partner, take turns. Say where you were, then say the three lines you circled out loud.

Task B ★★

First I ___-ed. Then I ___-ed.

Tell your partner about two days when you were somewhere new, using the sentence above.

  1. 1.
    Say the sentence about one day you were somewhere new: two things that happened, one right after the other.
  2. 2.
    Say the sentence again about a different day, with two different actions.

Task C ★★★

Work with a partner, taking turns.

  1. 1.
    Tell your partner about a day you were somewhere new: three or four things that happened, in order, using your own words to show when each one happened. Finish with what came of it and why.

Task D ★★★★

Work with a partner, taking turns.

  1. 1.
    Tell your partner a fuller account of a day you were somewhere new: what happened in order, why the day mattered to you, and one action or detail that shows how you felt without naming the feeling.
Discuss It

Get into a small group. Your group will get one question at a time. Take turns talking, using a starter phrase from the list. While someone else is talking, write their turn as one sentence of your own on the lines under the questions.

1.
Darrell says Marcus "looked at me and said, come on." Tell your group about one time somebody did something small for you that you still remember.
2.
Tell your group one thing you did the first time you were in a new place.
3.
Darrell says that at his next school he started noticing the loners. Tell your group about one time you noticed somebody who was on their own, or somebody noticed you.

Starter phrases:

  • ★ "I ___-ed."
  • ★★ "First I ___-ed. Then I ___-ed."
  • ★★★ "At first ___, and after that ___, because ___."
  • ★★★★ "What I remember most is ___, and it mattered because ___."
Wrap It Up

With a partner, think of something a person said to you outside class this week, at home, at work, in a shop, or on the phone. Say what they said in one sentence of your own. Then swap.

  • ★ "He said, '___.'"
  • ★★★★ "What she said was that ___, and she said it because ___."
