## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Visual Inquiry:** two photographs are printed in the packet, an Iberian lynx and a stretch of
  Spanish dehesa (open grassland with scattered oak trees). Students look at both from where they sit
  and answer in pairs: "What do you notice about this animal's ears, face, and color?" and "What kind
  of place is this, and what would be easy or hard about living there?" Take call-outs and build a
  live board list of noticed features under two headings, LOOKS and PLACE.
  Image: `Lesson5_IberianLynx_Img_HookLynx.jpg` - "Lince iberico (Lynx pardinus), Almuradiel, Ciudad Real, Espana, 2021-12-19, DD 02" by Diego Delso, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lince_ib%C3%A9rico_(Lynx_pardinus),_Almuradiel,_Ciudad_Real,_Espa%C3%B1a,_2021-12-19,_DD_02.jpg.
  Image: `Lesson5_IberianLynx_Img_HookHabitat.jpg` - "Dehesa - paisaje" by Miguel Angel Masegosa Martinez, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dehesa_-_paisaje.jpg.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing listening for stated versus implied opinion.
  A stated opinion is one the speaker says straight out, like 'This is a huge success.' An implied
  opinion is one the speaker never says, but their word choice gives it away, like calling something a
  'problem' or saying we are 'not safe yet.'" (This is exactly what today's Listening Closing Transfer
  Check will ask students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they are about to hear and who is speaking.
- **Good to Know:** The IUCN Red List is a worldwide list, updated every year, that records which
  plants and animals are close to disappearing. An animal moves off the endangered part of the list
  only after its numbers have gone up and stayed up for years.
- **Good to Know:** Spain and Portugal share one large peninsula in southwest Europe, which is why this
  cat is called "Iberian." It lives nowhere else in the world.
- Pre-teach the 6 target items above using the context sentences, matching each to the hook photos
  where it fits (medium-sized, dense forests and grasslands, and the description words pointed ears,
  facial hair like a beard, shades of brown all point at something visible in the two images).

**Phase 2: Listen & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Play the full audio once, straight through.
- While listening, students fill a description outline printed in their packet, with four headings and
  room for indented examples under each: WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE / WHERE IT LIVES / WHAT ALMOST HAPPENED /
  WHAT PEOPLE SAY ABOUT THE NEWS.
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - **Level 2 (choose what was heard):** the six target items are printed in a row. Circle the ones the
    audio actually says and cross out the ones it does not. (Two of the printed words never appear in
    the audio.)
  - **Level 3:** "Listen for two words that tell you what this cat looks like."
  - **Level 4:** "Listen for how many lynx there were in 2001 and how many there are now, and for the
    reason the speaker gives for the change."
  - **Level 5:** "Listen for a moment where somebody clearly feels a certain way about this news but
    never says so directly."

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach stated versus implied opinion directly from two real, named moments.
  - **Stated,** from **[Segment 2: From 62 to more than 2,000]**: Hilton-Taylor says, "It's really (a)
    huge success, an exponential increase in the population size." Ask what word tells you his opinion
    outright. Establish: "huge success" is the opinion itself, said out loud.
  - **Implied,** from **[Segment 4: Not out of danger just yet]**: the narrator says Hilton-Taylor
    "warns that the lynx is not out of danger just yet." Nobody says "I am still worried." Ask which
    words carry the feeling anyway. Gloss "not out of danger just yet" as still at risk, even though
    things have improved.
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt: play the line in **[Segment 3: Rabbits, people, and
  habitat]** where Ortiz calls it "the greatest recovery of a cat species ever achieved through
  conservation." Students decide in pairs whether this is stated or implied and say which word settles
  it.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level. Item counts are calibrated for balanced time-on-task.

- **Level 2 (4 items).**
  1. **The frame, produced once.** Three photographs are printed side by side, labeled a, b, and c.
     The teacher says the frame sentence aloud: "It is medium-sized." Circle the photograph that
     matches, then say the animal's name aloud.
     Image: `Lesson5_IberianLynx_Img_ChoiceLynx.jpg` - "Lince iberico (Lynx pardinus), Almuradiel, Ciudad Real, Espana, 2021-12-19, DD 07" by Diego Delso, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lince_ib%C3%A9rico_(Lynx_pardinus),_Almuradiel,_Ciudad_Real,_Espa%C3%B1a,_2021-12-19,_DD_07.jpg.
     Image: `Lesson5_IberianLynx_Img_ChoiceWolf.jpg` - "Iberian Wolf AdF 001" by Arturo de Frias Marques, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Iberian_Wolf_AdF_001.jpg.
     Image: `Lesson5_IberianLynx_Img_ChoiceDeer.jpg` - "Spanish red deer female" by Ignacio Suarez, public domain, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Spanish_red_deer_female.jpg.
     **Answer note:** photograph a, the lynx.
  2. **Fix the wrong word.** This printed line has one word changed from what the audio said. Cross out
     the wrong word and write the right one above it: "They have round ears and facial hair that looks
     like a beard."
     **Answer note:** round should be pointed.
  3. **Fix the wrong word.** Same instruction, second line: "Native to Spain and Portugal, the wild
     cats are medium-sized with different shades of grey."
     **Answer note:** grey should be brown.
  4. **Circle one: yes / no.** The man who counts the animals said the number went up a lot. Does he
     think that is good news?
     **Answer note:** yes; his words "huge success" say so outright. This is the interpretive step
     required by Quality Standards Section B, not fact retrieval.

- **Level 3 (4 items).** A model card is printed once at the top of this Level's task: **It is \_\_\_
  and \_\_\_.**
  1. Circle the sentence that best describes the lynx: (a) It is large and grey. (b) It is
     medium-sized and brown. (c) It is small and black.
     **Answer note:** b.
  2. Complete the frame about the animal's ears and face, using the word bank (pointed, round, flat /
     a beard, a mask, a crown): "They are \_\_\_ and its facial hair looks like \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** pointed; a beard.
  3. Complete the frame about where the lynx lives, using the word bank (dense, empty, frozen / forests
     and grasslands, cities and roads, mountains and snow): "Its habitat is \_\_\_ \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** dense; forests and grasslands.
  4. One speaker calls the lynx's return a "huge success." Circle the word in his sentence that tells
     you his opinion, then circle one: he said it straight out / he only hinted at it.
     **Answer note:** success (huge is acceptable); he said it straight out.

- **Level 4 (2 items).**
  1. In three or four sentences, name two features of the lynx that the speaker describes, then make
     one comparison between the lynx's situation in 2001 and its situation now, and give the reason the
     speaker states for the change. Choose your own connectors.
     **Answer note:** features from medium-sized, shades of brown, pointed ears, beard-like facial
     hair; the comparison runs from 62 adults in 2001 to more than 2,000 by 2023, or from 449 square
     kilometers of habitat in 2005 to at least 3,320 now; the stated reason is the recovery of the
     European rabbit, its main food source, together with protection projects and restored habitat.
  2. The speaker describes the same habitat twice, once as it was and once as it is. Write two
     sentences that put those two descriptions next to each other, and add a brief reason for the
     difference, in your own connectors.
     **Answer note:** the habitat grew from 449 square kilometers in 2005 to at least 3,320 square
     kilometers now, because more than 400 lynx were reintroduced and their natural environments were
     restored.

- **Level 5 (3 items).**
  1. In one paragraph, summarize how this piece is organized: what the speaker puts first, what comes
     next, and what she saves for the end.
     **Answer note:** it opens by naming the animal and describing what it looks like and where it
     lives, moves to numbers showing the rise from 62 to more than 2,000, then explains the rabbit link
     and the people involved, and closes on what could still go wrong.
  2. Find one word or phrase where a speaker clearly feels something about this news but never says the
     feeling outright. Write it down and explain what the word choice signals.
     **Answer note:** "warns," "not out of danger just yet," "the biggest uncertainty," or "we also
     worried"; each signals continuing concern without any speaker saying "I am still worried."
  3. Compare that moment with the "huge success" quote earlier in the piece. Write two or three
     sentences on what changes between the two, and on why a speaker might choose to hint rather than
     state.
     **Answer note:** the earlier moment states the opinion outright while the later one leaves it to
     word choice; hinting keeps a good-news story from being contradicted head-on and leaves room for
     uncertainty about rabbits, disease, climate, and fire.

- **Board-dependent moment:** students who worked at different Levels read out one line each into the
  shared Phase 2 outline on the board. The WHAT PEOPLE SAY ABOUT THE NEWS heading stays empty until
  Levels 3, 4, and 5 have all contributed, so no single Level's work fills the picture.

- **Respectful Tiers check:** Level 2's item 4 is a genuine, simplified interpretive judgment about a
  speaker's attitude, not fact retrieval alone.

**Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**

- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling the target vocabulary):**

  > Our school put a small garden on the roof last spring. It is medium-sized, about the size of two
  > classrooms, with low walls and a lot of open sun. We planted flowers there for bees, because one
  > local species is close to extinct and has almost no habitat left in the city. In one year the
  > number of visiting bees showed an exponential jump. The garden club says the roof is fine for now.
  > They also say next summer will tell us something.

  (84 words; recycles medium-sized, species, extinct, habitat, and exponential on a topic unrelated to
  the lynx. The final two sentences carry an implied opinion, not a stated one.)

- The teacher reads the script aloud once, at a natural but clear pace. **Students do not read it
  first.** It is printed in the packet only after the response space, upside down, under a "don't read
  ahead" instruction.
- Out loud to a partner, each student names one stated opinion and one implied opinion from what they
  heard, and says which words gave the implied one away. The teacher cold-calls two or three pairs.
  Demonstration, not a self-report vote.
  **Answer note:** the stated opinion is "the roof is fine for now"; the implied one sits in "for now"
  and "next summer will tell us something," which signal that the club is not yet convinced.
- After sharing, students may turn the packet around and read the script to check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Panel Round (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- The skill is drawn from the real speakers' own language. In **[Segment 4: Not out of danger just
  yet]**, Perez de Ayala says, "every four years or so we revise the protocols," and Hilton-Taylor
  says, "We also worried about issues with climate change, how the habitat will respond to climate
  change." Neither man commits to a flat claim: "or so" softens a number and "we also worried about"
  softens a prediction into a concern.
- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing hedging an opinion, which means softening what you say so you
  are not claiming more than you know. It is what these scientists do every time they talk about what
  might happen next." (This is exactly what this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask
  students to reproduce.)
- Teach the hedging frames: "It seems \_\_\_." / "It might be \_\_\_." / "I think it's probably
  \_\_\_." / "About \_\_\_, or so." / "I'm not completely sure, but \_\_\_."

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level. Item counts are calibrated for balanced time-on-task.

- **Level 2 (3 items).** The frame is printed once at the top of this Level's task: **It is \_\_\_.**
  1. **Better of two.** Two lines about the lynx are printed. Circle the one that fits the animal in
     the photographs, then say aloud the word that makes it fit: "It is medium-sized." / "It is
     enormous."
     **Answer note:** the first line; the word is medium-sized.
  2. **Better of two.** Same instruction, second pair: "It is spotted." / "It is striped."
     **Answer note:** the first line; the word is spotted.
  3. **A partner's object, the Day 2 production.** Choose one thing your partner has with them: a bag,
     a bottle, a pencil case. Say the frame about it once, out loud, using one describing word. Then add
     one softening word from Phase 1: "It seems \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** any accurate describing word; the softener must be audible.

- **Level 3 (3 items).** A model card is printed once at the top of this Level's task: **It is \_\_\_
  and \_\_\_.**
  1. Using the model, describe the lynx to a partner in one sentence with both slots filled.
     **Answer note:** for example, It is medium-sized and brown.
  2. Using the model, describe something you own to a partner in one sentence with both slots filled.
     **Answer note:** any two accurate describing words.
  3. Say your second sentence again with a hedge from Phase 1 in front of it, so it sounds less
     certain, and have your partner tell you which word did the softening.
     **Answer note:** any Phase 1 frame; the partner names the hedge, for example seems or probably.

- **Level 4 (2 items).**
  1. Describe an animal or a place you know well in four or five sentences: two features, one
     comparison with something else, and a brief reason for that comparison, using your own
     connectors. Deliver it to a partner without notes in front of you.
     **Answer note:** the comparison and the reason must both be audible, for example warmer than my
     old one because it faces south.
  2. Give the same description again, this time hedging the comparison so you are not claiming
     certainty, and have your partner repeat back the hedged sentence.
     **Answer note:** for example, It's probably warmer than my old one, or It seems warmer than my
     old one.

- **Level 5 (2 items).**
  1. Give an extended description of a place you know well, at least six sentences, organized so a
     listener can follow the order you chose. Include one clearly evaluative word that signals your
     opinion without your ever stating it.
     **Answer note:** the organizing order should be nameable afterwards, for example outside to
     inside; the evaluative word might be cramped, peaceful, tired, or bright.
  2. Have your partner name your evaluative word and say what opinion it signalled. Then give the same
     opinion again as a hedged statement, and discuss which version sounds stronger.
     **Answer note:** the hedged version is usually judged weaker but more careful; either judgment is
     acceptable if the student names what makes it so.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- **Feature: contrastive stress.** Play the two number moments in **[Segment 2: From 62 to more than
  2,000]** back to back: "In 2001, there were only 62 adult Iberian lynx" and "By 2023, the IUCN said
  its latest count shows more than 2,000 wild cats." The numbers and the dates carry heavy stress
  because they are being set against each other; the surrounding words do not.
- Students underline the two words in each printed line that they expect to be stressed, then check
  against the real audio. The teacher models live from the recording, since real emphasis can differ
  from a written prediction.
- **Say from sound (Level 2's slot for this shape):** a partner says one target word from Day 1 at
  normal speed. The student repeats it, then points to it on the printed list and checks with the
  partner.
- Everyone then applies contrastive stress to their own Phase 2 language, in brief paired rehearsal,
  saying one sentence twice with the stress on a different word each time.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Panel Round (20 min)**

- Three or four students at a time answer from where they sit as the panel; the rest of the class has
  the tracking task below. Rotate so every student sits on the panel once. Nobody moves furniture and
  nothing is posted around the room.
- **Rotated prompts** (one per panel round):
  1. "Should people spend money to bring back one animal species? Why or why not?"
  2. "Describe a place near you where an animal lives. What makes it a good habitat, or a poor one?"
  3. "The scientists said the lynx is 'not out of danger just yet.' Do you think this story ends well?"
- **Every panelist must use at least one hedging frame from Phase 1**, because none of these questions
  has a certain answer.
- **Active task for every non-speaker:** keep a running tally of each hedging frame you hear, and write
  down one describing word a panelist uses that you would like to borrow.
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 students rehearse their one prepared line with a partner
  before their panel round begins, and may read it from their packet when their turn comes.
- **Board-dependent moment:** as panelists speak, the teacher builds a live board list of the hedges
  actually used, in the panelists' own words. This list is what Phase 5 draws on.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- In pairs, out loud, every student produces one new hedged opinion on a subject other than the lynx
  and other than their Phase 4 prompt: a film, a rule at school, the weather this weekend. The teacher
  cold-calls two or three pairs. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** Level 2 had a genuine interpretive task on Day 1 (item
  4, judging a speaker's attitude) and a genuine production role on Day 2 (the partner's-object frame
  plus a rehearsed panel line), not fact retrieval and silence.
- Brief whole-class reflection: "Which is easier to hear, an opinion someone states or an opinion
  someone only hints at? Which do you use more yourself?"
