Listening & Speaking · Describing Intermediate 1.2.5.0

Objective: I can hear the difference between an opinion a speaker says out loud and an opinion a speaker only signals through the words they choose.

Good to Know

The IUCN Red List is a worldwide list, updated every year, that records which plants and animals are close to disappearing. An animal moves off the endangered part of the list only after its numbers have gone up and stayed up for years.

Spain and Portugal share one large peninsula in southwest Europe, which is why this cat is called "Iberian." It lives nowhere else in the world.

Words to Know

1. speciesa group of animals or plants of the same kind, with one shared name
2. extinctno longer existing anywhere
3. medium-sizedneither large nor small
4. habitatthe place where an animal or plant naturally lives and grows
5. exponentialgrowing very fast, and faster and faster over time
6. dense forests and grasslandsthick woods and open grassy land, the two kinds of place this animal needs

Learn the Phrase
Softening What You Say

Near the end of the story, two scientists talk about what might happen next, and neither one commits to a flat claim. One says, "every four years or so we revise the protocols." The other says, "We also worried about issues with climate change, how the habitat will respond to climate change." The small words "or so" and "we also worried about" are doing the softening.

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll practice softening an opinion using lines like these:

  • • "It seems ___."
  • • "It might be ___."
  • • "I think it's probably ___."
  • • "About ___, or so."
  • • "I'm not completely sure, but ___."

Say It Like a Native Speaker

When a speaker sets two numbers against each other, they push extra force onto the words that are being contrasted and leave the rest of the sentence light. Underline the two words you expect to be stressed in each line below, then listen again and check.

"In 2001, there were only 62 adult Iberian lynx."

"By 2023, the IUCN said its latest count shows more than 2,000 wild cats."

Then say one of your own sentences twice, putting the force on a different word each time, and ask your partner what changed.
