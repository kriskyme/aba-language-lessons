## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Listen & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Take a Side:** the class reads one statement and each student marks agree or disagree in their own
  packet, from where they sit, then says why to a neighbour. Statement: "A machine can weave in one hour
  what takes a person a month. Hand weaving is not worth keeping." Take a show of hands for each side
  and collect two or three reasons from each on the board.
- Two photographs are printed alongside the statement so students can see what hand weaving actually
  involves before they judge it.
  Image: `Lesson6_TapestryFactory_Img_HookLoom.jpg` - "Gobelinpinde" by Mamamimus, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gobelinpinde.JPG.
  Image: `Lesson6_TapestryFactory_Img_HookFactory.jpg` - "Real Fabrica de Tapices de Madrid en abril de 2022 01" by Emilio J. Rodriguez Posada, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Real_F%C3%A1brica_de_Tapices_de_Madrid_en_abril_de_2022_01.jpg.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing listening for signposts. Signposts are the
  small words that tell you where you are in a description: when something happened, whether the speaker
  is adding one more thing, or whether they have moved on to a new part." (This is exactly what today's
  Listening Closing Transfer Check will ask students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they are about to hear and who is speaking.
- **Good to Know:** A tapestry is a picture made by weaving colored thread, not by painting. It is made
  to hang on a wall, and a large one can take months to finish.
- **Good to Know:** In weaving, a "cartoon" is not a drawing for children. It is the full-size drawing on
  paper that a weaver copies from while working.
- Pre-teach the 6 target items above using the context sentences, pointing at what is visible in the
  loom photograph (the warp threads running up and down, the bobbins hanging from the finished part).

**Phase 2: Listen & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Play the full audio once, straight through.
- While listening, students fill a two-column T-chart printed in their packet, headed THEN (when the
  factory started) and NOW (what it does today), with three rows: who works or buys here, what the tools
  and materials are, what the factory is known for.
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - **Level 2 (fix the wrong word):** one line from the story is printed with a single word changed.
    Listen for it, cross it out, and write the right word above it.
  - **Level 3:** "Listen for two words that tell you what the factory's cloth is made of."
  - **Level 4:** "Listen for what the factory used to make and who it makes things for now, and for the
    reason the director gives for keeping every leftover."
  - **Level 5:** "Listen for a word the speaker chooses that shows admiration, without anyone saying
    they admire this place."

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach signposting directly from two real, named moments.
  - From **[Segment 2: How it started]**: "At the time, the area had been under Spanish control." Ask
    which words tell you the sentence is about the past and not about today. Establish that "At the
    time" is a signpost pointing backwards.
  - From **[Segment 4: What it does now]**: "Now, 70 percent of its buyers are individuals from Latin
    America, Europe, and the Middle East." Ask what "Now" is doing at the front of that sentence.
    Establish that the speaker has just moved the description forward in time.
- Also name the adding signpost: in **[Segment 3: The materials and the tools]** and
  **[Segment 4: What it does now]**, the piece twice starts a sentence with "And" and once with "The
  factory also," each time to add one more thing to the same part of the description.
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt: play the opening of **[Segment 5: How a tapestry is made]**,
  "A tapestry begins with 'cartoons'." Students say in pairs what "begins with" tells them is coming
  next, then listen on to check.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level. Item counts are calibrated for balanced time-on-task.

- **Level 2 (3 items).**
  1. **Choose what was heard.** Eight words are printed in a row: wool, silk, plastic, cotton, linen,
     glass, jute, paper. Circle the ones the story says the factory works with and cross out the ones it
     does not.
     **Answer note:** circle wool, silk, cotton, linen, jute; cross out plastic, glass, paper.
  2. **Better of two.** Two lines about the factory's old wooden machines are printed. Circle the one
     that fits what you heard, and say the word that makes it fit: "The factory still uses its old
     wooden machines." / "The factory threw away its old wooden machines."
     **Answer note:** the first line; the word is still. This is the interpretive step required by
     Quality Standards Section B: the story never says the factory values the old machines, and the
     student has to hear "are still in use" as showing that it does.
  3. **The frame, produced once.** The teacher says one thing the factory makes. Complete the frame in
     writing and then say it aloud, using one word from the story: "It is \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** any accurate material or quality from the story, for example wool, old, or
     handmade.

- **Level 3 (4 items).** A model card is printed once at the top of this Level's task: **It is \_\_\_
  and \_\_\_.**
  1. Circle the sentence that best describes what this factory makes: (a) It is printed and new. (b) It
     is woven and old. (c) It is painted and small.
     **Answer note:** b.
  2. Complete the frame about the thread the factory uses, using the word bank (silk, plastic, paper /
     wool, glass, stone): "It is \_\_\_ and \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** silk; wool.
  3. Complete the frame about where the factory stands, using the word bank (loud, quiet, empty /
     narrow, new, old): "The street is \_\_\_ and the building is \_\_\_."
     **Answer note:** quiet; old.
  4. The story says the factory keeps the water from the dyes. Write the word in that sentence that
     tells you what happens to it, then circle one: the leftovers are thrown away / the leftovers are
     used again.
     **Answer note:** recycled; the leftovers are used again.

- **Level 4 (2 items).**
  1. In three or four sentences, name two tools or materials the factory uses, make one comparison
     between how the factory worked when it opened and how it works today, and give the reason the
     director states for keeping every leftover. Choose your own connectors.
     **Answer note:** tools and materials from wool, silk, jute, cotton, linen, bobbins, spinning
     wheels, the original wooden machines; the comparison runs from 1721, when a king brought weavers
     from Flanders, to today, when most buyers are individuals from three continents, or from
     hand-knowledge alone to old knowledge combined with new methods; the stated reason is that
     everything has a double, a second use.
  2. Describe the two halves of how a tapestry is made, the drawing and the weaving, in two sentences
     that put them next to each other, and add a brief reason why the order matters, in your own
     connectors.
     **Answer note:** the cartoon is drawn on paper first and then copied onto the warps, which are
     woven over afterwards, because the weaver needs the full-size picture in place before any thread
     goes down.

- **Level 5 (3 items).**
  1. In one paragraph, summarize how this story is organized: what the speaker puts first, what she
     puts in the middle, and what she saves for the end.
     **Answer note:** it opens with what the factory makes and where it is, goes back to how it started
     in 1721, moves through the materials and tools, comes forward to today's repairs, records, and
     buyers, and closes with how a tapestry is actually made and with Goya.
  2. Find one word or phrase where the speaker clearly admires this place but never says so. Write it
     down and explain what the word choice signals.
     **Answer note:** "master painter," "well-known," "some of the most important cloth and material
     records in Europe," or "long-held knowledge"; each signals admiration with no speaker saying they
     admire the factory.
  3. List the signposts the speaker uses to move between the parts of the description, in the order she
     uses them, and write one sentence on what the order tells you about how she wants the story
     understood.
     **Answer note:** "for more than 300 years," "At the time," "also," "Now," "recently," "In 2018,"
     "begins with," "later," "who began working there in 1780"; the run of time signposts frames the
     factory as one unbroken line from 1721 to today.

- **Board-dependent moment:** students who worked at different Levels read one line each into the shared
  THEN and NOW chart on the board. The NOW column's tools-and-materials row cannot be completed from any
  single Level's task, so it stays open until Levels 2, 3, and 4 have all contributed.

- **Respectful Tiers check:** Level 2's item 2 is a genuine, simplified interpretive judgment about what
  the factory values, not fact retrieval alone.

**Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**

- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling the target vocabulary):**

  > Our school's costume room is in the oldest part of the building, which was a small palace a hundred
  > years ago. First, the students there cut up old curtains instead of buying new cloth, so almost
  > everything is recycled. Then they dye the pieces in big pots in the sink. After that, they weave
  > strips together on a wooden frame, pulling the colors across the warp threads. Bobbins and spinning
  > wheels sit on a shelf, still working.

  (86 words; recycles all six target items on a topic unrelated to the Madrid factory, and carries four
  plain signposts, "First," "Then," "After that," and "so," for students to catch.)

- The teacher reads the script aloud once, at a natural but clear pace. **Students do not read it
  first.** It is printed in the packet only after the response space, upside down, under a "don't read
  ahead" instruction.
- Out loud to a partner, each student retells the costume room's steps in order and names the signpost
  words that told them the order. The teacher cold-calls two or three pairs. Demonstration, not a
  self-report vote.
  **Answer note:** the order is cut up old curtains, dye the pieces, weave the strips; the signposts are
  First, Then, and After that, with "so" marking the result.
- After sharing, students may turn the packet around and read the script to check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Jigsaw (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- The model is drawn from the source's own reporting language. Twice the narrator takes what the
  director told her and gives it back in her own shorter words rather than quoting him: "The order is
  worth more than 1 million dollars and it will provide work for up to five years, Klecker de Elizalde
  said," in **[Segment 4: What it does now]**. Play that line and the director's own long quote in
  **[Segment 3: The materials and the tools]** back to back so students hear the difference between the
  full quote and the shortened version.
- **Deviation, flagged:** this source is a narrated report, not a conversation, so it contains no moment
  where one speaker summarizes another speaker back to their face. The model above is real reported
  speech from the source, which is the nearest genuine instance the transcript offers; no invented
  dialogue is presented as if it were said.
- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing summarizing what someone said, which means giving back the
  short version of what you just heard so the other person can check you got it right." (This is exactly
  what this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask students to reproduce.)
- Teach the frames: "So you're saying \_\_\_." / "In other words, \_\_\_." / "If I understood you,
  \_\_\_." / "Basically, \_\_\_." / "Let me check I've got it: \_\_\_."

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level. Item counts are calibrated for balanced time-on-task.

- **Level 2 (1 item, three rounds).**
  1. **Say from sound.** A partner says one target word from Day 1 out loud. Repeat it, write it down,
     and check it together against the printed list. Do this three times, with a different word each
     time, then swap roles.
     **Answer note:** any three of palace, dye, recycle, warp, weave, bobbins.

- **Level 3 (3 items).** A model card is printed once at the top of this Level's task: **It is \_\_\_
  and \_\_\_.**
  1. Using the model, describe the factory's cloth to a partner in one sentence with both slots filled.
     **Answer note:** for example, It is woven and old.
  2. Using the model, describe something in this room to a partner in one sentence with both slots
     filled.
     **Answer note:** any two accurate describing words.
  3. Your partner says their sentence. Give it back in your own shorter words, starting with one of the
     Phase 1 frames, and let them tell you whether you got it right.
     **Answer note:** any accurate short version opening with a frame.

- **Level 4 (2 items).**
  1. Describe a place where things are made that you know well, a kitchen, a garage, a classroom, in
     four or five sentences: two tools or materials, one comparison with somewhere else, and a brief
     reason for that comparison, using your own connectors. Deliver it to a partner without notes in
     front of you.
     **Answer note:** the comparison and the reason must both be audible, for example tidier than my
     brother's because everything hangs on the wall.
  2. Your partner gives their description back to you as a two-sentence summary, opening with a Phase 1
     frame. Say what they left out, and whether leaving it out changed the picture.
     **Answer note:** the student names one dropped detail and judges whether it mattered.

- **Level 5 (2 items).**
  1. Give an extended description of a place where things are made, at least six sentences, organized so
     a listener can follow the order you chose, and signpost the order out loud as you go. Include one
     clearly evaluative word that signals your opinion without your ever stating it.
     **Answer note:** the signposts should be audible and the evaluative word nameable afterwards, for
     example cramped, spotless, tired, or proud.
  2. Your partner summarizes your description back to you in three sentences. Say whether their summary
     kept your organization and your evaluative word, and what changed if it did not.
     **Answer note:** the student judges the summary against their own order and word choice.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- **Feature: thought-group pausing.** The director's list in **[Segment 3: The materials and the tools]**
  is the model: "Here the only products we work with are silk, wool, jute, cotton, linen." Each material
  is its own short group, with a small pause after it, and the voice does not run the five words
  together.
- Students mark the pause points with a slash on the printed line, then check against the real audio.
  The teacher models live from the recording, since real pausing can differ from a written prediction.
- Everyone then applies the feature in brief paired rehearsal, saying one of their own Phase 2 sentences
  first as one rushed string and then broken into groups, and telling each other which was easier to
  follow.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Jigsaw (20 min)**

- Students get into groups of four from where they sit. Each person takes one of the four questions
  below and becomes the person in the group who has thought about it, then the group takes turns, one
  question at a time. Nobody moves furniture and nothing is posted around the room.
- **The four questions:**
  1. What does this factory make, and what is it made from?
  2. How has the factory changed between 1721 and today?
  3. How is a tapestry actually made, from the first drawing to the finished cloth?
  4. Why does the factory keep every leftover, and do you think that is worth the trouble?
- **Every listener must summarize:** after each person speaks, the person on their left gives the short
  version back using a Phase 1 frame, and the speaker says whether it was right. That is the active task
  for every non-speaker.
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 students take question 1, rehearse their one prepared
  sentence with a partner before the group round begins, and may read it from their packet.
- **A partner's object, the Day 2 production:** before the group round starts, each Level 2 student
  chooses one thing their partner has with them, a bag, a bottle, a pencil case, and says the frame
  about it once, out loud, with one describing word.
- **Board-dependent moment:** as groups report out, the teacher builds a live board list of the summary
  frames actually used and of any point a summary dropped. This list is what Phase 5 draws on.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- In pairs, out loud, one student describes something in two or three sentences on a subject other than
  the factory, a meal, a journey, a room at home, and the other gives it straight back using a Phase 1
  frame. Then they swap. The teacher cold-calls two or three pairs. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** Level 2 had a genuine interpretive task on Day 1 (item 2,
  judging what the factory values) and a genuine production role on Day 2 (the partner's-object frame
  plus a rehearsed jigsaw question), not fact retrieval and silence.
- Return to the Day 1 statement about hand weaving: as a class, ask whether anybody's side has changed
  and why. Brief reflection: "What is one thing you now know about how cloth is made that you did not
  know yesterday?"
