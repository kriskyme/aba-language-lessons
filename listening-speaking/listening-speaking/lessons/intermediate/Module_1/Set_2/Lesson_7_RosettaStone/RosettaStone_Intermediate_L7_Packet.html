Good to Know

Hieroglyphs were the writing system of ancient Egypt for thousands of years. After Egyptians began using other kinds of writing, the knowledge of how to read hieroglyphs was lost completely for around 1,400 years.

The British Museum is a very large public museum in London. Many of the objects in it were taken from other countries during the years when Britain ruled or fought in them, which is why several countries now ask for objects back.

Last winter a builder found a wooden box under the floor of our town library. Inside were forty letters with strange carvings on the covers, and nobody could read a word of them. A retired teacher spent three months on the decipherment, because she recognized an old shorthand her grandmother used. Once she managed to decode the first page, the whole set made sense. They were written during the reign of Queen Victoria. The library is putting them in a small exhibition this spring.

Learn the Phrase
Taking a Turn Politely

Listen again to the woman from the museum. She finishes one long thought about why the show is being held, and only then adds a second, separate one: "It's a wonderful moment to celebrate," she added. She takes her whole turn first, and nobody cuts across her.

Now it's your turn. In the tasks below, you'll practice both halves of this, coming in and holding on, using lines like these:

  • • "Can I add something here?"
  • • "Sorry to come in, but ___."
  • • "Could I just finish this part?"
  • • "Go ahead, you first."
  • • "I'd like to hear the rest of that."
