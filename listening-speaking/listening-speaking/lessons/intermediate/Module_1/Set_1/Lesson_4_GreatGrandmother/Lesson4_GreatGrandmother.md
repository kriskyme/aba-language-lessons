## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Listen & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- K-W-L Chart (K and W only): students fill the "K: What We Know" and "W: What We Want to Know" columns of the
  chart printed in their own packet, on the topic of learning something new later in life (the "L: What We
  Learned" column is completed in Day 2, Phase 5), then compare with a neighbor. Take call-outs and build the
  shared K and W lists live on the board. Prompt questions: "What do you already know about older family members learning new skills?" /
  "What do you want to know about how families teach each other?"
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing predicting from context - making a guess about what
  comes next before we hear it confirmed." (This is what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check will ask
  students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they're about to listen to and who Setsuko Takamizawa is.
- **Good to Know:** The Tokyo Olympics that Takamizawa hoped to volunteer at were originally planned for 2020
  but were delayed to 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic. The EF English Proficiency Index, mentioned in the
  story, is a real yearly ranking that compares how well people speak English across different countries.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words above using the context sentences, in-context picture matching. No idiom to gloss
  in this lesson's source material.
- Using the W column: "Based on what you want to know, what do you predict this story will be about?" Collect
  2-3 predictions.
  **Phase 2: Listen & Listening Notes (15 min)**
- Listen to the full audio once. While listening, students fill in a comparison T-chart: columns "Then (WWII years)"
  vs. "Now (today)," rows "Could she learn English?", "Who helps her?", "Why does it matter to her?"
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - Level 2/3: "Listen for who is in this family and what they are doing together."
  - Level 4/5: "Listen for how the story is organized, and what Takamizawa's final quote suggests about how she
    feels about her life."
    **Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**
- Teach predicting-from-context directly: pause right before **[Segment 1: Never too late to learn]**'s WWII
  quote and ask, "Given that she's 91 now and volunteering for the Olympics, what do you predict she'll say
  about learning English when she was young?" Take predictions, then play the segment to confirm: "In my second
  year there, English was banned because it was the enemy language."
- Guided practice: pause right before **[Segment 3: Global Perspectives]** and have students predict what
  Takamizawa will say about Japan and the rest of the world, then play the segment to check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Rotating Partners (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- As flagged above, this source does not contain a quotable clarification exchange. Ground the spotlight in the
  source's real, verified context instead: Natsuko "sends a new English word to her grandmother's phone every
  day" and they "work together directly on phrases" - exactly the situation where a learner needs to ask for
  clarification. Name the skill: "Today we're practicing asking for clarification - useful phrases for when you
  don't understand something and need to ask again, exactly like Takamizawa needs to do every day while
  learning new words from Natsuko." This is the exact skill this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask
  students to reproduce.
- Teach clarification frames: "What does \_\_\_ mean?" / "Could you say that again?" / "Sorry, could you repeat
  that?"
  **Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task):
  - **Level 2 (3 items):** Complete: "What does **_ mean?" (fill in any target vocabulary word). Complete:
    "Could you say that _**?" (again / fast / never). Practice saying both frames to a partner using one of
    today's vocabulary words.
  - **Level 3 (2 items):** Two-slot frame with a model card: "Sorry, could you **_? I didn't _**." Practice with
    a partner: one person says a target vocabulary word quickly and quietly, the other asks for clarification
    and gets it repeated clearly.
  - **Level 4 (2 items):** Original clarification exchange with a stated reason, own connectors: write a short
    exchange where one person asks for clarification about a new word and explains why they're confused.
    Perform the exchange with a partner, then switch roles.
  - **Level 5 (2 items):** Extended exchange including a polite hedge before asking for clarification (e.g.,
    "I don't want to interrupt, but...") without directly stating "I'm confused." Ask a partner a genuinely
    difficult vocabulary word from a different subject, then use your clarification frames for real.
    **Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**
- Teach question intonation using a real source sentence - Takamizawa's own practiced English phrase: "Welcome
  to Tokyo, this is the Olympic stadium, how can I help you?" Mark the falling intonation on the statement parts
  and the question intonation on "how can I help you?" Teacher models live from the actual audio, since
  exact natural intonation can vary from a written prediction.
- Students mark rising/falling arrows on the sentence above, check against the real audio, then apply the
  question-intonation pattern to their (invented) Phase 2 clarification frames in brief paired rehearsal.
  **Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Rotating Partners (20 min)**
- Sentence stems per task Level. **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 does a rehearsed pair-share of their
  stem with a partner before the Rotating Partners round begins.
- Students pair up from where they sit and discuss the first prompt; when a pair finishes, each student finds a
  new partner and takes the next prompt, so each of the three is discussed with a different person: 1) "Do you think it's ever too late to learn
  something new? Why or why not?" 2) "What's one thing an older family member has taught you, or could teach
  you?" 3) "If you were Takamizawa, what English phrase would you want to learn first?"
- **Active listening task:** each student keeps a running tally, across partners, of every clarification phrase
  they hear a partner use ("What does \_\_\_ mean?", "Could you say that again?"), plus one vocabulary word from
  Day 1's list.
- **Board-dependent moment:** running board record of clarification phrases actually spoken during Rotating
  Partners, feeding the Closing Transfer Check.
  **Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, one new clarification exchange about something other than
  Takamizawa's story (a new recipe, a video game, a school subject) using the Phase 1 frames. Teacher cold-calls
  two or three pairs. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, did Level 2 get a genuine production role (the rehearsed
  pair-share), not silence?
- Return to the K-W-L chart from Day 1: as a class, fill in the "L: What We Learned" column together, closing
  the loop on the Day 1 hook. Brief reflection: "What's one new thing you learned today about someone older
  than you?" No Part 2/presentation-project extension is being used for this cycle (not yet built - see Master
  Generation Document).
