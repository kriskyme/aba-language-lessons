> Listening: Can listen to one short spoken description using a two-slot frame ("It is ___ and

## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Listen & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- Visual Inquiry: show the lesson's two photos, whole wheat grains next to white flour (`Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_HookWheat.jpg`,
  `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_HookFlour.jpg`, embedded in the student packet). Ask: "What do you see? Where does bread
  actually come from?"

  Image: `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_HookFlour.jpg` - "All-Purpose Flour (4107895947)" by Veganbaking.net, CC BY-SA 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:All-Purpose_Flour_(4107895947).jpg.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing listening for the main idea - figuring out a speaker's
  biggest point even when they give us a lot of detail." (This is what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check
  will ask students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they're about to listen to and who Jonathan Bethony is.
- **Good to Know:** Senegal is a country in West Africa - Bethony traveled there to study drumming, which is
  where his interest in farming communities began. Washington State University's Bread Lab is a real research
  program that helps bakers find new ways to use wheat grown in their own region.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words above using the context sentences, in-context picture matching (a mill, a bag of
  organic wheat, a tray of pastries). Gloss "mixing and matching" briefly as a transparent chunk.
  **Phase 2: Listen & Listening Notes (15 min)**
- Listen to the full audio once. While listening, students fill in a comparison T-chart (Describing module's
  organizer): columns "Seylou Bakery" vs. "A Typical Bakery," rows "Where the grain comes from," "Who mills the
  grain," "How many kinds of grain."
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - Level 2/3: "Listen for what Seylou makes and where the grain comes from."
  - Level 4/5: "Listen for what makes Seylou different from a typical bakery, and why Bethony made that choice."
    **Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**
- Teach gist-listening directly: model on **[Segment 3: Milling local grains]** - "You don't need every word.
  Listen for what gets repeated (grain, mill, organic, local) and for the reason a speaker gives for a choice."
- Guided practice: replay **[Segment 5: It's the taste]** and have students state the gist in one sentence to a
  partner before checking against the group.
  **Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level and completes it (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task, per the
  Lesson Generation Prompt's fourth addendum):
  - **Level 2 (5 items):** Circle the picture that answers: "What does Seylou make?" (bread / cars / shoes;
    `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_ChoiceBread.jpg`, `Img_ChoiceCar.jpg`, `Img_ChoiceShoes.jpg`, embedded in the packet).
    Circle: "Seylou is a **_." (bakery / factory / farm). Complete: "It is _** and **_." using two words from a
    bank (organic, local, imported, plastic). Circle: "Seylou uses _** grain." (organic / plastic / old).
    According to the text, why do people keep coming back to Seylou? (a) the taste (b) the price (c) the
    location [Answer: a] - also ask "Do you think Bethony is happy about this? Circle: yes / no."
    Image: `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_ChoiceBread.jpg` - "Loaves of Bread (Unsplash)" by Clark Young, CC0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Loaves_of_Bread_(Unsplash).jpg.
    Image: `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_ChoiceCar.jpg` - "Volkswagen Golf VIII IMG 4023" by Alexander Migl, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Volkswagen_Golf_VIII_IMG_4023.jpg.

  - **Level 3 (4 items):** Choose the best sentence: "It is **_ and _**." (a) a bakery, small (b) a bakery,
    local (c) a factory, large [Answer: b]. Complete: "Seylou's grain comes from **_ and _**" (word bank: local
    farmers, organic growers, a factory, far away). Circle: "Bethony traveled to **_ to study drumming."
    (Senegal / France / Peru). Circle: "Besides bread, Seylou also makes _**." (pastries / pizza / cakes only).
  - **Level 4 (3 items):** In 2-3 sentences, name one feature of Seylou, one way it's different from a typical
    bakery, and the stated reason for that difference. List two features of Seylou's process (own mill, ~20
    kinds of grain), one comparison to a typical bakery, and the stated reason (freshness/using underutilized
    grains). Match each background fact to what it explains: WSU Bread Lab -> helps bakers use regionally grown
    wheat; Senegal -> where Bethony's interest in farming began.
  - **Level 5 (3 items):** Write a one-paragraph gist summary of the whole piece: who Bethony is, what Seylou

  **Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**
- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary):**
  > Every Sunday, my neighbor makes fresh pasta by hand in her kitchen. She uses just three ingredients: wheat
  > flour, eggs, and salt. She buys organic wheat from a small farm nearby, and she grinds part of it herself
  > with an old hand mill before mixing the dough. She says fresh pasta tastes completely different from the
  > pastry-shop kind you buy in a package. It takes her about an hour to make, but she says it's worth it for a
  > good Sunday dinner.
  > (79 words; recycles ingredient, wheat, organic, mill, pastry - 5 of the lesson's 6 target words - on a topic
  > unrelated to Seylou.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, at a natural but clear pace. **Students do not read it themselves first** - it
  is printed in the student packet only after their response space, under a "don't read ahead" instruction.
- Students apply gist-listening: state the main idea out loud to a partner in one sentence (e.g., "A neighbor
  makes fresh pasta by hand every Sunday using organic ingredients"). Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to
  share. Demonstration, not a self-report vote. After sharing, students may read the script to check what they
  heard.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Panel Round (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Play **[Segment 3: Milling local grains]** again for Fromartz's real quote: "When you start baking with grains
  from particular farmers, the grains will be different in each farm." Also point to the article's own line:
  "different from most bakeries around the country" and "few other bakeries in the U.S. do" - real comparison
  language from the actual source, not invented.
- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing making comparisons - showing how one thing is different from or similar
  to another." This is the exact skill this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask students to
  reproduce.
- Teach comparison frames: "It is **_, not _**." / "**_ is different from _** because **_." / "Unlike most
  _**, this one \_\_\_."
  **Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task):
  - **Level 2 (4 items):** Shown two pictures (a hand mill vs. a factory machine; `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_HandMill.jpg`,
    `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_FactoryMachine.jpg`, embedded in the packet), complete: "It is **_, not _**."
    (small / big). Complete: "It is **_, not _**." (organic / plastic). Complete: "Seylou is **_, not _**."
    (local / far away). Practice saying one of your completed frames to a partner.
    Image: `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_HandMill.jpg` - "Basalt quern" by Davidbena, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basalt_quern.jpg.
    Image: `Lesson1_NewBakery_Img_FactoryMachine.jpg` - "Roll Stand in Roller Mill with miller" by Roberta Strickler, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Roll_Stand_in_Roller_Mill_with_miller.jpg.
  - **Level 3 (3 items):** Two-slot frame with a model card: "**_ is different from _**." using bakery
    vocabulary. Second frame: "Unlike most **_, this one _**." Put your two sentences together and read them
    aloud to a partner as one connected comparison.
  - **Level 4 (2 items):** Original comparison + reason, own connectors: "Seylou is different from a normal
    bakery because \_\_\_." Now compare Seylou to one more thing you know well (a favorite restaurant, a chain
    store) using the same frame.
  - **Level 5 (2 items):** Extended comparison including one evaluative word signaling an opinion (e.g.,
    describing Seylou as "impressive" or "unusual" without directly saying "I like it"). Ask a partner if they
    agree with your evaluative word choice, and why or why not.
    **Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**
- Teach content-word stress using a real source sentence: "Bethony uses about 20 different kinds of organic
  grains in making his bread." Mark stressed content words (uses, 20, different, organic, grains, making, bread).
  Teacher models live from the actual audio, since exact natural stress can vary from a written
  prediction.
- Students label content words (N/V/Adj) in Fromartz's quote from Segment 3, then check their marking against
  the real audio, then apply the stress pattern in brief paired rehearsal of their Phase 2 comparison.
  **Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Panel Round (20 min)**
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 does a rehearsed pair-share of their comparison stem with a partner
  before the Panel Round begins, so their first public attempt is already a repetition, rather than an
  on-demand live turn.
- Four to six students discuss from their seats, working through: 1) "Is Seylou's approach worth the extra effort, compared to a
  typical bakery?" 2) "Would you rather eat at Seylou or a typical bakery? Why?" 3) "What is one thing Seylou
  does that you think other bakeries should copy?"
- **Listener task:** every student not currently speaking keeps a running tally of every comparison phrase they
  hear a speaker use ("different from," "unlike," "more/less than"), plus one vocabulary word from
  Day 1's list. Swap the speaking group at each prompt changeover so everyone takes a turn.
- **Board-dependent moment:** running board record of comparisons actually spoken during the Panel Round, feeding
  the Closing Transfer Check.
  **Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, one new comparison about something other than Seylou (two
  foods, two stores, two hobbies) using the Phase 1 comparison frames. Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs.
  Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, did Level 2 get a genuine interpretive step (Day 1's
  yes/no question) and a real production role (the rehearsed pair-share feeding into the Panel Round), not just
  fact-retrieval and silent participation?
- Brief reflection: "What's one thing you learned about how bread is made that you didn't know before?" No Part
  2/presentation-project extension is being used for this cycle (not yet built for this lesson type).
