## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Watch & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Four-Corner Debate:** post four statements, one per corner of the room - "I love trying new foods," "I get
  overwhelmed by too many choices," "I like talking to vendors," "I'd rather just order the same thing every
  time." Students move to the corner that best describes them, then briefly discuss with others in their corner
  why they chose it.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing recognizing examples - noticing when a speaker names
  one specific stop or food as an example of a bigger idea." (This is what today's Listening Closing Transfer
  Check will ask students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above.
- **Good to Know:** Porto is a real historic city in northern Portugal, famous for its food and for port wine,
  which is actually named after the city. A "tasca" is a small, traditional Portuguese tavern - a simple,
  casual place to eat and drink, found all over Portugal, not just in Porto.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words using the context sentences. Gloss "in spite of" briefly as a transparent chunk.
  **Phase 2: Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**
- Watch the full video once. While watching, students fill in a comparison T-chart: "The Old Grocery Store
  (Mercearia do Bolhão)" vs. "A Typical Modern Grocery Store" - rows: "When it opened," "What it sells," "Why
  people like it."
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - Level 2/3: "Listen for what foods Andre shows us."
  - Level 4/5: "Listen for what makes this market special, and why Andre thinks it's worth a visit."
    **Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**
- Teach "recognize examples" directly: model on the opening narration's general claim - Porto's traditional
  market "manages to survive in spite of competition from modern shopping malls" - followed by **[Segment 4]**,
  Andre's stop at a real grocery store still open since 1896, as a specific example of that survival.
- Guided practice: replay **[Segment 3: The canned seafood shop]** and ask what general idea about Porto's food
  culture this specific stop is an example of (Andre's opening claim that he'll show visitors "the best of
  Porto's gastronomy").
  **Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task, per the Lesson
  Generation Prompt's fourth addendum):
  - **Level 2 (5 items):** Circle the picture that answers: "What kind of tour is this?" (food tour / bike tour
    / museum tour). Complete: "Andre shows us **_." (sardines / cars / shoes). Circle: "Andre is a real
    _**." (tour guide / chef / taxi driver). Circle: "The tour takes visitors to the old \_\_\_." (market / museum
    / hospital). According to the video, when did the grocery store open? (a) 1896 (b) 1996 (c) 2018 [Answer: a]
    - also ask "Do you think that's a long time or a short time for one store to stay open? Circle: long /
      short."
  - **Level 3 (4 items):** Choose the best sentence: "It is **_ and _**." (a) a bus tour, long / (b) a food
    tour, local / (c) a museum tour, quiet [Answer: b]. Complete: "The grocery store is **_ and _**." (word
    bank: old, new / small, big / historic, modern) [Answer: historic, old]. Circle: "The market survives in
    spite of competition from **_." (modern shopping malls / other markets / restaurants). Circle: "Andre wants
    to show visitors the best of Porto's _**." (gastronomy / history / weather).
  - **Level 4 (2 items):** In 2-3 sentences, name one stop on the tour, one way it's different from a regular
    grocery store, and the stated reason Andre gives for visiting it. List two features of the grocery store
    (opened 1896; started as a grocery store, cafe, and bakery all at once), one comparison to a typical modern
    store, and the stated reason people still visit.
  - **Level 5 (3 items):** Write a one-paragraph gist summary of the whole tour: who Andre is, what stops the
    tour covers, and why he thinks it matters. Summarize how the tour is organized (market intro, sardines,
    canned seafood shop, grocery store, tavern, closing thoughts) and note the order of stops. Identify the
    evaluative language in the opening narration ("manages to survive in spite of competition from modern
    shopping malls") and explain what unstated opinion or admiration it signals about the market's importance,
    even though nobody directly says "this market is special."
- **Board-dependent moment:** students who worked on different Levels briefly compare and add their row(s) to
  the shared T-chart from Phase 2.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** Level 2's task includes a genuine interpretive step (the long/short question), not
  fact-retrieval alone.
  **Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**
- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary and applying the day's
  actual skill - recognizing examples):**
  > My aunt loves showing visitors around her hometown, especially its food. She says the best way to
  > understand a place's gastronomy is to taste it, not just read about it. Last week she took me to a tiny
  > tavern near the old grocery store downtown, the kind of place where they grill fresh fish right in front of
  > you. We tried a whole variety of small dishes, but the grilled sardines were still her favorite. She's been
  > eating there for twenty years and says nothing else compares.
  > (recycles gastronomy, tavern, grocery, grill, variety, sardines - all 6 target words - on a topic unrelated to
  > Porto, and contains a clear general claim followed by a specific supporting example.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, at a natural but clear pace. **Students do not read it themselves first** - it
  is printed in the student packet only after the response space.
- Students state, out loud to a partner, the general claim the speaker makes ("the best way to understand a
  place's gastronomy is to taste it") and the specific example she gives to support it (the tavern with the
  grilled sardines). Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs. After sharing, students may read the script to
  check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Jigsaw (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Point to how Andre's whole tour works: instead of just saying "Porto has great food," he takes visitors to
  specific, named stops - the sardine stand, the canned-seafood shop, the old grocery store - as real examples.
- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing giving examples - naming one specific case to support a bigger point,
  the same way Andre uses specific shops and foods to show off Porto's food culture." This is the exact skill
  this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check will ask students to reproduce.
- Teach giving-examples frames: "For example, **_." / "Like _**, for instance." / "Take \_\_\_, for example."
  **Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task):
  - **Level 2 (4 items):** Shown a picture of sardines, complete: "For example, **_." (sardines). Shown a
    picture of the old grocery store, complete: "For example, _**." (the grocery store). Complete: "Like \_\_\_,
    for instance." (any tour stop). Practice saying one of your completed frames to a partner.
  - **Level 3 (3 items):** Two-slot frame with a model card: "For example, **_ is a great place because _**."
    Second frame: "Take **_, for example - it's _**." Put your two sentences together and read them aloud to a
    partner as one connected description.
  - **Level 4 (2 items):** Original example + reason, own connectors: "One good example of Porto's food culture
    is **_, because _**." Now give a second example from a completely different stop on the tour, using a
    different connector than the first.
  - **Level 5 (2 items):** Extended example including one evaluative word signaling an opinion (e.g., calling a
    stop "unforgettable" or "overrated" without directly saying "I like it"). Ask a partner if they'd pick the
    same stop as their example, and why or why not.
    **Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**
- Teach content-word stress using a real source sentence: "If you want a good sardine, it has to be like this
  because the fat content will keep all of the flavors in when you grill them." Mark stressed content words
  (good, sardine, fat, content, flavors, grill). Teacher models live from the actual video audio, since exact
  natural stress can vary from a written prediction.
- Students label content words (N/V/Adj) in the sentence above, check against the real audio, then apply the
  stress pattern in brief paired rehearsal of their Phase 2 giving-examples frame.
  **Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Jigsaw (20 min)**
- Assign each small group one of the tour's four real stops to become "experts" on (sardines / canned seafood
  shop / grocery store / tavern), using their Day 1 notes. Sentence stems per task Level.
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 does a rehearsed pair-share of their stem with their expert group
  before the jigsaw's mixed-group round.
- In expert groups, students prepare one "for example" statement about their assigned stop. Regroup so each new
  mixed group has one expert from each of the four stops; each expert shares their stop using a Phase 1
  giving-examples frame, rotating through all four stops so every group hears about the whole tour.
- **Board-dependent moment:** running board record of the examples actually shared during the jigsaw, feeding
  the Closing Transfer Check.
  **Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, one new example about something other than Porto's market (two
  restaurants, two stores, two hobbies) using the Phase 1 giving-examples frames. Teacher cold-calls two or
  three pairs. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, did Level 2 get a genuine interpretive step (Day 1) and a
  real production role (the rehearsed pair-share and their turn in the mixed group), not silence?
- Brief reflection: "What's one food or place from the video you'd want to visit yourself, and why?" No Part
  2/presentation-project extension is being used for this cycle (not yet built).
