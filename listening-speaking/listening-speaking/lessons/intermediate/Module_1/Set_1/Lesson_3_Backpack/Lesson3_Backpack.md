**Revision note (2026-09-03, after user review of the student packet):** three changes made to this lesson
following feedback on the first draft. (1) Day 1's Mystery Quote hook was too easy to solve as written - it
named "liters" and "hips" up front, which gave the answer away instead of building suspense, and it was purely

work of the highest, so lower-Level students finished long before higher-Level students. Item counts below are
now calibrated so every task Level takes roughly the same total time to complete, via more (but individually
shorter) items at the lower Levels and fewer (but individually deeper) items at the higher Levels - not by
padding either end with busywork. (3) The Day 4 Closing Transfer Check script is now formatted upside-down in
the student packet (a print-layer fix, not a content change) as a physical deterrent against reading ahead.

## Governing CSV objective (Level 3, Listening/Speaking, Module 1: Describing)

> Listening: Can listen to one short spoken description using a two-slot frame ("It is ___ and
> ___.") and identify the described object among a small set of choices, with a model available
> for reference. Speaking: Can describe a familiar object using the memorized frame with two
> open slots, with a model available rather than being prompted step-by-step.

## Target vocabulary (grounded in the real source, paraphrased to Level 3 register)

capacity (n) - how much a backpack can hold, usually measured in liters
frame (n) - the structure inside or along a backpack that gives it shape and support
pocket (n) - a small, separate section of a backpack for storing things
waterproof (adj) - able to keep water from getting in
comfortable (adj) - causing no pain; easy and pleasant to wear
adjust (v) - to change the fit of something slightly, usually with straps

Context sentences (paraphrased from the source, not quoted verbatim):

4. Most backpacks are treated to be waterproof, but water can still get in through the seams.
5. A backpack that fits your torso length will feel much more comfortable on a long hike.
6. Straps on the shoulders and hips let a hiker adjust the pack until it fits well.

## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Watch & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Mystery Quote, image-anchored:** show a single cropped, close-up photo of just one backpack detail - a
  padded hip belt with its buckle - with no straps, pockets, or full pack shape visible. Do not say what it is.
  In pairs, students describe what they see in 2-3 sentences and guess what larger object it's part of. Give 2
  minutes before taking guesses as a class; write guesses on the board without confirming any yet. Add one more
  spoken clue, still without naming the object: "Whatever this belongs to, most of its weight is supposed to
  rest right here, not on your shoulders." Take final guesses, then reveal the full photo: it's the hip belt of
  a backpack.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're practicing listening for sequence markers - noticing words like
  'first,' 'once,' and 'then' that tell us the order steps happen in." (This is what today's Listening Closing
  Transfer Check will ask students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above.
- **Good to Know:** REI (Recreational Equipment, Inc.) has been an outdoor gear retailer since 1938. It is a
  co-op, which means shoppers who join as members can earn a yearly reward based on what they buy. A liter is a
  metric unit of volume - a backpack's capacity in liters tells you how much space is inside it, not how heavy
  it is.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words above using the context sentences, in-context picture matching (a small
  internal-frame daypack next to a bigger external-frame pack; a close-up of a side pocket; a raincover in the
  rain).
  **Phase 2: Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**
- Watch the full video once, following along with the article's three headings. While watching, students fill
  in a comparison T-chart: columns "Internal Frame" vs. "External Frame," rows "Where's the frame?", "Best for,"
  "Ventilation."
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - Level 2/3: "Listen for the three things the guide says you need to choose about a backpack."
  - Level 4/5: "Listen for why the guide says torso length matters more than height, and notice the order the
    guide gives its advice in."
    **Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**
- Teach sequence-marker listening directly: model on **[Segment 1: Capacity]** into **[Segment 3: Fit]** -
  "There are three main areas where you'll need to make choices," and later, "Once you've chosen the type of
  backpack you want, it's time to fit your pack." Point to "three main areas," "once," and "it's time to" as
  sequence markers.
- Guided practice: replay **[Segment 3: Fit]** and have students identify any sequence markers they hear there,
  stating one to a partner before checking against the group.
  **Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task, per the revision note
  above):
  - **Level 2 (5 items):** Circle the picture that answers: "What does this guide help you choose?" (a backpack
    / a bicycle / a tent). Circle: "This guide is about backpacks for **_." (school / hiking trips / shopping).
    Circle: "An internal frame backpack is _**." (hidden / visible / heavy). Circle: "A side pocket is good for
    holding a \_\_\_." (water bottle / shoes / a phone). According to the guide, where should most of a backpack's
    weight go? (a) shoulders (b) hips (c) feet [Answer: b] - also ask "Do you think most people get this right
    without any help? Circle: yes / no."
  - **Level 3 (4 items):** Choose the best sentence: "It is **_ and _**." (a) a big bag, colorful (b) a bag,
    adjustable (c) a small bag, cheap [Answer: b]. Circle the correct capacity range for a weekend trip: 30-50
    liters / 70+ liters [Answer: 30-50 liters]. Complete: "An external frame pack is **_ and _**." (word bank:
    visible, good for big loads, heavy, cheap). Complete: "A raincover helps keep your backpack \_\_\_." (word
    bank: dry, colorful, light, cheap) [Answer: dry].
  - **Level 4 (3 items):** In 2-3 sentences, name one feature of a good backpack, one comparison between two
    backpack types, and the stated reason for that difference (own connectors). Match each trip length to its
    typical capacity: weekend / multiday / extended-trip -> 30-50 liters / 50-80 liters / 70+ liters. List two
    features of internal-frame packs, one comparison to external-frame packs, and the stated reason for the
    difference (stability on uneven trails vs. carrying bulky, irregular loads).
  - **Level 5 (3 items):** Write a one-paragraph gist summary of the whole guide: what it covers and in what
    order (capacity, then features, then fit). Summarize how the guide is organized and note the order the three
    sections appear in. Identify the evaluative signal in the guide's suggestion that "an REI sales specialist
    can help you do this," and explain what unstated attitude this signals about how easy or hard it is to fit a
    backpack correctly, even though the guide never says "this is hard to get right alone."
- **Board-dependent moment:** students who worked on different Levels briefly compare and add their row(s) to
  the shared T-chart from Phase 2.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** Level 2's task includes a genuine (simplified) interpretive step (the yes/no
  question), not fact-retrieval alone.
  **Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**
- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary), with a built-in pause
  point:**
  > Before Maya's camping trip, she needed a new tent. She looked at the capacity first - she wanted one for two
  > people, not just one. The tent had a strong frame that held its shape even in the wind. Inside, there was a
  > small pocket for her phone and keys. The material was waterproof, so rain wouldn't get through. It also felt
  > comfortable to sleep in, with enough room to stretch out. Before she left, she had to adjust the poles until
  > the tent stood up straight.
  > (88 words; recycles all 6 target words - capacity, frame, pocket, waterproof, comfortable, adjust - on a topic
  > unrelated to backpacks.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, at a natural but clear pace. **Students do not read it themselves first** - it
  is printed in the student packet only after the response space, under a "don't read ahead" instruction
  (printed upside-down in the packet as an added deterrent).
- Students apply sequence-marker listening: retell the order of steps Maya went through, out loud, to a partner
  (e.g., "First she checked the capacity, then she checked the frame and the pocket, and last she adjusted the
  poles"). Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to share. Demonstration, not a self-report vote. After sharing,
  students may read the script to check what they heard.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Town Hall (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- Point to the source's own real sequencing language: "There are three main areas where you'll need to make
  choices," and "Once you've chosen the type of backpack you want, it's time to fit your pack" - real ordering
  language from the actual source, not invented.
- Name the skill: "Today we're practicing sequencing language - showing the order steps happen in with words
  like 'first,' 'next,' 'once,' and 'then.'" This is the exact skill this day's Speaking Closing Transfer Check
  will ask students to reproduce.
- Teach sequencing frames: "First, **_. Next, _**." / "Once you've **_, it's time to _**." / "After that, \_\_\_."
  **Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task):
  - **Level 2 (4 items):** Given three step-pictures (choose size, choose type, check fit): complete "First,
    **_." (choose the size); complete "Then, _**." (choose the type); complete "After that, \_\_\_." (check the
    fit); write 1, 2, 3 next to the three pictures to put the steps in the right order.
  - **Level 3 (3 items):** Two-slot frame with a model card: "First, **_. Then, _**." using backpack vocabulary.
    Second frame: "Once you've **_, it's time to _**." Put the two sentences above together and read them aloud
    to a partner as one connected description.
  - **Level 4 (2 items):** Original sequencing description with a stated reason, own connectors: describe, in
    order, how they would choose a backpack for a weekend trip, and why they'd do one step before another. Now
    describe the same steps for a much longer, multi-week trip - what changes, and why?
  - **Level 5 (2 items):** Extended sequence including one evaluative word signaling which step matters most,
    without directly stating "I think fit matters most" (e.g., calling the fit step "the tricky part"). Ask a
    partner which step they think matters most and why; respond with one sentence agreeing or pushing back.
    **Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**
- Teach linking between words using a real source sentence: "Once you've chosen the type of backpack you want,
  it's time to fit your pack." Mark the linking points (chosen_the, type_of, want_it's, time_to). Teacher models
  live from the actual video audio, since exact natural linking can vary from a written prediction.
- Students mark linking points in a second sentence from the source ("Most backpacks today have an internal
  frame and a comfortable, adjustable fit"), check against the real audio, then apply the linking pattern in
  brief paired rehearsal of their Phase 2 sequencing frames.
  **Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Town Hall (20 min)**
- Sentence stems per task Level, each capped at that Level's own register ceiling.
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 2 does a rehearsed pair-share of their stem with a partner before the
  Town Hall round begins.
- Frame the Town Hall as a class "gear advice panel": a few students play panelists answering questions from a
  classmate planning a trip, rotating through: 1) "If you were going on a weekend trip, what size backpack
  would you choose, and in what order would you make your decisions?" 2) "Which matters more: how many pockets
  a backpack has, or how comfortable it feels? Why?" 3) "What's one step people might forget when choosing a
  backpack?"
- **Outer-circle active task:** every student not currently on the panel keeps a running tally of every
  sequencing phrase they hear a panelist use ("first," "once," "then," "after that"), plus one vocabulary word
  from Day 1's list.
- **Board-dependent moment:** running board record of sequencing phrases actually spoken during the Town Hall,
  feeding the Closing Transfer Check.
  **Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, one new sequence describing how they'd choose something else
  (a phone, a pair of shoes, a laptop) using the Phase 1 sequencing frames. Teacher cold-calls two or three
  pairs. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, did Level 2 get a genuine production role (the rehearsed
  pair-share), not silence?
- Brief whole-class reflection: "What's one thing you'll think about differently next time you buy gear?" No
  Part 2/presentation-project extension is being used for this cycle (not yet built for this lesson type).
