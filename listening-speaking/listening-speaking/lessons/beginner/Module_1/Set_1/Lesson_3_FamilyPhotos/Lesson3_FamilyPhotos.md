## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Watch & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Take a Side:** two options are printed in the packet - **a big family** / **a small family**.
  Every student circles one from their seat, then the teacher counts hands for each and writes the
  two totals on the board. No corners, no signs, no moving (Quality Standards §D11). One or two
  students say their choice aloud in a single word.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're listening for examples. When someone talks about
  their family, they give you examples - my mother, my brother, my sister. Each person they name
  is one example." (This is exactly what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check asks students to
  reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they are about to watch and who Julia and
  Sammy are.
- **Good to Know:**
  - "Starting Out" is a video series from the British Council, an organisation in the United
    Kingdom that teaches English. The people in it are actors.
  - The people in this video speak British English. "Have you got a message?" means the same as
    "Do you have a message?"
- Pre-teach the 6 target words using the context sentences. Teach `mother`/`father` and
  `sister`/`brother` as pairs first, then `uncle` and `nephew` last, since those two are the ones
  the story turns on.

**Phase 2: Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Play the video once, straight through (short enough to leave time for notes inside this phase).
- While watching, students fill a three-column **Name / Who they are to Sammy / Who they are to
  Gavin** grid, one row per person named (Claire, Gavin, Neil). This is the Describing module's
  organizer shaped for a set of relationships rather than a two-category comparison, and it is
  deliberately different from Lesson 1's order-and-name list and Lesson 2's who/when/what grid.
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback (Section 0.6, **Choose what was heard**):
  - **All Levels:** from the printed word list, circle every family word the video actually says
    and cross out the ones it does not. The list holds the 6 target words plus `daughter` and
    `cousin`, which the episode's own support pack teaches but which are **never spoken in this
    clip**, so the last item cannot be reached by elimination (Quality Standards §C4).
  - Level 1 listens for the family words alone; Levels 2-3 also listen for whose relative each
    person is.

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach recognizing examples directly. Model on **[Segment 2: Gavin's birthday]**, where Sammy
  says: "It's his birthday and that's my mother." Play it twice. Point out that Sammy is not
  explaining anything - he is naming one person as an example of his family.
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt: replay **[Segment 4: Who's that?]**, where Sammy says
  "That's Neil. He's my brother." Students say out loud which word is the example (brother).
- Point out the pattern the whole clip follows: every time a new photo appears, someone names one
  more family member.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level and completes it. Item counts are calibrated for balanced
time-on-task: Level 1 gets more, shorter items; Level 3 gets fewer, deeper ones. Per the approved
plan's slot-to-shape table, this slot carries **only the point-or-name shape** in this lesson;
Level 1's remaining items are recognition among printed choices, which is what the CSV's own
Level 1 row describes, and the fix-the-wrong-word shape moves to Day 2 Phase 1.

- **Level 1 (4 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **point-or-name** production: the teacher reads a line from the video;
    the student points to the matching picture in the packet, or says the word.
    Three pictures are printed side by side, captioned only by the relationship word, and the
    three words are the three people the clip's photos actually show: Gavin, Claire, and Neil.
    Image: `Lesson3_FamilyPhotos_Img_Nephew.jpg` - "Young boy" by Pixabay, CC0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Young_boy.jpg.
    Image: `Lesson3_FamilyPhotos_Img_Sister.jpg` - "Smiling woman pink shirt (cropped)" by Joshua McKnight, CC0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Smiling_woman_pink_shirt_(cropped).jpg.
    Image: `Lesson3_FamilyPhotos_Img_Brother.jpg` - "Always smile (Unsplash)" by michael m_minimal, CC0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Always_smile_(Unsplash).jpg.
    Captions: "a nephew" / "a sister" / "a brother". The three are told apart by an obvious age
    and gender difference, so the answer is decidable from what the clip says rather than from
    guessing which stranger looks like someone's relative. None of the three people pictured is
    presented as Gavin, Claire, or Neil. Credits are published in `Set1_Beginner_Image_Credits.md`;
    the packet prints none.
    **Answer note:** the line read is "He's two here," so the matching picture is the young boy,
    captioned "a nephew." 
  - Item 2: Gavin is Sammy's (son / nephew / brother).
    **Answer note:** nephew.
  - Item 3: Neil is Sammy's (father / uncle / brother).
    **Answer note:** brother.
  - Item 4: Claire is Sammy's (sister / mother / daughter).
    **Answer note:** sister.
- **Level 2 (3 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **frame** production: "Neil is Sammy's ___." with a word bank
    (brother, uncle, father, nephew) - two more words than the blank needs.
    **Answer note:** brother.
  - Item 2 is a 3-choice identification: "Who says 'That's my father'?" (Julia / Sammy / Neil).
    **Answer note:** Sammy.
  - Item 3: "Whose son is Gavin?" (Sammy's / Claire's / Neil's).
    **Answer note:** Claire's.
- **Level 3 (2 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **two-slot frame** production, with the model card printed above it
    ("Neil is Sammy's brother."): complete "___ is Gavin's ___." using a word bank (Neil, Claire,
    Sammy, uncle, mother, sister) - more words than the two blanks need. Two different correct
    pairs exist and either is accepted.
    **Answer note:** "Neil is Gavin's uncle." or "Claire is Gavin's mother." Both are stated in
    the clip. A student who writes "Sammy is Gavin's uncle" has reasoned correctly from the
    relationships but the clip never says it; accept it only if they can say which two lines they
    combined to get there.
  - Item 2 is interpretive and answered in writing: "Julia thinks Gavin is Sammy's son. Say why
    she thinks that."
    **Answer note:** because the boy looks like Sammy - Julia says he has the same hair and the
    same eyes, and earlier that he "looks like you." A student who says only "they look the same"
    has the idea; ask which two things Julia names.
- **Board-dependent moment** (Quality Standards §D4): students who worked at different Levels
  report their answers into the shared Name / Who-to-Sammy / Who-to-Gavin grid from Phase 2. The
  complete grid exists only once several Levels have contributed, since no single Level's task
  covers every person: Level 1 supplies Gavin, Neil, and Claire's relationship to Sammy, while
  Level 3 supplies the second column, how Neil and Claire relate to Gavin.
- **Respectful Tiers check** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1's set is not fact-retrieval alone.
  Items 2-4 each require the student to hold a relationship in mind and choose between three
  plausible kinship words - the same interpretive act the higher Levels perform, with the wording
  supplied - and Level 1 students contribute real rows to the shared grid at the board moment.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Practice (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Town Hall (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- **Asking for Clarification**, drawn from the real speakers' own words. This clip is unusually
  rich in it, because the whole scene runs on a misunderstanding. At **[Segment 2]** Julia repeats
  what she has just heard as a question: "That's your mother?" At **[Segment 4]** she asks
  "Who's that?" and then "Your brother?" At **[Segment 6]** she checks the surprising fact by
  repeating it back: "He's your sister's son."
- Name it in plain student-facing language: "When you are not sure you understood, you can say the
  words back as a question. *Your brother?* *That's your mother?* Or you can ask *Who's that?*
  Saying it back is a way of checking."
- Section 0.6 shape for this slot: **fix the wrong word**. Each printed line is a real line from
  the clip with one word changed; the student crosses it out and says or writes the right one.
  This teaches the skill and checks Day 1's vocabulary at the same time.
  - (a) "That's your father?" said about the photo of Sammy's mother.
    **Answer note:** mother.
  - (b) "And Neil is Gavin's brother?"
    **Answer note:** uncle.
  - (c) "He's your sister's father!"
    **Answer note:** son.
- This source needs no deviation flag for the speaking skill: every clarification move above is
  quoted directly from the real dialogue at a cited segment.

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Section 0.6 shape for this slot: **a partner's family member** (the bank's "a partner's object"
shape, applied to a relative rather than a belonging, since this lesson describes people). This is
each Level's single frame production for Day 2. Nothing needs to be present or shown: a relative
is a subject the student already knows about, never a prop (Quality Standards §D8).

Students work in pairs. One names a real person in their family and says how they are related; the
other listens, then produces their Level's form about **that partner's** relative.

- **Level 1:** says the single relationship word for the partner's person ("brother"), with the
  partner having said the whole sentence first as a model.
- **Level 2:** says the one-slot frame about the partner's person - "Ana's ___ is Marco."
- **Level 3:** says the two-slot frame - "___ is Ana's ___." - with the model card still available.

Each student then asks their partner **one** clarification question from Phase 1 to check they
heard it right ("Your brother?"), and the partner confirms or corrects. Switch roles.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Feature: **thought-group pausing** - English speakers break a sentence into short groups and
  pause a little between them, instead of running all the words together.
- Model from the source's own audio. At **[Segment 4]**, Sammy says "That's Neil. // He's my
  brother." - two short groups with a clear break. At **[Segment 6]**, "Yes. // He's my sister's
  son!" does the same. Replay each twice and have students listen for where the break falls.
- Students mark the printed excerpt in their packet with a slash where they hear the break, then
  check against the real audio on a replay.
- Section 0.6 shape for this slot: **say from sound**. One partner says a target word from the
  printed list; the other repeats it, writes it, and they check together against the list, then
  switch. Six rounds, one per target word. Students then say one of their Phase 2 lines twice,
  once run together and once with a real pause between the groups, and the partner says which was
  easier to follow.
- This feature does not repeat Lesson 1's rising and falling intonation or Lesson 2's content-word
  stress.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Town Hall (20 min)**

Run entirely from where students sit; no formations, stations, or anything posted on the walls
(Quality Standards §D11).

- **The question on the floor, rotated through three prompts** rather than one prompt held for the
  whole window (per the approved plan's Phase 3 scaffolding note):
  1. "Who is one person in your family? Say who they are to you."
  2. "Who is the funniest person in your family?"
  3. "Who in your family would you show a photo of first?"
- **How it runs:** the teacher opens the floor; students speak one at a time from their seats,
  hands up, roughly six to eight speakers per prompt. After each speaker, **one** other student
  asks that speaker a clarification question from Phase 1 ("Your uncle?"), and the speaker
  confirms or corrects. That exchange is the point of the protocol - it puts the day's speaking
  skill into every single turn rather than only into the prepared task.
- **Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1 rehearses their one sentence
  with their Phase 2 partner before the floor opens, so their first public turn is a repetition
  rather than an on-demand production; they may also take the clarification-question role, which
  is a single fixed phrase. Foundation Support students say the relationship word alone, or ask
  the one-word clarification question ("Brother?").
- **Listener task:** every student not currently speaking writes the speaker's name and the
  relationship word into their own Phase 2 grid, so nobody is idle.
- **Board-dependent moment:** the teacher keeps a running board record of every relationship word
  as it is actually spoken, which feeds Phase 5.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, **one new** clarification question on a fresh
  prompt unrelated to families and unrelated to the clip: **one partner says where they went at
  the weekend, and the other checks it by saying it back as a question** ("The market?"). Two or
  three pairs cold-called. Demonstration, not self-report (Quality Standards §D3).
- **Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** Level 1 had a genuine interpretive task on Day 1
  (three kinship choices, each a real judgment, plus a real contribution to the shared grid) and a
  genuine production role on Day 2 (a rehearsed sentence on the floor and a real clarification
  exchange with a partner), not fact-retrieval and silent participation.
- Brief whole-class reflection: the teacher reads three or four relationship words off the board
  record and the class says, for each, whether it is a man or a woman in the family.
