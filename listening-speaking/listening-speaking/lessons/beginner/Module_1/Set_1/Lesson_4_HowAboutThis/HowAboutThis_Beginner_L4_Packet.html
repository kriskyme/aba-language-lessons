Unit 4B: How About This?

Listening & Speaking · Describing Beginner 1.1.4.0

Objective: I can compare two things and say which one is better.

Learn the Phrase
Comparing Two Things

In this video, Genie compares things all the time. He says the dress is "too small" and the shirt is "too large." He says jeans are "too casual" and asks for "something more formal." At the end he says, "take off the hat. That's better."

Say too plus the problem, or more plus the word. When one is better than the other, say That's better.

  • • "It is too small."
  • • "How about something more formal?"
  • • "That's better."

Sound Focus

When you compare two things, say the word that is different louder and longer. Listen to these two lines, then underline the one word that changes.

It is too small.  ·  This shirt is too large.

Now say your own pair from the task above two times. The first time, say it flat. The second time, make the different word strong. Ask your partner which one made it clearer.

Talk About Your Things

Think of one thing you own. You do not need it with you. Tell your partner about it from what you know. Your teacher will tell you when to turn to a new partner.

1. Tell your partner about something you own. Is it big or small?

2. Tell your partner about something you wear. Is it casual or formal?

3. Which of your two things do you like more? Say why, using "more" or "better."

After you hear your new partner, say one sentence about their thing. Use their name: "Ana's bag is too small."

When you are not speaking, write your partner's thing and the word they used in your Listening Notes table.
