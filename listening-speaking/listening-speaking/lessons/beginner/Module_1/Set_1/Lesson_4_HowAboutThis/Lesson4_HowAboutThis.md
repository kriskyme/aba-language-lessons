## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Watch & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Mystery Quote:** one real line from the clip is printed in the packet before students know
  anything about it: **"No! Jeans and a t-shirt are too casual."** From their seats, students
  circle one of two printed guesses about where this is said - *at a party* / *before going out* -
  and the teacher counts hands and writes the two totals on the board. The guess is checked at the
  Phase 4 board moment, not now. No signs, no corners, no moving (Quality Standards §D11).
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're guessing before we know. When someone says 'How about
  this?', stop and guess: yes or no? Then listen and find out if you were right." (This is exactly
  what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check asks students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they are about to watch and who Anna and
  Genie are.
- **Good to Know:**
  - "Let's Learn English" is a VOA Learning English video series. It follows Anna, who moves to
    Washington, D.C. Genie is a magic character who appears to help her.
  - A theater is a place where people watch a play or a show. People often wear nicer clothes to
    the theater than they wear at home.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words using the context sentences. Teach `small`, `medium`, and `large`
  together as one scale, in order, then `casual` and `formal` as a pair.

**Phase 2: Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Play the video once, straight through (about 5 minutes; see the runtime flag in the self-check).
- While watching, students fill a three-column **What Anna tries / Yes or no? / Why** grid - the
  Describing module's organizer shaped for a run of judged items rather than a two-category
  comparison, and deliberately different from Lesson 1's order-and-name list, Lesson 2's
  who/when/what grid, and Lesson 3's relationship grid. One row per outfit: jeans and a t-shirt,
  the dress, the green shirt and skirt, the suit.
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback (Section 0.6, **Choose what was heard**):
  - **All Levels:** from the printed word list, circle every size or style word the video actually
    says and cross out the ones it does not. The list holds the 6 target words plus `tight` and
    `cheap`, which the clip never says, so the last item cannot be reached by elimination (Quality
    Standards §C4).
  - Level 1 listens for the size words alone; Levels 2-3 also listen for the reason each outfit is
    rejected.

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach predicting-then-confirming directly. Model on **[Segment 3: Too casual]**: play up to
  Anna's "How about jeans and a t-shirt?", **pause**, and ask the class to guess what Genie will
  say. Take two or three guesses aloud. Then play Genie's answer and check.
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt: replay **[Segment 4: Too small]** the same way. Pause
  after "this dress is beautiful" and ask whether Anna will keep it. Then play "But it's not the
  right size. It's too small." and check.
- Point out the pattern the whole clip follows: an idea is offered, then judged, and the reason is
  almost always about size or about how formal it is.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level and completes it. Item counts are calibrated for balanced
time-on-task: Level 1 gets more, shorter items; Level 3 gets fewer, deeper ones. Per the approved
plan's slot-to-shape table, this slot carries **point-or-name plus fix-the-wrong-word** in this
lesson.

- **Level 1 (4 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **point-or-name** production: the teacher reads a line from the video;
    the student points to the matching picture in the packet, or says the word. Three pictures are
    printed side by side, captioned only by the garment name. The three are told apart by their
    **shape**, which survives a grayscale print (Style Guide §A.2); no item here turns on colour.
    Image: `Lesson4_HowAboutThis_Img_Dress.jpg` - "Summer Dress LACMA M.2007.211.740 (1 of 5)", Los Angeles County Museum of Art, public domain, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Summer_Dress_LACMA_M.2007.211.740_(1_of_5).jpg.
    Image: `Lesson4_HowAboutThis_Img_Jacket.jpg` - "Blazer (AM 2005.105.1)", Auckland War Memorial Museum, CC BY 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blazer_(AM_2005.105.1).jpg.
    Image: `Lesson4_HowAboutThis_Img_Hat.jpg` - "Hat (AM 1982.127-2)", Auckland War Memorial Museum, CC BY 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hat_(AM_1982.127-2).jpg.
    All three were checked in grayscale before use: a full-length dress silhouette, a jacket on a
    hanger, and a brimmed hat stay distinguishable by outline alone once colour is gone.
    Credits are published in `Set1_Beginner_Image_Credits.md`; the packet prints none.
    The packet prints no credit.
    **Answer note:** the line read is "How about a hat?", so the matching picture is the hat.
  - Items 2-4 are **fix-the-wrong-word** items: each prints a line from the video with one word
    changed. The student crosses out the wrong word and writes or says the right one.
    (a) "It's not the right *colour*." (b) "Jeans and a t-shirt are too *formal*." (c) "This green
    shirt is too *small*."
    **Answer note:** (a) size; (b) casual; (c) large.
- **Level 2 (3 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **frame** production: "The dress is too ___." with a word bank (small,
    large, medium, formal) - two more words than the blank needs.
    **Answer note:** small.
  - Item 2 is a 3-choice identification: "What size is right for Anna?" (small / medium / large).
    **Answer note:** medium.
  - Item 3 is a **fix-the-wrong-word** item on a longer line: "How about something more *casual*?"
    **Answer note:** formal.
- **Level 3 (2 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **two-slot frame** production, with the model card printed above it
    ("The dress is too small, and it is not the right size."): complete "The shirt is too ___, and
    it is not ___." using a word bank (large, small, medium, formal, casual, right) - more words
    than the two blanks need.
    **Answer note:** "The shirt is too large, and it is not medium." Accept "not the right size"
    for the second blank, which is the clip's own phrase.
  - Item 2 is interpretive and answered in writing: "Genie says no to almost everything Anna tries.
    Say the two different reasons Genie gives."
    **Answer note:** one reason is about size (too small, too large), the other is about how formal
    the clothes are (too casual, needs something more formal). A student who gives only one has
    half the pattern and should be asked for the other.
- **Board-dependent moment** (Quality Standards §D4): students who worked at different Levels
  report into the shared What-Anna-tries grid from Phase 2, and the class returns to the Phase 1
  Mystery Quote board tally to check which guess was right. The complete grid exists only once
  several Levels have contributed, since no single Level's task covers all four outfits.
- **Respectful Tiers check** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1's set is not fact-retrieval alone. The
  three fix-the-wrong-word items each require holding the real line in mind and judging which
  single word is wrong, which is a genuine simplified interpretive act, and Level 1 students
  contribute real rows to the shared grid at the board moment.

**Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**

- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary):**
  > My brother needs a bag for school. The first bag is too small. His books do not fit. The next
  > bag is too large. It is bigger than he is! The shop has one more bag. It is medium, and it is
  > not too heavy. It is casual, not formal, but that is fine for school. He is happy with it.
  > (61 words; recycles size-scale words small, large, medium and the pair casual, formal - all 6
  > target words - on a topic unrelated to Anna's evening out.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, slowly and clearly, **pausing before the last sentence** so
  students can predict whether the brother buys the third bag. **Students do not read it themselves
  first.** It is printed in the student packet only after their response space, under a "don't read
  ahead" instruction, and printed upside-down as an added deterrent.
- Students apply the Phase 1 strategy out loud to a partner: say their guess, then say whether they
  were right once the teacher finishes. Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs. Demonstration, not
  self-report (Quality Standards §D2). After sharing, students may read the script to check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Practice (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Rotating Partners (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- **Making Comparisons**, drawn from the real speakers' own words. At **[Segment 3]** Genie
  compares two levels of dressiness: "Jeans and a t-shirt are too casual. How about something more
  formal?" At **[Segment 4]** and **[Segment 5]** the comparison is size: "It's too small." / "This
  green shirt is too large." At **[Segment 5]** Genie names the middle of the scale: "the right
  size for you is medium." At **[Segment 7]** he compares two states of the same outfit: "take off
  the hat. That's better."
- Name it in plain student-facing language: "To compare, say **too** plus the problem, or **more**
  plus the word. *Too small. Too casual. More formal.* And when one is better than the other, say
  *That's better.*"
- This source needs no deviation flag for the speaking skill: every comparison above is quoted
  directly from the real dialogue at a cited segment. It is the richest comparison source in the
  Set.

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Section 0.6 shape for this slot: **better of two**. Two printed lines about the clip's clothes sit
side by side; the student circles the one that fits and says the word that makes it fit. Because
the two lines are printed on the page and differ by a size or style word, the item is decidable
from the page in grayscale.

- **Level 1:** two lines about the dress - "The dress is too small." / "The dress is too large." -
  circle one and say the word.
- **Level 2:** two lines about the green shirt, differing in the same way, plus one about the
  jeans differing in casual/formal; circle one of each pair and say the word that makes it fit.
- **Level 3:** three pairs, including one where **both** lines are true of different garments, so
  the student must say which garment each belongs to as well as circling.

Each Level's frame production for Day 2 happens in Phase 4, about the student's own object.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Feature: **contrastive stress** - when you compare two things, you say the word that is different
  louder and longer than the rest.
- Model from the source's own audio. Replay **[Segment 4]** and **[Segment 5]** back to back: "It's
  too SMALL." then "This green shirt is too LARGE." The sentence frame barely changes; the stressed
  word carries the whole comparison. Replay **[Segment 7]**'s "That's BETTER." for the same effect.
- Students mark the printed pair in their packet, underlining the one word that changes between the
  two lines, then check against the real audio on a replay.
- Paired rehearsal: each student says their Phase 2 pair twice, once flat and once with the
  contrasting word stressed, and the partner says which one made the difference clearer.
- This feature does not repeat Lesson 1's rising and falling intonation, Lesson 2's content-word
  stress, or Lesson 3's thought-group pausing. It is the fourth distinct feature in four lessons.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Rotating Partners (20 min)**

Run entirely from where students sit; no formations, stations, or anything posted on the walls
(Quality Standards §D11). "Rotating partners" here means students change partner by turning to a
different neighbour on the teacher's call, not by moving seats.

- **Section 0.6 shape, a partner's object.** This is the one lesson in the Set whose subject really
  is an object, so Quality Standards §D8's first route applies directly: **the student's own
  belonging**. Each student picks one thing they own - a bag, a coat, a pair of shoes - and
  describes it from what they know of it. **Nothing is taken out, held up, or pointed at**: the
  object is a subject, not a prop. Because the student answers from their own knowledge and not
  from a printed picture, **a colour word is allowed here**, and this is the only place in the
  lesson where one is (Style Guide §A.2).
- **Three rounds, a new partner each time, one prompt per round** rather than one prompt held for
  the whole window (per the approved plan's Phase 3 scaffolding note):
  1. "Tell your partner about something you own. Is it big or small?"
  2. "Tell your partner about something you wear. Is it casual or formal?"
  3. "Which of your two things do you like more? Say why, using *more* or *better*."
- **Each Level's one frame production for Day 2** happens here, about the **partner's** object,
  after hearing them:
  - **Level 1:** says the single size or style word for the partner's object ("small"), with the
    partner having said the whole sentence first as a model.
  - **Level 2:** says the one-slot frame - "Ana's bag is too ___."
  - **Level 3:** says the two-slot frame - "Ana's bag is too ___, and it is not ___."
- **Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1 rehearses their one line with
  their first partner before the rounds begin, so their first turn with a new partner is a
  repetition rather than an on-demand production. Foundation Support students say the size word
  alone, or point to it on their own printed word list.
- **Listener task:** in each round, the student not currently speaking writes the partner's object
  and the size or style word into their own Phase 2 grid, so nobody is idle.
- **Board-dependent moment:** the teacher keeps a running board record of every comparison as it is
  actually spoken, which feeds Phase 5.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, **one new** comparison on a fresh prompt unrelated
  to clothes and unrelated to the clip: **"Compare two meals you know. Say which is better, and
  why."** Two or three pairs cold-called. Demonstration, not self-report (Quality Standards §D3).
- **Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** Level 1 had a genuine interpretive task on Day 1
  (three fix-the-wrong-word items, each a real judgment against the remembered line, plus a real
  contribution to the shared grid) and a genuine production role on Day 2 (a rehearsed line said to
  three different partners, and a word about each partner's object), not fact-retrieval and silent
  participation.
- Brief whole-class reflection: the teacher reads three or four comparisons off the board record
  and the class says, for each, whether it compares **size** or **style**.
- **This completes Set 1** for the Beginner Band. The Set's assessment is generated next, by
  `Generate_Assessment_Prompt_v2.1.md`.
