## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Watch & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **Visual Inquiry:** hold up (or show photos of) a real bag, pen, book, pillow, map, and lamp, one
  at a time. Ask the class: "What is it?" Students answer with whatever word they already know, in
  any language, then the teacher supplies/confirms the English word.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're listening for one word - the name of the thing someone
  is talking about." (This is what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check will ask students to
  reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they're about to watch and who Anna, Pete,
  and Marsha are.
- **Good to Know:**
  - "Let's Learn English" is a VOA Learning English video series. It follows two friends, Anna and
    Pete, who live in Washington, D.C.
  - Washington, D.C. is a city in the United States. It is the country's capital city.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words above using the context sentences and real objects/pictures (hold up
  a real book while saying "It is a book," etc.).

**Phase 2: Watch & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Watch the full video once (about 5 minutes, leaving time for notes within this phase).
- While watching, students fill in a simple order-and-name organizer (Describing module's
  organizer, adapted for a Beginner object-naming scene rather than a two-category comparison):
  a numbered list, "What did Anna find?", where students write or circle the object name in the
  order it appears (book, pillow, map, lamp, pen).
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback:
  - Level 1: "Listen for the name of each thing. Point to your picture when you hear it."
  - Level 2/3: "Listen for what each thing is, and what it is like (big, small)."

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach word-recognition listening directly: model on **[Segment 2: "It is a book"]** - "You don't
  need every word. Just listen for the name of the thing: book." Point to the real book while
  playing this segment.
- Guided practice: replay **[Segment 4: A map]** and **[Segment 5: A lamp]**; students point to the
  matching picture each time, then say the word out loud together as a class.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level and completes it (item counts calibrated for balanced
time-on-task: Level 1 gets more, shorter items; Level 3 gets fewer, deeper items):

- **Level 1 (6 items):** Circle the picture that matches the word: "book" (book / shoe / car).
  Circle: "pen" (pen / spoon / key). Circle: "map" (map / plate / hat). Circle: "lamp" (lamp / chair
  / cup). Circle: "pillow" (pillow / ball / box). Circle: "bag" (bag / bike / bird).
- **Level 2 (4 items):** Complete: "It is a ___." (book / map / lamp) for the first thing Pete
  finds. Complete: "It is a ___." (pillow / bag / pen) for the next thing Pete finds. Complete:
  "It is a ___." using a word bank (map, lamp, pillow, book) for what Marsha finds. Circle: Anna is
  looking for her ___. (pen / book / map).
- **Level 3 (3 items):** With a model card showing "It is big and heavy. = the book," complete:
  "It is ___ and ___." for the book, using a word bank (big, small, heavy, light). Choose the best
  two-slot sentence for the map: (a) "It is small and red" (b) "It is big and full of places"
  (c) "It is short and green" [correct: b]. Using a word bank (bright, tall / short, small),
  complete the two-slot frame for the lamp: "It is ___ and ___."
- **Board-dependent moment:** students who worked on different Levels briefly compare and add their
  item(s) to the shared order-and-name organizer from Phase 2, so the completed list of 5 objects
  exists only once multiple Levels have contributed.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** Level 1's task includes six genuine word-to-picture matches (not one
  isolated drill item), giving every student in that tier repeated practice with the actual
  interpretive act (matching a heard word to its meaning), not passive fact-retrieval.

**Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**

- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary):**
  > This is my bag. In my bag, I have a book. I have a pen. I have a small lamp too. My bag does not
  > have a pillow or a map. My bag is heavy!
  > (32 words; recycles bag, book, pen, lamp, pillow, map - all 6 of the lesson's target words - on
  > a topic unrelated to Anna's bag.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, slowly and clearly. **Students do not read it themselves first** -
  it is printed in the student packet only after their response space, under a "don't read ahead"
  instruction, and printed upside-down as an added deterrent.
- Students apply word-recognition listening: point to (or say) one word they heard from the script,
  out loud to a partner. Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to share. Demonstration, not a
  self-report vote. After sharing, students may read the script to check what they heard.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Task (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Fishbowl (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- **Speaking Skill grounded in the source's own context, not a directly quoted exchange (flagged
  per Section 0's Day 2 Phase 1 rule):** the confirmed dialogue fragments do not include an explicit
  "for example" phrase, but the source's whole scene is built around giving repeated examples of
  "things in a bag" - each character names one more thing as an example. Ground the Giving Examples
  spotlight in that real situational pattern: "Today we're practicing giving examples - naming one
  thing when someone asks, 'like what?'"
- Model: "I have things in my bag. Like what? A book. A pen." Point out that each new object Anna's
  friends name in the video is itself one more example of "a thing in the bag."
- Teach the frame: "Like what? A ___." / "It is a ___."
  **Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**
- Each student picks one task Level (item counts calibrated for balanced time-on-task):
  - **Level 1 (5 items):** Shown a picture (or real object), say the single word that names it, when
    prompted: "What is it?" - book, pen, map, lamp, pillow.
  - **Level 2 (4 items):** Shown a picture, complete and say aloud: "It is a ___." for book, then
    pen, then map, then lamp.
  - **Level 3 (3 items):** With a model card ("It is big and heavy. = the book."), complete and say
    aloud: "It is ___ and ___." for the pillow (soft, small), the map (big, full of places), and the
    lamp (tall, bright).
    **Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**
- Teach rising vs. falling intonation: questions rise ("What is it?" pitch goes up at the end),
  statements fall ("It is a book." pitch goes down at the end). Model live using the Phase 2 words.
- Students practice in pairs: one asks "What is it?" (rising) while pointing to a picture, the other
  answers "It is a ___." (falling), then switch roles.
  **Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Fishbowl (20 min)**
- **Differentiated participation:** Level 1 does a rehearsed pair-share of one object's name with a
  partner before the Fishbowl round begins, so their first public turn is already a repetition, not
  an on-demand live one.
- Inner circle takes turns: one student holds up a picture/real object and asks "What is it?"; the
  next names it. Rotate through 2-3 different small sets of objects (not one static set for the
  whole 20 minutes), per the Phase 3 scaffolding note in the approved plan.
- **Outer-circle active task:** every outer-circle student keeps a running tally of every object
  name they hear correctly answered.
- **Board-dependent moment:** running board record of every object named during the Fishbowl,
  feeding the Closing Transfer Check.
  **Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**
- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, one new object label (a classroom object other than
  the 6 target words) when a partner asks "What is it?" Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to
  share. Demonstration, not self-report.
- **Respectful Tiers check:** across the whole cycle, Level 1 got a genuine interpretive step on Day
  1 (six real word-to-picture matches, not one token item) and a real production role on Day 2 (the
  rehearsed pair-share feeding into the Fishbowl, plus the Closing Transfer Check), not just
  fact-retrieval and silent participation.
- Brief reflection: "What is one new word you learned today?" No Part 2/presentation-project
  extension is being used for this cycle (not yet built for this lesson type).
