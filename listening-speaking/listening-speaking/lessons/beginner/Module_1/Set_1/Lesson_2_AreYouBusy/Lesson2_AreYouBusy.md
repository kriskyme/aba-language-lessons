## DAY 1: Unit A - Listening

```
|--Hook, Good to Know & Vocab (20)--|--Listen & Notes (15)--|--Skill Instr. (10)--|--Task (20)--|--Transfer Check (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Hook, Good to Know & Vocabulary (20 min)**

- **K-W-L Chart:** students fill the first two columns of a three-column chart in their packet,
  from their seats, about a workday: **K** - "One thing I know people do at work" (any language
  accepted, then the teacher supplies the English). **W** - "One thing I want to know about Anna's
  workday." The **L** column stays empty until Phase 4's board-dependent moment.
- **Listening Skill Spotlight:** "Today we're listening for time words. A time word tells you *when*
  something happens - at 10 a.m., every day, this afternoon. Time words put a day in order." (This
  is exactly what today's Listening Closing Transfer Check asks students to reproduce.)
- Present the citation block above so students know what they are about to hear and who Anna and
  her co-workers are.
- **Good to Know:**
  - "Let's Learn English" is a VOA Learning English video series. It follows Anna, who moves to
    Washington, D.C. and starts a job at a radio station.
  - A radio station makes shows that people listen to. Some shows are in the morning and some are
    in the evening.
- Pre-teach the 6 target words above using the context sentences. Teach `morning`, `afternoon`, and
  `evening` as a set, in order, so the day's shape is established before the audio plays.

**Phase 2: Listen & Listening Notes (15 min)**

- Play the Conversation audio once, straight through (about 2 minutes, leaving time for notes
  inside this phase).
- While listening, students fill a three-column **Who / When / What they do** grid (the Describing
  module's organizer, shaped here for a routine rather than a two-category comparison): one row per
  co-worker, filling in the time word and the action word they hear.
- Differentiated listening purpose, set before playback (Section 0.6, **Choose what was heard**):
  - **All Levels:** from the printed target-word list, circle every word the audio actually says
    and cross out the ones it does not. The list holds the 6 target words plus two words the audio
    never says (`night`, `driving`), so the last item cannot be reached by elimination
    (Quality Standards §C4).
  - Level 1 additionally listens for the action words only; Levels 2-3 listen for the time word
    that goes with each action.

**Phase 3: Listening Skill Instruction (10 min)**

- Teach listening for time words directly. Model on **[Segment 2: Anne at 10 a.m.]**, where Anne
  says: "At 10 a.m. I am writing. Every day I do my morning show." Play it twice. First time,
  students raise a hand the moment they hear a time word; second time, the teacher names them aloud
  - "at 10 a.m.", "every day", "morning".
- Guided practice on a second short excerpt: replay **[Segment 4: Amelia this afternoon]**, where
  Amelia says "Come by this afternoon." Students say the time word out loud together as a class.
- Point out the pattern the whole conversation follows: every co-worker answers with *what* they are
  doing and *when*.

**Phase 4: Differentiated Listening Task, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Each student picks one task Level and completes it. Item counts are calibrated for balanced
time-on-task: Level 1 gets more, shorter items; Level 3 gets fewer, deeper ones.

- **Level 1 (4 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **point-or-name** production: the teacher reads a line from the audio;
    the student points to the matching picture in the packet, or says the action word.
    Three pictures are printed side by side, each showing something the conversation actually
    mentions: someone writing, someone recording a radio show, and a party.
    Image: `Lesson2_AreYouBusy_Img_Writing.jpg` - "Person writing in a notebook while sitting at a desk" by Shixart1985, CC BY 2.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Person_writing_in_a_notebook_while_sitting_at_a_desk.jpg.
    Image: `Lesson2_AreYouBusy_Img_Recording.jpg` - "Jim O'Hara at KSTT" by Jgohara, CC BY-SA 4.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jim_O%27Hara_at_KSTT.jpg.
    Image: `Lesson2_AreYouBusy_Img_Party.jpg` - "Johns Inc Birthday party" by Tudokin, CC BY-SA 3.0, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Johns_Inc_Birthday_party.jpg.
    Credits are published in `Set1_Beginner_Image_Credits.md`; the packet prints none.
    **Answer note:** the line read is Anne's, so the matching picture is the person writing.
  - Items 2-4 are **fix-the-wrong-word** items: each prints a line from the audio with one word
    changed. The student crosses out the wrong word and writes or says the right one.
    (a) "At 10 a.m. I am *driving*." (b) "Every day I do my *evening* show." (c) "Come by this
    *morning*."
    **Answer note:** (a) writing; (b) morning; (c) afternoon.
- **Level 2 (3 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **frame** production: "In the morning, Anne is ___." with a word bank
    (writing, recording, driving, sleeping) - two more words than the blank needs.
    **Answer note:** writing.
  - Item 2 is a 3-choice identification: "Who is recording an evening show?" (Anne / Jonathan /
    Amelia).
    **Answer note:** Jonathan.
  - Item 3 is a **fix-the-wrong-word** item on a longer line: "When the studio light is on, I am
    recording my *morning* show."
    **Answer note:** evening.
- **Level 3 (2 items).**
  - Item 1 is the day's one **two-slot frame** production, with the model card printed above it
    ("At 10 a.m., Anne is writing."): complete "At ___, Jonathan is ___." using a word bank
    (evening, morning, recording, writing, walking) - two more words than the two blanks need.
    **Answer note:** evening / recording. Accept "night" only if the student can say which line of
    the audio it came from; the audio does not say it.
  - Item 2 is interpretive and answered in writing: "Anna asks four people the same question and
    gets four different answers. Say what all four answers have in common."
    **Answer note:** every answer names both what the person is doing and when they do it. A
    student who names only one half has half the pattern and should be asked for the other.
- **Board-dependent moment** (Quality Standards §D4): students who worked at different Levels report
  their answers into the shared Who / When / What grid from Phase 2 and into the **L** column of the
  K-W-L chart. The complete grid - all four co-workers, each with a time and an action - exists only
  once several Levels have contributed, since no single Level's task covers all four.
- **Respectful Tiers check** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1's set is not fact-retrieval alone. The
  three fix-the-wrong-word items each require the student to hold the real line in mind and judge
  which single word is wrong, which is a genuine simplified interpretive act, and Level 1 students
  contribute real rows to the shared grid at the board moment alongside every other Level.

**Phase 5: Listening Closing Transfer Check (10 min)**

- **Teacher-read-aloud script (invented, unrelated topic, recycling target vocabulary):**
  > My sister is a baker. She is very busy. At 4 a.m. she is making bread. Every day she does this
  > in the morning. In the afternoon she is writing a list of new cakes. In the evening she is
  > recording a short video for the shop. At 8 p.m. she is not busy. She is happy.
  > (63 words; recycles busy, morning, afternoon, evening, writing, recording - all 6 target words -
  > on a topic unrelated to Anna's radio station.)
- Teacher reads this aloud once, slowly and clearly. **Students do not read it themselves first.**
  It is printed in the student packet only after their response space, under a "don't read ahead"
  instruction, and printed upside-down as an added deterrent.
- Students apply the Phase 1 strategy out loud to a partner: say one time word they heard, and what
  the baker was doing then. Teacher cold-calls two or three pairs. Demonstration, not self-report
  (Quality Standards §D2). After sharing, students may read the script to check.

## DAY 2: Unit B - Speaking

```
|--Skill Spotlight (15)--|--Practice (20)--|--Pronunciation (10)--|--Jigsaw (20)--|--Transfer Check & Wrap (10)--|
```

**Phase 1: Speaking Skill Spotlight (15 min)**

- **Sequencing Language**, drawn from the real speakers' own words at **[Segment 2: Anne at
  10 a.m.]**: Anne says "At 10 a.m. I am writing. Every day I do my morning show." Jonathan's line
  at **[Segment 3]** does the same thing with a condition instead of a clock: "When the studio light
  is on, I am recording my evening show." Amelia's "Come by this afternoon" and Caty's "My office.
  5:00 p.m." complete the pattern.
- Name it in plain student-facing language: "When you say what you do, put the time first. *At ten
  o'clock, I am writing.* *In the evening, I am recording.* The time word tells your listener when."
- This source needs no deviation flag for the speaking skill: the sequencing language is quoted
  directly from what the real speakers say, at cited segments.

**Phase 2: Differentiated Speaking Practice, Choose One Level (20 min)**

Section 0.6 shape for this slot: **say from sound**. Students work in pairs throughout.

- **All Levels, the shared shape:** one partner says a target word from the printed list; the other
  repeats it, then writes it in the blank next to the matching time of day, then they check together
  against the list and switch roles. Six rounds, one per target word.
- **Level 1:** the partner says a single action word (writing, recording); the student repeats it
  and writes it.
- **Level 2:** the partner says a whole short line ("In the morning, Anne is writing"); the student
  repeats it and writes only the action word.
- **Level 3:** the partner says a two-part line ("At 10 a.m., Anne is writing"); the student repeats
  it and writes both the time word and the action word.

No Level produces its own frame in this phase; each Level's one frame production for Day 2 happens
in Phase 4, about a partner's real day.

**Phase 3: Pronunciation Mini-Focus (10 min)**

- Feature: **content-word stress** - in English, the words that carry the meaning are said louder
  and longer than the small words around them.
- Model from the source's own audio, **[Segment 2]**: replay Anne's line and have students listen
  for which words are strongest. In "At TEN a.m. I am WRIting," `ten` and `writing` carry the
  stress; `at`, `I`, and `am` are quick and quiet. Replay **[Segment 3]** for the same effect on
  `studio`, `light`, `recording`, `evening`.
- Students mark the printed excerpt in their packet, underlining the two words they think are
  strongest, then check against the real audio on a replay.
- Paired rehearsal: each student says their own Phase 2 line twice, once flat and once with the time
  word and the action word stressed, and the partner says which one was easier to follow.

**Phase 4: Structured Oral Output - Jigsaw (20 min)**

Run entirely from where students sit; no formations, stations, or anything posted on the walls
(Quality Standards §D11).

- **Setup:** each pair is assigned one of three time-of-day prompts, so no two neighbouring pairs
  hold the same one. Every student becomes the class's expert on their own time of day.
  1. "In the morning, I am ___."
  2. "In the afternoon, I am ___."
  3. "In the evening, I am ___."
- **Round 1 (expert pairs, 8 min):** partners tell each other what they really do at their assigned
  time of day, and help each other put it into the frame. This is where each student rehearses the
  line they will say in Round 2.
- **Round 2 (mixed reporting, 12 min):** pairs re-form so that each new pair holds two different
  times of day, and each student says their line to their new partner. The teacher then calls on
  six to eight students in turn to say their line to the room, from their seat, rotating through all
  three prompts rather than running one prompt for the whole window (per the approved plan's Phase 3
  scaffolding note).
- **Section 0.6 shape, a partner's day** (the bank's "a partner's object" shape, applied to a
  partner's routine rather than a belonging, since this lesson's frame describes actions): after
  hearing their new partner's line, each student says **one** sentence about that partner using the
  frame - "In the evening, Mei is studying." This is each Level's single frame production for Day 2:
  - **Level 1:** says the action word alone for their partner ("studying"), with the frame said by
    the partner first as a model.
  - **Level 2:** says the one-slot frame ("In the evening, Mei is ___.").
  - **Level 3:** says the two-slot frame ("At seven o'clock, Mei is ___.").
- **Differentiated participation** (Quality Standards §B): Level 1 rehearses their one line with
  their Round 1 partner before any public turn, so their first turn in front of the room is a
  repetition rather than an on-demand production. Foundation Support students point to the time of
  day on their own printed chart and say the action word alone.
- **Listener task:** every student not currently speaking writes the speaker's time word and action
  word into their own Who / When / What grid, so nobody is idle during the reporting round.
- **Board-dependent moment:** the teacher keeps a running board record of every time-and-action pair
  as it is actually spoken, which feeds Phase 5.

**Phase 5: Speaking Closing Transfer Check & Wrap (10 min)**

- Every student produces, in pairs and out loud, **one new** time-and-action sentence on a fresh
  prompt unrelated to school and unrelated to the clip: **"Say one thing you do on Saturday, and
  when you do it."** Two or three pairs cold-called. Demonstration, not self-report
  (Quality Standards §D3).
- **Respectful Tiers check across the cycle:** Level 1 had a genuine interpretive task on Day 1
  (three fix-the-wrong-word items, each requiring a real judgment against the remembered line, plus
  a real contribution to the shared grid) and a genuine production role on Day 2 (a rehearsed line
  said to the room, and a sentence about a partner's real day), not fact-retrieval and silent
  participation.
- Brief whole-class reflection: the teacher reads three or four time-and-action pairs off the board
  record and the class says whether each one is morning, afternoon, or evening.
