## Plan

**Band Lesson #:** Intermediate 1 (matches the existing `Rotation_Log_Intermediate.md` row; this document does
not renumber or re-log it).

**Scenario:** Every student describes a phone case — a real, familiar object nearly every student owns or can
picture clearly, chosen (over the lesson's original water-bottle Scenario) for offering more natural angles for
comparison (color, thickness, durability, grip, price) and implied attitude (how worn it is, why it's never
replaced). Level 2-3 students work from one provided picture; Level 4-5 students describe their own actual case.
Writable at every task Level in the Band: a single word into a frame at Level 2-3, up through an extended,
detail-driven paragraph at Level 5 — no essay-regime Level to stretch toward.

**Grammar Focus A/B pairing:** Focus A (comparative adjectives, -er/more, plus "because" and its richer
alternatives since/as/given that) is Section 0.4's Level 4 row — Intermediate's own **native** Paragraph
Composition floor, not an accommodation on loan from another Band, so no row-selection correction was ever needed
here (unlike Advanced 1 — see that plan's note). Focus B (simple vs. compound sentences, and/but/so) is Level 4's
paired sentence-variety row, chosen because a compound sentence lets the comparison and its consequence sit side
by side, matching the Scenario directly. Level 5 gets an extension (hedging language, one concession
subordinator — "although") layered on top of Focus A/B rather than a separate row.

**Essay Focus A/B direction:** N/A. Intermediate's task Levels (2-5) never reach Essay Composition (Levels 6-8).

**Real-world writing form:** Descriptive paragraph (Levels 4-5); a fixed one/two-slot description frame
(Levels 2-3).

**Leveled Mentor Ladder direction:**
- **Level 2:** one describing word into the fixed frame ("It is ___."), drawn from the Scenario's object.
- **Level 3:** two describing words into the two-slot frame ("It is ___ and ___.").
- **Level 4:** a short original paragraph naming one comparison and one explicit "because" reason, built on a
  simple-present backbone.
- **Level 5:** an extended paragraph organizing several concrete details logically, with one detail (not a stated
  feeling word) that implies an attitude toward the object — carrying an optional concession subordinator
  ("although") as a natural upgrade from a flat "but."

**Task-Level basis:** as quoted verbatim in the table above — each task Level's Mentor Text and drafting task in
the generated lesson maps directly to its own row, not a generic simplification of the centerpiece task.
