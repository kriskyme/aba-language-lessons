## What this lesson works from

Nothing. This is the Set's first lesson and the arc starts here: no folder, no kept paragraph, no earlier draft
is needed, and nothing from Module 1's clothing piece is used or referred to (Quality Standards §D9). Every
student needs their own memory of **their charger**: the one they use for a phone, a tablet, or anything else
they charge. "The charger" below means that one.

The charger is a **subject, not a prop** (Quality Standards §D8): it need not be in the room or in a bag, and no
task asks the student to take it out, hold it up, or look at it. A student who owns no charger of their own
writes about the one they use at home, which every student has access to; the tasks ask only for things that
really happened with it.

This lesson drafts nothing. It teaches Module 2's first grammar focus and shows on the ladder what the Set is
building toward: a short piece that tells one real thing that happened, in order, with how it turned out and
why. **The charger is never drafted about.** It carries the grammar work through Lesson 2 Day 1 and then steps
aside; Lesson 2 Day 2 introduces the draft object, and Lessons 3-4 write about that (Lesson prompt 0.1a).

## Word Bank (teacher-provided; posted on the board and printed in the packet, used through Lesson 2 Day 1)

Three verb groups plus two supporting groups, all about a charger. Levels 2-3 draw their verbs from the three
verb groups; **every verb there is regular**, so its past form ends in -ed and can be spelled from sound. Levels
4-5 may use any group when a word is not coming to mind. The irregular past forms Focus A teaches sit in the
grammar box below, not here, so nothing in this bank contradicts the -ed rule it is used to practise.

> **Care verbs:** cleaned, wiped, wrapped, coiled, packed, checked
> **Use verbs:** plugged, charged, unplugged, pulled, tugged, connected
> **Getting verbs:** borrowed, ordered, needed, wanted, searched, carried
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last summer, one morning
> **People and places:** my brother, my friend, the classroom, the library, the bus, my bag, the wall

(32 words. The verb groups hold 18 verbs for one- and two-verb frame tasks, so no bank is exhausted by a task;
padding per Quality Standards §C4.)

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class):** the simple past for a sequence of events: regular -ed with
its four spelling rules, a short list of irregular past forms the Scenario needs, and time connectors that put
events in order (first, then, next, after that, finally) or place them (last summer, one morning, yesterday).
This is the Level 4 row of the Lesson prompt's 0.4 bank, chosen by hand for Module 2's purpose (the bank's
Alternate column is not yet authored); Intermediate's native Paragraph Composition Level, so no row-selection
correction applies. Level 4's required feature (a sequence of past events with one outcome and a reason) needs it
directly; Level 5's "why it mattered" and implied feeling are layered on top of it in Lesson 2.

**Reason words are assumed, not taught.** because, since, as, and given that are used from this lesson on to give
the outcome its reason. They are available vocabulary at this Band, and no phase in this Set teaches them as new
content or spends time on them.

**Frame-regime content, folded into the same mini-lesson (Levels 2-3, never a separate session):** the past frame
"I ___-ed it." (Level 2) and the two-slot past frame "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." (Level 3), with the -ed ending
spelled as part of the verb; the two slots hold two different actions in the order they happened. Verbs come from
the Word Bank's three verb groups.

**Level 5 heavier tier (Day 1 and Day 2):** placing an outcome sentence in time with an opening time phrase, and
combining a time connector, an outcome, and a reason word in one sentence, using only what this lesson has
taught; the fuller "why it mattered" work and the implied-feeling tools (past continuous, adverbs of manner) are
Lesson 2's Level 5 extension.

### Phase 1: Hook and Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Hook (8 min).** The teacher asks the room one question and takes four or five answers, fast, without writing
anything down yet: "Think about your charger. Has anything ever happened to it? Did you lose it, lend it, break
it, forget it somewhere?" Almost every answer that comes back is already a small story with an order and an
ending, which is the point: the teacher says so, and puts the two or three shortest on the board in the students'
own words, unedited and out of order.

Then one line that sets up the Set: "Every one of those is a story. By the end of these four lessons each of you
will have written one, finished, and other people in this room will read it."

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "For the next four lessons we're practicing telling what happened with something of
our own, in order, and saying how it turned out and why." One sentence, the same for every Level, naming the
skill and not the object (Quality Standards §D1); no "some of you will also." Level 5's implied-feeling feature
is introduced inside its own tasks, not here.

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: the simple past and time connectors (30 min)

_Regular past forms: the four spelling rules (8 min)._

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: add -ed | clean -> cleaned, check -> checked, want -> wanted |
| One vowel + one consonant at the end: double the consonant, add -ed | plug -> plugged, tug -> tugged, drop -> dropped |
| Consonant + y at the end: change y to i, add -ed | carry -> carried, try -> tried, dry -> dried |
| Verb already ends in -e: add -d only | charge -> charged, replace -> replaced, order -> ordered (no double r: two vowels before it) |

Point out the two rules students most often break (plug is not "pluged"; carry is not "carryed"), and that the
-ed ending sounds like /t/ (packed), /d/ (plugged), or /id/ (wanted) but is always written -ed.

_Irregular past forms the story will need (7 min)._ Recognition list, read aloud once, kept on the board all Set
and printed in the packet as this table, two pairs across (Style Guide §C).

| Now | Past | Now | Past |
|---|---|---|---|
| get | got | lose | lost |
| buy | bought | give | gave |
| choose | chose | see | saw |
| put | put | pay | paid |
| take | took | lend | lent |
| find | found | is / are | was / were |

These do not take -ed, and a student who needs one writes it from the board.

_Time connectors (10 min)._

| Job | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Put events in order | first, then, next, after that, finally | First I checked my bag. Then I found the cable under the bed. |

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** ask a volunteer to name one thing that happened with their
charger in three words ("lost, library, Friday"). The class builds the first sentence together on the board
("Last Friday I lost my charger at the library."), then a second one that must come next in time, with a time
connector nobody has said yet.

_Frame content, folded in (5 min)._ Show the Word Bank, then the frame "I ___-ed it." Elicit two verbs orally
from the whole class about their own chargers (a care verb, then a use verb), sounding out the -ed ending each
time. Then show "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." and elicit a pair that names two different actions in the order they
happened (plugged it and charged it, not cleaned it and wiped it). Say once that the frame is the model for the
day; every later task just says "the frame." This step is spoken only: the frame rules are not printed as a
grammar-box paragraph, because each frame task states them in its own instruction line.

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A, part 1 (30 min)

**Activity 1: Fill in the blank with the past form (whole class, 10 min).** Each sentence is about a charger of
the kind students own.

> 1. Last summer I ___ (buy) this charger at a shop near the station.
> 2. First I ___ (try) the cheap one, and it stopped working in a week.
> 3. Then my brother ___ (pay) for a better one.
> 4. I ___ (take) it to school every day in my bag.
> 5. One morning I ___ (plug) it into the classroom wall and walked away.
> 6. After that I ___ (search) the whole room twice.
> 7. Finally I ___ (find) it and ___ (put) it in the front pocket.
> 8. Last week my friend ___ (lend) me hers for an hour.

Answer key: bought, tried, paid, took, plugged, searched, found / put, lent. Items 1, 3, 4, 7 (second blank), and
8 come from the irregular board; 2, 5, 6, and 7 (first blank) use the four spelling rules.

**Activity 2: Choose the correct form (whole class, 8 min).** Both options are forms a student might really
write; the wrong one breaks a spelling rule or treats an irregular verb as regular.

> 1. Last spring I (buyed / bought) this charger online.
> 2. I (tryed / tried) it in three different sockets.
> 3. One morning I (pluged / plugged) it in and nothing happened.
> 4. My friend (carryed / carried) it home in her coat.
> 5. I (losed / lost) the old one in December.
> 6. I (wrapped / wraped) the cable around my hand.
> 7. My brother (gived / gave) me this one for my birthday.
> 8. I (packt / packed) it at the top of my bag.

Answer key: bought, tried, plugged, carried, lost, wrapped, gave, packed.

**Activity 3, Levels 4-5 (12 min): Underline the time word and circle the reason word in each sentence that has
one.** One instruction, both marks (Quality Standards §C11): what a sentence is doing is exactly what the two
marks show, so a separate step labelling each sentence would ask the same question a third time. Sentences use
every time connector and all four reason words, so finding the marks requires reading the whole sentence, and
three of the eight carry only one of the two.

Answer key (underline / circle):

> 1. First I checked my bag, then I checked my coat pocket. (first, then / none)
> 2. My phone died in the afternoon because I had left the charger at home. (none / because)
> 3. After that I borrowed one from my friend, so I got through the day. (after that / none)
> 4. One morning I found the cable chewed at one end. (one morning / none)
> 5. The new charger was slower, since it came from the cheap shop. (none / since)
> 6. Finally I bought a second one, because I was tired of asking people. (finally / because)
> 7. Next I coiled it and packed it. (next / none)
> 8. The cable broke early, as I pulled it out by the wire every night. (none / as)

Item 3 is the trap: "so" joins a result but is a joining word, not a reason word, so nothing is circled there.
Discuss it last. In 2 and 8, "in the afternoon" and "early" say when without being on the taught list; accept a
student who underlines them and say why the list is what the Set writes from.

**Activity 3b, Level 5 (heavier tier, inside the same 12 min):** sentence 2 is worked on the page, before and
after ("My phone died in the afternoon because I had left the charger at home." -> "Yesterday afternoon my phone
died because I had left the charger at home."), and students rewrite 5 and 8 the same way, keeping the outcome
and reason each already has. Sentence 2 is the example rather than a third item because the finished version of
it was already printed as the model, which made it that item's answer (Quality Standards §C9); showing both
states of it also makes the change itself visible, which one finished sentence did not. The condition is what
the opening must do, not how long it is: a one-word "Yesterday" places the event as well as "Yesterday
afternoon" does, so no word count is asked for (§C12). The packet Task names Activity 3's Task and adds this,
rather than restating it (§B). Built only from what Phase 2 taught; this is Level 5's own Day 1 task, not a share of Level 4's.

**Activity 3-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min), choose what fits, then the one guided round (0.4d):**
each student first circles, in the three verb groups of the Word Bank, every verb that really happened with their
own charger and crosses out the ones that never did (a genuine choice against the object as they know it). Then
Level 2 completes the frame three times, one circled verb from a different group each round (a care verb, a use
verb, a getting verb), sounding out each verb with its -ed ending, and reads each finished sentence to a partner.
Level 3 completes the two-slot frame three times, each round a pair of circled verbs from two different groups in
the order they happened ("I borrowed it and charged it."), so the two slots always carry two separate actions in
a real order. The teacher circulates and listens to each student sound out the verb before writing it. This is
the lesson's one complete-the-frame task; Day 2's frame-regime work takes a different shape.

### Phase 1: Editing a paragraph on Focus A (20 min)

**Activity 4: Find and fix the 8 mistakes (pairs, then whole class, 20 min).** The paragraph is a student's short
story about a charger, the kind of piece this Module writes. One instruction on the page, not a numbered list (Style Guide §F): pairs
cross out each mistake on the printed paragraph with a single line, then each student writes the whole paragraph
out again, corrected, on the twelve answer lines below it. Reading it, marking it and copying it out is one pass
over one paragraph, and only the copy leaves anything on the page. Nothing is written between the printed lines: at body leading there is no room for a word
above a word (Style Guide §F, "Writing on printed text"), and the corrected paragraph is the better product
anyway, since the connector error has to be fixed in place rather than listed as a loose word. Budget about seven
minutes of the twenty for the copying.

> Last summer I buyed this charger at a shop near the station. First I looked at a cheap one, then I choosed the
> black one because it was stronger. I taked it to school every day. One morning I pluged it into the classroom
> wall, and I walked out without it. Before that I searched my bag three times. A teacher findes it and kept it
> for me. I carryed it home that night, and I have never left it anywhere since.

Answer key, 8 errors verified by count: buyed -> bought; choosed -> chose; taked -> took; pluged -> plugged;
Before that -> After that (the searching came after he walked out; the connector puts the events in the wrong
order); findes -> found (a present form where the story is past, and the verb is irregular); carryed -> carried;
"I have never left it anywhere since" is correct and must be left alone, as are "because it was stronger" and
"and kept it for me," so students cannot assume every clause hides a mistake. Seven sentences. Five of the eight
are past-form errors (three irregular verbs treated as regular, two spelling-rule errors), one is a
time-connector error, one a tense error on an irregular verb, and one more spelling-rule error (carryed). In the
rewritten paragraph the corrected sentence reads "After that I searched my bag three times."

### Phase 2: Frame practice / original sentences with Focus A (25 min)

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), Activity 5-equivalent, fix the wrong word (0.4d):** the frame-regime parallel to the
editing paragraph, not another frame round. Three printed items each state one fact about a charger in a clause,
then give a frame sentence about it with a verb that does not fit the fact or is misspelled; the student crosses
out the wrong verb and writes the right one from the Word Bank. Each Level's packet Task carries this as one
instruction over its three items, the item format described in the same sentence as the marking (Style Guide §F).

- **Level 2:** (1) "My charger sits in a loop at the bottom of my bag. I plugged it." (cross out plugged;
  coiled); (2) "The cable was covered in dust from the floor. I charged it." (wiped); (3) "I put the cable into
  the socket. I pluged it." (misspelled; plugged).
- **Level 3:** the pair must name two different actions in a real order and be spelled right: (1) "First I took
  the cable out of the wall, then I rolled it up. I coiled it and unplugged it." (wrong order; unplugged it and
  coiled it); (2) "I got the dust off the cable with a cloth. I cleaned it and wiped it." (wiped repeats cleaned;
  cleaned it and packed it); (3) "I put it in the socket, then I left it for an hour. I plugged it and chargd
  it." (misspelled; charged).

Answer keys are above; the teacher circulates and hears each replacement verb sounded out.

**Activity 5, Level 4: write one original sentence using each target item**, about your own charger: bought,
first, then, lost, finally, because. At least two sentences must contain both a time connector and a past verb,
and at least one must be an outcome sentence with its reason.

**Activity 6, Level 5 (heavier tier, same 25 minutes):** Activity 5's six sentences, with three of the six
opening on the reason clause and its comma. The instruction on the page names Activity 5's Task and states only
that addition (Quality Standards §B), and Level 5 writes in that Task's own lines; the two example shapes are
printed with it, one ordinary and one inverted ("I kept the spare one in my bag after that, since I could not
afford to lose another." / "Given that I could not afford to lose another, I kept the spare one in my bag after
that."). Inverting the clause is the whole added demand: the reason has to be held in mind before the event it
explains, and the comma is the only thing marking the join. This uses only what Day 1 taught; Lesson 2 builds
the "why it mattered" work on it.

### Phase 3: Leveled Mentor Ladder (30 min)

Present four Mentor Texts, in Level order, on the board or read aloud. All four tell one model student's own
charger story, so the class watches one real sequence climb four steps. **This is the Set's only Mentor Ladder
presentation**, and there is no later second look. The ladder is the teacher's material and is not printed in the
packet (packet prompt 2.6). It stays on the practice object throughout; the draft object does not exist yet and
never appears here, because the ladder models form, not content (Lesson prompt 0.1a).

**Level 2 Mentor Text**

> Frame: I ___-ed it.
> Tomas's answer: I lost it.

**Level 3 Mentor Text**

> Frame: I ___-ed it and ___-ed it.
> Tomas's answer: I searched it and found it.

**Level 4 Mentor Text**

> Last summer I bought this charger at a shop near the station. First I looked at the cheap one on the counter,
> but then I saw a stronger one behind the glass. I asked the price and paid for the stronger one. After that I
> used it every single day for a year. I still have it because it has never stopped working.

(5 sentences, 63 words: inside the 40-90 word, 3-6 sentence target. Time connectors: last summer, first, then,
after that. Outcome with its reason: "I still have it because it has never stopped working.")

**Level 5 Mentor Text**

> One morning last winter I plugged my charger into the wall at the back of the classroom and forgot it there.
> I did not notice until the bus home, when my phone showed four percent. I got off two stops early and walked
> back to the school in the cold. The door was locked, and the classroom window was dark. The next morning I
> found it exactly where I had left it, still plugged in. I wound the cable around my hand three times before I
> put it in the front pocket of my bag, and I checked that pocket twice on the walk to the bus. That charger
> mattered more than it should have, because it was the only one I had.

(7 sentences, 123 words: inside the 90-150 word, 6-9 sentence target. Events in order: the forgetting, the
discovery, the walk back, the next morning. Why it mattered is stated in the last sentence. The implied feeling
is carried by winding the cable three times and checking the pocket twice; no feeling word appears. "when my
phone showed four percent" and "before I put it in the front pocket" preview Lesson 2's time clauses and Level
5's past-continuous background.)

**Walkthrough (teacher-led, from the board):** for each Level, name in one line what the model does (one past
action; two actions in order; events in order with an outcome and its reason; a day that mattered, with a feeling
the reader has to guess). Students find their own Level's model and the one directly above it on the board, and
say to a partner one thing the next model up does that theirs does not. Nothing here is printed.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a "what each step adds" board built from student observations
("Level 2 says one thing that happened. Level 3 says two, in order. Level 4 tells the whole story and how it
ended. Level 5 tells why it mattered and makes us guess how Tomas felt."). It stays up for Lesson 2.
