Grammar

Telling What Happened (Past Tense)

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -edclean → cleaned, check → checked, want → wanted
One vowel + one consonant at the end: double the consonant, add -edplug → plugged, tug → tugged, drop → dropped
Consonant + y at the end: change y to i, add -edcarry → carried, try → tried, dry → dried
Verb already ends in -e: add -d onlycharge → charged, replace → replaced, order → ordered (no double r: two vowels come before it)

Watch the two rules people break most: plug becomes plugged (not pluged), and carry becomes carried (not carryed). The -ed ending can sound like /t/ (packed), /d/ (plugged), or /id/ (wanted), but it is always written -ed.
