Unit 3A: Writing a Story About My Device

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.3.2

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Draft Word Bank
Care verbs: cleaned · wiped · charged · dried · covered · checked
Use verbs: pressed · switched · turned · pointed · tapped · dropped
Getting verbs: borrowed · ordered · needed · wanted · searched · carried
Time words: first · then · next · after that · finally · last summer · one morning
People and places: my brother · my friend · the classroom · the kitchen · the bus · my bag · the table

Use these words if you need them. You may also use your own.

Write It

Write about the device: one real thing that happened with it. When you finish, be ready to read one line aloud to someone working at a different star level.

Task C. ★ Complete the sentence: choose your verb, sound it out, and write it.

  1. 1.Pick two verbs from the Draft Word Bank that could go in the blank: or
  2. 2.Which one is the thing that really happened first, and why? Say it to a partner in one phrase (charged, because it was empty).
  3. 3.Sound out that verb, ending included, and write it: I -ed it.
  4. 4.Read your line to a partner. Your partner says in one phrase what happened to the device. Is that what you meant?

Task D. ★★ Complete the sentence: choose your two verbs, sound them out, and write them.

  1. 1.Pick two verbs from the Draft Word Bank that could go in the first blank: or
  2. 2.Which one is the thing that really happened first, and why? Say it to a partner in one phrase.
  3. 3.Pick a second verb that is a different action, the one that happened next (charged it and packed it, not cleaned it and wiped it).
  4. 4.Sound out both verbs, endings included, and write them in the order they happened: I -ed it and -ed it.
  5. 5.Read your line to a partner. Your partner says which action came first. Is that what you meant?

Task E. ★★★ Write your paragraph, one sentence per step. Aim for 3-6 sentences.

  1. 1.Open with when it happened and what you did first, in one sentence (Last summer I ordered these earbuds online.).
  2. 2.Write the next thing that happened, with a time word from the Draft Word Bank in front (then, next, after that).
  3. 3.Write one more event, with a different time word.
  4. 4.Join two of the events into one sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as. Put a comma after that part if it comes first. This can replace two of your sentences or be a new one.
  5. 5.Close with how it turned out and why, using because, since, as, or given that (I still use them every day because they were the first thing I bought with my own money.).

Count your sentences and write the number:

Task F. ★★★★ Write your paragraph: list the events, put them in order, then write. Aim for 6-9 sentences.

  1. 1.List four or five things that happened with it that day, in the order they happened.
  2. 2.Circle the one event or detail that most clearly hints at how you felt.
  3. 3.Write your paragraph, putting the circled detail where it carries the most weight, usually near the end. You may use a background sentence (I was standing at the counter when ...) or a word like slowly or carefully where it fits.
  4. 4.End with one sentence that says why the day mattered (That day mattered because it was the last thing my brother gave me before he moved.).
  5. 5.Check: does any sentence name the feeling (happy, sad, love, hate, nervous, proud)? "I was so happy" does not count; a specific detail does.

Share a Line

Get ready to read one line from your piece aloud.

  • ★Read your completed line.
  • ★★Read your completed line, then say which action came first.
  • ★★★Read your sentence that says how it turned out, or your sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as if you have written it.
  • ★★★★Read the sentence with your circled detail. Do not say what feeling it shows.

While you listen: write down one verb or time word from each line that you would want in your own piece. For a four-star line, write the feeling you guess instead.

Unit 3B: Writing a Story About My Device

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.3.2

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Check and Improve Your Writing

Anything you change: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task A. ★ Check your sentence, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Read your sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: what happened to the device? Say it.
  3. 3.Is that what you meant?
  4. 4.Does my verb end in -ed?
  5. 5.Is there a verb in the Draft Word Bank that says more exactly what happened than the one I chose (charged instead of checked, dropped instead of carried)? If yes, cross out your old verb.
  6. 6.Write the whole new sentence on the line below. If your verb was already the exact one, leave it and put a check next to it.
  7. 7.Read the result to your partner.

Task B. ★★ Check your sentence, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Read your sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: which action came first? Say it.
  3. 3.Are my two verbs two different actions, in the order they happened?
  4. 4.Do both verbs end in -ed?
  5. 5.Is there a verb in the Draft Word Bank that says more exactly what happened than one of the two I chose (charged instead of checked, dropped instead of carried)? If yes, cross out the old verb.
  6. 6.Write the whole new sentence on the line below. If both verbs were already the exact ones, leave them and put a check next to each.
  7. 7.Say your sentence to your partner with a time word from the Draft Word Bank in front (First I charged it and packed it.), so your partner hears when it happened.

Task C. ★★★ Check your paragraph, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Is every verb in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form (got, bought, took, lost)?
  2. 2.Do at least two time words put the events in order, the order things really happened? Circle each time word.
  3. 3.Does one sentence say how it turned out, with a reason word? Underline it.
  4. 4.Does my sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as have a comma when that part comes first, and no comma when it comes second? Put a box around the comma.
  5. 5.Is "then" used for what came next, and "than" only after a comparing word?
  6. 6.Are punctuation and capital letters correct, and does my paragraph have 3-6 sentences?
  7. 7.Make each change you marked (a verb left in the present, a missing time word, a comma in the wrong place, a "than" that should be "then").
  8. 8.Add one more event or one real detail. Stay inside 6 sentences.

Task D. ★★★★ Check your paragraph, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Is every verb in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form (got, bought, took, lost)?
  2. 2.Do at least two time words put the events in order, the order things really happened? Circle each time word.
  3. 3.Does one sentence say why the day mattered, and does one detail hint at how I felt with no feeling word (happy, sad, love, hate, nervous, proud) anywhere in it? Underline the first and circle the second.
  4. 4.Does my sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as have a comma when that part comes first, and no comma when it comes second? Put a box around the comma.
  5. 5.Is "then" used for what came next, and "than" only after a comparing word?
  6. 6.Are punctuation and capital letters correct, and does my paragraph have 6-9 sentences?
  7. 7.Is there at least one background sentence (I was ... when ...) or one word like slowly or carefully? If not, mark where one could go.
  8. 8.Make each change you marked.
  9. 9.If you found a feeling word, cross out that sentence and write a real detail (what you did, and how) that lets the reader guess the feeling. If you did not, make your circled detail sharper with a word like slowly or carefully, or a background sentence. Stay inside 9 sentences.
