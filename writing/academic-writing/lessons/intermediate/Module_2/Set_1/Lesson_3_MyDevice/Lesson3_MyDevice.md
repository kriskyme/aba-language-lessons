## What this lesson carries forward

**Nothing a student wrote earlier** (Quality Standards §D9). There is no folder to open, no kept paragraph, no
page from Lesson 2's packet. Lesson 2 Day 2's prewriting was rehearsal; every plan this lesson uses is built in
Day 1 Phase 1 inside this packet, and the Draft Word Bank is reprinted below.

**The one thing that carries is the choice of device,** made at Lesson 2 Day 2 and unchanged since (Lesson prompt
0.1a). It is circled again on this lesson's own page, so a student who was absent can still take part in full.
"The device" below means the one they circled. The device is a subject, not a prop (Quality Standards §D8): it
need not be in the room, and nothing asks the student to look at it or take it out.

**What students write today is the whole piece.** Levels 4-5 one paragraph telling one real thing that happened
with the device, in order, with how it turned out and why; Levels 2-3 one frame sentence in the past. Lesson 4
peer-edits it, revises it for effect, and publishes it; nothing is added to it in another Module's form.

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** Focus A: regular -ed with its spelling
rules, the irregular list on the board (got, bought, chose, put, took, found, lost, gave, saw, paid, lent, was /
were), and time connectors that order or place events. Focus B: complex sentences with a time clause (when,
before, after, while, as soon as), the comma after an opening clause, and then / than. Level 5 extension:
past-continuous background and adverbs of manner.

## Draft Word Bank (reprinted; posted on the board and printed in the packet)

Levels 2-3 draw their verbs from the three verb groups, every one of them regular; Levels 4-5 may use any group
when a word is not coming to mind.

> **Care verbs:** cleaned, wiped, charged, dried, covered, checked
> **Use verbs:** pressed, switched, turned, pointed, tapped, dropped
> **Getting verbs:** borrowed, ordered, needed, wanted, searched, carried
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last summer, one morning
> **People and places:** my brother, my friend, the classroom, the kitchen, the bus, my bag, the table

## DAY 1: DRAFTING THE PIECE (75 MIN)

```
0      10                              60         75  (minutes)
|Phase 1|------------Phase 2------------|-Phase 3--|
| Quick | Independent/guided drafting:   | Mid-draft |
| plan  | the piece                      | share-out |
```

### Phase 1: Quick plan (10 min)

Every student circles, on the printed list in the packet (my earbuds, my speaker, my watch, my calculator, my
flashlight, my remote control, my game controller, my mouse), the device they are writing about. Then, every
Level: circle one of the two printed choices, **the day I got it** / **one day it stopped working, or nearly
did**, and write three Draft Word Bank verbs that happened with it on that day, on the line under the choices.
That is the whole printed plan (Quality Standards §E6: actions with a visible product, no questions). Level 4
also writes, on one more line, how it turned out and the reason ("I still use it because ..."). Level 5 builds
its ordered event list inside its drafting task in Phase 2, not here.

**Teacher-facing, not printed:** while circulating, check that each plan already carries its Level's required
feature: a verb that really happened (Level 2), two verbs in a real order (Level 3), an outcome with a reason
(Level 4). If not, ask what happened first, or how the day ended.

### Phase 2: Independent/guided drafting, the piece (50 min)

Every student writes the real piece. The teacher circulates continuously; the circulation prompts below are what
to check while the draft is in progress, not only at the end.

**Levels 2-3 (frame-based), the real frame(s), once.**

- Level 2 completes "I ___-ed it." for their own device, spelling one regular verb from sound with its -ed
  ending. Before the verb goes on the page, the student says it aloud to the teacher, sound by sound, ending
  included. The teacher listens for the sounding-out, not for a perfect spelling.
- Level 3 completes "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." the same way, two verbs, each sounded out. The teacher checks
  that the two verbs are two different actions in the order they happened before the second verb is written.
- **Genuine-choice moment (both Levels).** Each student picks two candidate verbs from the bank for one slot (for
  example "charged" and "packed"), says to the teacher or a partner which one is the thing that really happened
  first and why in one phrase ("charged, because it was empty"), and writes that one. The verb is a choice the
  student makes and can defend, not a foregone answer.
- **When the frame is complete:** the student reads the line to a partner, who says in one phrase what happened
  to the device (Level 3: which action came first), and the student says whether that is what they meant. The
  frame is written once today (Lesson prompt 0.4d); Day 2's improvement round is different in kind (a more exact
  verb), and Lesson 4's reader's request adds a further sentence.
- **Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 2, not in place of it):** a student who cannot yet spell from
  sound chooses the slot verb from three printed Draft Word Bank verbs in the packet (the teacher reads them
  aloud), then traces it into the frame; or dictates the verb to the teacher, who scribes it, then the student
  says which written verb is which when the teacher reads two of them back.

**Level 4 (single paragraph), a five-step drafting path.** Steps, not a frame: each sentence is the student's
own, and each step uses only a tool from Lessons 1-2.

1. Open with when it happened and what you did first, in one sentence ("Last summer I ordered these earbuds
   online.").
2. Write the next thing that happened, with a time word from the bank in front (then, next, after that).
3. Write one more event, with a different time word.
4. Join two of the events into one complex sentence with a time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon as),
   punctuated by the rule. This can replace two of the sentences from steps 1-3 or be a new sentence.
5. Close with how it turned out and why, using a reason word (because, since, as, given that): "I still use them
   every day because they were the first thing I bought with my own money."

Circulation check: by the halfway point of the block, does the draft already contain two time words and every
verb in the past? If not, direct the student to steps 1-3 before they write anything else. The draft is completed
today: in the last five minutes of the block Levels 4-5 count their sentences and write the number at the bottom
of the page (students count sentences only; the word range in the targets table is the teacher's circulation
check, Style Guide §E), and a count at the bottom of the range is a signal to add one more event.

**Level 5 (extended paragraph), list, order, then draft.** Before writing sentences, students list four or five
events of that day on the short lines in their drafting task, in the order they happened, and circle the one
event or detail that most clearly hints at how they felt. Then draft, building the paragraph around the circled
detail so that it lands where it carries the most weight, usually late, and ending with one sentence that says
why the day mattered ("That day mattered because it was the last thing my brother gave me before he moved."). A
past-continuous background ("I was standing at the counter when ...") and adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully)
are available where they fit; they are not required. Remind students directly: no sentence may name the feeling
("I was so happy" fails the task; a specific, well-chosen detail succeeds).

Circulation spot test: read one detail sentence from the draft and ask, "What would a stranger guess you felt
that day from this sentence alone?" If the student cannot answer, or answers "nothing," the detail is not yet
doing its work; if the student names a feeling word that appears in the sentence, it is stated, not implied.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share-out (15 min)

Each student prepares one line to read, by Level, then a few students from each Level read aloud:

- **Level 2:** read your completed frame.
- **Level 3:** read your completed frame, then say which action came first.
- **Level 4:** read your outcome sentence, or your time-clause sentence if it is written.
- **Level 5:** read the sentence that carries your circled detail, without saying what feeling it implies.

**Task for every listener:** on a scrap of paper, write one verb or time word from each read line that you would
want in your own piece; for a Level 5 line, write the feeling you guess instead. The teacher takes two or three
of these guesses aloud after each Level 5 line, so the writer hears whether the detail landed.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** a running "strong sentence" board, one line per Level column, built
live from the share-out. It stays up for Day 2.

## DAY 2: SELF-EDIT AND IMPROVE (75 MIN)

```
0          25                        55                      75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1--|---------Phase 2---------|--------Phase 3---------|
| Self-edit  | Make it better           | Share-out / final       |
| checklist  | from the marks           | line check              |
```

The piece is complete from Day 1. Day 2 opens directly on checking. The checklist covers **one piece**, this
Set's own, and names only the focuses this Set taught. Phases 1 and 2 below are printed as one section, "Check
and Improve Your Writing," with one lettered Task per star holding that Level's check items and then its
improvement steps (Style Guide §F); the packet has no "finish your piece" task and no "from last time" wording
(Quality Standards §D9).

### Phase 1: Self-edit checklist pass (25 min)

Students check the piece against their Level's list and mark each item as they find it in the text (circle the
time words, underline the outcome sentence, box the comma after a time clause). One list per Level, printed as
one lettered Task per star; the cross-out rule applies to every Level and is stated once before the lists:
anything changed is crossed out with a single line, not erased, and rewritten next to or above it (Section 0.4a).

**Level 2 (oral):** read your sentence to a partner. Your partner says what happened to the device. Is that what
you meant? Does your verb end in -ed?

**Level 3 (oral):** read your sentence to a partner. Your partner says which action came first. Are your two
verbs two different actions, in the order they happened? Do both end in -ed?

**Level 4 checklist:**

1. Every verb is in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form from the board.
2. At least two time words put the events in order, and they are in the order things really happened. Circle each
   time word.
3. One sentence says how it turned out, with a reason word. Underline it.
4. My time-clause sentence has its comma when the clause comes first, and no comma when it comes second.
5. "Then" is used for what came next; "than" only after a comparing word.
6. Punctuation and capitalization are correct, and my paragraph has 3-6 sentences.

**Level 5 checklist:** items 1-2 and 4-6 as above (item 6's count is 6-9 sentences), plus:

3. One sentence says why the day mattered, and one detail implies how I felt with no feeling word (happy, sad,
   love, hate, nervous, proud) anywhere in it. Underline the first, circle the second.
7. I have at least one past-continuous background ("I was ... when ...") or one adverb of manner, or I have
   marked where one could go.

### Phase 2: Make it better from the marks (30 min)

Every student improves the piece from their own marks, one lettered Task per star in the packet. Visible revision
throughout: cross out, do not erase.

- **Level 2:** is there a Draft Word Bank verb that says more exactly what happened than the one chosen (charged
  instead of checked, dropped instead of carried)? If yes, cross out the old verb and write the whole new
  sentence on the line below; if the first verb was already the exact one, leave it and put a check next to it.
  Read the result to a partner.
- **Level 3:** the same more-exact-verb check on both slots, then say the sentence to a partner with a time word
  from the bank in front of it ("First I charged it and packed it."), so the partner hears when it happened.
- **Level 4:** make every change you marked in Phase 1 (a verb left in the present, a missing time word, a comma
  in the wrong place, a "than" that should be "then"). Then add one more event or one concrete detail, staying
  inside 6 sentences.
- **Level 5:** make every change you marked. If Phase 1 found a feeling word, replace that sentence with a
  concrete detail (what you did, and how) that lets the reader guess the feeling; if not, sharpen the circled
  detail with an adverb of manner or a background clause. Stay inside 9 sentences.

The teacher circulates for the same features as Day 1: every verb past and two time words on every Level 4 page,
an outcome with a reason underlined, no feeling word on any Level 5 page, a sounded-out verb rather than a copied
one at Levels 2-3.

### Phase 3: Share-out / final line check (20 min)

In pairs, each student reads only the last line of the piece to a partner (Levels 2-3: the completed frame;
Levels 4-5: the closing sentence). The partner answers one question, by Level:

- **Levels 2-3:** "What happened to the device?" The partner says it.
- **Level 4:** "Does the last sentence say how it turned out and why, or does it just add one more event?"
- **Level 5:** "What do you think the writer felt that day, and which sentence told you?"

Writers note the answer in one word in the margin. A mismatch (the partner cannot say what happened, or guesses
no feeling at all) is the first thing to fix in Lesson 4's revision.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** return to Day 1's "strong sentence" board and add newly completed
closing lines volunteered from the pair check, at least one per Level.

**What this lesson does not do.** There is no Peer Editing exchange, no publishing, and no Closing Transfer Check
at this position. Every student leaves Day 2 with a complete, self-checked, improved piece. Lesson 4 runs Peer
Editing, revises for effect, publishes, and closes the Set.
