Grammar: Two Events in One Sentence (Time Clauses)

Time words like first, then, and after that put events in order as a list. A time clause puts two events in one sentence, so the reader knows which came first without a list. It starts with when, before, after, while, or as soon as, and it has its own subject and verb.

Time wordWhat it saysClause first (comma)Clause second (no comma)
whenat that momentWhen I got home, I plugged it in again.I plugged it in again when I got home.
beforethis happened firstBefore I left for school, I coiled the cable.I coiled the cable before I left for school.
afterthis happened secondAfter I charged it, I put it in my bag.I put it in my bag after I charged it.
whileat the same timeWhile I was waiting for the bus, my phone died.My phone died while I was waiting for the bus.
as soon asright afterAs soon as I found it, I checked the cable.I checked the cable as soon as I found it.

Comma rule: put a comma after the time clause when it opens the sentence. When the clause comes second, there is no comma.

A time clause has its own subject and verb. "After that" is a time word with no verb, so it is not a clause; "After I charged it" is a clause.

Grammar: Setting the Scene

Two ways to let a reader guess how you felt without writing a feeling word:

ToolRuleExample
Past continuous for backgroundwas / were + verb-ing sets the scene; the simple past gives the event that interrupts itI was running for the bus when my phone shut off.
Adverbs of manneradjective + -ly says how an action was done (careful, carefully; slow, slowly; quiet, quietly; quick, quickly). Fast and hard do not change.I coiled it slowly. / I pushed it in quickly and left.

A reader guesses a feeling from how something was done. "I wound the cable slowly and put it at the bottom of my bag" tells more than "I was upset."

A Word That's Easy to Mix Up

then vs. than

FormMeaningExample
thenwhat came nextI unplugged it, then I coiled it.
thanfollows a comparing word (faster than, longer than)The new one charged faster than the old one.

Test: if the word says what happened next, write "then." If a comparing word comes right before it, write "than."
