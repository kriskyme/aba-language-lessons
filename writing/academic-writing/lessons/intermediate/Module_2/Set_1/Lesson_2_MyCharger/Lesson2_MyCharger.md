## Grammar focuses

**Focus B (this lesson's centerpiece, Day 1, whole class):** complex sentences with a time clause (when, before,
after, while, as soon as), so that two events sit in one sentence in the order they happened, with the comma
after an opening time clause and no comma when the clause comes second. This is the sentence-variety half of the
Level 4 row's pair, chosen by hand for Module 2 (Lesson prompt 0.4, 0.9): Level 4's row asks for a sequence of
events with the student's own connectors, and a time clause is the second way, after Focus A's time words, to
show order. Confusable pair: then / than.

**Level 5 extension, folded into the same mini-lesson (never a separate session):** the past continuous for
background ("I was waiting for the bus when my phone died") and adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully, quietly,
quickly), two ways a detail can imply a feeling without naming it. Taught to the whole room in the Focus B
mini-lesson; Level 5 practices them in its own tiers.

**Frame-regime content (Levels 2-3):** the same two frames; today's shapes are better of two and pair judgment
(Day 1) and spell from sound and plan the words (Day 2), per the Lesson prompt's 0.4d bank.

```

| Focus B    | Controlled practice B:   | Confusable pair      |
| mini-      | identify, combine,       | (then / than) and    |
```

### Phase 1: Focus B mini-lesson: time clauses (20 min)

Tie it to Focus A first: "Last time you put events in order with time words: first, then, after that. Today you
put two events in one sentence, with a time clause, so the reader knows which came first without a list."

_Time clauses (10 min)._

| Time word | What it says | Example (clause first, comma) | Example (clause second, no comma) |
|---|---|---|---|
| when | at that moment | When I got home, I plugged it in again. | I plugged it in again when I got home. |
| before | this happened first | Before I left for school, I coiled the cable. | I coiled the cable before I left for school. |
| after | this happened second | After I charged it, I put it in my bag. | I put it in my bag after I charged it. |
| while | at the same time | While I was waiting for the bus, my phone died. | My phone died while I was waiting for the bus. |
| as soon as | right after | As soon as I found it, I checked the cable. | I checked the cable as soon as I found it. |

The time clause has its own subject and verb, so it is a clause, not a phrase ("After that" is a time phrase from
Lesson 1; "After I charged it" is a clause). Comma rule: a comma after the clause when it opens the sentence,
none when it comes second.

_Level 5 extension, taught to everyone (6 min)._

| Tool | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past continuous for background | was / were + verb-ing sets the scene; the simple past gives the event that interrupts it | I was running for the bus when my phone shut off. |
| Adverbs of manner | adjective + -ly says how an action was done (careful -> carefully, slow -> slowly, quiet -> quietly, quick -> quickly); fast and hard do not change | I coiled it slowly. / I pushed it in quickly and left. |

A reader guesses a feeling from how something was done: "I wound the cable slowly and put it at the bottom of my
bag" tells more than "I was upset."

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live (4 min):** ask a volunteer for two things that happened with their
charger, in order ("I found it under the bed. I plugged it in."). The class builds one complex sentence on the
board with a time word nobody has used yet, first with the clause opening (and its comma), then with the clause
second (and no comma).

### Phase 2: Controlled practice B (30 min)

**Activity 1: Mark each sentence CX (it has a time clause) or N (it does not); underline the time clause, and add
the comma where it is missing (whole class, 8 min).** Compound sentences and time phrases are in the mix as
decoys, so a student has to find a clause with its own subject and verb.

> 1. Last summer I bought this charger at the station. (N)
> 2. When I got home I plugged it in again. (CX; comma after "home")
> 3. I used it every day, and it never failed. (N; compound)
> 4. Before I left for school, I coiled the cable. (CX; comma present)
> 5. I lost it on the bus while I was looking for my ticket. (CX; no comma, the clause is second)
> 6. After that I searched my bag twice. (N; "after that" is a time phrase, not a clause)
> 7. As soon as it charged I unplugged it. (CX; comma after "charged")
> 8. My friend forgot hers first, so I lent her mine. (N; compound)

**Activity 2, Levels 4-5: Combine each pair into one sentence with a time word (12 min).** Use the time word in
parentheses where one is given; where two are given, choose the one that fits the order. Write four of the eight
with the clause first and four with it second, and punctuate each correctly.

> 1. I found the cable under the bed. I plugged it in. (as soon as)
> 2. I was waiting for the bus. My phone died. (while)
> 3. I charged it. I put it in my bag. (after)
> 4. I coiled the cable. I went out. (before)
> 5. My brother saw the frayed end. He taped it that night. (when)
> 6. I tried three sockets. I found the one that worked. (after / before)
> 7. I lent it to my friend. She wanted one of her own. (when / as soon as)
> 8. I paid for it. I saw the same one cheaper across the street. (after)

Answer key (one accepted form each; the other order of the two clauses is also correct with the comma rule
applied): 1 As soon as I found the cable under the bed, I plugged it in. 2 While I was waiting for the bus, my
phone died. 3 After I charged it, I put it in my bag. 4 Before I went out, I coiled the cable. 5 When my brother
saw the frayed end, he taped it that night. 6 After I tried three sockets, I found the one that worked (or:
Before I found the one that worked, I tried three sockets). 7 When I lent it to my friend, she wanted one of her
own. 8 After I paid for it, I saw the same one cheaper across the street. Item 6's two options force a choice
about which event the time word attaches to.

**Activity 3, Level 5 heavier tier (same 12 minutes, after Activity 2):** take your sentences for items 2, 5, and
8 and rewrite each so that it opens with a past-continuous background ("I was standing at the counter when ...")
and contains one adverb of manner that lets a reader guess how the writer felt, with no feeling word. Under each,
write the feeling a reader would guess, in one word, for the teacher to check against the adverb.

**Activity 2-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min), better of two, then pair judgment (0.4d).** Working from
the Word Bank and their own charger as they know it:

- **Better of two (both Levels):** three printed items, each a one-line fact about a charger and then two frame
  sentences about it. Circle the sentence that tells the reader more exactly what happened, and write the verb
  that made it better on the line. Level 2 items: (1) "The cable was dusty, not broken. I checked it. / I wiped
  it." (wiped); (2) "The cable went into the socket in the wall. I connected it. / I plugged it." (plugged); (3)
  "The charger went into my bag for the trip. I carried it. / I packed it." (packed). Level 3 items, two-slot:
  (1) "The cable came out of the wall, then went into a loop. I coiled it and unplugged it. / I unplugged it and
  coiled it." (unplugged it and coiled it: two actions, real order); (2) "I asked my friend for hers, then I used
  it for an hour. I borrowed it and charged it. / I charged it and borrowed it." (borrowed it and charged it);
  (3) "The cable got dusty, then it went into the front pocket. I cleaned it and wiped it. / I wiped it and
  packed it." (wiped it and packed it).
- **Pair judgment (Level 3; Level 2 as its lighter receptive tier):** six printed two-slot pairs about a charger.
  Level 3 circles the pairs whose two verbs are two different actions in a real order and crosses out the ones
  that repeat an action or put it out of order; Level 2 circles the pairs that name two different things. Pairs:
  plugged it and charged it (circle); cleaned it and wiped it (cross out: repeat); coiled it and packed it
  (circle); packed it and coiled it (cross out: order); borrowed it and returned it (circle); checked it and
  checked it (cross out: repeat).

The teacher circulates and hears each student say why the circled sentence or pair is the better one.

### Phase 3: Confusable pair and mixed editing (25 min)

**Activity 4: Choose then or than (whole class, 6 min).** "Then" says what came next; "than" follows a comparing
word. Items 7 and 8 carry two blanks each, one of each.

> 1. First I tried the old cable, (then / than) I tried the new one.
> 2. The new one charged faster (then / than) the old one.
> 3. I looked at the price, and (then / than) I put it back.
> 4. My friend's cable is longer (then / than) mine.
> 5. I unplugged it first, (then / than) I coiled it.
> 6. After a week it worked better (then / than) new.
> 7. The black one was cheaper (then / than) the white one, so (then / than) I bought it.
> 8. I packed it, and (then / than) I ran, faster (then / than) the bus.

Answer key: then, than, then, than, then, than, than / then, then / than.

**Activity 5, Levels 4-5: Find and fix the 8 mistakes (pairs, then whole class, 14 min).** Errors mix Focus A
(past forms), Focus B (time-clause commas), and then / than. A student's short story about a charger:

> One morning I finded this charger on the floor of the bus. When I picked it up I saw a name on the tape. Then I
> looked around, but nobody claim it. I carryed it in my bag for a week, than I gave it to the driver. Before I
> handed it over I wiped it. As soon as the owner seen it, she smiled. I still remember it because it was nicer
> then mine.

Answer key, 8 errors verified by count: finded -> found; comma after "When I picked it up"; claim -> claimed;
carryed -> carried; than -> then (what came next); comma after "Before I handed it over"; seen -> saw; then ->
than (after "nicer"). Seven sentences; "she smiled," "but nobody," and "because it was nicer" carry no error of
their own, and sentence 6's comma is correct, a decoy for a student adding commas everywhere.

**Level 5 addition to Activity 5:** after fixing, rewrite sentence 1 so it opens with a past-continuous
background ("I was riding the bus home when I found this charger on the floor."), and add one adverb of manner to
sentence 5.

**Levels 2-3 during Activity 5 (oral editing pass, 14 min):** read the three sentences you circled in Phase 2 to a
partner, one at a time. For each, the partner says what happened first (Level 3: which of the two actions came
first, and how they can tell). If the partner cannot tell, say the sentence again with a time word from the bank
in front of it ("First I unplugged it.").

### Phase 1: Spell from sound / required-feature warm-up (25 min)

Still on the charger; the new object arrives in Phase 2.

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), spell from sound (0.4d):** verbs only, no frame. Partners take turns: the partner
picks three verbs from three different verb groups of the Word Bank and reads them aloud one at a time, with the
-ed ending; the student writes each verb without looking at the bank, then checks it against the bank and fixes
any letter (the doubled consonant in plugged, the i in carried). Swap roles.

- **Level 2:** three verbs written from sound and checked.
- **Level 3:** three verbs written from sound and checked; then number the two that would go together in one
  frame in the order they would really happen, and say why to the partner.

**Activity 6, Level 4 (required-feature warm-up):** write two sentences about things that really happened with
your charger, each with a time word from the bank (one that places it, one that orders it), and one outcome
sentence with a reason word (because, since, as, given that) that says how it turned out. Then join the two event
sentences into one complex sentence with a time clause, punctuated by the rule. Three sentences plus one combined
sentence, four lines.

**Activity 7, Level 5 (implied-feeling warm-up):** write two sentences about one moment with your charger, each
carrying one concrete detail and no feeling word (no happy, sad, love, hate, nervous). One of the two must use a
past-continuous background or an adverb of manner from Day 1. Trade with a partner and ask, for each sentence:
"What does this detail make you think I felt?" Write the partner's guess under each sentence. A wrong guess means
the detail is not doing its job yet; note which one to replace.

### Phase 2: Prewriting on a new object: my device (30 min)

**This is where the Set's writing subject is fixed.** Everything from here to the published piece is about the
object chosen now, and it is not the charger: the charger carried the grammar, and the draft is the first time
that grammar moves to something the class did not drill on (Lesson prompt 0.1a). The teacher says this plainly in
one line, so no student thinks their charger work was wasted: "You practised on your charger. Now you write about
something else you own, so the writing is yours and not the practice."

**The draft object.** One everyday device the student really owns and uses. Students circle one from this printed
list, so nobody is without one:

> **my earbuds / my speaker / my watch / my calculator / my flashlight / my remote control / my game controller /
> my mouse**

A student who owns none of the eight writes about one they use at home, and says which; the list is there so no
one is stuck, not to rule anything out. The device is a **subject, not a prop**: nothing asks for it to be
brought in, taken out, or shown, and no picture of it is printed or found (Quality Standards §D8).

**Draft Word Bank (the same five groups, refilled for a device; printed here and reprinted in every later
lesson).** Every verb in the three verb groups is regular, so Levels 2-3 can spell any of them from sound.

> **Care verbs:** cleaned, wiped, charged, dried, covered, checked
> **Use verbs:** pressed, switched, turned, pointed, tapped, dropped
> **Getting verbs:** borrowed, ordered, needed, wanted, searched, carried
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last summer, one morning
> **People and places:** my brother, my friend, the classroom, the kitchen, the bus, my bag, the table

(32 words. 18 verbs for one- and two-verb frame tasks; padding per Quality Standards §C4.)

**What the piece is:** one real thing that happened with the device, told in order. Two choices, printed for
every Level to circle: **the day I got it** / **one day it stopped working, or nearly did.**

- **Level 2, plan the words (0.4d):** circle your device, then circle one of the two choices. On the Draft Word
  Bank, write three verbs that really happened with it on that day, from three different groups. Circle the one
  verb you would put in the frame, and tell a partner why that verb and not the other two.
- **Level 3, plan the words:** circle your device and one of the two choices. Write three verbs that happened on
  that day, from three different groups. Number the two you would put in the two-slot frame in the order they
  happened; the pair must pass Day 1's test (two different actions, real order). Say the sentence aloud to a
  partner.
- **Level 4:** circle your device and one of the two choices, then fill a three-line plan: "First ___" / "Then
  ___" / "Finally ___" (one event on each line, in the order it happened), and under it an outcome line, "How it
  turned out: ___, because ___." Then mark, with a bracket, the two events that could go in one sentence with a
  time clause.
- **Level 5:** circle your device and one of the two choices, then list four or five events in the order they
  happened. Under the list, one line: "Why it mattered: ___." Circle the one event or detail in the list that
  most clearly hints at how you felt, and write under it: "What I want the reader to guess: ___." Then read the
  list to a partner without the last line; the partner says what they guess, and a miss shows which detail to
  sharpen.

**This planning is rehearsal.** Lesson 3 builds its own quick plan inside its own packet and reprints the Draft
Word Bank, so nothing written here has to survive to be useful (Quality Standards §D9). No task here says "for
your draft" or "next time." The one thing that does carry is the **choice of device itself**, which is circled
again on Lesson 3's own page and never changes after today (Lesson prompt 0.1a).

### Phase 3: Prewriting share and board synthesis (20 min)

Two or three students from each task Level share one verb (Levels 2-3) or one event and its time word (Levels
4-5); Level 5 sharers give the event, not the feeling.

**Board-dependent moment, synthesized live:** a two-column class board, "Time words we used" / "How it turned
out," built only from what students share, so it holds phrasings no single handout has ("the first hot day," "on
the way home"; "it never charged again," "I still use it"). Level 2-3 verbs go under the Draft Word Bank group
they belong to. The board is this lesson's; Lesson 3 does not depend on it.
