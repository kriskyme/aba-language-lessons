Unit 4A: Finishing a Story About My Device

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.4.2

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Draft Word Bank
Care verbs: cleaned · wiped · charged · dried · covered · checked
Use verbs: pressed · switched · turned · pointed · tapped · dropped
Getting verbs: borrowed · ordered · needed · wanted · searched · carried
Time words: first · then · next · after that · finally · last summer · one morning
People and places: my brother · my friend · the classroom · the kitchen · the bus · my bag · the table
Name Your Piece

Task A. Give your piece a title.

  1. 1.Write a title on the line: the name of your device, or a few words from your story ("My Earbuds," "The Day I Dropped It").
  2. 2.Write 1 in the margin next to your piece, so your partner knows where it starts.
Trade and Check

Trade pieces with a partner. Read your partner's piece once all the way through. Then do the Task that matches your partner's piece, writing on your partner's page. When you are both done, give the pieces back.

Task B. ★ Read your partner's sentence and ask for one more.

  1. 1.Read the sentence aloud to your partner.
  2. 2.Say what happened to the device.
  3. 3.On your partner's page, write one word for the event you would like a second sentence about. Use one of these: What happened next? / Tell me about: the .

Task C. ★★ Read your partner's sentence and ask for one more.

  1. 1.Read the sentence aloud to your partner.
  2. 2.Say which action came first, and what happened to the device.
  3. 3.On your partner's page, write one word for the event you would like a second sentence about. Use one of these: What happened next? / Tell me about: the .

Task D. ★★★ Mark your partner's paragraph, then write them one compliment and one suggestion.

  1. 1.Number the time words in the order they appear, and put a box around the sentence that says how it turned out.
  2. 2.Underline the reason word in that sentence. If you cannot find one, write "no reason yet" in the margin.
  3. 3.Copy out one sentence you would keep exactly as it is.
  4. 4.Write one thing you want to know more about, or one place where you could not tell what happened first.

Task E. ★★★★ Mark your partner's paragraph, then write them one compliment and one suggestion.

  1. 1.Number the time words in the order they appear, and put a box around the sentence that says how it turned out.
  2. 2.Underline the reason word in that sentence. If you cannot find one, write "no reason yet" in the margin.
  3. 3.Write the feeling you guess, and the sentence that told you.
  4. 4.Copy out one sentence you would keep exactly as it is.
  5. 5.Write one thing you want to know more about, or one place where you could not tell what happened first.
Make It Stronger

Anything you change: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task F. ★ Answer your partner, then choose your best.

  1. 1.Find what your partner asked about on your page.
  2. 2.Write one more sentence about it, spelling the verb by its sounds: I -ed it.
  3. 3.Read both of your "I ___-ed it" sentences (this one and the one from before) to a partner.
  4. 4.Circle the one that tells the most about what happened. That is the one that goes first in your final copy.

Task G. ★★ Answer your partner, then choose your best.

  1. 1.Find what your partner asked about on your page.
  2. 2.Write one more sentence about it, with two actions in the order they really happened, spelling each verb by its sounds: I -ed it and -ed it.
  3. 3.Read both of your two-action sentences (this one and the one from before) to a partner.
  4. 4.Circle the one that tells the most about what happened. That is the one that goes first in your final copy.

Task H. ★★★ Make three changes that make your story stronger.

  1. 1.Find two event sentences that belong together and join them with a time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon as), with a comma where the rule puts it; or turn a "then" sentence into a time-clause sentence. If your piece already has a time-clause sentence, join a different pair. Cross out the old two and write the new one.
  2. 2.Find one plain verb (got, put, went, had). Cross it out and write a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank or your own (ordered, tapped, searched).
  3. 3.Add the event or detail your partner asked for, with a real time word in it. Stay inside 6 sentences: if you now have 7, join two sentences or cut the weakest.
  4. 4.Write each change in the table: three rows.

Task I. ★★★★ Make four changes that make your story stronger.

  1. 1.Find two event sentences that belong together and join them with a time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon as), with a comma where the rule puts it; or turn a "then" sentence into a time-clause sentence. If your piece already has a time-clause sentence, join a different pair. Cross out the old two and write the new one.
  2. 2.Find one plain verb (got, put, went, had) and replace it with a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank or your own; or add an adverb of manner, or a "was ___-ing" background, to one plain event sentence.
  3. 3.Add the event or detail your partner asked for, with a real time word in it.
  4. 4.Number your sentences in the margin. Move one sentence so that the ending (how it turned out, or why the day mattered) lands last, with the detail that lets a reader guess your feeling just before it. Stay inside 9 sentences.

Unit 4B: Finishing a Story About My Device

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.4.2

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Final Copy

Task A. ★ Copy your two sentences cleanly under your title.

  1. 1.Write your title on the first line.
  2. 2.Write the sentence you circled on the next line, with a capital letter and a period.
  3. 3.Write your other sentence on the last line, with a capital letter and a period.

Task B. ★★ Copy your two sentences cleanly under your title.

  1. 1.Write your title on the first line.
  2. 2.Write the sentence you circled on the next line, with a capital letter and a period.
  3. 3.Write your other sentence on the last line, with a capital letter and a period.

Task C. ★★★ Copy your title and your paragraph cleanly.

  1. 1.Write your title on the first line.
  2. 2.Write your paragraph with every change from Unit 4A in place, each sentence starting with a capital letter and ending with a period.

Task D. ★★★★ Copy your title and your paragraph cleanly.

  1. 1.Write your title on the first line.
  2. 2.Write your paragraph with every change from Unit 4A in place, in the new order, each sentence starting with a capital letter and ending with a period.
Share Your Writing

Your piece is finished. Now the class reads it.

Task E. Read two finished pieces.

  1. 1.Pass your final copy to the person next to you, and take the one passed to you.
  2. 2.Read the whole piece, then write one line on your card (Tasks F to I say what to write).
  3. 3.Pass that piece on and take another, so you read two pieces that are not your partner's.
  4. 4.When your own piece comes back, choose the sentence you like best, ready to read aloud.

Task F. ★ At each piece, write on your card one verb from the story and one word for what happened.

Task G. ★★ At each piece, write on your card one verb from the story and one word for what happened.

Task H. ★★★ At each piece, write on your card one thing you now know about that device that you could not have guessed from the title.

Task I. ★★★★ At each piece, write on your card one thing you now know about that device that you could not have guessed from the title.

Wrap It Up

Sit with a partner. Take a card and write about the pen or pencil in your hand. Read your card to your partner.

Task J. ★ Write one sentence about your pen.

  1. 1.Write "I ___-ed it." with one thing that really happened with it, spelling the verb by its sounds (dropped, borrowed, packed).

Task K. ★★ Write one sentence about your pen.

  1. 1.Write "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." with two things that really happened with it, in the order they happened.

Task L. ★★★ Tell what happened with your pen, in order.

  1. 1.Write three sentences in order, using at least two time words.
  2. 2.End with how it turned out and why. Example: I borrowed one from Ana because mine ran out in the middle of the test.

Task M. ★★★★ Tell what happened with your pen, in order, then add a detail.

  1. 1.Write three sentences in order, using at least two time words, ending with how it turned out and why.
  2. 2.Add one detail that lets your partner guess how you felt, with no feeling word.
  3. 3.Your partner reads it and says the guess out loud.
  4. 4.If the guess is wrong, write a sharper detail.
