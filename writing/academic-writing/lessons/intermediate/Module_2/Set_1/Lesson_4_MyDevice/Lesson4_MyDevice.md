## What this lesson works from

Every student arrives with their complete piece, checked once and improved at Lesson 3 Day 2: Levels 4-5 one
paragraph about one real thing that happened with their device, Levels 2-3 one past frame sentence. Below, "the
device" means the one they circled at Lesson 2 Day 2. It need not be present (Quality Standards §D8). No new
grammar is taught, and Lesson 3 Day 2's checklist is not run again: this lesson trades the piece, revises it for
effect, publishes it, and closes the Set. The revision moves use only what this Set already taught:

- **Focus A (Lesson 1):** past forms (-ed rules; the irregular board) and time connectors.
- **Focus B (Lesson 2):** complex sentences with a time clause and the comma rule; then / than.
- **Level 5 extension (Lesson 2):** past-continuous background; adverbs of manner.
- **Reason words,** used for the outcome throughout and never taught as new content in this Set.

**Draft Word Bank (reprinted; posted on the board and printed in the packet).** Levels 2-3 draw their verbs from
it; Levels 4-5 use it for the upgrade move.

> **Care verbs:** cleaned, wiped, charged, dried, covered, checked
> **Use verbs:** pressed, switched, turned, pointed, tapped, dropped
> **Getting verbs:** borrowed, ordered, needed, wanted, searched, carried
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last summer, one morning
> **People and places:** my brother, my friend, the classroom, the kitchen, the bus, my bag, the table

**Output targets for the finished piece (Section 0.2), checked by the teacher on the final copy:** Level 2, two
frame sentences (the drafted one and the one written for the reader); Level 3, two two-slot frame sentences;
Level 4, one paragraph of 3-6 sentences (40-90 words); Level 5, one paragraph of 6-9 sentences (90-150 words).
The ranges are the teacher's; students count nothing today.

## DAY 1: PEER EDITING AND REVISION FOR EFFECT (75 MIN)

```
0              25                            60         75  (minutes)
|---Phase 1-----|-----------Phase 2-----------|-Phase 3--|
| Title and     | Revise for effect, before/   | Revision  |
| Peer Editing  | after evidence               | share     |
```

### Phase 1: Title the piece, then Peer Editing (25 min)

**Title (7 min).** Every student writes a title on the packet's title line: the device's name, or a few words
from the story ("My Earbuds," "The Day I Dropped It"). Levels 2-3 write the title and number their sentence 1.
Nothing is copied yet.

**Peer Editing exchange (18 min), the Set's one exchange (Section 0.6).** A standalone Set runs it here, at
Lesson 4 Day 1, where the piece is complete and there is still a day to act on what the reader says. Partners
trade pieces and read them once through. Then the reader fills the printed form on the writer's page:

_Levels 4-5, the form:_
1. Number the time words in the order they appear, and put a box around the sentence that says how it turned out.
2. Underline the reason word in that sentence. If you cannot find one, write "no reason yet" in the margin.
3. (Reading a Level 5 piece) Write the feeling you guess and the sentence that told you.
4. One specific compliment: one sentence you would keep exactly as it is, copied out.
5. One specific suggestion: one thing you want to know more about, or one place where you could not tell what
   happened first.

_Levels 2-3, the form (oral, with two written words):_ the reader reads the sentence aloud, says what happened
to the device, and writes on the writer's page one word for the event they would like a second sentence about
("What happened next?" / "Tell me about: the ___").

The pieces go back. This is Peer Editing, not a reader's request: it has the form, the compliment, and the
suggestion, and it runs once in the Set, here.

**Teacher-facing:** while pairs read, note two or three suggestions to use in Phase 3.

### Phase 2: Revise for effect (35 min)

Every change is visible (cross out, do not erase) and, at Levels 4-5, recorded in the before/after table printed
in the packet. This is revision **for effect**, not a second correctness check: the correctness pass ran once, at
Lesson 3 Day 2, and is not repeated.

| What I first wrote | What I changed it to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

**Level 4, three moves:**

1. **Join.** Find two event sentences that belong together and join them with a time clause (when, before,
   after, while, as soon as), comma by the rule; or turn a "then" sentence into a time-clause sentence. If the
   piece already has a time-clause sentence, join a different pair.
2. **Upgrade.** Find one plain verb (got, put, went, had) and replace it with a more exact one from the Draft
   Word Bank or your own (ordered, tapped, searched).
3. **Answer the reader.** Add the event or detail your partner asked for on the form, with a real time word in
   it. Stay inside 6 sentences: if adding it makes 7, join two sentences or cut the weakest.

Each move is one row of the table; three rows at this Level.

**Level 5, four moves (heavier tier, same 35 minutes):** moves 1-3 above, where move 2 may instead add an adverb
of manner or a past-continuous background to a plain event sentence, plus:

4. **Reorder.** Number the sentences in the margin. Move one sentence so that the outcome, or the sentence that
   says why the day mattered, lands last, and make sure the implied-feeling detail still sits just before it,
   where it carries the most weight. Stay inside 9 sentences.

Four rows of the table at this Level; the "Why" column for the reorder row says what the new position does.

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), the reader's part, then choose (0.4d):** this day produces one more past frame
sentence, about the event the reader asked for, sounded out and, at Level 3, with two actions in a real order.
Then the student reads both past sentences to a partner and circles the one that tells the most about what
happened; the circled sentence goes first in the final copy, with the other under it. Answering the reader and
choosing between two of their own sentences is this Level's revision move. This is the lesson's one
complete-the-frame task.

**Foundation Support** (alongside Level 2, not in place of it): a student who cannot yet spell from sound says
the verb, the teacher writes it on a card, and the student copies it into the frame and says what happened to
the device.

**Teacher circulation:** three visible changes on every Level 4 page (four at Level 5) with a filled table row
for each; two past sentences on every Level 2-3 page with one circled. A change that only corrects (a comma, a
spelling) is not one of the three; send the student back to the move list.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a before/after board in two columns. Two or three volunteers
read one row of their table aloud (Level 5 first, since the reorder row is the hardest to hear; then one Level 4
join or upgrade; then one Level 2-3 student reads their two past sentences and says which they circled and why).
The teacher writes each "before" on the left and each "after" on the right, and the class names in one line what
the change did (put two events in one sentence; a sharper verb; answered the reader; put the ending last). The
board stays up for Day 2.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY, PUBLISH, CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0              20                     50                   75  (minutes)
|---Phase 1-----|--------Phase 2-------|-------Phase 3------|
| Final copy    | Publish / share      | Closing Transfer    |
|               | (read two pieces)    | Check + share       |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy (20 min)

Students write a clean copy of the finished piece on the packet's lines: the title, then Levels 4-5 the paragraph
with every Day 1 change in place, capitals and periods intact; Levels 2-3 the circled past sentence, then the
other, on two lines under the title. No proofread checklist and no counting task (the piece was checked once, at
Lesson 3 Day 2); the teacher's circulation check is that each Day 1 table row shows up in the copy.

**The piece is finished here.** Nothing in this lesson or its packet says the student will keep working on it: a
continuation line belongs only to a Module Pair's hand-off position, which this Set does not have (Style Guide's
do-not-print table).

### Phase 2: Publish / share (30 min)

The piece is genuinely finished. **Pass and read:** every final copy is passed to a neighbor, read, then passed on
again, so each student reads two pieces that are not their partner's without leaving their seat (Quality
Standards §D11: nothing that moves the class out of its seats or onto the walls). At each, they write on their
card one line, by Level: Levels 2-3, one verb from the story and one word for what happened; Levels 4-5, one
thing they now know about that device that they could not have guessed from the title. Then three or four
writers are cold-called to read their favorite sentence from their own piece aloud, one per Level where possible.
Nothing is collected for grading; the copies go home.

### Phase 3: Closing Transfer Check (25 min)

One check (Section 0.8), on cards, a demonstration rather than self-report: **this Set's own skill applied to
something new.** A standalone Set has one Spotlight and one Check, and the Check covers the skill the Spotlight
named in Lesson 1: telling what happened with something of your own, in order, with how it turned out and why.
There is no second, separate verb task, which belongs only to a Module Pair's Lesson 8, where the shared piece
was written in the other Module's genre.

**The object: your own pen or pencil.** A third object, used nowhere else in the Set (Lesson prompt 0.1a); every
student has one in front of them, and a student without one borrows a classmate's for the task, managed by the
teacher and not printed. In pairs, each student writes on a card:

- **Level 2:** "I ___-ed it." about your pen, with one regular verb spelled from sound for something that really
  happened with it (dropped, borrowed, packed).
- **Level 3:** "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." with two actions in the order they happened.
- **Level 4:** three sentences in order using at least two time words, ending with how it turned out and why ("I
  borrowed one from Ana because mine ran out in the middle of the test").
- **Level 5:** the same three sentences plus one detail that lets your partner guess how you felt, with no
  feeling word. The partner reads it and says the guess out loud; a wrong guess sends the writer back for a
  sharper detail.

**Transfer share (inside the 25 min):** the teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to read a card aloud.
Demonstration only: no thumbs, no hands, nothing collected.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** capture two or three of the read-aloud instances on the board
under the Day 1 before/after board, one per Level where possible, so the class sees the same skill on a pen that
it saw on a device.

   implied-feeling demand.
