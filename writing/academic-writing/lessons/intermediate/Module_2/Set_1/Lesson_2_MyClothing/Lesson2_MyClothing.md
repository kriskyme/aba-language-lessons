## Grammar focuses

**Focus B (this lesson's centerpiece, Day 1, whole class):** complex sentences with a time clause (when,
before, after, while, as soon as), so that two events sit in one sentence in the order they happened, with the
comma after an opening time clause and no comma when the clause comes second. This is the sentence-variety
half of the Level 4 row's pair, chosen by hand for Module 2 (Lesson prompt 0.4, 0.9): Level 4's row asks for
a sequence of events with the student's own connectors, and a time clause is the second way, after Focus A's
time words, to show order. Confusable pair: then / than (Focus A taught "then"; Module 1 taught "than").

**Level 5 extension, folded into the same mini-lesson (never a separate session):** the past continuous for
background ("I was waiting for the bus when the rain started") and adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully,
quietly, quickly), two ways a detail can imply a feeling without naming it. Taught to the whole room in the
Focus B mini-lesson; Level 5 practices them in its own tiers.

**Frame-regime content (Levels 2-3):** the same two frames; today's shapes are better of two and pair judgment
(Day 1) and spell from sound and plan the words (Day 2), per the Lesson prompt's 0.4d bank.

### Phase 1: Focus B mini-lesson: time clauses (20 min)

Tie it to Focus A first: "Last time you put events in order with time words: first, then, after that. Today
you put two events in one sentence, with a time clause, so the reader knows which came first without a
list."

_Time clauses (10 min)._

| Time word | What it says | Example (clause first, comma) | Example (clause second, no comma) |
|---|---|---|---|
| when | at that moment | When I got home, I tried it on again. | I tried it on again when I got home. |
| before | this happened first | Before I left for school, I zipped it up. | I zipped it up before I left for school. |
| after | this happened second | After I washed it, I hung it on the line. | I hung it on the line after I washed it. |
| while | at the same time | While I was waiting for the bus, the rain started. | The rain started while I was waiting for the bus. |
| as soon as | right after | As soon as it dried, I folded it. | I folded it as soon as it dried. |

The time clause has its own subject and verb, so it is a clause, not a phrase ("After that" is a time phrase
from Lesson 1; "After I washed it" is a clause). Comma rule: a comma after the clause when it opens the
sentence, none when it comes second.

_Level 5 extension, taught to everyone (6 min)._

| Tool | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Past continuous for background | was / were + verb-ing sets the scene; the simple past gives the event that interrupts it | I was running for the bus when my phone slipped out of my pocket. |
| Adverbs of manner | adjective + -ly says how an action was done (careful -> carefully, slow -> slowly, quiet -> quietly, quick -> quickly); fast and hard do not change | I folded it slowly. / I put it on quickly and left. |

A reader guesses a feeling from how something was done: "I folded it slowly and put it at the bottom of the
drawer" tells more than "I was sad."

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live (4 min):** ask a volunteer for two things that happened with
their item, in order ("I found it in the closet. I put it on."). The class builds one complex sentence on the
board with a time word nobody has used yet, first with the clause opening (and its comma), then with the
clause second (and no comma).

### Phase 2: Controlled practice B (30 min)

**Activity 1: Mark each sentence CX (it has a time clause) or N (it does not); underline the time clause, and
add the comma where it is missing (whole class, 8 min).** Compound sentences and time phrases are in the mix
as decoys, so a student has to find a clause with its own subject and verb.

> 1. Last winter I bought this jacket at the market. (N)
> 2. When I got home I tried it on again. (CX; comma after "home")
> 3. I wore it every day, and it kept me warm. (N; compound)
> 4. Before I left for school, I zipped it up. (CX; comma present)
> 5. I dropped it on the bus while I was looking for my ticket. (CX; no comma, the clause is second)
> 6. After that I washed it twice. (N; "after that" is a time phrase, not a clause)
> 7. As soon as it dried I folded it. (CX; comma after "dried")
> 8. My friend wore hers first, so I wore mine. (N; compound)

**Activity 2, Levels 4-5: Combine each pair into one sentence with a time word (12 min).** Use the time word in
parentheses where one is given; where two are given, choose the one that fits the order. Write four of the
eight with the clause first and four with it second, and punctuate each correctly.

> 1. I found the hoodie in the closet. I put it on. (as soon as)
> 2. I was waiting for the bus. The rain started. (while)
> 3. I washed it. I hung it on the line. (after)
> 4. I zipped it up. I went out. (before)
> 5. My mother saw the hole. She sewed it that night. (when)
> 6. I tried on three sweaters. I chose this one. (after / before)
> 7. I wore it to the game. My friend wanted one too. (when / as soon as)
> 8. I paid for it. I saw the same one cheaper across the street. (after)

Answer key (one accepted form each; the other order of the two clauses is also correct with the comma rule
applied): 1 As soon as I found the hoodie in the closet, I put it on. 2 While I was waiting for the bus, the
rain started. 3 After I washed it, I hung it on the line. 4 Before I went out, I zipped it up. 5 When my
mother saw the hole, she sewed it that night. 6 After I tried on three sweaters, I chose this one (or: Before I
chose this one, I tried on three sweaters). 7 When I wore it to the game, my friend wanted one too. 8 After I
paid for it, I saw the same one cheaper across the street. Item 6's two options force a choice about which
event the time word attaches to.

**Activity 3, Level 5 heavier tier (same 12 minutes, after Activity 2):** take your sentences for items 2, 5,
and 8 and rewrite each so that it opens with a past-continuous background ("I was standing at the counter when
...") and contains one adverb of manner that lets a reader guess how the writer felt, with no feeling word.
Under each, write the feeling a reader would guess, in one word, for the teacher to check against the adverb.

**Activity 2-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min), better of two, then pair judgment (0.4d).** Working
from the Draft Word Bank and a piece of clothing as they know it:

- **Better of two (both Levels):** three printed items, each a one-line fact about a piece of clothing and then
  two frame sentences about it. Circle the sentence that tells the reader more exactly what happened, and
  write the verb that made it better on the line. Level 2 items: (1) "The shirt was wrinkled, not dirty. I
  cleaned it. / I ironed it." (ironed); (2) "The scarf went around my neck twice. I wore it. / I wrapped it."
  (wrapped); (3) "The hoodie went into my bag for the trip. I carried it. / I packed it." (packed). Level 3
  items, two-slot: (1) "The sweater went in the sink, then on the line. I washed it and dried it. / I cleaned
  it and washed it." (washed it and dried it: two actions, real order); (2) "The scarf goes around, then the
  ends get knotted. I tied it and wrapped it. / I wrapped it and tied it." (wrapped it and tied it); (3) "The
  shirt came off the line and went in the drawer. I folded it and washed it. / I dried it and folded it."
  (dried it and folded it).
- **Pair judgment (Level 3; Level 2 as its lighter receptive tier):** six printed two-slot pairs about a
  hoodie. Level 3 circles the pairs whose two verbs are two different actions in a real order and crosses out
  the ones that repeat an action or put it out of order; Level 2 circles the pairs that name two different
  things. Pairs: washed it and dried it (circle); cleaned it and washed it (cross out: repeat); folded it and
  packed it (circle); packed it and folded it (cross out: order); picked it and carried it (circle); tied it
  and tied it (cross out: repeat).

The teacher circulates and hears each student say why the circled sentence or pair is the better one.

### Phase 3: Confusable pair and mixed editing (25 min)

**Activity 4: Choose then or than (whole class, 6 min).** "Then" says what came next; "than" follows a
comparing word from Module 1. Items 7 and 8 carry two blanks each, one of each.

> 1. First I tried on the hoodie, (then / than) I tried on the sweater.
> 2. The sweater was warmer (then / than) the hoodie.
> 3. I looked at the price, and (then / than) I put it back.
> 4. My friend's jacket is longer (then / than) mine.
> 5. I washed it first, (then / than) I dried it.
> 6. After a wash it looked better (then / than) new.
> 7. The gray one was cheaper (then / than) the red one, so (then / than) I bought it.
> 8. I zipped it up, and (then / than) I ran, faster (then / than) the bus.

Answer key: then, than, then, than, then, than, than / then, then / than.

**Activity 5, Levels 4-5: Find and fix the 8 mistakes (pairs, then whole class, 14 min).** Errors mix Focus A
(past forms), Focus B (time-clause commas), and then / than. A student's second paragraph about a hat:

> One morning I finded this hat on the bus seat next to me. When I picked it up I saw a name inside. Then I
> looked around, but nobody claim it. I carryed it in my bag for a week, than I wore it to school. Before I put
> it on I brushed it. As soon as my friend seen it, she laughed. I still wear it because it is warmer then my
> old one.

Answer key, 8 errors verified by count: finded -> found; comma after "When I picked it up"; claim -> claimed;
carryed -> carried; than -> then (what came next); comma after "Before I put it on"; seen -> saw; then -> than
(after "warmer"). Seven sentences; "she laughed," "but nobody," and "because it is warmer" carry no error of
their own, and sentence 6's comma is correct, a decoy for a student adding commas everywhere.

**Level 5 addition to Activity 5:** after fixing, rewrite sentence 1 so it opens with a past-continuous
background ("I was riding the bus home when I found this hat on the seat next to me."), and add one adverb of
manner to sentence 5.

**Levels 2-3 during Activity 5 (oral editing pass, 14 min):** read the three sentences you circled in Phase 2
to a partner, one at a time. For each, the partner says what happened first (Level 3: which of the two
actions came first, and how they can tell). If the partner cannot tell, say the sentence again with a time
word from the bank in front of it ("First I washed it.").

### Phase 1: Spell from sound / required-feature warm-up (25 min)

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), spell from sound (0.4d):** verbs only, no frame. Partners take turns: the partner
picks three verbs from three different verb groups of the Draft Word Bank and reads them aloud one at a time,
with the -ed ending; the student writes each verb without looking at the bank, then checks it against the
bank and fixes any letter (the doubled consonant in zipped, the i in carried). Swap roles.

- **Level 2:** three verbs written from sound and checked.
- **Level 3:** three verbs written from sound and checked; then number the two that would go together in one
  frame in the order they would really happen, and say why to the partner.

**Activity 6, Level 4 (required-feature warm-up, on your own kept paragraph):** re-read your own first
paragraph, then write two sentences about things that really happened with the item, each with a time word
from the bank (one that places it, one that orders it), and one outcome sentence with a reason word (because,
since, as, given that) that says how it turned out. Then join the two event sentences into one complex
sentence with a time clause, punctuated by the rule. Three sentences plus one combined sentence, four lines.

**Activity 7, Level 5 (implied-feeling warm-up, on your own kept paragraph):** re-read your own first
paragraph, then write two sentences about one moment with the item, each carrying one concrete detail and no
feeling word (no happy, sad, love, hate, nervous). One of the two must use a past-continuous background or an
adverb of manner from Day 1. Trade with a partner and ask, for each sentence: "What does this detail make you
think I felt?" Write the partner's guess under each sentence. A wrong guess means the detail is not doing its
job yet; note which one to replace.

### Phase 2: Planning Module 2's part against the kept paragraph (30 min)

Not prewriting on a new object: every student already has paragraph 1 (or the describing sentence), and the
plan here is for the part Module 2 adds to it, on the same item. Students take the kept part from their folder
and re-read it once before planning, so the story matches the description (the same item, the same details
where they overlap). Nothing from Lesson 1's packet is needed (Quality Standards §D9). This planning is
rehearsal: Lesson 3 builds its own quick plan inside its own packet, so no task here says "for your draft" or
"next time."

**What paragraph 2 is:** one real thing that happened with the item, told in order. Two choices, printed for
every Level to circle: **the day I got it** / **one day I wore it that mattered.**

- **Level 2, plan the words (0.4d):** circle one of the two choices. On the Draft Word Bank, write three verbs
  that really happened with the item on that day, from three different groups. Circle the one verb you would
  put in the frame, and tell a partner why that verb and not the other two.
- **Level 3, plan the words:** circle one of the two choices. Write three verbs that happened on that day, from
  three different groups. Number the two you would put in the two-slot frame in the order they happened; the
  pair must pass Day 1's test (two different actions, real order). Say the sentence aloud to a partner.
- **Level 4:** circle one of the two choices, then fill a three-line plan: "First ___" / "Then ___" / "Finally
  ___" (one event on each line, in the order it happened), and under it an outcome line, "How it turned out:
  ___, because ___." Then mark, with a bracket, the two events that could go in one sentence with a time
  clause.
- **Level 5:** circle one of the two choices, then list four or five events in the order they happened. Under
  the list, one line: "Why it mattered: ___." Circle the one event or detail in the list that most clearly hints
  at how you felt, and write under it: "What I want the reader to guess: ___." Then read the list to a partner
  without the last line; the partner says what they guess, and a miss shows which detail to sharpen.

### Phase 3: Plan share and board synthesis (20 min)

Two or three students from each task Level share one verb (Levels 2-3) or one event and its time word (Levels
4-5); Level 5 sharers give the event, not the feeling.

**Board-dependent moment, synthesized live:** a two-column class board, "Time words we used" / "How it turned
out," built only from what students share, so it holds phrasings no single handout has ("the first cold day,"
"on the way home"; "it shrank," "I still wear it"). Level 2-3 verbs go under the Draft Word Bank group they
belong to. The board is this lesson's; Lesson 3 does not depend on it.
