Grammar: Two Events in One Sentence (Time Clauses)

Time words like first, then, and after that put events in order as a list. A time clause puts two events in one sentence, so the reader knows which came first without a list. It starts with when, before, after, while, or as soon as, and it has its own subject and verb.

Time wordWhat it saysClause first (comma)Clause second (no comma)
whenat that momentWhen I got home, I tried it on again.I tried it on again when I got home.
beforethis happened firstBefore I left for school, I zipped it up.I zipped it up before I left for school.
afterthis happened secondAfter I washed it, I hung it on the line.I hung it on the line after I washed it.
whileat the same timeWhile I was waiting for the bus, the rain started.The rain started while I was waiting for the bus.
as soon asright afterAs soon as it dried, I folded it.I folded it as soon as it dried.

Comma rule: put a comma after the time clause when it opens the sentence. When the clause comes second, there is no comma.

A time clause has its own subject and verb. "After that" is a time word with no verb, so it is not a clause; "After I washed it" is a clause.

Grammar: Setting the Scene

Two ways to let a reader guess how you felt without writing a feeling word:

ToolRuleExample
Past continuous for backgroundwas / were + verb-ing sets the scene; the simple past gives the event that interrupts itI was running for the bus when my phone slipped out of my pocket.
Adverbs of manneradjective + -ly says how an action was done (careful, carefully; slow, slowly; quiet, quietly; quick, quickly). Fast and hard do not change.I folded it slowly. / I put it on quickly and left.

A reader guesses a feeling from how something was done. "I folded it slowly and put it at the bottom of the drawer" tells more than "I was sad."

A Word That's Easy to Mix Up

then vs. than

FormMeaningExample
thenwhat came nextI washed it, then I dried it.
thanfollows a comparing word (warmer than, longer than)The sweater was warmer than the hoodie.

Test: if the word says what happened next, write "then." If a comparing word comes right before it, write "than."
