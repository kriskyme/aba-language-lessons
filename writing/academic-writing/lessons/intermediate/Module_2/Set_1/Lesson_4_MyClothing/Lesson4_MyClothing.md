## What this lesson works from

Every student arrives with both parts of the piece, checked once at Lesson 3 Day 2: Levels 4-5 Module 1's
descriptive paragraph and Module 2's story paragraph about one piece of their own clothing; Levels 2-3 a
describing frame sentence and a past frame sentence. Below, "the item" means that piece of clothing. It need
not be present (Quality Standards §D8). No new grammar is taught, and Lesson 3 Day 2's checklist is not run
again: this lesson assembles the piece, trades it, revises it for effect, and publishes it. The revision moves
use only what the Pair already taught:

- **Module 2 Focus A (Lesson 1):** past forms (-ed rules; the irregular board) and time connectors.
- **Module 2 Focus B (Lesson 2):** complex sentences with a time clause and the comma rule; then / than.
- **Level 5 extension (Lesson 2):** past-continuous background; adverbs of manner.
- **Module 1's tools:** comparatives with a reason word; compound sentences with and / but / so; it's / its;
  hedges and although at Level 5; the "It is ___." frames.

**Draft Word Bank, Module 2 (reprinted; posted on the board and printed in the packet).** Levels 2-3 draw their
verbs from it; Levels 4-5 use it for the upgrade move.

> **Care verbs:** washed, folded, packed, dried, cleaned, ironed
> **Wearing verbs:** zipped, buttoned, tied, wrapped, tucked, stretched
> **Getting verbs:** picked, wanted, needed, ordered, carried, saved
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last winter, one morning
> **People and places:** my mother, my friend, the store, the market, school, the bus, the rain

**Output targets for the finished piece (Section 0.2), checked by the teacher on the final copy:** Level 2, two
frame sentences (one describing, one past); Level 3, two two-slot frame sentences; Level 4, two paragraphs of
3-6 sentences each (40-90 words each); Level 5, two paragraphs of 6-9 sentences each (90-150 words each). The
ranges are the teacher's; students count nothing today.

## DAY 1: ASSEMBLE, TRADE AND CHECK, REVISE FOR EFFECT (75 MIN)

```
0              25                            60         75  (minutes)
|---Phase 1-----|-----------Phase 2-----------|-Phase 3--|
| Assemble the  | Revise for effect with       | Revision  |
| whole; Peer   | Module 2's tools, before/    | share     |
| Editing       | after evidence               |           |
```

### Phase 1: Assemble the whole piece, then Peer Editing (25 min)

**Assemble (7 min).** Every student writes a title on the packet's title line (the item's name: "My Jacket,"
"My Gray Scarf"), then puts the two parts in order under it: Levels 4-5 lay the first paragraph above the
second and number them 1 and 2 in the margin; Levels 2-3 number their describing sentence 1 and their past
sentence 2. This is the first time the piece exists as a whole; nothing is copied yet.

**Peer Editing exchange (18 min), the Pair's one exchange (Section 0.6),** covering both Modules' features.
Partners trade whole pieces and read them once through. Then the reader fills the printed form on the writer's
page:

_Levels 4-5, the form:_
1. In paragraph 1, underline the comparison and circle its reason word.
2. In paragraph 2, number the time words in the order they appear, and put a box around the sentence that says
   how it turned out.
3. (Reading a Level 5 piece) Write the feeling you guess from paragraph 2 and the sentence that told you.
4. One specific compliment: one sentence you would keep exactly as it is, copied out.
5. One specific suggestion: one thing you want to know more about, or one place where you could not tell
   what happened first.

_Levels 2-3, the form (oral, with two written words):_ the reader reads both sentences aloud, says what the
item is like and what happened to it, and writes on the writer's page one word for the part or event they
would like a second sentence about ("Tell me about: the ___" / "What happened next?").

The pieces go back. This is Peer Editing, not a reader's request: it has the form, the compliment, and the
suggestion, and it runs once in the Pair, here.

**Teacher-facing:** while pairs read, note two or three suggestions to use in Phase 3.

### Phase 2: Revise for effect with Module 2's tools (35 min)

Every change is visible (cross out, do not erase) and, at Levels 4-5, recorded in the before/after table
printed in the packet:

| What I first wrote | What I changed it to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

**Level 4, three moves, on the whole piece (most will land in paragraph 2):**

1. **Join.** Find two event sentences that belong together and join them with a time clause (when, before,
   after, while, as soon as), comma by the rule; or turn a "then" sentence into a time-clause sentence. If
   paragraph 2 already has a time-clause sentence, join a different pair, or join two sentences in paragraph 1
   with and / but / so.
2. **Upgrade.** Find one plain verb (got, put, went, had) and replace it with a more exact one from the Draft
   Word Bank or your own (ordered, tucked, carried).
3. **Answer the reader.** Add the event or detail your partner asked for on the form, with a real time word
   in it. Stay inside 6 sentences per paragraph: if adding it makes 7, join two sentences or cut the weakest.

Each move is one row of the table; three rows at this Level.

**Level 5, four moves (heavier tier, same 35 minutes):** moves 1-3 above, where move 2 may instead add an adverb
of manner or a past-continuous background to a plain event sentence, plus:

4. **Reorder.** Number the sentences of paragraph 2 in the margin. Move one sentence so that the outcome, or
   the sentence that says why the day mattered, lands last, and make sure the implied-feeling detail still
   sits just before it, where it carries the most weight. Stay inside 9 sentences.

Four rows of the table at this Level; the "Why" column for the reorder row says what the new position does.

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), the reader's part, then choose (0.4d):** this day produces one more past frame
sentence, about the part or event the reader asked for ("Tell me about the zipper" / "What happened next?"),
sounded out and, at Level 3, with two actions in a real order. Then the student reads both past sentences to a
partner and circles the one that tells the most about what happened; the circled sentence is the one that goes
under the describing sentence in the final copy. Answering the reader and choosing between two of their own
sentences is this Level's revision move. This is the lesson's one complete-the-frame task.

**Foundation Support** (alongside Level 2, not in place of it): a student who cannot yet spell from sound says
the verb, the teacher writes it on a card, and the student copies it into the frame and says what happened to
the item.

**Teacher circulation:** three visible changes on every Level 4 page (four at Level 5) with a filled table row
for each; two past sentences on every Level 2-3 page with one circled. A change that only corrects (a comma, a
spelling) is not one of the three; send the student back to the move list.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a before/after board in two columns. Two or three volunteers
read one row of their table aloud (Level 5 first, since the reorder row is the hardest to hear; then one Level
4 join or upgrade; then one Level 2-3 student reads their two past sentences and says which they circled and
why). The teacher writes each "before" on the left and each "after" on the right, and the class names in one
line what the change did (put two events in one sentence; a sharper verb; answered the reader; put the ending
last). The board stays up for Day 2.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY, PUBLISH, CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0                    25                    50                   75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1-------|-------Phase 2-------|-------Phase 3------|
| Final copy of the   | Publish / share     | Closing Transfer   |
| whole piece         | (gallery walk)      | Check + share      |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy of the whole piece (25 min)

Students write a clean copy of the finished piece on the packet's lines: the title, then Levels 4-5 both
paragraphs with every Day 1 change in place, capitals and periods intact, paragraph 2 starting on a new line;
Levels 2-3 the describing sentence, then the circled past sentence, on two lines under the title. No
proofread checklist and no counting task (the piece was checked once, at Lesson 3 Day 2); the teacher's
circulation check is that each Day 1 table row shows up in the copy and that the two parts read as one piece.

### Phase 2: Publish / share (25 min)

The piece is genuinely finished. **Gallery walk:** every final copy lies open on its desk. Students walk with a
small card and stop at two pieces that are not their partner's; at each, they read the whole piece and write
on their card one line, by Level: Levels 2-3, one word they learned about that item and one verb from the
story; Levels 4-5, one thing they now know about the item that they could not have guessed from the title.
Then, back in seats, three or four writers are cold-called to read their favorite sentence from their own
piece aloud, one per Level where possible. Nothing is collected for grading; the copies go home or onto the
class wall.

### Phase 3: Closing Transfer Check (25 min)

Two short checks (Section 0.8), both on cards, both demonstrations: first the two skills of the Pair together
on a new object, then Module 2's own skill on a new prompt. Neither object appears elsewhere in either Set.

**Check 1, both skills together: your own key** (one key each student carries; a classmate's if a student has
none with them, managed by the teacher and not printed). In pairs, each student writes on a card:

- **Level 2:** "It is ___." about your key, with a describing word spelled from sound (small, silver, old),
  then "I ___-ed it." with one thing you did with it (dropped, packed, saved).
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___." with two different observations, then "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." with two
  actions in the order they happened.
- **Level 4:** one sentence comparing your key to your partner's with a reason word and a real reason ("My key
  is smaller than Lena's, because it opens a locker, not a door"), then one sentence telling something that
  happened with it, with a time word ("Last week I dropped it under the bus seat").
- **Level 5:** the same two sentences, then a third with one concrete detail about the moment that lets your
  partner guess how you felt, with no feeling word. The partner reads it and says the guess out loud; a wrong
  guess sends the writer back for a sharper detail.

**Check 2, Module 2's own skill on a new prompt: this morning, from waking up to arriving here.** On the same
card, on the other side:

- **Level 2:** "I ___-ed." with one regular verb spelled from sound (walked, brushed, packed).
- **Level 3:** "I ___-ed and ___-ed." with two actions in the order they happened.
- **Level 4:** three sentences in order using at least two time words, ending with how the morning turned out
  and why ("I was late because I missed the first bus").
- **Level 5:** the same three sentences plus one detail that lets a partner guess how you felt, with no
  feeling word; the partner says the guess aloud.

**Transfer share (inside the 25 min):** the teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to read a card aloud (one
side each). Demonstration only: no thumbs, no hands, nothing collected.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** capture two or three of the read-aloud instances on the board
under the Day 1 before/after board, one per Level where possible, so the class sees the same two skills on a
key and a morning that it saw on a piece of clothing.
