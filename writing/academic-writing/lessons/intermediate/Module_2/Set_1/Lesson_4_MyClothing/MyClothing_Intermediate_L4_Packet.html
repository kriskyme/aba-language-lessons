Unit 4A: Telling a Story About My Clothing

Writing Narrating Intermediate 2.1.4.0

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Word Bank
Care verbs: washed · folded · packed · dried · cleaned · ironed
Wearing verbs: zipped · buttoned · tied · wrapped · tucked · stretched
Getting verbs: picked · wanted · needed · ordered · carried · saved
Time words: first · then · next · after that · finally · last winter · one morning
People and places: my mother · my friend · the store · the market · school · the bus · the rain
Put It Together

Task A. Give your piece a title and put its two parts in order.

  1. 1.Write a title on the line: the name of your piece of clothing ("My Jacket," "My Gray Scarf").
  2. 2.Put the two parts of your piece in order under the title: the part that describes the clothing first, the part that tells what happened with it second.
  3. 3.Write 1 in the margin next to the first part and 2 next to the second. If your parts are paragraphs, number the paragraphs; if your parts are sentences, number the sentences.
Trade and Check

Trade whole pieces with a partner. Read your partner's piece once all the way through. Then do the Task that matches your partner's piece, writing on your partner's page. When you are both done, give the pieces back.

Task B. ★ Read your partner's two sentences and ask for one more.

  1. 1.Read both sentences aloud to your partner.
  2. 2.Say what the clothing is like, and say what happened to it.
  3. 3.On your partner's page, write one word for the part or the event you would like a second sentence about. Use one of these: Tell me about: the . / What happened next?

Make It Stronger

Anything you change: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task F. ★ Answer your partner, then choose your best.

  1. 1.Find what your partner asked about on your page (a part of the clothing, or what happened next).
  2. 2.Write one more sentence about it, spelling the verb by its sounds: I -ed it.
  3. 3.Read both of your "I ___-ed it" sentences (this one and the one from before) to a partner.
  4. 4.Circle the one that tells the most about what happened. That is the one that goes in your final copy.

Task G. ★★ Answer your partner, then choose your best.

  1. 1.Find what your partner asked about on your page (a part of the clothing, or what happened next).
  2. 2.Write one more sentence about it, with two actions in the order they really happened, spelling each verb by its sounds: I -ed it and -ed it.
  3. 3.Read both of your two-action sentences (this one and the one from before) to a partner.
  4. 4.Circle the one that tells the most about what happened. That is the one that goes in your final copy.

Task H. ★★★ Make three changes that make your story stronger.

  1. 1.Find two event sentences that belong together and join them with a time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon as), with a comma where the rule puts it; or turn a "then" sentence into a time-clause sentence. If paragraph 2 already has a time-clause sentence, join a different pair, or join two sentences in paragraph 1 with and, but, or so. Cross out the old two and write the new one.
  2. 2.Find one plain verb (got, put, went, had). Cross it out and write a more exact one from the Word Bank or your own (ordered, tucked, carried).
  3. 3.Add the event or detail your partner asked for, with a real time word in it. Stay inside 6 sentences in each paragraph: if you now have 7, join two sentences or cut the weakest.

Share Your Writing

Your piece is finished. Now the class reads it.

Task E. Walk the room and read two finished pieces.

  1. 1.Leave your final copy open on your desk.
  2. 2.Take a small card and walk around the room.
  3. 3.Stop at two pieces that are not your partner's. At each one, read the whole piece, then write one line on your card (Tasks F to I say what to write).
  4. 4.Back in your seat, choose the sentence from your own piece that you like best, ready to read aloud.

Wrap It Up

Sit with a partner. Take a card. Write on one side about your own key, then on the other side about this morning. Read each side to your partner.

Your Key

Task J. ★ Write two sentences about your key.

  1. 1.Write "It is ___." with a describing word you spell by its sounds (small, silver, old).
  2. 2.Write "I ___-ed it." with one thing you did with it (dropped, packed, saved).

Task K. ★★ Write two sentences about your key.

  1. 1.Write "It is ___ and ___." with two words that say two different things about it.
  2. 2.Write "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." with two things you did with it, in the order they happened.

Task L. ★★★ Compare your key to your partner's, then tell one thing that happened with it.

  1. 1.Write one sentence comparing your key to your partner's, with a reason word (because, since, as, or given that) and a real reason. Example: My key is smaller than Lena's, because it opens a locker, not a door.
  2. 2.Write one sentence telling something that happened with it, with a time word. Example: Last week I dropped it under the bus seat.

Task M. ★★★★ Compare your key to your partner's, tell one thing that happened with it, then add a detail.

  1. 1.Write one sentence comparing your key to your partner's, with a reason word (because, since, as, or given that) and a real reason.
  2. 2.Write one sentence telling something that happened with it, with a time word.
  3. 3.Write a third sentence with one concrete detail about that moment that lets your partner guess how you felt, with no feeling word.
  4. 4.Your partner reads it and says the guess out loud.
  5. 5.If the guess is wrong, write a sharper detail.

This Morning

On the other side of the card, write about this morning, from waking up to arriving here.

Task N. ★ Write "I ___-ed." with one thing you did this morning, spelling the verb by its sounds (walked, brushed, packed).

Task O. ★★ Write "I ___-ed and ___-ed." with two things you did this morning, in the order they happened.

Task P. ★★★ Tell what happened this morning, in order.

  1. 1.Write three sentences in order, using at least two time words.
  2. 2.End with how the morning turned out and why. Example: I was late because I missed the first bus.

Task Q. ★★★★ Tell what happened this morning, in order, then add a detail.

  1. 1.Write three sentences in order, using at least two time words, ending with how the morning turned out and why.
  2. 2.Add one detail that lets your partner guess how you felt, with no feeling word.
  3. 3.Your partner reads it and says the guess out loud.
