Unit 3A: Telling a Story About My Clothing

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.3.0

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Word Bank
Care verbs: washed · folded · packed · dried · cleaned · ironed
Wearing verbs: zipped · buttoned · tied · wrapped · tucked · stretched
Getting verbs: picked · wanted · needed · ordered · carried · saved
Time words: first · then · next · after that · finally · last winter · one morning
People and places: my mother · my friend · the store · the market · school · the bus · the rain

Use these words if you need them. You may also use your own.

Write It

Write about the clothing: one real thing that happened with it. When you finish, be ready to read one line aloud to someone working at a different star level.

Task C. ★ Complete the frame: choose your verb, sound it out, and write it.

  1. 1.Pick two verbs from the Word Bank that could go in the blank: or
  2. 2.Which one is the thing that really happened first, and why? Say it to a partner in one phrase (washed, because it was muddy).
  3. 3.Sound out that verb, ending included, and write it: I -ed it.
  4. 4.Read your line to a partner. Your partner says in one phrase what happened to the clothing. Is that what you meant?

Task D. ★★ Complete the frame: choose your two verbs, sound them out, and write them.

  1. 1.Pick two verbs from the Word Bank that could go in the first blank: or
  2. 2.Which one is the thing that really happened first, and why? Say it to a partner in one phrase.
  3. 3.Pick a second verb that is a different action, the one that happened next (washed it and dried it, not cleaned it and washed it).
  4. 4.Sound out both verbs, endings included, and write them in the order they happened: I -ed it and -ed it.
  5. 5.Read your line to a partner. Your partner says which action came first. Is that what you meant?

Task E. ★★★ Write your second paragraph, one sentence per step. Aim for 3-6 sentences.

  1. 1.Open with when it happened and what you did first, in one sentence (Last winter I bought this jacket at the market.).
  2. 2.Write the next thing that happened, with a time word from the Word Bank in front (then, next, after that).
  3. 3.Write one more event, with a different time word.
  4. 4.Join two of the events into one sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as. Put a comma after that part if it comes first. This can replace two of your sentences or be a new one.
  5. 5.Close with how it turned out and why, using because, since, as, or given that (I still wear it because it is the only one that fits over my hoodie.).

Count your sentences and write the number:

Task F. ★★★★ Write your second paragraph: list the events, put them in order, then write. Aim for 6-9 sentences.

  1. 1.List four or five things that happened with it that day, in the order they happened.
  2. 2.Circle the one event or detail that most clearly hints at how you felt.
  3. 3.Write your paragraph, putting the circled detail where it carries the most weight, usually near the end. You may use a background sentence (I was standing at the counter when ...) or a word like slowly or carefully where it fits.
  4. 4.End with one sentence that says why the day mattered (That day mattered because it was the first thing I bought with my own money.).
  5. 5.Check: does any sentence name the feeling (happy, sad, love, hate, nervous, proud)? "I was so happy" does not count; a specific detail does.

Share a Line

Get ready to read one line from your piece aloud.

  • ★Read your completed line.
  • ★★Read your completed line, then say which action came first.
  • ★★★Read your sentence that says how it turned out, or your sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as if you have written it.
  • ★★★★Read the sentence with your circled detail. Do not say what feeling it shows.

While you listen: write down one verb or time word from each line that you would want in your own piece. For a four-star line, write the feeling you guess instead.

Unit 3B: Telling a Story About My Clothing

Writing · Narrating Intermediate 2.1.3.0

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, and say how it turned out and why.

Check and Improve Your Writing

Put your first paragraph (or your first sentence) above your second one. Anything you change: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task A. ★ Check both parts, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Read your describing sentence and your new sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: which part of the clothing is the describing word about? Then, what happened to the clothing?
  3. 3.Is that what you meant?
  4. 4.Does my verb end in -ed?
  5. 5.Is there a verb in the Word Bank that says more exactly what happened than the one I chose (ironed instead of cleaned, packed instead of carried)? If yes, cross out your old verb.
  6. 6.Write the whole new sentence on the line below. If your verb was already the exact one, leave it and put a check next to it.
  7. 7.Read the result to your partner.

Task B. ★★ Check both parts, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Read your describing sentence and your new sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: which part of the clothing is each describing word about? Then, which action came first?
  3. 3.Are my two verbs two different actions, in the order they happened?
  4. 4.Do both verbs end in -ed?
  5. 5.Is there a verb in the Word Bank that says more exactly what happened than one of the two I chose (ironed instead of cleaned, packed instead of carried)? If yes, cross out the old verb.
  6. 6.Write the whole new sentence on the line below. If both verbs were already the exact ones, leave them and put a check next to each.
  7. 7.Say your sentence to your partner with a time word from the Word Bank in front (First I washed it and dried it.), so your partner hears when it happened.

Task C. ★★★ Check both parts, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Does my first paragraph have one comparison (-er or more) and a reason word (because, since, as, given that)? Circle the comparing word and underline the reason word.
  2. 2.Does my first paragraph's joined sentence have the comma before and, but, or so, and are "it's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) used correctly?
  3. 3.Is every verb in my second paragraph in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form (wore, got, bought, took)?
  4. 4.Do at least two time words put the events in order, the order things really happened? Circle each time word.
  5. 5.Does one sentence say how it turned out, with a reason word? Underline it.
  6. 6.Does my sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as have a comma when that part comes first, and no comma when it comes second? Put a box around the comma.
  7. 7.Is "then" used for what came next, and "than" only after a comparing word?
  8. 8.Are punctuation and capital letters correct in both paragraphs, and does my second paragraph have 3-6 sentences?
  9. 9.Make each change you marked (a verb left in the present, a missing time word, a comma in the wrong place, a "than" that should be "then").
  10. 10.Add one more event or one real detail to your second paragraph. Stay inside 6 sentences.

Task D. ★★★★ Check both parts, then make your story better.

  1. 1.Does my first paragraph have one comparison (-er or more) and a reason word (because, since, as, given that)? Circle the comparing word and underline the reason word.
  2. 2.Does my first paragraph's joined sentence have the comma before and, but, or so, and are "it's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) used correctly?
  3. 3.Is every verb in my second paragraph in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form (wore, got, bought, took)?
  4. 4.Do at least two time words put the events in order, the order things really happened? Circle each time word.
  5. 5.Does one sentence say why the day mattered, and does one detail hint at how I felt with no feeling word (happy, sad, love, hate, nervous, proud) anywhere in it? Underline the first and circle the second.
  6. 6.Does my sentence with when, before, after, while, or as soon as have a comma when that part comes first, and no comma when it comes second? Put a box around the comma.
  7. 7.Is "then" used for what came next, and "than" only after a comparing word?
  8. 8.Are punctuation and capital letters correct in both paragraphs, and does my second paragraph have 6-9 sentences?
  9. 9.Is there at least one background sentence (I was ... when ...) or one word like slowly or carefully? If not, mark where one could go.
  10. 10.Make each change you marked.
  11. 11.If you found a feeling word, cross out that sentence and write a real detail (what you did, and how) that lets the reader guess the feeling. If you did not, make your circled detail sharper with a word like slowly or carefully, or a background sentence. Stay inside 9 sentences.
