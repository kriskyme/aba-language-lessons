## What this lesson carries forward

**The piece so far.** Every student has Module 1's kept part in their folder: Levels 4-5 a descriptive
paragraph about one piece of their own clothing, Levels 2-3 one describing frame sentence. Below, "the item"
means that piece of clothing. This lesson writes **the second part**, in Module 2's form: Levels 4-5 a second
paragraph telling one real thing that happened with the item, in order, with how it turned out and why; Levels
2-3 a second sentence in the past frame. Neither part is a complete piece on its own; Lesson 4 assembles the
two. The item is a subject, not a prop (Quality Standards §D8): it need not be in the room, and nothing asks
the student to look at it.

**Grammar already taught (Module 2's Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** Focus A: regular -ed with
its spelling rules, the irregular list on the board (wore, got, bought, chose, put, took, found, lost, gave,
saw, paid, was / were), and time connectors that order or place events. Focus B: complex sentences with a
time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon as), the comma after an opening clause, and then / than.
Level 5 extension: past-continuous background and adverbs of manner. Module 1's tools stay in force for the
kept paragraph and are named again in Day 2's merged checklist: comparatives with a reason word, compound
sentences with and / but / so and the comma, it's / its, the hedges and although at Level 5.

**Nothing carried from Lesson 2 (Quality Standards §D9).** Lesson 2 Day 2's planning was rehearsal. Every plan
this lesson uses is built in Day 1 Phase 1 inside this packet. The kept paragraph is the one carried piece,
and it is needed on Day 2 only.

## DAY 1: DRAFTING THE SECOND PARAGRAPH (75 MIN)

```
0      10                              60         75  (minutes)
|Phase 1|------------Phase 2------------|-Phase 3--|
| Quick | Independent/guided drafting:   | Mid-draft |
| plan  | the second paragraph           | share-out |
```

### Phase 1: Quick plan (10 min)

Every student circles, on the printed list in the packet (my jacket, my hoodie, my sweater, my shirt, my
T-shirt, my dress, my scarf, my hat), the piece their first paragraph is about; the story is about the same
item. Then, every Level: circle one of the two printed choices, **the day I got it** / **one day I wore it that
mattered**, and write three Draft Word Bank verbs that happened with it on that day, on the line under the
choices. That is the whole printed plan (Quality Standards §E6: actions with a visible product, no
questions). Level 4 also writes, on one more line, how it turned out and the reason ("I still wear it because
..."). Level 5 builds its ordered event list inside its drafting task in Phase 2, not here.

**Teacher-facing, not printed:** while circulating, check that each plan already carries its Level's required
feature: a verb that really happened (Level 2), two verbs in a real order (Level 3), an outcome with a reason
(Level 4). If not, ask what happened first, or how the day ended.

### Phase 2: Independent/guided drafting, the second paragraph (50 min)

Every student writes the real second part of the piece. The teacher circulates continuously; the circulation
prompts below are what to check while the draft is in progress, not only at the end.

**Levels 2-3 (frame-based), the real frame(s), once.**

- Level 2 completes "I ___-ed it." for their own item, spelling one regular verb from sound with its -ed
  ending. Before the verb goes on the page, the student says it aloud to the teacher, sound by sound, ending
  included. The teacher listens for the sounding-out, not for a perfect spelling.
- Level 3 completes "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." the same way, two verbs, each sounded out. The teacher checks
  that the two verbs are two different actions in the order they happened before the second verb is written.
- **Genuine-choice moment (both Levels).** Each student picks two candidate verbs from the bank for one slot
  (for example "washed" and "dried"), says to the teacher or a partner which one is the thing that really
  happened first and why in one phrase ("washed, because it was muddy"), and writes that one. The verb is a
  choice the student makes and can defend, not a foregone answer.
- **When the frame is complete:** the student reads the line to a partner, who says in one phrase what
  happened to the item (Level 3: which action came first), and the student says whether that is what they
  meant. The frame is written once today (Lesson prompt 0.4d); Day 2's improvement round is different in kind
  (a more exact verb), and Lesson 4's reader's request adds a further sentence.
- **Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 2, not in place of it):** a student who cannot yet spell from
  sound chooses the slot verb from three printed Draft Word Bank verbs in the packet (the teacher reads them
  aloud), then traces it into the frame; or dictates the verb to the teacher, who scribes it, then the student
  says which written verb is which when the teacher reads two of them back.

**Level 4 (single paragraph), a five-step drafting path.** Steps, not a frame: each sentence is the student's
own, and each step uses only a tool from Lessons 1-2.

1. Open with when it happened and what you did first, in one sentence ("Last winter I bought this jacket at
   the market.").
2. Write the next thing that happened, with a time word from the bank in front (then, next, after that).
3. Write one more event, with a different time word.
4. Join two of the events into one complex sentence with a time clause (when, before, after, while, as soon
   as), punctuated by the rule. This can replace two of the sentences from steps 1-3 or be a new sentence.
5. Close with how it turned out and why, using a reason word (because, since, as, given that): "I still wear it
   because it is the only one that fits over my hoodie."

Circulation check: by the halfway point of the block, does the draft already contain two time words and every
verb in the past? If not, direct the student to steps 1-3 before they write anything else. The draft is
completed today: in the last five minutes of the block Levels 4-5 count their sentences and write the number
at the bottom of the page (students count sentences only; the word range in the targets table is the
teacher's circulation check, Style Guide §E), and a count at the bottom of the range is a signal to add one
more event.

**Level 5 (extended paragraph), list, order, then draft.** Before writing sentences, students list four or five
events of that day on the short lines in their drafting task, in the order they happened, and circle the one
event or detail that most clearly hints at how they felt. Then draft, building the paragraph around the
circled detail so that it lands where it carries the most weight, usually late, and ending with one sentence
that says why the day mattered ("That day mattered because it was the first thing I bought with my own
money."). A past-continuous background ("I was standing at the counter when ...") and adverbs of manner
(slowly, carefully) are available where they fit; they are not required. Remind students directly: no
sentence may name the feeling ("I was so happy" fails the task; a specific, well-chosen detail succeeds).

Circulation spot test: read one detail sentence from the draft and ask, "What would a stranger guess you felt
that day from this sentence alone?" If the student cannot answer, or answers "nothing," the detail is not yet
doing its work; if the student names a feeling word that appears in the sentence, it is stated, not implied.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share-out (15 min)

Each student prepares one line to read, by Level, then a few students from each Level read aloud:

- **Level 2:** read your completed frame.
- **Level 3:** read your completed frame, then say which action came first.
- **Level 4:** read your outcome sentence, or your time-clause sentence if it is written.
- **Level 5:** read the sentence that carries your circled detail, without saying what feeling it implies.

**Task for every listener:** on a scrap of paper, write one verb or time word from each read line that you
would want in your own piece; for a Level 5 line, write the feeling you guess instead. The teacher takes two
or three of these guesses aloud after each Level 5 line, so the writer hears whether the detail landed.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** a running "strong sentence" board, one line per Level column, built
live from the share-out. It stays up for Day 2.

## DAY 2: MERGED SELF-EDIT AND IMPROVE, OVER BOTH PARTS (75 MIN)

```
0          25                        55                      75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1--|---------Phase 2---------|--------Phase 3---------|
| Merged     | Make it better           | Share-out / final       |
| checklist  | from the marks           | line check              |
```

The second part is complete from Day 1. Day 2 opens directly on checking, and the checklist is **merged**: it
covers both parts, since the kept paragraph has not been checked since Module 1 and the two must read as one
piece. Students take the kept part from their folder and lay it above the new part. Phases 1 and 2 below are
printed as one section, "Check and Improve Your Writing," with one Task per star holding that Level's check
items for both parts and then its improvement steps (Style Guide §F); the packet has no "finish your piece"
task and no "from last time" wording (Quality Standards §D9).

### Phase 1: Merged self-edit checklist pass (25 min)

Students check both parts against their Level's list and mark each item as they find it in the text (circle
the time words, underline the outcome sentence, box the comma after a time clause; in the first paragraph,
circle the comparative and underline the reason word). One list per Level, printed as one lettered Task per
star; the cross-out rule applies to every Level and is stated once before the lists: anything changed is
crossed out with a single line, not erased, and rewritten next to or above it (Section 0.4a).

**Level 2 (oral):** read your describing sentence and your new sentence to a partner. Your partner says which
part of the item the describing word is about, and then what happened to the item. Is that what you meant?
Does your verb end in -ed?

**Level 3 (oral):** read both sentences to a partner. Your partner says which part each describing word is
about, then which action came first. Are your two verbs two different actions, in the order they happened? Do
both end in -ed?

**Level 4 checklist:**

_First paragraph (Module 1's part)_
1. It has one comparison with a comparative form (-er or more) and one reason with a reason word (because,
   since, as, given that). Circle the comparative, underline the reason word.
2. Its compound sentence has the comma before and / but / so, and it's / its are used correctly.

_Second paragraph (this Module's part)_
3. Every verb is in the past: -ed spelled by the rule, or the irregular form from the board.
4. At least two time words put the events in order, and they are in the order things really happened. Circle
   each time word.
5. One sentence says how it turned out, with a reason word. Underline it.
6. My time-clause sentence has its comma when the clause comes first, and no comma when it comes second.
7. "Then" is used for what came next; "than" only after a comparing word.
8. Punctuation and capitalization are correct in both paragraphs, and my second paragraph has 3-6 sentences.

**Level 5 checklist:** items 1-4 and 6-8 as above (item 8's count is 6-9 sentences), plus:

5. One sentence says why the day mattered, and one detail implies how I felt with no feeling word (happy, sad,
   love, hate, nervous, proud) anywhere in it. Underline the first, circle the second.
9. I have at least one past-continuous background ("I was ... when ...") or one adverb of manner, or I have
   marked where one could go.

### Phase 2: Make it better from the marks (30 min)

Every student improves the piece from their own marks, one lettered Task per star in the packet. Visible
revision throughout: cross out, do not erase.

- **Level 2:** is there a Draft Word Bank verb that says more exactly what happened than the one chosen (ironed
  instead of cleaned, packed instead of carried)? If yes, cross out the old verb and write the whole new
  sentence on the line below; if the first verb was already the exact one, leave it and put a check next to it.
  Read the result to a partner.
- **Level 3:** the same more-exact-verb check on both slots, then say the sentence to a partner with a time word
  from the bank in front of it ("First I washed it and dried it."), so the partner hears when it happened.
- **Level 4:** make every change you marked in Phase 1 (a verb left in the present, a missing time word, a
  comma in the wrong place, a "than" that should be "then"). Then add one more event or one concrete detail to
  the second paragraph, staying inside 6 sentences.
- **Level 5:** make every change you marked. If Phase 1 found a feeling word, replace that sentence with a
  concrete detail (what you did, and how) that lets the reader guess the feeling; if not, sharpen the circled
  detail with an adverb of manner or a background clause. Stay inside 9 sentences.

The teacher circulates for the same features as Day 1: every verb past and two time words on every Level 4
page, an outcome with a reason underlined, no feeling word on any Level 5 page, a sounded-out verb rather than
a copied one at Levels 2-3.

### Phase 3: Share-out / final line check (20 min)

In pairs, each student reads only the last line of the second part to a partner (Levels 2-3: the completed
frame; Levels 4-5: the closing sentence). The partner answers one question, by Level:

- **Levels 2-3:** "What happened to the item?" The partner says it.
- **Level 4:** "Does the last sentence say how it turned out and why, or does it just add one more event?"
- **Level 5:** "What do you think the writer felt that day, and which sentence told you?"

Writers note the answer in one word in the margin. A mismatch (the partner cannot say what happened, or
guesses no feeling at all) is the first thing to fix in Lesson 4's revision.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** return to Day 1's "strong sentence" board and add newly completed
closing lines volunteered from the pair check, at least one per Level.

**What this lesson does not do.** There is no Peer Editing exchange, no publishing, and no Closing Transfer
Check at this position; the piece is not yet assembled. Every student leaves Day 2 with both parts checked
once and the second part improved. Lesson 4 assembles the whole, runs Peer Editing on both Modules' features,
revises for effect, and publishes.
