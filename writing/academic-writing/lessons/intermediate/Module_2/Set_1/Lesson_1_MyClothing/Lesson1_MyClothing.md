## What this lesson works from

Every student arrives with Module 1's kept part, unmarked, in their folder: Levels 4-5 a self-revised
descriptive paragraph about one piece of their own clothing (circled from the printed list: jacket, hoodie,
sweater, shirt, T-shirt, dress, scarf, hat); Levels 2-3 one describing frame sentence about it. Below, "the
item" means whichever piece the student wrote about. The item is a subject, not a prop (Quality Standards
§D8): it need not be in the room, and nothing asks the student to look at it. The phone case, Module 1's
practice object, appears in this lesson only as Maria's Mentor Ladder on Day 2.

This lesson drafts nothing. It re-engages the piece, teaches Module 2's first grammar focus, and shows on the
ladder what Module 2 will add: a second paragraph (Levels 4-5) or a second sentence (Levels 2-3) that tells one
thing that happened with the item, in order, with how it turned out and why. Module 1's tools stay available
and are not re-taught: comparatives with a reason word (because, since, as, given that); compound sentences
with and / but / so and the comma; it's / its; the hedges and although at Level 5; the "It is ___." frames.

## Draft Word Bank, Module 2 (teacher-provided; posted on the board and printed in every Module 2 packet)

The Set's five groups, refilled for narrating. Levels 2-3 draw their verbs from the three verb groups (every
verb there is regular, so its past form ends in -ed and can be spelled from sound); Levels 4-5 may use any
group when a word is not coming to mind. The irregular past forms Focus A teaches sit in the grammar box, not
here.

> **Care verbs:** washed, folded, packed, dried, cleaned, ironed
> **Wearing verbs:** zipped, buttoned, tied, wrapped, tucked, stretched
> **Getting verbs:** picked, wanted, needed, ordered, carried, saved
> **Time words:** first, then, next, after that, finally, last winter, one morning
> **People and places:** my mother, my friend, the store, the market, school, the bus, the rain

(32 words. The verb groups hold 18 verbs for one- and two-verb frame tasks, so no bank is exhausted by a task;
padding per Quality Standards §C4.)

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class):** the simple past for a sequence of events: regular -ed
with its three spelling rules, a short list of irregular past forms the Scenario needs, and time connectors
that put events in order (first, then, next, after that, finally) or place them (last winter, one morning,
yesterday), with the comma after an opening time phrase. This is the Level 4 row of the Lesson prompt's 0.4
bank, chosen by hand for Module 2's purpose (the bank's Alternate column is not yet authored); Intermediate's
native Paragraph Composition Level, so no row-selection correction applies. Level 4's required feature (a
sequence of past events with one outcome and a reason) needs it directly; Level 5's "why it mattered" and
implied feeling are layered on top of it in Lesson 2. Module 1's reason words are reused for the outcome's
reason and are not re-taught.

**Frame-regime content, folded into the same mini-lesson (Levels 2-3, never a separate session):** the past
frame "I ___-ed it." (Level 2) and the two-slot past frame "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." (Level 3), with the
-ed ending spelled as part of the verb; the two slots hold two different actions in the order they happened.
Verbs come from the Draft Word Bank's three verb groups.

**Level 5 heavier tier (Day 1 and Day 2):** placing an outcome sentence in time with an opening time phrase, and
combining a time connector, an outcome, and a reason word in one sentence, using only what this
lesson has taught; the fuller "why it mattered" work and the implied-feeling tools (past continuous, adverbs
of manner) are Lesson 2's Level 5 extension.

### Phase 1: Re-engagement and Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Re-engagement (8 min), not a hook.** Students take Module 1's kept part out of their folder and re-read it
silently, once. Then the teacher gives a short recap from the board, in these four lines, not a student note:

1. "Your piece is about one piece of your own clothing, for a classmate who has never seen it."
2. "What is written: a paragraph (or a sentence) that says what it is like, by comparing it to something else
   and giving a real reason. That part stays exactly as it is."
3. "What the last lessons added: comparing words, reason words, and sentences joined with and, but, so."
4. "What we add now: a second paragraph (or a second sentence) that tells one thing that happened with it: the
   day you got it, or one day you wore it that mattered. In order, with how it turned out and why."

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "Today and tomorrow we're practicing telling what happened with something of our
own, in order, and saying how it turned out and why." One sentence, the same for every Level (Quality
Standards §D1). The one-line reminder of Module 1's skill: "You already know how to describe it by comparing
it and giving a reason; that skill stays in your first paragraph." Level 5's implied-feeling feature is
introduced inside its own tasks, not here.

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: the simple past and time connectors (30 min)

_Regular past forms: the three spelling rules (8 min)._

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: add -ed | wash -> washed, fold -> folded, want -> wanted |
| One vowel + one consonant at the end: double the consonant, add -ed | drop -> dropped, zip -> zipped, plan -> planned |
| Consonant + y at the end: change y to i, add -ed | try -> tried, carry -> carried, dry -> dried |
| Verb already ends in -e: add -d only | tie -> tied, save -> saved, order -> ordered (no double r: two vowels before it) |

Point out the two rules students most often break (drop is not "droped"; try is not "tryed"), and that the -ed
ending sounds like /t/ (washed), /d/ (tied), or /id/ (wanted) but is always written -ed.

_Irregular past forms the story will need (7 min)._ Recognition list, read aloud once, kept on the board all
Set: wear -> wore, get -> got, buy -> bought, choose -> chose, put -> put, take -> took, find -> found, lose
-> lost, give -> gave, see -> saw, pay -> paid, is / are -> was / were. These do not take -ed, and a student
who needs one writes it from the board.

_Time connectors (10 min)._

| Job | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Put events in order | first, then, next, after that, finally | First I tried on the black hoodie. Then I saw the gray one. |
| Say when it happened | last winter, one morning, yesterday, on the first day | Last winter I bought this scarf at the market. |
| Comma | optional after a short opening time phrase; this Set writes it without one | Last winter I bought this scarf. / One morning I dropped it on the bus. (Lesson 2 teaches the comma that is required, after an opening time clause.) |

A story needs one word that says when it started and at least two words that keep the events in order. The
outcome sentence (how it turned out) uses a reason word from Module 1, because, since, as, or given that, and
those are not new: "I still wear it because it has never torn."

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** ask a volunteer to name their item and one thing that happened
with it, in three words ("bought, market, winter"). The class builds the first sentence together on the board
("Last winter I bought this jacket at the market."), then a second one that must come next in time, with a
time connector nobody has said yet.

_Frame content, folded in (5 min)._ Show the Draft Word Bank, then the frame "I ___-ed it." Elicit two verbs
orally from the whole class about their own items (a care verb, then a wearing verb), sounding out the -ed
ending each time. Then show "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." and elicit a pair that names two different actions in
the order they happened (washed it and folded it, not washed it and cleaned it). Say once that the frame is
the model for the day; every later task just says "the frame."

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A, part 1 (30 min)

**Activity 1: Fill in the blank with the past form (whole class, 10 min).** Each sentence is about a piece of
clothing of the kind students own.

> 1. Last winter I ___ (buy) this hoodie at the market.
> 2. First I ___ (try) it on in the store.
> 3. Then my mother ___ (pay) for it.
> 4. I ___ (wear) it to school the next day.
> 5. One morning I ___ (drop) it in the mud.
> 6. After that I ___ (wash) it twice.
> 7. Finally I ___ (fold) it and ___ (put) it away.
> 8. Last week I ___ (find) it at the bottom of the drawer.

Answer key: bought, tried, paid, wore, dropped, washed, folded / put, found. Items 1, 3, 4, 7 (second blank),
and 8 come from the irregular board; 2, 5, 6, 7 (first blank) use the three spelling rules.

**Activity 2: Choose the correct form (whole class, 8 min).** Both options are forms a student might really
write; the wrong one breaks a spelling rule or treats an irregular verb as regular.

> 1. Last spring I (buyed / bought) this dress.
> 2. I (tryed / tried) it on twice.
> 3. One morning I (droped / dropped) my hat on the bus.
> 4. My friend (carryed / carried) my jacket home.
> 5. I (weared / wore) it every day in January.
> 6. I (zipped / ziped) it up before I went out.
> 7. My mother (gived / gave) me this scarf.
> 8. I (washt / washed) it by hand.

Answer key: bought, tried, dropped, carried, wore, zipped, gave, washed.

**Activity 3, Levels 4-5 (12 min): Classify each sentence as a sequence sentence (S: a time word puts an event
in order or in time), an outcome sentence (O: how it turned out, with a reason word), or both (SO); then
underline the time word and circle the reason word where there is one.** Sentences use every time connector
and all four reason words, so the classifying step requires reading the whole sentence.

> 1. First I looked at the blue jacket, then I looked at the black one. (S)
> 2. I was late for class because I could not find my scarf. (O)
> 3. After that I put on my hoodie, so I was warm on the bus. (S; "so" is a joining word, not a reason word)
> 4. One morning I found a hole in the pocket. (S)
> 5. The sweater shrank since I washed it in hot water. (O)
> 6. Finally I bought the gray one, because it was cheaper. (SO)
> 7. Next I folded it and packed it. (S)
> 8. My hat got wet, as I left it on the porch all night. (O)

Item 3 is the trap: "so" joins a result but is Module 1's compound connector, not a reason word, so the
sentence is S, not SO. Discuss it last.

**Activity 3b, Level 5 (heavier tier, inside the same 12 min):** rewrite sentences 2, 5, and 8 so each opens
with a time phrase of two or more words ("Yesterday morning I was late for class because I could not find my
scarf."), turning each O sentence into an SO sentence. Built only from what Phase 2 taught;
this is Level 5's own Day 1 task, not a share of Level 4's.

**Activity 3-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min), choose what fits, then the one guided round (0.4d):**
each student first circles, in the three verb groups of the Draft Word Bank, every verb that really happened
with their own item and crosses out the ones that never did (a genuine choice against the item as they know
it). Then Level 2 completes the frame three times, one circled verb from a different group each round (a
care verb, a wearing verb, a getting verb), sounding out each verb with its -ed ending, and reads each
finished sentence to a partner. Level 3 completes the two-slot frame three times, each round a pair of
circled verbs from two different groups in the order they happened ("I picked it and carried it."), so the
two slots always carry two separate actions in a real order. The teacher circulates and listens to each
student sound out the verb before writing it. This is the lesson's one complete-the-frame task; Day 2's
frame-regime work takes a different shape.

### Phase 1: Editing a paragraph on Focus A (20 min)

**Activity 4: Find and fix the 8 mistakes (pairs, then whole class, 20 min).** The paragraph is a student's
second paragraph about a scarf, the kind of piece this Module writes.

> Last winter I buyed this scarf at the market. First I looked at a red one, then I choosed the gray one because
> it was warmer. I weared it every day in January. One morning I droped it on the bus, and a woman gave it back
> to me. Before that I tyed it tighter. I still have it, and I carryed it in my bag all spring. It is faded now,
> but it keep me warm.

Answer key, 8 errors verified by count: buyed -> bought; choosed -> chose; weared -> wore; droped -> dropped;
Before that -> After that (the tying came after the drop; the connector puts the events in the wrong order);
tyed -> tied; carryed -> carried; it keep -> it keeps (Module 1's -s rule, a present-tense sentence about the
item now). Seven sentences; "and a woman gave it back to me" and "because it was warmer" are correct, so
students cannot assume every clause hides a mistake. Five of the eight are past-form errors (three irregular
verbs treated as regular, two spelling-rule errors), one is a time-connector error, one an -s error, and one
more spelling-rule error (tyed).

### Phase 2: Frame practice / original sentences with Focus A (25 min)

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), Activity 5-equivalent, fix the wrong word (0.4d):** the frame-regime parallel to
the editing paragraph, not another frame round. Three printed items each state one fact about a piece of
clothing in a clause, then give a frame sentence about it with a verb that does not fit the fact or is
misspelled; the student crosses out the wrong verb and writes the right one from the Draft Word Bank.

- **Level 2:** (1) "My scarf stays flat in a drawer. I zipped it." (cross out zipped; folded); (2) "The jacket
  has a zipper up to the chin. I buttoned it." (zipped); (3) "I cleaned the shirt in the sink. I washt it."
  (misspelled; washed).
- **Level 3:** the pair must name two different actions in a real order and be spelled right: (1) "First I got
  the shirt wet in the sink, then I hung it on the line. I dried it and washed it." (wrong order; washed it
  and dried it); (2) "I put the sweater in the sink, then I hung it on the line. I washed it and cleaned it."
  (cleaned repeats washed; washed it and dried it); (3) "I zipped the jacket, then I did the top button. I
  zipped it and buttonned it." (misspelled; buttoned).

Answer keys are above; the teacher circulates and hears each replacement verb sounded out.

**Activity 5, Level 4: write one original sentence using each target item**, about your own item: bought,
first, then, wore, finally, because. At least two sentences must contain both a time connector and a past
verb, and at least one must be an outcome sentence with its reason.

**Activity 6, Level 5 (heavier tier, same 25 minutes):** write three original sentences about your own item,
each combining a time connector, an outcome (how something turned out), and a reason word (since, as, or given
that, not only because). Example shape: "After that I wore it to every practice, since it was the only warm
one I had." One of the three must open with the reason clause and its comma ("Given that it was the only warm
one I had, I wore it to every practice after that."). Then, for one of the three, write a second version with
the time connector removed, and note in one line what a reader can no longer tell (which event came first).
This uses only what Day 1 taught; Lesson 2 builds the "why it mattered" work on it.

### Phase 3: Mentor Ladder second look (30 min)

Present the same four Mentor Texts from Module 1's Lesson 1 (Maria and her plain blue phone case), in Level
order, on the board or read aloud, each now completed by its Module 2 part, so the class sees once what a
finished piece looks like. A before/after framing, not a new ladder: for each Level, show Module 1's model
first, then the added part, and ask what the new part tells that the old one could not. The ladder is the
teacher's material and is not printed in the packet (packet prompt 2.6).

**Level 2 Mentor Text, completed**

> Module 1's part: It is blue.
> Frame: I ___-ed it.
> Maria's answer: I dropped it.

**Level 3 Mentor Text, completed**

> Module 1's part: It is blue and thin.
> Frame: I ___-ed it and ___-ed it.
> Maria's answer: I dropped it and cleaned it.

**Level 4 Mentor Text, completed (second paragraph)**

> Last spring I bought this case at a small shop near my school. First I picked a black one, but then I saw the
> blue one on the bottom shelf. I tried both on my phone, and the blue one fit better. After that I paid and
> put it on right there. I still have it because it has never cracked.

(5 sentences, 61 words: inside the 40-90 word, 3-6 sentence target. Time connectors: last spring, first, then,
after that. Outcome with its reason: "I still have it because it has never cracked." Two compound sentences
carry Module 1's and / but, unchanged tools.)

**Level 5 Mentor Text, completed (second paragraph)**

> One morning last winter I was running for the bus when my phone slipped out of my coat pocket. It hit the
> sidewalk hard and slid under a parked car. I stood there for a moment, then I got down on my knees on the wet
> ground and reached for it. The blue case had a new scrape along one edge, but the screen was fine. I turned
> the phone over twice before I put it back in my pocket. That night I sat at the kitchen table and rubbed the
> scrape with my thumb for a long time. This case mattered that day because it did its job, and I have not
> taken it off since.

(7 sentences, 117 words: inside the 90-150 word, 6-9 sentence target. Events in order: the fall, the search,
the check, the evening. Why it mattered is stated in the last sentence. The implied feeling is carried by
turning the phone over twice and rubbing the scrape for a long time; no feeling word appears. "I was running
... when" and "before I put it back" preview Lesson 2's time clauses and Level 5's past-continuous background,
the way Module 1's Level 4 model previewed and / but / so.)

**Walkthrough (teacher-led, from the board):** for each Level, name in one line what the added part does (one
past action; two actions in order; events in order with an outcome and its reason; a day that mattered, with a
feeling the reader has to guess). Students find their own Level's model and the one directly above it on the
board, and say to a partner one thing the next model up does that theirs does not. Nothing here is printed.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a "what Module 2 adds at each Level" board built from
student observations ("Level 2 says one thing that happened. Level 3 says two, in order. Level 4 tells the
whole story and how it ended. Level 5 tells why it mattered and makes us guess how Maria felt."). It stays up
for Lesson 2.
