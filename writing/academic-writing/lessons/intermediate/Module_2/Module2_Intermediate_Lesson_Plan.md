## Scenario: My Charger, then My Device

Every student tells a reader one real thing that happened with something they use every day — what happened, in
what order, how it turned out, and why. **Purpose (Module 2, Narrating):** recount a sequence of past events with
an outcome and a brief reason for it, choosing their own time connectors, so a reader who was not there can
follow what happened and knows why it ended the way it did.

- **Practice object (Lessons 1-2 and the Mentor Ladder): my charger.** Grammar input, controlled practice, the
  frame rounds, and every Mentor Text use it. Chosen for Narrating because a charger generates real, small,
  ordered events with outcomes — forgot it, lent it and got it back, it stopped working, bought another, found
  the old one afterwards — where a purely descriptive object would not. It is a **subject, not a prop** (Quality
  Standards §D8): it is written about from memory, and no task asks the student to bring it, hold it, take it
  out, or look at it.
- **Draft object (introduced at Lesson 2 Day 2's prewriting; Lessons 3-4 write about it): my device.** One
  everyday device the student really owns, circled from a printed list of eight so nobody is stuck without one:
  **my earbuds, my speaker, my watch, my calculator, my flashlight, my remote control, my game controller, my
  mouse.** The list deliberately spans price points and includes several items costing very little, so no student
  is asked to write about something they do not have. Category title and slug for Lessons 3-4: "My Device." The
  Draft Word Bank is built for the category, not one device.
- **Closing Transfer Check object (Lesson 4 Day 2, a third object, used nowhere else in the Set):** the
  student's own pen or pencil.
- **No named reader and no second audience.** A named reader with a stated concern is Level 6's required feature
  and two distinct readers is Level 8's (Lesson prompt 0.2); Intermediate reaches neither. Its reader is the
  partner in Lesson 4 Day 1's Peer Editing exchange and the classmates who read the published piece.

**Both objects are writable at every task Level.** Levels 2-3 need a past-tense action that really happened with
the object (lost it, packed it, charged it, dropped it, lent it, fixed it, cleaned it) — every item on the draft
list supplies several. Levels 4-5 need a sequence with an outcome and a reason, which each object supplies from
ordinary use rather than from anything dramatic; Level 5 additionally needs a detail that implies a feeling
without naming it, and small everyday objects are unusually good for this, since the feeling shows in what the
student did with their hands rather than in an event.

**The practice object is never drafted about** (Lesson prompt 0.1a), and the draft object does not change after
Lesson 2 Day 2. The charger's last appearance is Lesson 2 Day 1.

## Grammar Focus A/B

**Retained unchanged from the superseded paired plan.** The de-pairing changes the Scenario, not the grammar:
this pairing is Module 2's own native content, it cleared the cross-Module check against Intermediate Module 1
above, and changing it would have discarded work for no stated reason. It remains **hand-selected at the Level 4
row**, since Lesson prompt 0.4's Grammar Focus Bank has no authored Alternate column — a gap already logged in
`Index.md`'s Pending work.

- **Focus A (Lesson 1, the Set's centerpiece): the simple past for a sequence of events.** Regular `-ed` with its
  three spelling rules; a short irregular list the Scenario actually needs (got, bought, chose, put, took,
  found, lost, gave, saw, paid, lent, was/were); and time connectors that order events (first, then, next, after that,
  finally) or place them (last winter, one morning, yesterday), with the comma after an opening time phrase.
- **Focus B (Lesson 2 Day 1, taught as its own point): complex sentences with a time clause** — when, before,
  after, while, as soon as — so two events sit in one sentence in the order they happened, with the comma after
  an opening time clause and none when the clause comes second. Different in kind from Module 1's Focus B
  (compound sentences with and/but/so): coordination there, subordination here.
- **Confusable pair (Lesson 2 Day 1): then / than.** See the Rotation Log check above for why this repeat is
  deliberate.
- **Level 5 extension, folded into the same mini-lesson and never a separate session:** the past continuous for
  background ("I was waiting for the bus when the strap broke") and adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully,
  quietly, quickly), which is what lets Level 5 imply a feeling through how an action was done.
- **Frames (Levels 2-3):** `I ___-ed it.` and `I ___-ed it and ___-ed it.`
- **Module 1's reason words are reused for the outcome, not re-taught.** A student who wrote "because" in Module
  1 uses it here to say why the outcome happened; no lesson in this Set teaches reason clauses as new content.

## Leveled Mentor Ladder direction (on the practice object, my charger)

Presented by the teacher on Lesson 1 Day 2, on the board or read aloud, in Level order; never printed in the
packet (Lesson prompt 0.1b, packet prompt 2.6). Intermediate has **4 Mentor Texts** and no Mentor Essay. All four
tell one model student's own charger story, so the class watches one real sequence climb four steps. This is the
ladder's **only** presentation — the paired version's Lesson 5 "second look" does not exist in a standalone Set.
Directions only; the texts themselves are written at generation time.

- **Level 2:** one regular past verb in `I ___-ed it.`, with the `-ed` visibly added to a base verb the class has
  just met, and the capital and period visible.
- **Level 3:** two verbs in the two-slot frame, two genuinely different actions in a real order, plus the
  counter-model the teacher names aloud: the same action twice, or two verbs naming one action from two angles,
  is not a sequence.
- **Level 4:** a short paragraph, 3-6 sentences, telling one thing that happened with the charger in order, with
  the outcome stated and one brief reason for it, and at least two different time connectors so the ladder shows
  connector *choice* rather than one connector repeated.
- **Level 5:** a longer paragraph, 6-9 sentences, whose outcome matters and whose feeling is carried by one
  concrete detail rather than named — the model detail is physical and small, and **no feeling word appears
  anywhere in it**, which the teacher names explicitly as the thing to notice.

The ladder is distinct in kind across the four Levels: one action told as a sentence, two actions ordered against
each other, a sequence with an outcome and a reason, and a sequence whose feeling is implied. It stays on the
charger throughout; the device never appears in it (0.1a: the ladder models form, not content).

## Task-Level basis (`learningobjectives.csv`, Modality = Writing, Module 2: Narrating)

| Level | Regime | Description (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Guided Frame Composition | Can spell the open-slot verb in the fixed frame ("I ___-ed.") from sound to narrate a single past action, prompted by a picture. |
| 3 | Guided Frame Composition | Can spell both open-slot verbs in the two-slot past-tense frame from sound to narrate two sequential actions. |
| 4 (native) | Paragraph Composition | Can write a short original narrative recounting a sequence of past events with one outcome and a brief reason for it, choosing their own time connectors, working independently. |
| 5 | Paragraph Composition | Can write an extended narration of a personal event, stating why it mattered and including at least one detail that implies a feeling without naming it directly, working independently. |

**How each Level's own row is satisfied.** Levels 2-3: the fixed past-tense frames above, the verbs spelled from
sound; the rows' "prompted by a picture" is met by the student's own remembered event with their own object,
which Quality Standards §D8 ranks first among picture-or-object prompts, so **no image is fetched or printed
anywhere in this Set** and no `Set1_Intermediate_Image_Credits.md` is created. Level 4: the student chooses their
own time connectors, which is why Focus A teaches a range rather than a fixed one, and both the outcome and its
reason are required. Level 5: why it mattered is stated, and the implied-feeling detail is a hard requirement, not
an encouragement — the Level 5 extension (past continuous, adverbs of manner) exists to make it reachable.
