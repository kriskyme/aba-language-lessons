## Scenario (fixed by Module 1; inherited unchanged)

Every student writes about one piece of their own clothing. **Purpose across the Pair:** describe it by
comparing it to something else with a real reason (Module 1), then tell one real thing that happened with it,
in order, with how it turned out and why (Module 2), so a reader can picture the item and knows one story
about it.

- **Practice object (Lessons 1-2 and the Mentor Ladder): my phone case.** Used by Module 1's grammar work and
  by Maria's four Mentor Texts. In Module 2 it returns in exactly one place: Lesson 5 Day 2's Mentor Ladder
  second look, where each of Maria's models is completed by its second paragraph (still on her phone case, so
  the ladder stays one ladder). No Module 2 task drafts or practices on it.
- **Draft object (Lesson 2 Day 2 onward, and all of Module 2): my clothing,** one piece of the student's own
  clothing circled from the printed list of eight (my jacket, my hoodie, my sweater, my shirt, my T-shirt, my
  dress, my scarf, my hat). Module 2's Lessons 5-8 write about this object only (Lesson prompt 0.1a): every
  practice sentence, frame shape, plan, and draft is about the student's own item. Lesson 7's printed list
  says to circle the piece paragraph 1 is about; the kept paragraph is the one thing a Set carries (Quality
  Standards §D9).
- **Paragraph 2's subject (Module 2):** one real past event with the item: the day the student got it, or one
  day they wore it that mattered. Levels 4-5 choose which; Levels 2-3 narrate one (Level 3: two) past action
  with the item.
- **Draft Word Bank, Module 2:** the Set's five groups refilled for narrating, printed in every Module 2
  packet (the Module 1 bank of describing words is not reprinted; Lesson 7's merged checklist names the
  describing features but needs no bank for them). Groups: care verbs, wearing verbs, getting verbs, time
  words, people and places. About 30 words, with regular past forms in the verb groups so the frame Levels
  can spell them from sound, and the irregular forms Focus A teaches listed once in the grammar box, not in
  the bank.
- **Closing Transfer Check objects:** Module 1 used the partner's pen or pencil (Lesson 4). Module 2's Lesson
  8 uses **the student's own keys** for the combined check and **this morning, from waking up to arriving here**
  as the small new prompt for Module 2's own verb task (below). Neither is used anywhere else in either Set.

Both objects are writable at every Level: a phone case and a piece of clothing each yield one sounded-out word
in a frame (Levels 2-3), a comparison with a reason and a short story with an outcome (Level 4), and an
extended paragraph with an implied attitude or feeling (Level 5). The draft object sustains the whole arc: a
piece of clothing has a look (Module 1) and a history (Module 2).

## Genre / real-world writing form (Lesson prompt 0.9)

Module 1's row: **descriptive paragraph** (Levels 4-5), a fixed one/two-slot description frame (Levels 2-3).
Module 2 inherits the piece and its subject unchanged and adds its own paragraph in Module 2's form (Conventions

## Grammar Focus A/B (Module 1, as generated)

Anchored at **Level 4's row**, Intermediate's own native Paragraph Composition Level.

- **Focus A (Lesson 1):** comparative adjectives (-er / more, with the spelling rules and irregular better /
  worse / less) and reason clauses with because, since, as, given that (comma after an opening reason clause).
- **Focus B (Lesson 2 Day 1):** simple vs. compound sentences (and / but / so) with the comma before the
  connector. Confusable pair: it's / its.
- **Level 5 extension:** hedges (a bit, a little, seems, at least, probably) and the concession subordinator
  although / even though.
- **Frame content (Levels 2-3):** "It is ___." and "It is ___ and ___."; the third-person -s form.

## Grammar Focus A/B (Module 2, hand-selected; the bank's Alternate column does not exist)

Anchored at **Level 4's row** again (the same native Level), so the pair is chosen by hand from adjacent
content fitting Module 2's purpose (Lesson prompt 0.4, 0.9). Confirmed distinct from Module 1's above and
from Advanced Set 1's (Section 0).

- **Focus A (Lesson 5): the simple past for a sequence of events.** Regular -ed with three spelling rules
  (add -ed: washed, folded; one vowel + one consonant, double it: dropped, zipped; consonant + y, y to i:
  tried, carried), a short list of irregular past forms the Scenario needs (wore, got, bought, put, took,
  found, lost, gave, was / were), and **time connectors** that order events (first, then, next, after that,
  finally) and place them (last winter, one morning, yesterday, on the first day), with the comma after an
  opening time phrase. Module 1's reason words (because, since, as, given that) are reused for the outcome's
  reason and are not re-taught: one line in the grammar box says so.
- **Focus B (Lesson 6 Day 1): complex sentences with a time clause** (when, before, after, while, as soon as),
  the comma after an opening time clause and none when the clause comes second, so two events sit in one
  sentence in the order they happened. Confusable pair: **then / than** (Module 1 taught "than"; Focus A
  teaches "then").
- **Level 5 extension (Lesson 6):** past continuous for background ("I was waiting for the bus when the rain
  started") and adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully, quietly, quickly) as the tools for implying a feeling
  through a detail rather than naming it.
- **Frame content (Levels 2-3):** Level 2 "I ___-ed it." (the CSV's single-action frame "I ___-ed." with the
  item as its object so the frame stays on the draft object); Level 3 "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." (the two-slot
  past-tense frame, two sequential actions). Regular verbs only, spelled from sound with their -ed ending; the
  frame is printed once per day as the model and referred to as "the frame" after that.

## CSV-objective coverage (Pair): Lesson 8's separate Module 2 verb task

The shared piece's paragraph 2 is narrative, but Module 2's own row is exercised on a new prompt as well, in
Lesson 8 Day 2's Closing Transfer Check, after the combined check on the keys. **Prompt: this morning, from
waking up to arriving here.** On a card:

- **Level 2:** "I ___-ed." with one regular verb spelled from sound (walked, brushed, packed).
- **Level 3:** "I ___-ed and ___-ed." with two actions in the order they happened.
- **Level 4:** three sentences in order using at least two time connectors, ending with one outcome and its
  reason (on time, late, hungry, tired: "I was late because I missed the first bus").
- **Level 5:** the same three sentences plus one detail that lets a partner guess how the writer felt, with no
  feeling word; the partner says the guess aloud.

## Leveled Mentor Ladder direction (on the practice object, Maria's phone case)

Presented by the teacher on Lesson 1 Day 2 (Module 1's ladder, as generated) and again on Lesson 5 Day 2 as a
second look, never printed.

- **Level 2:** Module 1: one describing word in "It is ___." (blue). **Second look adds:** one past action in
  "I ___-ed it." (I dropped it.), and asks what the new sentence tells that the first could not (something
  happened).
- **Level 3:** Module 1: two different observations in "It is ___ and ___." (blue and thin). **Second look
  adds:** two actions in order in "I ___-ed it and ___-ed it." (I dropped it and cleaned it.), asking which
  happened first and how the reader knows.
- **Level 4:** Module 1: a short paragraph with one comparison and one stated reason, on a simple-present
  backbone. **Second look adds:** a 3-6 sentence paragraph telling the day Maria bought the case, events in
  order with time connectors, one outcome, and its reason; the class names the time words and the outcome
  sentence.
- **Level 5:** Module 1: an extended paragraph with details in a logical order and one detail that implies an
  attitude. **Second look adds:** a 6-9 sentence narrative of one day the case mattered (the day the phone
  survived a fall), stating why it mattered and carrying one detail that implies a feeling without naming it;
  the class guesses the feeling and says which sentence told them.

The ladder is distinct in kind across the 3/4 boundary in both Modules: Levels 2-3 supply one or two verbs
into a frame; Levels 4-5 author a paragraph with no frame.
