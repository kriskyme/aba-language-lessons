Unit 1A: Describing My Phone Case

Writing Intermediate 1.1.1.6

Objective: I can describe something of my own by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison.

Word Bank
Color/pattern: blue · black · pink · clear · patterned · striped
Material/texture: soft · hard · smooth · rubbery
Size/thickness: thin · thick · big · small
Condition: new · scratched · worn · shiny · cracked
Quality: sturdy · cheap · light · heavy
Grammar

Comparing Two Things

RuleExample
Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -erthin → thinner, hard → harder
One syllable, ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant, add -erbig → bigger, thin → thinner
Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i, add -ershiny → shinier, heavy → heavier
Two-syllable adjectives not ending in -y: use "more"modern → more modern
Longer adjectives (3+ syllables): use "more"durable → more durable, expensive → more expensive
Irregular formsgood → better, bad → worse, little → less

Giving a Reason

WordExample
becauseI like this case because it's light.
sinceSince it's light, I take it everywhere.
asI take it everywhere, as it's light.
given thatGiven that I carry my phone all day, a light case matters most.

"Since" and "given that" can start the sentence. Put a comma after that opening part: Since it's light, I take it everywhere.

Use the frame "It is ___." with a word from the Word Bank. For "It is ___ and ___.", pick two words that say two different things (blue and thin, not thin and slim).

When you write a full sentence instead of "It is ___," the verb needs its own -s: it holds up well. / it looks new.
