Unit 1A: Describing My Phone Case

Writing Intermediate 1.1.1.0

Objective: You will describe your phone case by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Some of you will also describe it in a way that lets your reader guess how you feel about it, without ever saying the feeling out loud.

Word Bank

Color/pattern: blue, black, pink, clear, patterned, striped

Material/texture: soft, hard, smooth, rubbery

Size/thickness: thin, thick, big, small

Condition: new, scratched, worn, shiny, cracked

Quality: sturdy, cheap, light, heavy

Grammar

Comparing Two Things

RuleExample
Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -erthin → thinner
Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i, add -ershiny → shinier, heavy → heavier
Longer adjectives (2+ syllables): use "more"durable → more durable, colorful → more colorful
Irregular formsgood → better, bad → worse, far → farther, little → less

Giving a Reason

WordExample
becauseI like this case because it's light.
sinceSince it's light, I take it everywhere.
asI take it everywhere, as it's light.
given thatGiven that I carry my phone all day, a light case matters most.

See How It's Done

These examples all describe the same phone case, at every star level, so you can see what changes as the description grows.

★ Example

[Picture: a plain blue phone case]

It is blue.

★★ Example

[Picture: a plain blue phone case]

It is blue and thin.

★★★ Example

This is my phone case. It is blue and thin, thinner than my old one, because I wanted something that fits easily in my pocket. It has a strong grip, so it never slips out of my hand. I chose the case with the best reviews online, and I clean it once a week. It is more expensive than my old case, but that does not bother me.

★★★★ Example

My phone case travels everywhere with me. It's a deep blue with a corner that's gone soft and rounded, the kind of wear you get from years of dropping it on hardwood floors and shoving it into tight coat pockets. The stickers cover almost the whole back now: one from a hiking trail up north, one from a coffee shop that closed years ago, one my daughter picked out herself. I wipe it down two or three times a day, more out of habit than anything else at this point. Although a newer, sleeker case would probably protect my phone better, I've never once thought about switching it out.

What changes at each star level? One star says one word. Two stars say two words. Three stars explain why. Four stars make you guess how the writer feels.
