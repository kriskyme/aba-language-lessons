## Scenario

Every student describes a phone case: a real, familiar object nearly every student in the room owns, carries, or
can picture clearly, with more natural angles for comparison (color, thickness, durability, grip, price) and for
implied attitude (how worn it is, what it's been through, why they never replace it). This Scenario is shared
across all 4 lessons in this Set - the same object carried from grammar input (Lessons 1-2) through drafting
(Lesson 3) to a finished, published piece (Lesson 4). Level 2-3 students work from a single provided picture of a
phone case (color and style clearly visible). Level 4-5 students describe their own actual phone case, or the
same provided picture if they do not have one with them.

## Word Bank (teacher-provided, used throughout Lessons 1-2 and 4)

Levels 2-3 draw their describing word from this list rather than inventing one untaught, and Levels 4-5 may pull
from it too when a specific word isn't coming to mind. Post it on the board or hand it out alongside the picture.

> **Color/pattern:** blue, black, pink, clear, patterned, striped
> **Material/texture:** soft, hard, smooth, rubbery
> **Size/thickness:** thin, thick, big, small
> **Condition:** new, scratched, worn, shiny, cracked
> **Quality:** sturdy, cheap, light, heavy

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, all task Levels):** comparative adjectives (-er / more) and "because"
reason clauses, on a simple-present backbone. This is the Level 4 row from Section 0.4's bank, the row Levels 4
and 5 both build on: Level 4's required feature needs it directly, and Level 5's implied-attitude work is
layered on top of it, not built independently.

**Frame-regime warm-up (Levels 2-3, short slots inside both days, never the centerpiece):** the simple present
"is" frame, third-person singular -s spelling (Level 3), and light "and" coordination built into the two-slot
frame itself, drawing describing words from the Word Bank above.

## DAY 1: GRAMMAR FOCUS A, INPUT AND MODELING (75 MIN)

```
0        15                        45                    75  (minutes)
|-Phase 1-|--------Phase 2----------|-------Phase 3-------|
| Hook,   | Focus A mini-lesson:    | Controlled practice  |
| Skill   | comparatives + because  | A, part 1             |
| Spot-   | (+ frame warm-up fold-in)|                       |
| light   |                          |                       |
```

### Phase 1: Hook, Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Hook (8 min):** Hold up two phone cases that are easy to compare (a thin plain case and a thick decorated
case). Ask: "Which one is bigger? Which one would you rather carry all day? Why?" Take 2-3 quick verbal answers.
Keep the language at Level 3-4, not higher.

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "Today and tomorrow we're practicing describing something by comparing it to
something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Later this week, some of you will also practice
describing something in a way that lets the reader guess how you feel about it, without you ever saying the
feeling out loud."

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: comparatives and because-clauses (30 min)

_Comparative adjectives._

| Rule                                                                       | Example                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er                                     | thin -> thinner                                               |
| Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i, add -er                            | shiny -> shinier, heavy -> heavier                            |
| Longer adjectives (2+ syllables, not ending in -y): use "more" + adjective | durable -> more durable, colorful -> more colorful            |
| Irregular forms                                                            | good -> better, bad -> worse, far -> farther, little -> less |

Point out the -y spelling change explicitly (heavy is not "heavyer"); it is the rule students most often get wrong
because the short-adjective "add -er" rule alone does not cover it. "Farther" and "less" are given as recognition
vocabulary here, not as forms this Scenario's controlled practice drills directly.

Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7), co-constructed live: holding up the two comparison cases from the hook,
elicit the comparative sentence from the class together ("This one is thinner than that one." / "This one is more
colorful than that one.").

_Reason words._

| Word       | Example                                                          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------|
| because    | I like this case because it's light.                            |
| since      | Since it's light, I take it everywhere.                         |
| as         | I take it everywhere, as it's light.                             |
| given that | Given that I carry my phone all day, a light case matters most. |

"Because" is Level 2-5's own required feature and every student still needs it. Since, as, and given that are
taught alongside it as recognition-and-light-use vocabulary, present so students meet a real reason-word toolkit
rather than one isolated word.

_Frame-regime warm-up, folded in here (8 min inside the 30):_ show the phone case picture and the Word Bank, then
the frame "It is \_\_\_." Elicit one or two words orally from the whole class first, drawn from the Word Bank (color,
size) before Levels 2-3 practice independently on Day 2. For Level 3, introduce the third-person singular -s
spelling rule briefly (it holds up well. / it looks nice.) since the two-slot frame's own second sentence variant
can use it.

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A, part 1 (30 min)

**Activity 1: Fill in the blank (whole class, 10 min).** Complete the comparative form.

> 1. My old case was thin, but my new one is much **\_\_\_** (thick).
> 2. My old one was plain, but this one is **\_\_\_** (colorful).
> 3. My old one cracked easily, but this one is **\_\_\_** (durable) than my old one ever was.
> 4. This case is **\_\_\_** (heavy) than my last one.
> 5. My friend's case is **\_\_\_** (expensive) than mine.
> 6. A leather case is usually **\_\_\_** (durable) than a plastic one.
> 7. This case's grip is **\_\_\_** (tight) than my old one's.
> 8. My new case is **\_\_\_** (good) than my old one in every way.

**Activity 2: Choose the correct comparative form (whole class, 8 min).**

> 1. My case is (bigger / more bigger) than my sister's.
> 2. This case is (more colorful / colorfuller) than my old one.
> 3. My old case was (worse / more bad) than this one.
> 4. This case is (gooder / better) at protecting my phone.
> 5. A silicone case is (more heavy / heavier) than a plastic one.
> 6. This design is (more modern / moderner) than my old case's design.

**Activity 3, Levels 4-5 (12 min): Classify each sentence as using a comparison (C), a because-reason (B), or
both (CB).**

> 1. My case is bigger than my sister's. (C)
> 2. I like this color because it doesn't show fingerprints. (B)
> 3. I chose the bigger case because I drop my phone a lot. (CB)
> 4. This case is stronger than my old one. (C)
> 5. I bought this case because the store had a sale. (B)
> 6. My case is more durable than my old one because it never cracks. (CB)
> 7. I picked this color because it matches my bag. (B)
> 8. This case is heavier than a plastic one. (C)

**Activity 3-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min):** working from their own actual phone case (no picture
needed here) and the Word Bank, Level 2 completes "It is \_\_\_." three separate times, using a different describing
word each round (color, then size, then condition); Level 3 completes "It is \_\_\_ and \_\_\_." twice, choosing a new
pair of words the second round. Both Levels pull their words from the Word Bank rather than inventing untaught
vocabulary. The teacher circulates and listens to each student sound out their word before it's written.

## DAY 2: GRAMMAR FOCUS A, DEEPER PRACTICE AND THE LEVELED MENTOR LADDER (75 MIN)

```
0            20                45                        75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1----|-----Phase 2-----|---------Phase 3----------|
| Editing a   | Frame practice / | Leveled Mentor Ladder     |
| paragraph   | original         | walkthrough                |
| (Focus A)   | sentences        |                            |
```

### Phase 1: Editing a paragraph on Focus A (20 min)

**Activity 4: Editing a paragraph, 8 errors (whole class or pairs, 20 min).**

> My phone case is more good than my old one. It hold my phone better, and it don't crack. I like it because it
> are light. It is easyer to carry than my old case. My sister's case is more small than mine, but she don't
> mind. Her case are older than mine too.

Tell students there are 8 errors to find (verified: "more good" -> "better"; "It hold" -> "It holds"; "it don't
crack" -> "it doesn't crack"; "it are light" -> "it is light"; "easyer" -> "easier"; "more small" -> "smaller";
"she don't mind" -> "she doesn't mind"; "Her case are older" -> "Her case is older").

### Phase 2: Frame practice / original sentences with Focus A (25 min)

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime):** four more guided rounds, this time on a personal belonging of the student's own
choosing (a bag, a watch, a wallet, a pair of shoes, and so on) rather than the phone case again. Same frames,
"It is \_\_\_." and "It is \_\_\_ and \_\_\_.," each round choosing a genuinely different observation from the Word Bank.

**Activity 5, Levels 4-5: write one original sentence using each target item.** Target items: bigger, more
colorful, because, more durable, heavier, easier.

### Phase 3: Leveled Mentor Ladder walkthrough (25 min)

Present all four Mentor Texts together, in Level order, on the shared Scenario (a model student, "Maria," and
her phone case).

**Level 2 Mentor Text**

> [Picture: a plain blue phone case]
> Frame: It is \_\_\_.
> Maria's answer: It is blue.

**Level 3 Mentor Text**

> [Picture: a plain blue phone case]
> Frame: It is \_\_\_ and \_\_\_.
> Maria's answer: It is blue and thin.

**Level 4 Mentor Text**

> This is my phone case. It is blue and thin, thinner than my old one, because I wanted something that fits
> easily in my pocket. It has a strong grip, so it never slips out of my hand. I chose the case with the best
> reviews online, and I clean it once a week. It is more expensive than my old case, but that does not bother me.
>
> (5 sentences, approximately 65 words: inside the 40-90 word, 3-6 sentence target in Section 0.2. Note the
> three different compound-sentence connectors used across the paragraph, so, and, but, previewing Lesson 2's
> Focus B rather than only demonstrating Focus A.)

**Level 5 Mentor Text**

> My phone case travels everywhere with me. It's a deep blue with a corner that's gone soft and rounded, the kind
> of wear you get from years of dropping it on hardwood floors and shoving it into tight coat pockets. The
> stickers cover almost the whole back now: one from a hiking trail up north, one from a coffee shop that closed
> years ago, one my daughter picked out herself. I wipe it down two or three times a day, more out of habit than
> anything else at this point. Although a newer, sleeker case would probably protect my phone better, I've never
> once thought about switching it out.
>
> (5 sentences, approximately 100 words. The implied detail is the sticker collection and the refusal to switch
> cases, suggesting sentimental attachment without the word "sentimental" appearing anywhere. The final sentence
> previews Lesson 2's Level 5 extension: a concession subordinator, "although," carrying a hint of reservation more
> naturally than a flat "but" would.)

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** as the class examines the ladder, co-construct live a "what changes at
each Level" board from student observations, for example: "Level 2 says one word. Level 3 says two words. Level 4
explains why. Level 5 makes us guess how Maria feels."
