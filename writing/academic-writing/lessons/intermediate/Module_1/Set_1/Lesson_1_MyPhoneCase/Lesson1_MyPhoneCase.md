## Scenario

Every student describes a phone case: a real, familiar object nearly every student owns or can picture, with
natural angles for comparison (color, thickness, durability, grip, price) and for implied attitude (how worn it
is, what it has been through, why it never gets replaced). The Scenario is shared across all 8 lessons of Module
Pair 1-2: Module 1's Set carries it from grammar input (Lessons 1-2) through drafting (Lesson 3) to a
self-revised draft handed off at Lesson 4; Module 2's Set revises, expands, and publishes it. The phone case is
the Set's practice object (this lesson, Lesson 2, and the Mentor Ladder); from Lesson 2 Day 2's prewriting on, the
draft itself is about the student's own jacket, the Set's draft object (Conventions §C). Every student,
Levels 2-5 alike, describes their own actual phone case; a student without one with them borrows a classmate's
for the lesson. Nothing is provided or shown by the teacher (Quality Standards §D8).

## Word Bank (teacher-provided; posted on the board and printed in the packet, used through Lesson 2 Day 1)

Levels 2-3 draw their describing words from this list; Levels 4-5 may use it when a word is not coming to mind.

> **Color/pattern:** blue, black, pink, clear, patterned, striped
> **Material/texture:** soft, hard, smooth, rubbery
> **Size/thickness:** thin, thick, big, small
> **Condition:** new, scratched, worn, shiny, cracked
> **Quality:** sturdy, cheap, light, heavy

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class):** comparative adjectives (-er / more) and "because" reason
clauses, with since / as / given that taught alongside, on a simple-present backbone. This is the Level 4 row of
Section 0.4's bank, Intermediate's own native Paragraph Composition floor, so no row-selection correction
applies. Level 4's required feature (one comparison, one explicit reason) needs it directly; Level 5's implied
attitude is layered on top of it in Lesson 2.

**Frame-regime content, folded into the same mini-lesson (Levels 2-3, never a separate session):** the simple
present "is" frame; third-person singular -s spelling (Level 3); "and" built into the two-slot frame. Words come
from the Word Bank.

**Level 5 heavier tier (Day 2):** one hedge word (a bit, seems, at least) combined with a comparison and a
reason in a single sentence, using only what this lesson has taught; the fuller hedging and concession work is
Lesson 2's Level 5 extension.

## DAY 1: GRAMMAR FOCUS A, INPUT AND MODELING (75 MIN)

```
0        15                        45                    75  (minutes)
|-Phase 1-|--------Phase 2----------|-------Phase 3-------|
| Hook,   | Focus A mini-lesson:    | Controlled practice  |
| Skill   | comparatives + reasons  | A, part 1             |
| Spot-   | (+ frame fold-in)        |                       |
| light   |                          |                       |
```

### Phase 1: Hook, Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Hook (8 min):** Ask two students to hold up their own phone cases, picked because they are easy to compare (a
thin plain one and a thick decorated one). Ask: "Which one is thicker? Which one would you rather carry all day? Why?" Take 2-3 quick oral answers.
Keep the language at Level 3-4.

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "Today and tomorrow we're practicing describing something by comparing it to
something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Later in this unit, some of you will also practice
describing something in a way that lets the reader guess how you feel about it, without saying the feeling out
loud."

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: comparatives and reason words (30 min)

_Comparative adjectives (10 min)._

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er | thin -> thinner, hard -> harder |
| One-syllable adjectives ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant, add -er | big -> bigger, thin -> thinner |
| Adjectives ending in -y: change y to i, add -er | shiny -> shinier, heavy -> heavier |
| Two-syllable adjectives not ending in -y: use "more" + adjective | modern -> more modern, rubbery is an exception (rubberier) |
| Longer adjectives (3+ syllables): use "more" + adjective | durable -> more durable, expensive -> more expensive |
| Irregular forms | good -> better, bad -> worse, little -> less |

Point out the two spelling rules explicitly (heavy is not "heavyer"; big is not "biger"); they are the two
students most often get wrong. "Less" is recognition vocabulary here, not drilled.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** holding up the two hook cases, elicit the comparative sentence
from the class together ("This one is thinner than that one." / "This one is more colorful than that one."), then
a second one nobody has said yet.

_Reason words (12 min)._

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| because | I like this case because it's light. |
| since | Since it's light, I take it everywhere. |
| as | I take it everywhere, as it's light. |
| given that | Given that I carry my phone all day, a light case matters most. |

"Because" is every Level's required reason word. Since, as, and given that are taught alongside it, each with
its own example, so students meet a real reason-word toolkit rather than one isolated word; note that since and
given that can open the sentence, and that a comma follows an opening reason clause.

_Frame content, folded in (8 min)._ Every student puts their own phone case on the desk. Show the Word Bank, then
the frame "It is ___." Elicit two words orally from the whole class about their own cases (color, then size). Then show "It is ___ and ___." and elicit a pair
that gives two different observations, not two words for the same thing (blue and thin, not thin and slim). For
Level 3, introduce the -s rule briefly (It holds up well. / It looks new.), since the two-slot frame's sentence
variant uses it on Day 2.

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A, part 1 (30 min)

**Activity 1: Fill in the blank with the comparative form (whole class, 10 min).**

> 1. My old case was thin, but my new one is much ___ (thick).
> 2. My old one was plain, but this one is ___ (colorful).
> 3. My old one cracked easily, but this one is ___ (durable) than my old one ever was.
> 4. This case is ___ (heavy) than my last one.
> 5. My friend's case is ___ (expensive) than mine.
> 6. A leather case is usually ___ (soft) than a plastic one.
> 7. This case's grip is ___ (tight) than my old one's.
> 8. My new case is ___ (good) than my old one in every way.

Answer key: thicker, more colorful, more durable, heavier, more expensive, softer, tighter, better.

**Activity 2: Choose the correct form (whole class, 8 min).** Both options are forms a student might really
write; the wrong one breaks a spelling or syllable rule from the table.

> 1. My case is (bigger / biger) than my sister's.
> 2. This design is (moderner / more modern) than my old case's.
> 3. A silicone case is (heavier / heavyer) than a plastic one.
> 4. My old case was (worse / badder) than this one.
> 5. This case feels (more smooth / smoother) than the last one.
> 6. The clear case is (shinyer / shinier) than the black one.
> 7. My phone is (safer / more safe) in a thick case.
> 8. A thin case is (more simple / simpler) to slide into a pocket.

Answer key: bigger, more modern, heavier, worse, smoother, shinier, safer, simpler (both "more simple" and
"simpler" are heard in real English; for this lesson teach "simpler," the form the two-syllable rule predicts,
and mention the other as one students will meet).

**Activity 3, Levels 4-5 (12 min): Classify each sentence as a comparison (C), a reason (R), or both (CR), then
underline the reason word.** Sentences use all four reason words, not only "because," so the classifying step
requires reading the sentence rather than scanning for one word.

> 1. My case is bigger than my sister's. (C)
> 2. I like this color since it doesn't show fingerprints. (R)
> 3. I chose the thicker case because I drop my phone a lot. (CR)
> 4. Given that I ride my bike to work, a sturdy case matters. (R)
> 5. This case is heavier than a plastic one, as it has a metal ring on the back. (CR)
> 6. My case is more worn than my brother's. (C)
> 7. I picked the clear one because I wanted to see the phone's color. (R)
> 8. A rubbery case is safer than a hard one because it bounces instead of cracking. (CR)

**Activity 3b, Level 5 (heavier tier, inside the same 12 min):** after classifying, rewrite sentences 2, 4, and 5
using a different reason word from the table (because, since, as, or given that) without changing the meaning,
moving the reason clause to the front of the sentence where the new word allows it and adding the comma. Built
only from what Phase 2 taught; this is Level 5's own Day 1 task, not a share of Level 4's.

**Activity 3-equivalent, Levels 2-3 (parallel, 12 min):** working from their own phone case on the desk and the
Word Bank,
Level 2 completes "It is ___." three separate times, one word from a different Word Bank group each round
(color, then size, then condition), and reads each finished sentence to a partner. Level 3 completes "It is ___
and ___." three times, each round a pair from two different groups, so the two slots always carry two separate
observations. The teacher circulates and listens to each student sound out the word before writing it.

## DAY 2: GRAMMAR FOCUS A, DEEPER PRACTICE AND THE LEVELED MENTOR LADDER (75 MIN)

```
0            20                45                        75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1----|-----Phase 2-----|---------Phase 3----------|
| Editing a   | Frame practice / | Leveled Mentor Ladder     |
| paragraph   | original         | walkthrough                |
| (Focus A)   | sentences        |                            |
```

### Phase 1: Editing a paragraph on Focus A (20 min)

**Activity 4: Find and fix the 8 mistakes (pairs, then whole class, 20 min).**

> My phone case is more good than my old one. It hold my phone better, and it don't crack. I like it because it
> are light. It is easyer to carry than my old case, since it is thiner. My sister's case is more small than
> mine, but she doesn't mind. Her case is more shiny than mine, and it is older too.

Answer key, 8 errors verified by count: more good -> better; It hold -> It holds; it don't -> it doesn't; it are
-> it is; easyer -> easier; thiner -> thinner; more small -> smaller; more shiny -> shinier. Two of the six
sentences are error-free ("but she doesn't mind" and "and it is older too" are correct), so students cannot
assume every clause hides a mistake.

### Phase 2: Frame practice / original sentences with Focus A (25 min)

**Levels 2-3 (frame regime), Activity 5-equivalent:** three more guided rounds each, on a belonging of the
student's own choosing (a bag, a watch, a wallet, a pair of shoes), not the phone case again. Same frames; each
round a genuinely different observation from a different Word Bank group. Level 3 also writes one sentence in
the -s form about the same belonging ("It looks new." / "It holds a lot.").

**Activity 5, Level 4: write one original sentence using each target item**, about your own phone case or
another belonging: bigger, more colorful, because, more durable, heavier, easier. At least two sentences must
contain both a comparison and a reason.

**Activity 6, Level 5 (heavier tier, same 25 minutes):** write three original sentences, each combining a
comparison, a reason word (since, as, or given that, not only because), and one hedge from this list: a bit, a
little, seems, at least. Example shape: "This case seems a bit heavier than my old one, since it has a metal
ring on the back." Then, for one of the three, write a second version that removes the hedge, and note in one
line which version sounds more certain. This uses only what Day 1 taught plus three hedge words introduced here
for recognition; Lesson 2 builds the fuller hedging and concession work on it.

### Phase 3: Leveled Mentor Ladder walkthrough (30 min)

Present all four Mentor Texts together, in Level order, on the shared Scenario (a model student, "Maria," and her
phone case). Maria's own case is plain and blue.

**Level 2 Mentor Text**

> Maria looked at her own phone case.
> Frame: It is ___.
> Maria's answer: It is blue.

**Level 3 Mentor Text**

> Maria looked at her own phone case.
> Frame: It is ___ and ___.
> Maria's answer: It is blue and thin.

**Level 4 Mentor Text**

> This is my phone case. It is blue and thin, thinner than my old one, because I wanted something that fits
> easily in my pocket. It has a strong grip, so it never slips out of my hand. I chose the case with the best
> reviews online, and I clean it once a week. It is more expensive than my old case, but that does not bother me.

(5 sentences, 68 words: inside the 40-90 word, 3-6 sentence target. Comparison: "thinner than my old one";
explicit reason: "because I wanted something that fits easily in my pocket." The three compound-sentence
connectors, so / and / but, preview Lesson 2's Focus B.)

**Level 5 Mentor Text**

> My phone case travels everywhere with me. It is a deep blue, and one corner has gone soft and rounded from
> years of dropping it on hardwood floors. The stickers cover almost the whole back now. One is from a hiking
> trail up north, one is from a coffee shop that closed years ago, and one my daughter picked out herself. I
> wipe the case down two or three times a day, more out of habit than anything else. A newer, sleeker case would
> probably protect my phone a bit better. I have never once thought about switching it out.

(7 sentences, 100 words: inside the 90-150 word, 6-9 sentence target. Details are organized from the case's
look, to its history, to the writer's habits. The implied attitude is carried by the sticker collection, the
daily wiping, and the refusal to switch even after admitting a newer case would protect the phone better; the
word "sentimental" never appears. "A bit" and "probably" are the hedges Level 5's Activity 6 practiced.)

**Walkthrough:** for each text, name in one line what makes it that Level's version (one word in a frame; two
different observations in a frame; a comparison with a stated reason; details that make the reader guess a
feeling). Students find their own Level's text and the one directly above it, and say to a partner one thing
the next text up does that theirs does not.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a "what changes at each Level" board built from student
observations ("Level 2 says one word. Level 3 says two different things. Level 4 explains why. Level 5 makes us
guess how Maria feels.").
