Unit 3A: Describing My Clothing

Writing Intermediate 1.1.3.6

Objective: You will describe one piece of your own clothing by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Some of you will also describe it in a way that lets your reader guess how you feel about it, without ever saying the feeling out loud.

Word Bank
Color/pattern: black · gray · green · red · plain · printed
Material: cotton · fleece · denim · wool · leather · nylon
Size/fit: big · small · long · loose · tight · baggy
Condition: new · faded · worn · torn · stained · clean
Feel/quality: warm · light · heavy · soft · thick · sturdy

Use these words if you need them. You may also use your own.

Get Ready to Write

Task A. Pick one of these things to write about and circle it: my jacket · my hoodie · my sweater · my shirt · my T-shirt · my dress · my scarf · my hat.

Look at it. Write three words from the Word Bank that fit it.

Write It

Write about the thing you circled. When you finish, be ready to read one line aloud to someone working at a different star level.

Task B. ★ Pick two words from the list that could go in the blank. Say which one fits it better and why. Then sound out that word and write it in the blank.

  1. 1.My two words: or
  2. 2.It is .

Then do it once more about a different part of it (a sleeve, a pocket, the collar). Point to the part, sound out the word, write it, and read the line to a partner.

  1. 3.It is .

Task C. ★★ Pick two words from the list that could go in the first blank. Say which one fits it better and why. Then sound out both words for the sentence and write them. The two words must describe two different things (color and size, not size and size).

  1. 1.My two words for the first blank: or
  2. 2.It is and .

Then do it once more about a different part of it (a sleeve, a pocket, the collar). Point to the part, sound out two words that say two different things, write them, and read the line to a partner.

  1. 3.It is and .

Task D. ★★★ Write one paragraph describing the thing you circled. Follow these steps, one sentence each. Aim for 3-6 sentences.

  1. 1.Say what the object is.
  2. 2.Compare it to another one of the same kind (an old one, a sibling's, a friend's, one in a store) using -er or more.
  3. 3.Give the reason using because, since, as, or given that.
  4. 4.Join two of your ideas into one sentence with and, but, or so, with a comma before the connector.
  5. 5.End with one more real detail (the pockets, the weight, how it looks after a winter).

Task E. ★★★★ First, list four or five real details about it on the short lines, in the order your paragraph will use them (a good order: what it looks like, then what has happened to it, then when you wear it). Circle the one detail that most clearly hints at how you feel. Then write one paragraph, putting your circled detail where it carries the most weight, usually near the end. Aim for 6-9 sentences. No sentence may say the feeling itself ("I love it" does not count; a specific detail does).

Share a Line

Get ready to read one line from your piece aloud.

  • ★Read your finished sentence.
  • ★★Read your finished sentence, then say which two things your two words describe.
  • ★★★Read your comparison sentence, or your joined sentence if you have written it.
  • ★★★★Read the sentence with your circled detail. Do not say what feeling it shows.

While you listen: write down one word from each line that you would want in your own piece. For a four-star line, write the feeling you guess instead.

Check Your Own Work

Anything you change now: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task E. ★ Read your sentence to a partner. Does your word match it? Point to the part your word describes. Does the sentence make sense out loud?

Task F. ★★ Read your sentence to a partner. Point to the part each word describes. Are your two words about two different things? Does the sentence make sense out loud?

Task G. ★★★ Mark each item on your own paragraph as you find it: circle the comparing word, underline the reason word, and put a box around the comma in your joined sentence.

  1. 1.Every sentence has a subject and a verb, and verbs after "it" or "she" end in -s where needed (it holds, it fits).
  2. 2.I used at least one comparing form (-er or more) correctly, and it compares it to something specific.
  3. 3.I gave one clear reason using because, since, as, or given that.
  4. 4.I used at least one sentence joined with and, but, or so, with the comma before the connector.
  5. 5.I have one comparison and one reason that is stated directly. I can point to both.
  6. 6."It's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) are used correctly everywhere they appear.
  7. 7.Punctuation and capital letters are correct, including a capital letter after every period.
  8. 8.My sentence count is inside my range (3-6 sentences).

Task H. ★★★★ Mark each item on your own paragraph as you find it: circle the comparing word, underline the reason word, and put a box around the comma in your joined sentence.

  1. 1.Every sentence has a subject and a verb, and verbs after "it" or "she" end in -s where needed (it holds, it fits).
  2. 2.I used at least one comparing form (-er or more) correctly, and it compares it to something specific.
  3. 3.I gave one clear reason using because, since, as, or given that.
  4. 4.I used at least one sentence joined with and, but, or so, with the comma before the connector.
  5. 5.I have one detail that shows how I feel about it, and there is no feeling word (love, like, hate, favorite) anywhere in that sentence.
  6. 6."It's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) are used correctly everywhere they appear.
  7. 7.Punctuation and capital letters are correct, including a capital letter after every period.
  8. 8.My sentence count is inside my range (6-9 sentences).
  9. 9.I have at least one softening word (a bit, a little, seems, at least) or one although sentence, or I have marked where one could go.
Share a Line

Read only the last line of your piece to a partner. Your partner answers one question. Write their answer in one word in the margin.

  • ★Partner: which part does the word describe? Point to it.
  • ★★Partner: which part does each word describe? Point to both.
  • ★★★Partner: does the last sentence add a new detail, or repeat something already said?
  • ★★★★Partner: what do you think the writer feels about it, and which sentence told you?

Keep your piece. You'll keep working on it soon.
