Unit 3A: Describing My Clothing

Writing Intermediate 1.1.3.12

Objective: I can describe something of my own by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison.

Word Bank
Color/pattern: black · gray · green · red · plain · printed
Material: cotton · fleece · denim · wool · leather · nylon
Size/fit: big · small · long · loose · tight · baggy
Condition: new · faded · worn · torn · stained · clean
Feel/quality: warm · light · heavy · soft · thick · sturdy

Use these words if you need them. You may also use your own.

Get Ready to Write

Task A. Pick one of these things to write about and circle it: my jacket · my hoodie · my sweater · my shirt · my T-shirt · my dress · my scarf · my hat.

  1. 1.Write three words from the Word Bank that fit it:
Write It

Write about the clothing you circled. When you finish, be ready to read one line aloud to someone working at a different star level.

Task B. ★ Choose your word, sound it out, and write it.

  1. 1.Pick two words from the Word Bank that could go in the blank: or
  2. 2.Which one fits it better, and why? Say it to a partner.
  3. 3.Sound out that word and write it: It is .
  4. 4.Read your sentence to a partner. Your partner says which part of the clothing the word describes. Is that the part you meant?

Task C. ★★ Choose your two words, sound them out, and write them.

  1. 1.Pick two words from the Word Bank that could go in the first blank: or
  2. 2.Which one fits it better, and why? Say it to a partner.
  3. 3.Pick a second word that says a different thing (color and size, not size and size).
  4. 4.Sound out both words and write them: It is and .
  5. 5.Read your sentence to a partner. Your partner says which part of the clothing each word describes. Are those the parts you meant?

Task D. ★★★ Write one paragraph describing the clothing, one sentence per step, 3-6 sentences.

  1. 1.Say what the object is.
  2. 2.Compare it to another one of the same kind (an old one, a sibling's, a friend's, one in a store) using -er or more.
  3. 3.Give the reason using because, since, as, or given that.
  4. 4.Join two of your ideas into one sentence with and, but, or so, with a comma before the connector.
  5. 5.End with one more real detail (the pockets, the weight, how it looks after a winter).

Task E. ★★★★ Plan your details, then write one paragraph of 6-9 sentences.

  1. 1.List four or five real details about it, in the order your paragraph will use them (what it looks like, then what has happened to it, then when you wear it).
  2. 2.Circle the one detail that most clearly hints at how you feel.
  3. 3.Write your paragraph, putting the circled detail where it carries the most weight, usually near the end.
  4. 4.Check: does any sentence say the feeling itself? ("I love it" does not count; a specific detail does.)
Share a Line

Get ready to read one line from your piece aloud.

  • ★Read your finished sentence.
  • ★★Read your finished sentence, then say which two things your two words describe.
  • ★★★Read your comparison sentence, or your joined sentence if you have written it.
  • ★★★★Read the sentence with your circled detail. Do not say what feeling it shows.

While you listen: write down one word from each line that you would want in your own piece. For a four-star line, write the feeling you guess instead.

Unit 3B: Describing My Clothing

Objective: I can describe something of my own by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison.

Check and Improve Your Writing

Anything you change: cross out the old version with a single line, do not erase it, and write the new version next to it or above it, so both stay on the page.

Task A. ★ Check your sentence, then make it better.

  1. 1.Read your sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: which part does the word describe? Is that the part you meant?
  3. 3.Does the sentence make sense out loud?
  4. 4.Is there a word in the Word Bank that fits better than the one you chose (faded instead of old, fleece instead of soft)? If yes, cross out your old word.
  5. 5.Write the whole new sentence. If your word was already the best one, leave it and put a check next to it: It is .
  6. 6.Read it to your partner.

Task B. ★★ Check your sentence, then make it better.

  1. 1.Read your sentence to a partner.
  2. 2.Partner: which part does each word describe?
  3. 3.Are your two words about two different things? Does the sentence make sense out loud?
  4. 4.Is there a word in the Word Bank that fits better than one of the two you chose? If yes, cross out the old word.
  5. 5.Write the whole new sentence: It is and .
  6. 6.Add one sentence about what it does, with a verb that ends in -s (It keeps me warm. It looks new. It fits well.): It .
  7. 7.Read both lines to your partner.

Task C. ★★★ Check your paragraph, then make it better.

  1. 1.Circle your comparing word, underline your reason word, and put a box around the comma in your joined sentence.
  2. 2.Does every sentence have a subject and a verb, with -s on verbs after "it" or "she" where needed (it holds, it fits)?
  3. 3.Is there at least one comparing form (-er or more), and does it compare it to something specific?
  4. 4.Is there one clear reason with because, since, as, or given that?
  5. 5.Is there a sentence joined with and, but, or so, with the comma before the connector?
  6. 6.Can you find one comparison and one reason that is stated directly?
  7. 7.Are "it's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) used correctly everywhere they appear?
  8. 8.Are punctuation and capital letters correct, including a capital letter after every period?
  9. 9.Is your sentence count inside 3-6?
  10. 10.Make each change you marked (a missing comma before and, but, or so; a reason that just says the comparison again; a comparing word spelled wrong).
  11. 11.Add one more real detail sentence about the clothing. Stay inside 6 sentences.

Task D. ★★★★ Check your paragraph, then make it better.

  1. 1.Circle your comparing word, underline your reason word, and put a box around the comma in your joined sentence.
  2. 2.Does every sentence have a subject and a verb, with -s on verbs after "it" or "she" where needed (it holds, it fits)?
  3. 3.Is there at least one comparing form (-er or more), and does it compare it to something specific?
  4. 4.Is there one clear reason with because, since, as, or given that?
  5. 5.Is there a sentence joined with and, but, or so, with the comma before the connector?
  6. 6.Is there one detail that shows how you feel about it, with no feeling word (love, like, hate, favorite) anywhere in that sentence?
  7. 7.Are "it's" (it is) and "its" (belonging to it) used correctly everywhere they appear?
  8. 8.Are punctuation and capital letters correct, including a capital letter after every period?
  9. 9.Is your sentence count inside 6-9?
  10. 10.Is there at least one softening word (a bit, a little, seems, at least) or one although sentence? If not, mark where one could go.
  11. 11.Make each change you marked.
  12. 12.If you found a feeling word, cross out that sentence and write a real detail that lets the reader guess the feeling. If you did not, make your circled detail sharper: more specific, more real. Stay inside 9 sentences.
Share a Line

Read only the last line of your piece to a partner. Your partner answers one question. Write their answer in one word in the margin.

  • ★Partner: which part does the word describe? Say it.
  • ★★Partner: which part does each word describe? Say both.
  • ★★★Partner: does the last sentence add a new detail, or repeat something already said?
  • ★★★★Partner: what do you think the writer feels about it, and which sentence told you?
