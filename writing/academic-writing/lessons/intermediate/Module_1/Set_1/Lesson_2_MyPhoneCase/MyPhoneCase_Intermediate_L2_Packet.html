Unit 2A: Describing My Phone Case

Writing Intermediate 1.1.2.0

Objective: You will describe your phone case by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Some of you will also describe it in a way that lets your reader guess how you feel about it, without ever saying the feeling out loud.

Grammar

A simple sentence has one subject-verb combination (or more than one subject and/or verb, still one sentence). A compound sentence joins two simple sentences with a comma and a connector: and, but, or so.

ConnectorMeaningExample
andadds informationMy case is blue, and it is thin.
butadds contrastMy case is thin, but it is heavy.
sogives a resultMy case cracked, so I bought a new one.

Yesterday you learned to compare two things and give a reason. Today you'll join that comparison to its reason, or its result, in one sentence.

The two-word frame "It is ___ and ___." already uses "and" to join two words, not two full sentences — the same joining job "and" does in a compound sentence.

A Word That's Easy to Mix Up

it's vs. its

FormMeaningExample

itsbelonging to it (no apostrophe)Its corner is cracked.
