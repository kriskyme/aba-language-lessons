Unit 4A: Describing My Clothing

Writing Intermediate 1.1.4.6

Objective: describe one piece of your own clothing by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Some of you will also describe it in a way that lets your reader guess how you feel about it, without ever saying the feeling out loud.

Check Your Own Work

Read back through the piece you wrote last time. Mark it as each line says. A line is done when the mark is on your page.

Task A. ★ Look at the thing you wrote about. Point to the part your word describes. Is it there? Say the word slowly. Does every sound you hear have a letter on the page? Then read your sentence to a partner. Your partner points to what your word describes. If they point to the wrong thing, or cannot point, mark that word.

Task B. ★★ Point to the thing your first word describes, then the thing your second word describes. Are they two different things (a color and a size, not two words for the same idea)? Say each word slowly and check its letters. Then read your sentence to a partner. Your partner points to what each word describes. If they point to the wrong thing, or cannot point, mark that word.

Task C. ★★★ On your own piece:

  1. 1.Circle your comparing word (-er or more). Does it compare it to one other thing?
  2. 2.Underline your reason, and put a box around the reason word (because, since, as, or given that). Is it a real reason, not the same idea said again?
  3. 3.Put a star next to one sentence joined with and, but, or so. Is there a comma before the joining word?
  4. 4.Check every "it's" and "its." It's = it is. Its = belonging to it.
  5. 5.Count your sentences: 3 to 6.

Task D. ★★★★ On your own piece:

  1. 1.Circle your comparing word (-er or more). Does it compare it to one other thing?
  2. 2.Underline your reason, and put a box around the reason word (because, since, as, or given that). Is it a real reason, not the same idea said again?
  3. 3.Put a star next to one sentence joined with and, but, or so. Is there a comma before the joining word?
  4. 4.Check every "it's" and "its." It's = it is. Its = belonging to it.
  5. 5.Number your details in the margin in the order they appear. Do they move in one direction (how it looks, then its history, then your habits), or do they jump around?
  6. 6.Draw a wavy line under the one detail that shows how you feel about it. Then scan the whole paragraph for a feeling word (love, like, hate, favorite, boring). If one is there, mark that sentence: you will change it in the next task.
  7. 7.Put a check next to one softening word (a bit, a little, seems, at least, probably) or one sentence with although or even though. If there is none, mark where one could go.
  8. 8.Count your sentences: 6 to 9.
Fix It Up

Change your piece wherever you made a mark. Do not erase: draw one line through what you are changing and write the new version above it or next to it, so both stay readable.

Task E. ★ Look at the Word Bank. Is there a word that describes it more exactly than the one you chose (faded instead of old, fleece instead of soft)? If yes, cross out your old word, write the new one next to it, and write the whole new sentence on the line below. If your first word was already the best one, write one new "It is ___." sentence about a different part of it, with a word from a different part of the Word Bank.

Task F. ★★ Look at the Word Bank. Is there a word that describes it more exactly than one of the two you chose? If yes, cross out the old word, write the new one next to it, and write the whole new sentence on the line below. If both words were already the best ones, write one new "It is ___ and ___." sentence about a different part of it, with two words from two different parts of the Word Bank.

Objective: describe one piece of your own clothing by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison. Some of you will also describe it in a way that lets your reader guess how you feel about it, without ever saying the feeling out loud.

Last Look

Read your piece quietly one more time, looking hardest at the sentences you changed last time: a changed sentence often loses its capital letter or its final period, and a swapped word can change the verb next to it. Then count.

Task A. ★ Every blank is filled, and the whole sentence is copied out once, cleanly.

Task B. ★★ Both blanks are filled, and the whole sentence is copied out once, cleanly.

Task C. ★★★ 3 to 6 sentences. Add or cut until you are inside the range.

Task D. ★★★★ 6 to 9 sentences. Add or cut until you are inside the range.

Before It Moves On

You'll keep working on this piece soon, with new tools. Leave it a note on its last line.

Task E. ★ Copy your final sentence cleanly on the line. Circle the word you chose. Draw a small picture of it, with an arrow from the circled word to the thing it describes.

Task F. ★★ Copy your final sentence cleanly on the line. Circle both words you chose. Draw a small picture of it, with an arrow from each circled word to the thing it describes.

Task G. ★★★ On your piece: circle your comparing word, put a box around your reason word, and put a star next to your sentence joined with and, but, or so. Then finish this sentence on the line.

Next time I will add (a detail, a reason, or a comparison).

Wrap It Up

Sit with a partner. Put the pen or pencil you are each holding side by side. Write on your card, then read it to your partner.

Task I. ★ Write "It is ___." about your partner's pen, with a word you spell by its sounds (long, short, blue, thin).

Task J. ★★ Write "It is ___ and ___." about your partner's pen, with two words that say two different things.

Task K. ★★★ Write one sentence comparing your pen to your partner's, with a reason word (because, since, as, or given that) and a real reason. If it fits, join the comparison and the reason with and, but, or so.

My pen is thinner than Ana's, because I like to keep it in my pocket.

Task L. ★★★★ Write one sentence comparing your pen to your partner's, with a reason word and a real reason. Then write a second sentence with one real detail about your own pen that lets your partner guess how you feel about it, with no feeling word. Your partner reads it and says the guess out loud. If the guess is wrong, write a sharper detail.
