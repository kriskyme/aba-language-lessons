Quick Review: Comparing and Giving a Reason

To compare your item with another one, add -er to a short word (thicker, lighter, older) or put more in front of a long word (more comfortable, more expensive). Then say what you are comparing it to with than.

Reason wordExample
becauseThese boots are heavier than my sneakers because the soles are thick.
sinceMy scarf is longer than my sister's since I like to wrap it twice.
asThis shirt is looser than my others, as I bought it one size up.
given thatGiven that I walk to work, a lighter shoe is the better choice for me.

A real reason tells the reader something true about you or the item. "Because I like it" is not a reason yet; "because the pockets are deep enough for my keys" is.

Quick Review: Joining Two Sentences

Join two complete sentences with a comma and and (adds), but (contrasts), or so (gives a result): "My jeans are faded, but they still fit." The comma goes before the connector, and only when a complete sentence follows it. "My jeans are faded and soft" takes no comma, because "soft" is not a sentence.

ShapeExampleComma?
although, clause secondI wear this cap every day although the brim is bent.No comma.
Although, clause firstAlthough the brim is bent, I wear this cap every day.Comma after the first part.

"But" puts two facts side by side as equals. "Although" or "even though" pushes one fact into the background and makes the other one the point.
