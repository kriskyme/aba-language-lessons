## What this lesson carries forward

**Scenario.** Every student writes about one trip they have really taken, chosen from the printed list below. It
is remembered, not shown, and nothing is brought in.

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** What `-ed` does, its four spelling rules
and three sounds (Lesson 1); the time word at the front of the sentence with its comma, and what puts two actions
in an order (Lesson 2); the three frames. Every verb in a Word Bank, model, or item is regular.

**Nothing carried from Lesson 2 (Quality Standards §D9).** Lesson 2's prewriting was rehearsal. Every student
starts this lesson by choosing a trip from the printed list and building a fresh three-word plan on this lesson's
own page, from the Draft Word Bank reprinted below. A student who was absent, or who lost Lesson 2's page, starts
every task here without a gap.

**The three frames, stated once for this day and called "the frame" afterward:**

> **Level 1:** First, ___.
> **Level 2:** I ___-ed.
> **Level 3:** First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.

## Draft Word Bank (reprinted from Lesson 2 Day 2; posted on the board and printed in the packet)

> **Event words (for the one-word frame):** bus, walk, line, stop, rain, shop, game, snack
> **Getting-ready verbs:** packed, dressed, zipped, locked, tidied
> **Moving verbs:** walked, rushed, stepped, hurried, climbed
> **Doing-there verbs:** shopped, looked, picked, counted, carried
> **Waiting and looking verbs:** waited, checked, watched, smiled, noticed
> **Time words (part of the frame, not spelled from sound):** first, then, next, after that, last

## Drafting targets by task Level (Section 0.2)

| Task Level | Output form | Length | Required feature (checked in the Day 2 pass) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One event word spelled from sound into "First, ___." | One word | The word names something that really happened on the student's own trip, sounded out, not copied off the board. |
| 2 | One regular past verb spelled from sound into "I ___-ed." | One word | The same genuine choice, inside a whole sentence with a capital and a period, and the `-ed` built with the right one of the four rules. |
| 3 | Two verbs spelled from sound into "First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed." | Two words | The two verbs name two genuinely different actions (waited, then climbed), never one action twice (rushed, then hurried), and they are in the order they really happened. |

## DAY 1: DRAFTING (75 MIN)

```
0            15                                 60                75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1---|-------------Phase 2--------------|----Phase 3-----|
| Quick plan | Say it, write it, read it back   | Mid-draft       |
|            |                                  | share-out       |
```

### Phase 1: Quick plan (15 min)

Two actions, both on this lesson's own page. No questions are printed at this phase (Quality Standards §E6).

**Step 1.** Circle the trip you are writing about:

> to the store, to school, to the park, to a friend's house, on the bus, to the market, to a game, to the library

**Step 2.** Write three words from the Draft Word Bank that name things that happened on the trip you circled,
then circle the one you will use. Level 3 circles two and numbers them 1 and 2 in the order they happened.

**Answer note:** answers vary by trip. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: do the three words come
from at least two different groups, and at Level 3 are the two circled words two different actions in a real
order? A plan whose three words all name the same moment is the one to catch here, before any writing starts.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the student chooses their word by
circling one on the printed Draft Word Bank instead of writing it out, then says the word aloud. In Phase 2 they
say what happened and the teacher writes it, and the student then says which word names which part of the trip.

### Phase 2: Say it, write it, read it back (45 min)

_Say it (15 min)._ In pairs, each student says their circled word or words aloud and stretches each one into its
sounds for their partner, who counts the sounds on their fingers and says the number back. At Level 3 the partner
also says which of the two actions they heard first. No writing yet.

_Write it (20 min)._ Each student writes the frame once, with the word or words they circled. This is the
lesson's one round.

- **Level 1:** First, ___.
- **Level 2:** I ___-ed.
- **Level 3:** First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.

The teacher circulates and hears each student sound their word out rather than reading it off the bank.

_Read it back (10 min)._ Each student reads their own finished frame aloud, quietly, and checks three things by
marking the page: one letter or letter pair for each sound they hear; at Levels 2-3, a capital at the start and a
period at the end, and the `-ed` built with the right rule; at Level 3, that the two actions are in the order
they really happened.

**Answer note:** answers vary. What the marks look like: an underline under the `-ed` ending, a circle around the
capital and the period, and at Level 3 a 1 and a 2 written above the two verbs.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share-out (15 min)

Five or six students read their finished frame aloud to the room.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** a running "strong sentence" board, built live from the share-out, one
column per Level. It is not on any student page, and it stays up for Day 2.

**Rotated prompts (three, as the share moves):** which word did you choose that nobody else has used; which verb
was the hardest to build the `-ed` on; which part of your trip would a reader not be able to guess.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "First, ___."
- **Level 2:** "I ___-ed."
- **Level 3:** "First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed, and that is the order it happened."

**Task for every listener:** put a mark next to any word on your own page that you also hear someone else use.

## DAY 2: CHECK AND IMPROVE (75 MIN)

The frame was finished on Day 1. Today opens directly on checking it; there is no finish-your-writing phase.

```
0                    25                          55                75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1------|----------Phase 2----------|----Phase 3-----|
| Read it to a       | Swap in a more exact verb | Final line     |
| partner and check  |                           | check          |
```

### Phase 1: Read it to a partner and check (25 min)

In pairs. Each student reads their frame aloud to a partner. The partner says what happened, out loud, in their
own words, and at Level 3 says which of the two actions came first, without being shown anything. The writer then
works down a printed yes-or-no list, marking the page for each one (Quality Standards §E6: every item is answered
by a mark, not by a sentence).

**Levels 1-2 check list:**

> 1. My partner could say what happened. (yes / no)
> 2. My word has one letter or letter pair for each sound I hear. (yes / no)
> 3. My word names something that really happened on my own trip. (yes / no)
> 4. (Level 2) My `-ed` follows the right rule, and my sentence starts with a capital and ends with a period.
>    (yes / no)

**Level 3 check list:** items 1 to 4 above, plus:

> 5. My two verbs name two different actions, not one action twice. (yes / no)
> 6. My partner said the same action came first that I meant. (yes / no)

**Answer note:** answers vary. The one to watch is item 6: if the partner names the second action first, the time
words are doing no work, and Phase 2's swap is where it gets fixed.

### Phase 2: Swap in a more exact verb (30 min)

Each student changes one word for a word that tells a reader more, using the printed rows below as a starting
point and the Draft Word Bank for anything not listed. The old word is crossed out, never erased, and the new one
is written above it, so both are visible (Section 0.4a).

> moved -> walked, rushed, climbed
> used it -> packed, zipped, locked
> looked -> watched, checked, noticed
> helped -> carried, tidied, counted
> started -> opened, unlocked, boarded

- **Levels 1-2:** cross out your word and write a more exact one above it, then read the new frame aloud.
- **Level 3:** cross out one of your two verbs and write a more exact one above it; if both verbs are already
  exact and in the right order, swap the one your partner found hardest to picture.

**Answer note:** answers vary. A swap is genuine when the new verb could not be said about most trips in the
room: "climbed" is more exact than "moved," and "boarded" is more exact than "started," but "walked" for
"rushed" is a change, not an improvement, and is the one to send back. Every verb on the list is regular, so the
`-ed` rule still applies to whatever the student chooses.

**Board-dependent moment:** the "strong sentence" board from Day 1 stays up and gains a second line under each
column: the verb that was crossed out and the verb that replaced it, added live as students volunteer swaps.

### Phase 3: Final line check (20 min)

Each student reads their improved frame aloud to a different partner from Phase 1. The listener says the one
thing they can picture best about the trip. Nothing is rewritten in this phase.

**Task for every listener:** say the part you can picture best, then say whether it happened at the start, the
middle, or the end of the trip.

**What this lesson does not do.** There is no Peer Editing form, no publishing, and no Closing Transfer Check at
this position; every student leaves Day 2 with a finished frame that has been checked once and improved once.
Lesson 4 runs the reader's request, Peer Editing, the final copy, publishing, and the Closing Transfer Check, and
never re-runs today's check list.
