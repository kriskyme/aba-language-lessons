## What this lesson works from

**The carried piece.** Each student brings the frame they wrote and improved at Lesson 3, the one thing a Writing
Set carries forward (Quality Standards §D9). Everything else this lesson needs is on its own pages.

**Scenario.** The same trip, chosen from the printed list at Lesson 3 and reprinted below for anyone starting
fresh. It is remembered, not shown.

**The three frames, stated once for this day and called "the frame" afterward:**

> **Level 1:** First, ___.
> **Level 2:** I ___-ed.
> **Level 3:** First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.

**Trip list (reprinted so a student without a carried page can start here):** to the store, to school, to the
park, to a friend's house, on the bus, to the market, to a game, to the library.

**Draft Word Bank (reprinted from Lesson 2 Day 2; posted on the board and printed in the packet).**

> **Event words (for the one-word frame):** bus, walk, line, stop, rain, shop, game, snack
> **Getting-ready verbs:** packed, dressed, zipped, locked, tidied
> **Moving verbs:** walked, rushed, stepped, hurried, climbed
> **Doing-there verbs:** shopped, looked, picked, counted, carried
> **Waiting and looking verbs:** waited, checked, watched, smiled, noticed
> **Time words (part of the frame, not spelled from sound):** first, then, next, after that, last

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** What `-ed` does, its four spelling rules
and three sounds; the time word at the front with its comma, and what puts two actions in an order. Every verb in
a Word Bank, model, or item is regular.

**What this lesson never re-runs.** Lesson 3 Day 2 already checked the spelling, the `-ed` rule, the capital and
period, and whether the two actions were in the right order. Today's writing is one more sentence for a reader
who asked for it, and a choice between two, not a second correctness pass (Lesson prompt 0.4a).

## DAY 1: A READER'S REQUEST, PEER EDITING, AND ONE MORE LINE (75 MIN)

```
0            15                                 60                75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1---|-------------Phase 2--------------|----Phase 3-----|
| A reader's | Peer Editing, then one more      | Revision       |
| request    | line and the better of two       | share          |
```

### Phase 1: A reader's request (15 min)

Partners trade pages and read each other's frame. Each reader writes one line on the writer's page: **which
moment of the trip they would like a second line about.** Printed moments to choose from, so the request is
always answerable:

> before you left, the way there, the waiting, the middle, the way back, right at the end

This is one request. There is no form, no compliment, and no suggestion at this phase.

**Answer note:** answers vary. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: every page has exactly one
moment written on it, and the moment named is one the writer could actually say something about. A reader who
names a moment their partner's trip did not have (the way back, on a trip that ended somewhere else) is sent back
to choose again.

### Phase 2: Peer Editing, then one more line (45 min)

_Peer Editing (15 min)._ Beginner runs its Peer Editing exchange here, at Lesson 4 Day 1 (Lesson prompt 0.6).
With the same partner, each student reads their frame aloud a second time and the partner marks the printed form
on the writer's page:

> 1. I could say what happened. (yes / no)
> 2. It tells me something about the trip I could not have guessed. (yes / no)
> 3. The word I liked best: ___
> 4. The moment I would like to hear about: ___ (from the list above)

**Answer note:** answers vary. Item 3 is the compliment and item 4 restates the request in the partner's own
hand, so the writer has both in front of them for the next step. What to catch: an item 1 "no" means the partner
could not follow what happened, and that word is the one to change in the next step.

_One more line (20 min)._ Each student writes the frame once more, about the moment their reader asked for. This
is the lesson's one round.

- **Level 1:** First, ___.
- **Level 2:** I ___-ed.
- **Level 3:** First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.

_The better of the two (10 min)._ Each student reads both of their lines, the carried one and the new one, and
circles the one that tells a reader more about their trip. The circled one is what goes into the final copy
tomorrow; the other stays on the page, uncrossed, so both are still visible.

**Answer note:** answers vary. The choice is genuine either way; what is checked is that the student can say in
one sentence why the circled one tells more ("it says what happened while I waited, and nobody would guess
that"). A student who circles the new one only because it is newer is asked to read both aloud again.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the student says what happened at that
moment and the teacher writes it into the frame, then the student reads both lines back and says which one they
want and which word decided it. The choosing is the student's; only the writing is scribed.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

Three or four students read out both of their lines and say which one they circled and why.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a before-and-after board built from the volunteered pairs, one
line per Level: the first line, the second line, and the one word that decided the choice. Nothing on it comes
from a handout.

**Rotated prompts (three, as the share moves):** which of your two lines tells a reader more; which moment did
your partner ask you to write about; which verb on the board would also fit your trip.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "I circled First, ___, because it says ___."
- **Level 2:** "I circled I ___-ed, because it says ___."
- **Level 3:** "I circled First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed, because it says what happened first and what happened
  after."

**Task for every listener:** put a mark next to the word on your own page that your neighbour's choice makes you
want to change.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY, PUBLISHING, AND THE CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0                20                            50                    75  (minutes)
|----Phase 1-----|-----------Phase 2-----------|-------Phase 3------|
| Final copy     | Publish: read to two        | Closing Transfer    |
|                | partners in turn            | Check (last night)  |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy (20 min)

Each student writes a clean copy of the line they circled, on the final-copy line, with the capital and the
period in place at Levels 2-3. Nothing is counted and no check list is run; the checking happened at Lesson 3 Day
2 and the choosing happened yesterday.

**Answer note:** answers vary. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: the copy matches the circled
line word for word, and the letters are formed clearly enough for a partner to read at arm's length, since the
next phase hands it to two of them.

### Phase 2: Publish: read it to two partners in turn (30 min)

The piece is finished. Each student reads their final copy aloud to one partner, who says what happened in their
own words, and then to a second partner, who does the same. Students stay where they sit; the pages move between
neighbours rather than the class moving (Quality Standards §D11). Nothing is posted, hung, or walked around, and
nothing is collected.

- **Level 1:** read your line, then say which trip it was.
- **Level 2:** read your line.
- **Level 3:** read your line, then say which action happened first.

**Task for every listener:** say back what happened, in your own words, before you read your own.

**Answer note:** answers vary. What the teacher listens for across the room: a listener who says back something
that did not happen means the verb was not exact, and that is worth naming aloud once, gently, using the board's
before-and-after lines rather than the student's page.

### Phase 3: Closing Transfer Check (25 min)

Every student applies the Set's spotlighted skill, telling a reader what happened in the order it happened and
spelling each word from its sounds, to something new: **last night**, which appears nowhere else in this Set. In
pairs, on a small card:

- **Level 1:** write First, ___. about last night, with a word you sound out. Then say the sounds aloud to your
  partner in order.
- **Level 2:** write I ___-ed. about last night, with a capital and a period, and say which of the four rules
  your `-ed` used.
- **Level 3:** write First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed. about last night, with two verbs that name two different
  actions.

**The partner test, every Level:** your partner reads your card and says what happened. At Level 3 the partner
must also say which action came first; if they name the wrong one, the writer changes a time word or a verb on
the card.

**Answer note:** answers vary; no verb for last night is prepared or printed. The check is a demonstration, not a
report: nobody is asked whether they feel confident and nothing is collected or graded. Two or three pairs are
cold-called to read their cards.

**Board-dependent moment:** the cold-called cards are captured live on the board as they are read, grouped by
which part of the evening the action names, so the room sees how many different true sequences one evening holds.

**The Set ends here.** The piece is finished and published; nothing is kept back, and no lesson follows it in
this Module.
