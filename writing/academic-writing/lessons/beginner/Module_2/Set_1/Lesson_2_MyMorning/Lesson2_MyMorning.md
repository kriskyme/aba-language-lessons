## Grammar focuses

**Focus B (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class): the time word goes first, and it says the order.** The rule,
stated plainly: a time word sits at the front of the sentence, a comma comes after it, and the word itself tells
the reader where that action sits in the story. First and last are fixed ends; then, next, and after that move
the story along in the middle. This is Level 1's own Module 2 row, which makes the time chunk the frame, taught
to the whole room.

**How Focus B pairs with Focus A (named aloud at the start of Phase 1):** yesterday's work was saying an action
was already over by putting `-ed` on it; today's is putting two finished actions in the order they really
happened, so a reader knows which came first.

**Levels 1-2 receive Focus B as whole-class content, not as a separate session** (Lesson prompt 0.4). Level 1's
frame already carries a time word, given rather than chosen; Level 2's frame carries none. What both take from
the mini-lesson is the judgment about order, which their own tasks use in Phase 2 and Phase 3.

**Confusable pair (Phase 3): the doubling rule.** hoped / hopped, taped / tapped, planed / planned, pined /
pinned. Both spellings are real verbs, one letter apart, and only the sentence decides which is meant, so a
spelling scan alone does not finish an item. It drills the hardest of Focus A's four rules. New to the Rotation
Log.

## DAY 1: PUTTING TWO ACTIONS IN ORDER (75 MIN)

```
0            20                          50                    75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1---|---------Phase 2-----------|-------Phase 3-------|
| Focus B    | Fix the wrong word,       | Confusable pair:     |
| mini-lesson| put each verb in its group| the doubling rule    |
```

### Phase 1: Focus B mini-lesson: the time word goes first (20 min)

The rule and its counter-model together, printed before any task that uses it:

> **First, I washed.**
> **Then, I packed my bag.**
> **Next, I walked to the bus.**
> **Last, I waited.**
>
> The time word comes first, then a comma, then what you did.
> First and last are the two ends. Then, next, and after that move the story along in the middle.
>
> This is not an order: First, I walked. First, I waited. (Two firsts. A reader cannot tell which came
> first.)

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** the class builds one new two-part sequence about this morning
that nobody has said yet, choosing the two verbs from different groups of the Word Bank as the teacher writes
them up with their time words. Then the class tries putting the same two verbs in the opposite order and says
what changes about the story. Both stay on the board for Phase 2.

### Phase 2: Fix the wrong word, then sort the verbs (30 min)

**Activity 1: Fix the wrong word (15 min).** Three printed sentences per Level. Each has one thing wrong: a past
form built with the wrong rule, or a time word that puts the story in an impossible order. Students cross it out
and write the correction above it. Nothing is erased.

- **Levels 1-2:**

> 1. I walkd.
> 2. I hoped to the bus stop.
> 3. Last, I washed. First, I dressed.

- **Level 3:**

> 1. First, I hurryed. Then, I walked.
> 2. First, I rushed. Then, I hurried.
> 3. Then, I packed. First, I dressed.

**Answer note:** Levels 1-2: walkd becomes walked (rule 1, the e is missing); hoped becomes hopped (rule 3, one
short vowel and one consonant, so the p doubles, and hoped means something else); item 3's time words are
backwards, so either the sentences swap or the time words do. Level 3: hurryed becomes hurried (rule 4, the y
becomes i); item 2's two verbs name one action twice, so the second is replaced with a verb from another group;
item 3 opens with Then and follows with First, which no reader can order.

**Activity 2: Put each verb in its group (15 min).** Twelve verbs from the Word Bank, scrambled. Students write
each one under the group it belongs to. The groups do not hold the same number of verbs, so the last few cannot
be placed by counting.

> tasted, hurried, zipped, watched, cooked, stepped, dressed, checked, filled, walked, packed, smiled

**Answer note:** Moving: hurried, stepped, walked. Getting-ready: zipped, dressed, packed. Eating: tasted,
cooked, filled. Waiting and looking: watched, checked, smiled. Three, three, three, three here, but the printed
order scrambles them so no group's members sit together.

### Phase 3: The doubling rule (25 min)

**Activity 3: Circle the verb that belongs in the sentence (25 min).** Eight sentences, each with two spellings
one letter apart. Both are real verbs, so only the meaning of the sentence decides it.

> 1. I (hoped / hopped) off the bus at my stop.
> 2. I (hoped / hopped) for a dry morning.
> 3. I (taped / tapped) the note on the door.
> 4. I (taped / tapped) my pen on the desk while I waited.
> 5. I (planed / planned) my morning the night before.
> 6. I (pined / pinned) my name tag to my bag.
> 7. I (hoped / hopped) the bus would come early.
> 8. I (taped / tapped) the box shut.

**Answer note:** hopped, hoped, taped, tapped, planned, pinned, hoped, taped. Every rejected option is a real
English verb, so no item can be finished by crossing out a nonsense spelling. Items 1 and 2, and 3 and 4, use the
same pair in opposite directions on purpose: a student who learns "the double one is always right" gets items 2
and 3 wrong.

**Level 3, heavier tier (inside the same 25 minutes):** choose two of the eight correct verbs that could make one
sequence in a real morning, write them in order with their time words, and say which happened first.

**Answer note:** planned then hopped, pinned then tapped, and taped then hopped all work. What fails: two verbs
that name one action, or a pair the student cannot put in a real order.

## DAY 2: SPELLING FROM SOUND, AND PREWRITING ON A NEW EVENT (75 MIN)

```
0                     25                            55                75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1-------|-----------Phase 2-----------|-----Phase 3-----|
| Spell from sound    | Prewriting: my trip and the | Prewriting share |
| with a partner      | Draft Word Bank             | and board        |
```

### Phase 1: Spell from sound with a partner (25 min)

In pairs. Partner A reads three verbs aloud from the printed Word Bank while Partner B covers the list with one
hand and writes each verb from its sounds. Then they check the three together against the list and swap roles. No
frame is written in this phase; these are verbs only.

**Reader's three verbs, Round 1:** hurried, zipped, waited.
**Reader's three verbs, Round 2:** closed, watched, stepped.

**Answer note:** the reader's own printed list is the key. Each round takes one verb from a different rule on
purpose (round 1: y becomes i, doubling, add -ed with the /id/ sound; round 2: add -d, add -ed, doubling), so the
writer cannot carry one rule through all three. hurried and stepped are the two most often written with the wrong
number of letters.

- **Levels 1-2:** write each verb, then check it against the list.
- **Level 3:** write each verb, then mark the two of the six that could make one sequence, and number them 1 and
  2 in the order they would happen.

**Answer note (Level 3):** zipped then hurried, closed then stepped, and waited then stepped all work. Marking
hurried with stepped, or any two verbs the student cannot order, is the error to catch.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the reader says the verb and the student
traces it from the printed list, then says what the action was. The tracing copy is already printed on the page;
nothing is prepared in advance.

### Phase 2: Prewriting on a new event: my trip (30 min)

**Introducing the draft event.** From here to the end of the Set, the writing is about one short trip the student
has really taken. Students circle one from this printed list, so nobody is without one:

> to the store, to school, to the park, to a friend's house, on the bus, to the market, to a game, to the library

**Draft Word Bank (the same groups, refilled for a trip; printed here and reprinted in every later lesson):**

> **Event words (for the one-word frame):** bus, walk, line, stop, rain, shop, game, snack
> **Getting-ready verbs:** packed, dressed, zipped, locked, tidied
> **Moving verbs:** walked, rushed, stepped, hurried, climbed
> **Doing-there verbs:** shopped, looked, picked, counted, carried
> **Waiting and looking verbs:** waited, checked, watched, smiled, noticed
> **Time words (part of the frame, not spelled from sound):** first, then, next, after that, last

**Activity 4: Plan the words (30 min).** Three words from the Draft Word Bank are written on the page by the
student, chosen to fit the trip they circled. Then one is circled.

- **Levels 1-2:** write three words from the Draft Word Bank that fit your trip, then circle the one that names
  the biggest thing that happened.
- **Level 3:** write three words that fit your trip, then circle two that name two different actions, and number
  them 1 and 2 in the order they happened.

**Answer note:** answers vary by trip. Circulation check: the three words come from at least two different
groups, and at Level 3 the circled two are not two words for one action (rushed and hurried). A student whose
trip was a five-minute walk to the store has as much to say as one who went to a game; check they have not
written words that belong to a different trip.

**This is rehearsal, not a plan to keep** (Quality Standards §D9). Lesson 3 rebuilds its own plan on its own page,
from its own reprinted Draft Word Bank, so nothing written here has to survive to be useful, and no task here
says "next time."

### Phase 3: Prewriting share and board synthesis (20 min)

Three students from each Level read out one of their words and the trip it belongs to. The teacher builds one
shared list on the board, grouped by the Word Bank's own groups, so the room can see which parts of a trip the
class has words for and which are still nearly empty.

**Board-dependent moment:** the list exists nowhere on any student page; it is built live from what students
report, and it holds more words than any one student wrote.

**Rotated prompts (three, in order, as the share moves around the room):** which word did you write that nobody
else has said yet; which of your words names something that took the longest; which group on the board is still
almost empty, and what word could go in it.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "On my trip, there was a ___."
- **Level 2:** "I wrote ___, and that happened on my trip to ___."
- **Level 3:** "I wrote ___ and ___, and ___ happened first."

**Task for every listener:** write on your own page one word you hear that also fits your trip, from a group you
have not used yet.
