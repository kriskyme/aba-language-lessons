## Scenario

Every student tells a reader what happened, in order, so a reader who was not there can follow it. The event for
this lesson and the next is **the student's own morning**, what they actually did before class today, written
from memory. Nothing is brought in, drawn, or shown.

**Where Level 1's open slot sits.** The CSV's Level 1 row makes the time-order chunk the frame *around* the
spelled word, and its own worked example spells an event word ("park"), not a verb. So Level 1's slot holds an
event word and the time word is given. In Phase 3's guided round only, the time word rotates across the three
rounds so the three captions come out as one real three-part story instead of the same opener three times.

**The three frames, stated once here and called "the frame" from this point on:**

> **Level 1:** First, ___.
> **Level 2:** I ___-ed.
> **Level 3:** First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.

## Word Bank (teacher-provided; posted on the board and printed in the packet, used through Lesson 2 Day 1)

Every verb here is a regular verb, so nothing in this Set contradicts the `-ed` rule it teaches. The list is
longer than any one task needs, so no task can be finished by elimination (Quality Standards §C4).

> **Event words (for the one-word frame):** nap, bus, wash, snack, rush, line, rain, stop
> **Moving verbs:** walked, rushed, stepped, hopped, hurried
> **Getting-ready verbs:** washed, dressed, packed, zipped, tidied
> **Eating verbs:** cooked, mixed, tasted, filled, closed
> **Waiting and looking verbs:** waited, looked, checked, watched, smiled
> **Time words (part of the frame, not spelled from sound):** first, then, next, after that, last

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class): the regular past `-ed`.** What it does (a verb names what you
did; `-ed` says it already happened), its four spelling rules, and its three sounds. This is hand-selected for
Module 2 at Level 1's row: Beginner has no Paragraph Composition Level for Section 0.4's row-selection rule to
anchor on, and the Bank's own Levels 1-3 rows are written for Describing, so neither resolves here. See
`Module2_Beginner_Lesson_Plan.md` and `Index.md`'s Pending work for both gaps.

**Level 2's own row, folded into the same mini-lesson (never a separate session):** the frame "I ___-ed." is one
form with one slot, and it is a whole sentence, so it takes a capital I and a period. Level 3's two-sentence
frame is shown alongside it; the time words that order it are Lesson 2's business and are not taught today.

**No irregular verbs in the taught material.** Every verb in every Word Bank, model, and item is regular, so
the rule this Set teaches is never contradicted by the material it is taught on. Ordinary instruction wording is
not bound by this ("which came first" is a phrase to read, not a form to copy). A student
who writes "I went" or "I got up" is answered by the teacher in the moment, not by a printed rule; irregulars
belong to a later Set.

## DAY 1: THE REGULAR PAST, INPUT AND MODELING (75 MIN)

```
0        15                        45                    75  (minutes)
|-Phase 1-|--------Phase 2----------|-------Phase 3-------|
| Hook,   | Focus A mini-lesson:    | Controlled practice  |
| Skill   | what -ed does, its four | A: pairs, choosing,   |
| Spot-   | rules, its three sounds | the guided round      |
| light   |                         |                       |
```

### Phase 1: Hook, Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Hook (8 min):** two students each say one thing that happened in their morning, and then say which of the two
happened first. The teacher writes both on the board in the order the class decides. A third student adds
something that happened between them. Nothing is brought out; students tell what they already remember.

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "Today we're practicing telling a reader what happened, in the order it happened,
and spelling each word from its sounds." One sentence, the same for every Level. No Level's own required feature
is named here (Quality Standards §D1); Level 3's two-different-actions demand lives inside Level 3's own tasks.

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: the regular past `-ed` (30 min)

_What `-ed` does (8 min)._ A verb names what you did. Adding `-ed` says it already happened, so a reader knows it
is over: I walk (now) becomes I walked (before now). Two examples built on the board from the hook's own
sentences.

_The four spelling rules (12 min)._ The grammar box, printed in the packet before any task that uses it:

| Rule | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add `-ed` | most verbs | walk, walked; wash, washed; look, looked |
| Add `-d` | the verb already ends in e | close, closed; smile, smiled; taste, tasted |
| Double the last letter, then add `-ed` | one short vowel and one consonant at the end | hop, hopped; zip, zipped; step, stepped |
| Change y to i, then add `-ed` | a consonant before the y | hurry, hurried; tidy, tidied; carry, carried |

_The three sounds of `-ed` (10 min)._ The ending is spelled the same and heard three ways:

> `/t/` in walked, packed, washed, hopped
> `/d/` in filled, smiled, closed, hurried
> `/id/` in waited, tasted, needed, counted

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** the class supplies one verb from this morning that is not on any
printed list, and the teacher builds its past form on the board: which of the four rules it takes, then which of
the three sounds the class hears at the end. It stays on the board for Day 2.

_The frames, folded in (last few minutes)._ The three frames from the Scenario section are shown once, side by
side, and read aloud. What the teacher names: Level 1's frame gives the time word and the student spells the
event; Level 2's frame is a whole sentence, so it takes a capital I and a period; Level 3's frame is two
sentences, and what orders them is Lesson 2's work. No frame is completed here.

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A (30 min)

**Activity 1: Two actions, or one action twice? (whole class, 10 min).** Eight printed pairs. Students circle the
pairs that name two different actions and cross out the pairs that name one action twice.

> 1. washed and dressed
> 2. rushed and hurried
> 3. cooked and tasted
> 4. walked and stepped
> 5. packed and zipped
> 6. looked and watched
> 7. waited and checked
> 8. tidied and cleaned

**Answer note:** circle 1, 3, 5, and 7 (two different actions each: washing then putting clothes on; making food
then trying it; putting things in the bag then closing it; standing still then looking at something). Cross out
2, 4, 6, and 8, which name one action with two words. Item 7 is the hard one and is meant to be argued: accept it
circled if the student can say what the difference is, and crossed out if they say checking is just what waiting
looks like.

**Activity 2: Choose what fits (10 min).** The eight words below are printed. Each student circles the ones that
name something that really happened in their own morning and crosses out the ones that did not. Every student's
page comes out different; the point is the judgment, not a key.

> nap, bus, wash, snack, rush, line, rain, stop

- **Levels 1-2:** circle the words that name something that happened to you this morning, cross out the ones that
  did not.
- **Level 3:** circle two that happened, and write a 1 next to the one that happened first.

**Answer note:** no fixed key; answers depend on the student's own morning. Circulation check: each student can
say out loud why one crossed-out word does not fit ("there was no bus, I walked"). At Level 3, check the two
circled really happened in that order, not in the order they are printed.

**Activity 3: The guided round (10 min).** The lesson's one round of frame writing, and the only task in this Set
that asks for the frame more than once.

- **Level 1:** write the frame three times, with a different time word each time and a different event word each
  time: First, ___. Then, ___. Last, ___.
- **Level 2:** write the frame three times, taking one verb from each of three groups: a Moving verb, a
  Getting-ready verb, and a Waiting and looking verb.
- **Level 3:** write one three-part sequence, one verb from each of those same three groups: First, I ___-ed.
  Then, I ___-ed. Last, I ___-ed.

**Answer note:** answers vary by morning. What is checked: the verb is spelled from sound with the right one of
the four rules (circulate and hear it stretched); at Levels 2-3 the capital and the period are present; at Level
3 the three actions are in the order they really happened, not the order the groups are printed in.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the eight event words are printed as
words to choose from rather than to spell. The student circles one for each time word and reads the finished
three-part story aloud, then says which part of the morning each word names. Nothing here is prepared in advance;
the words are already on the printed page.

## DAY 2: DEEPER PRACTICE AND THE MENTOR LADDER (75 MIN)

```
0                20                       45                        75  (minutes)
|----Phase 1-----|--------Phase 2---------|---------Phase 3---------|
| Better of two  | Build the past from    | Leveled Mentor Ladder    |
|                | the base verb          | walkthrough              |
```

### Phase 1: Better of two (20 min)

Six pairs of printed sentences about a morning. In each pair, one sentence tells a reader what actually happened
and the other could be said about almost anything. Students circle the better one and write the verb that made it
better.

> 1. First, I started. / First, I washed.
> 2. Then, I moved. / Then, I hurried.
> 3. Next, I used it. / Next, I zipped it.
> 4. Then, I finished. / Then, I tasted it.
> 5. First, I helped. / First, I cooked.
> 6. Last, I looked. / Last, I checked the clock.

**Answer note:** washed, hurried, zipped, tasted, cooked, checked. Items 1, 2, 4, and 5 turn on a verb that names
no particular action (started, moved, finished, helped); items 3 and 6 both name real actions, and the better one
is the more exact of the two (zipped says how it was used; checked the clock says what was looked at), so the
task is not a scan for one vague word.

- **Level 1:** circle the better sentence, then write the verb that made it better next to it.
- **Level 2:** circle the better sentence, then write item 5's better sentence out in the frame.
- **Level 3:** circle the better sentence in each pair, then write items 1 and 5's better verbs as one sequence.

### Phase 2: Build the past from the base verb (25 min)

Eight verbs are printed in their plain form. Students write each one in its past form and write the number of the
rule it follows from the grammar box (1 add -ed, 2 add -d, 3 double, 4 y becomes i).

> 1. walk
> 2. like
> 3. hop
> 4. hurry
> 5. wash
> 6. close
> 7. zip
> 8. tidy

**Answer note:** walked (1), liked (2), hopped (3), hurried (4), washed (1), closed (2), zipped (3), tidied (4).
Two verbs per rule, in a scrambled order, so the rule cannot be guessed from the item's position. Items 3 and 7
are the two most often written with a single consonant; 4 and 8 the two most often written with the y left in.

**Level 3, heavier tier (inside the same 25 minutes):** choose two of the eight past forms that could make one
sequence in a real morning, write them in order, and say which happened first.

**Answer note:** several correct pairs (washed then dressed is not available here, but washed then hurried,
tidied then closed, and hopped then walked all work). What fails: two verbs that name one action (hurried and
hopped), or a pair the student cannot put in a real order.

### Phase 3: Leveled Mentor Ladder walkthrough (30 min)

Present all three Mentor Texts together, in Level order, on the board or read aloud: the ladder is the teacher's
material and is never printed in the packet (packet prompt 2.6). All three tell one model student's own morning,
so the class watches one real sequence climb three steps. Nina's morning: it rained, she waited a long time for
the bus, and she hurried to class.

**Level 1 Mentor Text**

> Nina said the word first: bus.
> She stretched it: /b/ /u/ /s/.
> She wrote: First, bus.

**Level 2 Mentor Text**

> Nina picked a different moment of the same morning: the waiting.
> Base verb: wait. Rule 1, add -ed. The ending sounds like /id/.
> Frame: I ___-ed.
> Nina's answer: I waited.

**Level 3 Mentor Text**

> Frame: First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.
> Nina's answer: First, I waited. Then, I hurried.
> What Nina did not write: First, I hurried. Then, I rushed. Those two verbs name one action twice, and they
> are not in any order.

**Walkthrough (teacher-led, from the board):** for each text, name in one line what makes it that Level's version
(an event named inside a time word; one action told as a whole sentence, with its rule and its sound; two
actions put in an order against each other). Every model takes a different moment of the same morning, so the
ladder never repeats itself.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** as the class looks at the three texts, build a "what changes
each step" list on the board from what students notice, one line per step, in their own words. It sits under
yesterday's co-constructed verb, which stays up.

**What students do (5 min, every Level):** find your own Level's text on the board and say to a partner one thing
the text one step up does that yours does not. Nothing is printed and nothing is written.

**Task for every listener:** each partner repeats back the one difference their partner named before saying their
own.
