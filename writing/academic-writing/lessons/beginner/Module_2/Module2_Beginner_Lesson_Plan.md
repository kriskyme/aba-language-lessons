## Scenario: My Morning, then My Trip

Every student tells a reader what happened, in order, so the reader knows what came first and what came after.
**Purpose (Module 2, Narrating):** name real past events in the order they happened, spelling each word from
sound, so a reader who was not there can follow the sequence. Nothing in this Set describes an object, explains a
cause, or judges anything.

- **Practice event (Lessons 1-2 and the Mentor Ladder): my morning.** What the student actually did before class
  today. Grammar input, controlled practice, the frame rounds, and every Mentor Text use it. It is written from
  memory: nothing is brought in, drawn, or shown.
- **Draft event (introduced at Lesson 2 Day 2's prewriting; Lessons 3-4 write about it): my trip.** One short
  trip the student has really taken, chosen from a printed list of eight so nobody is without one: **to the
  store, to school, to the park, to a friend's house, on the bus, to the market, to a game, to the library.**
  Category title and slug for Lessons 3-4: "My Trip." The Draft Word Bank is built for the category, not one
  trip.
- **Closing Transfer Check event (Lesson 4 Day 2, a third event, used nowhere else in the Set):** last night.
- **No named reader and no second audience.** A named reader with a stated concern is Level 6's required feature
  and two distinct readers is Level 8's (Lesson prompt 0.2); Beginner reaches neither. Its reader is the partner
  who hears the sequence read aloud at Lesson 4.

**Both events are writable at every task Level.** Level 1 spells one event word into the time-chunk frame
("First, ___."): a morning and a trip both hand a student several decodable one-word events (nap, bus, rush,
wash, snack, rain, line, stop). Level 2 spells one regular past verb into "I ___-ed." (walked, waited, packed,
rushed, looked). Level 3 spells two verbs into a two-sentence sequence, and both events supply two genuinely
*different* actions in a real order, which is Level 3's own demand.

**Where Level 1's open slot sits, and why the chunk rotates.** The CSV's Level 1 row makes the memorized
time-order chunk the frame *around* the spelled word, and its own worked example spells an event word ("park"),
not a verb. So Level 1's slot holds an event word, and the chunk itself is given. In Lesson 1 Day 1's guided
round only, the chunk rotates across the three rounds (First / Then / Last) so the three captions come out as one
real three-part story rather than the same opener three times. Everywhere else the chunk is "First," and the task
says "the frame." This keeps Level 1 clearly distinct from Level 2's past-tense sentence and does not hand the
Band's floor Level the Set's hardest spelling pattern.

## Grammar Focus A/B

**Row anchoring, and a second gap in the same bank.** Lesson prompt 0.4's row-selection rule anchors Focus A/B at
a band's own native Paragraph Composition row and names only Intermediate, Advanced, and Proficient; Beginner has
no Paragraph Composition Level, so the rule does not resolve. This Set anchors at **Level 1's row** again, the
Band's calibration Level (Conventions §A), the same resolution Module 1 recorded and already logged in
`Index.md`'s Pending work. Module 2 exposes a **second** gap Module 1 did not: the Bank's Levels 1-3 rows are
written for Describing. Level 3's Focus A cell is the simple-present `-s` form and its Focus B cell is `"and"
inside a two-slot frame`, neither of which fits a Narrating Set whose frames are past-tense and sequential. The
pair below is therefore **hand-selected against Module 2's own CSV rows**, the way a Module Pair's second Module
has to hand-select until the Bank's Alternate column exists. Logged as its own Pending work entry.

- **Focus A (hand-selected for Module 2, at Level 1's row): the regular past `-ed`.** Its four spelling rules
  (most verbs add -ed: walk, walked; a verb ending in e adds -d: like, liked; a short vowel and one consonant
  doubles that consonant: hop, hopped; a consonant plus y turns y into ied: hurry, hurried) and its three sounds
  (/t/ in walked and packed, /d/ in filled and listened, /id/ in waited and needed). Still spelling-from-sound,
  which is what Level 1's row requires, but genuinely different content from Module 1's short-vowel work.
- **Level 2's own row, folded into the same mini-lesson (never a separate session):** the fixed frame "I ___-ed."
  is one form with one slot, and it is a whole sentence, so it takes a capital I and a period.
- **No irregular verbs anywhere in this Set.** Every verb in every Word Bank, model, and item is regular, so the
  `-ed` rule the Set teaches is never contradicted by the material it is taught on. Irregulars belong to a later
  Set, and to Intermediate's own Module 2.
- **Focus B (Lesson 2 Day 1, taught as its own point): time-order words** (first, then, next, after that, last)
  at the front of a sentence, with a comma after them, marking which action came first and which came after. This
  is Level 1's own Module 2 row, which makes the time chunk the frame, taught to the whole room. Different in
  kind from Module 1's Focus B ("and" joining two describing words).
- **Confusable pair (Lesson 2 Day 1): the doubling rule** - hoped / hopped, taped / tapped, planed / planned,
  pined / pinned. Both spellings are real words and only the sentence decides which, so a spelling scan alone
  does not finish an item, and it drills exactly the hardest of Focus A's four rules. New to the Rotation Log.
- **Level 3's frame is a two-sentence sequence,** `First, I ___-ed. Then, I ___-ed.`, not the CSV example's "and"
  version. Lesson prompt 0.2's Level 3 entry allows either ("a two-slot frame or a two-sentence sequence"), and
  the sequence is what carries Focus B and the Module's own verb, where an "and" frame would repeat Beginner
  Module 1's shape and Intermediate Module 2's at once.
- **Essay Focus A/B direction: N/A.** Beginner's task Levels never reach Essay Composition, so Lesson prompt 0.4c
  does not apply, and neither does its Level 5 receptive bridge.

## Leveled Mentor Ladder direction (on the practice event, my morning)

Presented by the teacher on Lesson 1 Day 2, on the board or read aloud, in Level order; never printed in the
packet (Lesson prompt 0.1b, packet prompt 2.6). All three models tell one model student's own morning, so the
class watches one real sequence climb three steps. Beginner has **3 Mentor Texts** and no Mentor Essay.
Directions only; the texts themselves are written at generation time.

- **Level 1:** one event word sounded out into the time chunk, with the sounding-out made visible (the sounds
  named before the letters are written), so the model teaches the routine and not a word to copy. The event named
  is a thing that happened, not a thing the student owns.
- **Level 2:** one regular past verb in "I ___-ed.", about a **different** moment of the same morning from Level
  1's, so the step up the ladder is a step and not a restatement, with the `-ed` visibly added to a base verb the
  class has just met and the capital and period visible.
- **Level 3:** two verbs in the two-sentence sequence, two genuinely different actions in a real order, plus the
  counter-model the teacher names aloud: the same action twice, or two verbs that name one action from two angles
  (rushed then hurried), is not a sequence.

The ladder is distinct in kind across the three Levels: an event named inside a time chunk, one action told as a
whole sentence, and two actions ordered against each other. It stays on the practice event throughout; the trip
never appears in it (0.1a: the ladder models form, not content).

## Lesson-by-lesson activity shapes (Quality Standards §D10; Lesson prompt 0.4d)

Every task Level in this Band is fixed-output, so this table is what keeps the Set from becoming twelve rounds of
the same frame. The frame is produced at most once per Level per day; Lesson 1 Day 1's guided round is the only
task that says "three times."

**Deliberate deviation from 0.4d's Lesson 1-4 column, recorded rather than silent.** 0.4d says a Beginner Set
uses that column as written, which would give this Set the identical shapes in the identical slots as the Module
1 Set the class has just finished. The four shapes that Lessons 1-2 are free to place (sort, choose what fits,
fix the wrong word, better of two, pair judgment) are therefore rotated: pair judgment moves from Lesson 2 Day 1
to Lesson 1 Day 1, better of two from Lesson 2 Day 1 to Lesson 1 Day 2, and fix the wrong word and sort move from
Lesson 1 to Lesson 2 Day 1. The shapes Lessons 3-4 use are fixed by the arc itself (plan the words, the draft,
check and improve, the reader's part, transfer) and are left where 0.4d puts them.

| Lesson | Day | Shape 1 | Shape 2 | Shape 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Pair judgment: circle the printed pairs that name two different actions, cross out the pairs that name one action twice | Choose what fits: circle the words that name something that really happened in your morning, cross out the ones that did not | **Complete the frame** - the Set's one guided round, the time chunk rotating First / Then / Last at Level 1 and one verb from each of three groups at Levels 2-3 |
| 1 | 2 | Better of two: circle the more exact of two printed frame sentences about a morning and write the verb that made it better | Build the past from the base verb: printed base verbs written back in their `-ed` form under the rule that governs each (Focus A practice, no frame produced) | Mentor Ladder walkthrough (no student production) |
| 2 | 1 | Fix the wrong word: three printed frame sentences about a morning, each with a verb misspelled under one of the four rules or an action that does not belong in the order | Put each verb in its group: the Word Bank sorted by what the action does | Confusable-pair drill: the doubling rule, circle the spelling the sentence needs |
| 2 | 2 | Spell from sound with a partner: a partner reads three Word Bank verbs aloud, the student writes each without looking, then checks (Level 3 also marks which two could make one sequence) | Plan the words: three Draft Word Bank words that fit the trip, circle the one (Level 3: two, in the order they happened) | Prewriting share and board synthesis (no printed production) |
| 3 | 1 | Circle the trip on the printed list, then plan the words | **The draft** - the frame completed once on the trip, the lesson's one round | Mid-draft share-out (read aloud, no new writing) |
| 3 | 2 | Check and improve: read the sequence to a partner, who says which action came first; swap in a more exact verb where one fits | Final line check, read aloud | - |
| 4 | 1 | A reader's request, then Peer Editing (oral partner check, Beginner's placement per 0.6) | **The reader's part** - one more frame sentence about the moment the reader asked for; circle the better of the two for the final copy | Revision share |
| 4 | 2 | Final copy | Publish: read aloud to two partners in turn, or copies passed around (room-neutral, Quality Standards §D11; **not** a gallery walk) | **Transfer** - the frame on a third event, last night |

Lessons 1-2 carry the item minimums (Lesson prompt 0.4b: at least 3 distinct activities per day, 6-8 items per
fill-in, classify, choose-the-form, or combine activity, at least 6 in the confusable-pair drill); Lessons 3-4 are
drafting and revision days and are exempt.

**Foundation Support (below Level 1, alongside it and never in place of it; Quality Standards §B, Lesson prompt
0.5).** Plugs in at three named points: Lesson 1 Day 1's guided round (match a printed event word to the moment
of the morning it names, select rather than spell), Lesson 2 Day 2's dictation (trace a pre-written verb, then
say what the action was), and Lesson 3 Day 1's draft (teacher-scribed dictation: the student says what happened
first, the teacher writes it, and the student says afterward which word names which action). Nothing here needs a
card set the teacher prepares.
