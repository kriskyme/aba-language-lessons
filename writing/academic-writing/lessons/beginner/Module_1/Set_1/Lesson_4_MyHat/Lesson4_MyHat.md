## DAY 1: A READER'S REQUEST, PEER EDITING, AND ONE MORE SENTENCE (75 MIN)

```
0            15                                 60                75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1---|-------------Phase 2--------------|----Phase 3-----|
| A reader's | Peer Editing, then one more      | Revision       |
| request    | sentence and the better of two   | share          |
```

### Phase 1: A reader's request (15 min)

Partners trade pages and read each other's frame. Each reader writes one line on the writer's page: **which part
of the hat they would like a second sentence about.** Printed parts to choose from, so the request is always
answerable:

> the top, the brim, the color, the inside, the band, the front

This is one request. There is no form, no compliment, and no suggestion at this phase.

**Answer note:** answers vary. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: every page has exactly one part
written on it, and the part named is one the writer could actually say a word about. A reader who names a part
their partner's hat does not have (a brim on a beanie) is sent back to choose again.

### Phase 2: Peer Editing, then one more sentence (45 min)

_Peer Editing (15 min)._ Beginner runs its Peer Editing exchange here, at Lesson 4 Day 1 (Lesson prompt 0.6).
With the same partner, each student reads their frame aloud a second time and the partner marks the printed form
on the writer's page:

> 1. I could say what each word describes. (yes / no)
> 2. The word tells me something I could not guess about the hat. (yes / no)
> 3. (Level 3) The two words told me two different things. (yes / no)
> 4. The word I liked best: ___
> 5. The part I would like to hear about: ___ (from the list above)

**Answer note:** answers vary. Item 4 is the compliment and item 5 restates the request in the partner's own
hand, so the writer has both in front of them for the next step. What to catch: an item 1 "no" means the partner
could not picture the word, and that word is the one to change in the next step.

_One more sentence (20 min)._ Each student writes the frame once more, about the part their reader asked for.
This is the lesson's one round.

- **Level 1:** ___ hat
- **Level 2:** It is ___.
- **Level 3:** It is ___ and ___.

_The better of the two (10 min)._ Each student reads both of their sentences, the carried one and the new one,
and circles the one that tells a reader more about their hat. The circled one is what goes into the final copy
tomorrow; the other stays on the page, uncrossed, so both are still visible.

**Answer note:** answers vary. The choice is genuine either way; what is checked is that the student can say in
one sentence why the circled one tells more ("it says what the top is like, and nobody can see that in a
picture"). A student who circles the new one only because it is newer is asked to read both aloud again.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the student says the new word aloud and
the teacher writes it into the frame, then the student reads both sentences back and points out loud to which one
they want, saying the word that decided it. The choosing is the student's; only the writing is scribed.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

Three or four students read out both of their sentences and say which one they circled and why.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a before-and-after board built from the volunteered pairs, one
line per Level: the first sentence, the second sentence, and the one word that decided the choice. Nothing on it
comes from a handout.

**Rotated prompts (three, as the share moves):** which of your two sentences tells a reader more; which word did
your partner ask you to write about; which word on the board would also fit your hat.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "I circled ___ hat, because it says ___."
- **Level 2:** "I circled It is ___, because it says ___."
- **Level 3:** "I circled It is ___ and ___, because those two words say ___ and ___."

**Task for every listener:** put a mark next to the word on your own page that your neighbour's choice makes you
want to change.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY, PUBLISHING, AND THE CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0                20                            50                    75  (minutes)
|----Phase 1-----|-----------Phase 2-----------|-------Phase 3------|
| Final copy     | Publish: read to two        | Closing Transfer    |
|                | partners in turn            | Check (the shoe)    |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy (20 min)

Each student writes a clean copy of the sentence they circled, on the final-copy line, with the capital and the
period in place at Levels 2-3. Nothing is counted and no check list is run; the checking happened at Lesson 3 Day
2 and the choosing happened yesterday.

**Answer note:** answers vary. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: the copy matches the circled
sentence word for word, and the letters are formed clearly enough for a partner to read at arm's length, since
the next phase hands it to two of them.

### Phase 2: Publish: read it to two partners in turn (30 min)

The piece is finished. Each student reads their final copy aloud to one partner, who says the one thing the
sentence tells them about the hat, and then to a second partner, who does the same. Students stay where they sit;
the pages move between neighbours rather than the class moving (Quality Standards §D11). Nothing is posted, hung,
or walked around, and nothing is collected.

- **Level 1:** read your phrase, then say the name of your hat.
- **Level 2:** read your sentence.
- **Level 3:** read your sentence, then say which two things your two words describe.

**Task for every listener:** say back the one thing the sentence tells you about the hat, in your own words,
before you read your own.

**Answer note:** answers vary. What the teacher listens for across the room: a listener who says back something
the sentence does not actually say means the word was not exact, and that is worth naming aloud once, gently,
using the board's before-and-after lines rather than the student's page.

### Phase 3: Closing Transfer Check (25 min)

Every student applies the Set's spotlighted skill, describing something of their own by choosing the word that
really fits it and spelling it from its sounds, to something new: **their own shoe**, which appears nowhere else
in this Set. In pairs, on a small card:

- **Level 1:** write ___ shoe, with a word you sound out. Then say the sounds aloud to your partner in order.
- **Level 2:** write It is ___. about your shoe, with a capital and a period.
- **Level 3:** write It is ___ and ___. about your shoe, with two words that say two different things.

**The partner test, every Level:** your partner reads your card and says what your word describes. At Level 3 the
partner must name two different things; if they say one thing twice, the writer changes one word on the card.

**Answer note:** answers vary; no shoe word is prepared or printed. The check is a demonstration, not a report:
nobody is asked whether they feel confident and nothing is collected or graded. Two or three pairs are cold-called
to read their cards.

**Board-dependent moment:** the cold-called cards are captured live on the board as they are read, grouped by
which part of the shoe the word describes, so the room sees how many different true words one object holds.

**The Set ends here.** The piece is finished and published; nothing is kept back, and no lesson follows it in
this Module.
