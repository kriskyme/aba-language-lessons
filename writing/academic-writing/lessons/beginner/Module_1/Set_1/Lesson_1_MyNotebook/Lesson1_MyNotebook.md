## Scenario

Every student describes something of their own so that a reader who has never seen it can picture it. The object
for this lesson and the next is **the notebook the student writes in**, described from what they know of it. The
notebook is a subject, not a prop (Quality Standards §D8): no task asks anyone to look at it, point to a part,
hold it up, or put it on the desk.

**Where Level 1's open slot sits.** The CSV's Level 1 row fixes a memorized adjective-noun frame; this Set puts
the open slot on the adjective ("___ book"), because the object is fixed by the Scenario and a noun slot would
have exactly one right answer, which cannot meet Level 1's required feature (a genuine choice the student sounds
out). The frame stays adjective-noun and the output stays one word spelled from sound.

**The three frames, stated once here and referred to as "the frame" from this point on:**

> **Level 1:** ___ book
> **Level 2:** It is ___.
> **Level 3:** It is ___ and ___.

## Word Bank (teacher-provided; posted on the board and printed in the packet, used through Lesson 2 Day 1)

Every describing word here is decodable from the letter-sound patterns Focus A teaches. Students draw their words
from this list all lesson; the list is longer than any one task needs, so no task can be finished by elimination
(Quality Standards §C4).

> **Color:** red, black, tan, gray, blue
> **Size and shape:** big, thin, flat, slim, long
> **Feel:** soft, hard, smooth, rough, bumpy
> **Condition:** new, old, bent, torn, clean
> **Cover and marks:** plain, lined, blank, spotted, striped

## Grammar focuses

**Focus A (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class): letter-sound spelling patterns for describing words.** Short
vowels (a in tan and flat, e in red and bent, i in big and thin, o in soft and long, u in bumpy), the blends and
digraphs those words carry (fl-, sl-, bl-, gr-, sm-, th-, -nt, -ft), and the routine itself: say the word, stretch
it into its sounds, write a letter or letter pair for each sound, read it back. This is Level 1's row of Section
0.4's bank. Beginner has no Paragraph Composition Level for 0.4's row-selection rule to anchor on, so this Set
anchors at Level 1's row, the Band's calibration Level; see `Module1_Beginner_Lesson_Plan.md` and `Index.md`'s
Pending work for the prompt gap that decision records.

**Level 2's own row, folded into the same mini-lesson (Levels 2-3, never a separate session):** the fixed frame
"It is ___." is one form with one slot, and it is a whole sentence, so it takes a capital letter at the start and
a period at the end. Level 3's two-slot frame is shown alongside it as the frame those students will use; the word
"and" inside it is Lesson 2's business and is not taught today.

**Not taught in this Set:** the simple-present -s form (Level 3's Focus A cell). It belongs to a row this Set does
not anchor on, so no task in any of the four lessons asks for a sentence about what the object does.

## DAY 1: LETTER-SOUND SPELLING, INPUT AND MODELING (75 MIN)

```
0        15                        45                    75  (minutes)
|-Phase 1-|--------Phase 2----------|-------Phase 3-------|
| Hook,   | Focus A mini-lesson:    | Controlled practice  |
| Skill   | sounds to letters       | A: sort, choose,      |
| Spot-   | (+ the frame, folded in)| the guided round      |
| light   |                         |                       |
```

### Phase 1: Hook, Skill Spotlight (15 min)

**Hook (8 min):** two students say one word about their own notebook, from memory, without naming a colour. The
class hears two different words for the same kind of thing, and the teacher writes both on the board. Then a
third student adds a word neither of the first two used. Nothing is brought out and nothing is shown; students
describe what they already know.

**Skill Spotlight (7 min):** "Today we're practicing describing something of our own by choosing the word that
really fits it, and spelling that word from its sounds." One sentence, the same for every Level. No Level's own
required feature is named here (Quality Standards §D1); Level 3's two-different-observations demand lives inside
Level 3's own tasks.

### Phase 2: Focus A mini-lesson: from sounds to letters (30 min)

_The sounding-out routine (10 min)._ Four steps, in this order, modeled by the teacher on two words the class
just said: say the word; stretch it slowly and count the sounds on your fingers; write a letter or letter pair
for each sound; read it back and check it says the word you meant.

_Short vowels in describing words (10 min)._ The grammar box, printed in the packet before any task that uses it:

> **a** as in t-a-n, fl-a-t, bl-a-ck
> **e** as in r-e-d, b-e-nt
> **i** as in b-i-g, th-i-n, sl-i-m
> **o** as in s-o-ft, l-o-ng
> **u** as in b-u-mpy
>
> Two letters, one sound: **th** in thin, **ck** in black, **sm** in smooth, **gr** in gray.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** the class chooses one describing word for a notebook that nobody
has said yet, and the teacher builds it on the board sound by sound as students call out the letters, then reads
it back. The word stays on the board for Day 2.

_The frame, folded in (10 min)._ The three frames from the Scenario section are shown once, side by side, and
read aloud. What the teacher names: Level 1's frame is a phrase and takes no period; Level 2's frame is a whole
sentence, so it needs a capital I at the start and a period at the end; Level 3's frame has two slots, and what
goes between them is Lesson 2's work. No frame is completed here.

### Phase 3: Controlled practice A (30 min)

**Activity 1: Put each word in its group (whole class, 10 min).** Twelve words from the Word Bank, scrambled.
Students write each one under the group it belongs to. The groups do not hold the same number of words, so the
last few cannot be placed by counting.

> flat, red, bent, smooth, tan, lined, thin, clean, rough, gray, torn, plain

**Answer note:** Color: red, tan, gray. Size and shape: flat, thin. Feel: smooth, rough. Condition: bent, clean,
torn. Cover and marks: lined, plain. Three groups take three, two, two, three, two, so counting does not finish
the task.

**Activity 2: Choose what fits (10 min).** The eight words below are printed. Each student circles the ones that
fit their own notebook and crosses out the ones that do not. Every student's page comes out different; the point
is the judgment, not a key.

> big, thin, red, black, soft, bent, new, lined

- **Levels 1-2:** circle the words that fit your notebook, cross out the words that do not.
- **Level 3:** circle a pair of words that says two different things about your notebook, and cross out any word
  that says the same thing as one you circled.

**Answer note:** no fixed key; answers depend on the student's own notebook. Circulation check: each student can
say out loud why one crossed-out word does not fit ("it is not red, it is black"). At Level 3, check the circled
pair really says two things (black and soft), not one thing twice (thin and slim).

**Activity 3: The guided round (10 min).** The lesson's one round of frame writing, and the only task in this Set
that asks for the frame more than once. Each student writes the frame three times, using one word from each of
three groups: Color, Size and shape, and Condition.

- **Level 1:** ___ book, three times.
- **Level 2:** It is ___. three times, each with a capital and a period.
- **Level 3:** It is ___ and ___. three times; each time the two words must come from two different groups.

**Answer note:** answers vary by notebook. What is checked: the word is spelled from sound, not copied off the
board (circulate and hear it stretched); at Level 2 the capital and period are present; at Level 3 no round pairs
two words from the same group.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the same three groups are printed as
words to choose from rather than to spell. The student circles one word per group and reads the finished phrase
aloud, then says which part of the notebook each word describes. Nothing here is prepared in advance; the words
are already on the printed page.

## DAY 2: DEEPER PRACTICE AND THE MENTOR LADDER (75 MIN)

```
0                20                       45                        75  (minutes)
|----Phase 1-----|--------Phase 2---------|---------Phase 3---------|
| Fix the wrong  | Build the word from    | Leveled Mentor Ladder    |
| word           | its sounds             | walkthrough              |
```

### Phase 1: Fix the wrong word (20 min)

Three printed sentences per Level, each holding one word that is misspelled or that does not describe a notebook
at all. Students cross out the wrong word and write the right one above it. Nothing is erased.

- **Levels 1-2:**

> 1. It is bluu.
> 2. It is thn.
> 3. It is loud.

- **Level 3:**

> 1. It is black and blak.
> 2. It is flatt and clean.
> 3. It is thin and slim.

**Answer note:** Levels 1-2: bluu -> blue (the /oo/ sound is spelled ue); thn -> thin (the short i is missing);
loud is a sound word and describes nothing you can see or feel about a notebook, so it is crossed out and
replaced with any Word Bank word that fits the student's own. Level 3: black and blak is the same word twice, so
the second is replaced with a word from another group; flatt -> flat (one t); thin and slim say the same thing,
so one is replaced from another group.

### Phase 2: Build the word from its sounds (25 min)

Eight sound strings are printed. Students say each string, blend it, and write the whole word. This practices
Focus A's routine without producing the frame at all.

> 1. /f/ /l/ /a/ /t/
> 2. /th/ /i/ /n/
> 3. /r/ /e/ /d/
> 4. /b/ /l/ /a/ /ck/
> 5. /s/ /o/ /f/ /t/
> 6. /t/ /a/ /n/
> 7. /b/ /e/ /n/ /t/
> 8. /s/ /l/ /i/ /m/

**Answer note:** flat, thin, red, black, soft, tan, bent, slim. Items 4 and 8 are the two that need a two-letter
spelling for one sound (ck) and a blend the ear splits wrongly (sl); expect those two to need a second pass.

**Level 3, heavier tier (inside the same 25 minutes):** after writing all eight, mark the two words that could go
in one frame together and say why they are two different things.

**Answer note:** several correct pairs (flat and red; thin and tan; soft and black). What is wrong: flat with
thin, or slim with thin, which say the same thing twice.

### Phase 3: Leveled Mentor Ladder walkthrough (30 min)

Present all three Mentor Texts together, in Level order, on the board or read aloud: the ladder is the teacher's
material and is never printed in the packet (packet prompt 2.6). All three describe one model student's own
notebook, so the class watches one object climb three steps. Ben's notebook is black, thin, soft-covered, and
bent at one corner.

**Level 1 Mentor Text**

> Ben said the word first: thin.
> He stretched it: /th/ /i/ /n/.
> He wrote: thin book

**Level 2 Mentor Text**

> Ben said a different word about the same notebook: bent.
> Frame: It is ___.
> Ben's answer: It is bent.

**Level 3 Mentor Text**

> Frame: It is ___ and ___.
> Ben's answer: It is black and soft.
> What Ben did not write: It is thin and slim. Those two words say one thing twice.

**Walkthrough (teacher-led, from the board):** for each text, name in one line what makes it that Level's version
(one word inside a phrase; one word inside a whole sentence, with its capital and period; two words that say two
different things, joined in one sentence). Every model shows a different observation about the same notebook, so
the ladder never repeats itself.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** as the class looks at the three texts, build a "what changes
each step" list on the board from what students notice, one line per step, in their own words. It sits under
yesterday's sounded-out word, which stays up.

**What students do (5 min, every Level):** find your own Level's text on the board and say to a partner one
thing the text one step up does that yours does not. Nothing is printed and nothing is written.

**Task for every listener:** each partner repeats back the one difference their partner named before saying their
own.
