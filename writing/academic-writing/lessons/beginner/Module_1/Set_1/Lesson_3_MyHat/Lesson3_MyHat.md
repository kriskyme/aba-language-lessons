## What this lesson carries forward

**Scenario.** Every student writes about one hat they own, chosen from the printed list below. The hat is a
subject, not a prop: no task asks anyone to look at it, point to a part, hold it up, or bring it in.

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** The sounding-out routine and the
short-vowel patterns (Lesson 1); "and" joining two words that say two different things (Lesson 2); the three
frames.

**Nothing carried from Lesson 2 (Quality Standards §D9).** Lesson 2's prewriting was rehearsal. Every student
starts this lesson by choosing a hat from the printed list and building a fresh three-word plan on this lesson's
own page, from the Draft Word Bank reprinted below. A student who was absent, or who lost Lesson 2's page, starts
every task here without a gap.

**Not asked for anywhere in this lesson:** a sentence about what the hat does. The simple-present -s form was not
taught in this Set (Focus A is anchored at Level 1's row), so Day 2's improving work is a word swap only. See
`Module1_Beginner_Lesson_Plan.md` and `Index.md`'s Pending work for the Lesson-prompt gap this records.

**The three frames, stated once for this day and called "the frame" afterward:**

> **Level 1:** ___ hat
> **Level 2:** It is ___.
> **Level 3:** It is ___ and ___.

## Draft Word Bank (reprinted from Lesson 2 Day 2; posted on the board and printed in the packet)

> **Color:** red, black, tan, gray, blue
> **Size and shape:** big, flat, long, round, wide
> **Feel:** soft, warm, thick, smooth, rough
> **Condition:** new, old, worn, clean, damp
> **Cover and marks:** plain, striped, spotted, checked, bright

## Drafting targets by task Level (Section 0.2)

| Task Level | Output form | Length | Required feature (checked in the Day 2 pass) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One word spelled from sound into "___ hat" | One word | The word is the student's own choice about their own hat, sounded out, not copied off the board. |
| 2 | One word spelled from sound into "It is ___." | One word | The same genuine choice, inside a whole sentence: capital at the start, period at the end. |
| 3 | Two words spelled from sound into "It is ___ and ___." | Two words | The two words are two genuinely different observations (black and soft), never two words for the same thing (big and wide). |

## DAY 1: DRAFTING (75 MIN)

```
0            15                                 60                75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1---|-------------Phase 2--------------|----Phase 3-----|
| Quick plan | Say it, write it, read it back   | Mid-draft       |
|            |                                  | share-out       |
```

### Phase 1: Quick plan (15 min)

Two actions, both on this lesson's own page. No questions are printed at this phase (Quality Standards §E6).

**Step 1.** Circle the hat you are writing about:

> baseball cap, beanie, sun hat, wool hat, bucket hat, rain hat, visor, headband

**Step 2.** Write three words from the Draft Word Bank that fit the hat you circled, then circle the one you will
use. Level 3 circles two, from two different groups.

**Answer note:** answers vary by hat. Circulation check, teacher-facing and not printed: do the three words come
from at least two different groups, and at Level 3 are the two circled words from two different groups? A plan
whose three words are all colors is the one to catch here, before any writing starts.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the student chooses their word by
circling one on the printed Draft Word Bank instead of writing it out, then says the word aloud. In Phase 2 they
say the word and the teacher writes it, and the student then says which part of the hat it describes.

### Phase 2: Say it, write it, read it back (45 min)

_Say it (15 min)._ In pairs, each student says their circled word or words aloud and stretches each one into its
sounds for their partner, who counts the sounds on their fingers and says the number back. No writing yet.

_Write it (20 min)._ Each student writes the frame once, with the word they circled. This is the lesson's one
round.

- **Level 1:** ___ hat
- **Level 2:** It is ___.
- **Level 3:** It is ___ and ___.

The teacher circulates and hears each student sound their word out rather than reading it off the bank.

_Read it back (10 min)._ Each student reads their own finished frame aloud, quietly, and checks three things by
marking the page: one letter or letter pair for each sound they hear; at Levels 2-3, a capital at the start and a
period at the end; at Level 3, that the two words are from two different groups.

**Answer note:** answers vary. What the marks look like: an underline under each sound spelling, a circle around
the capital and the period, and at Level 3 a line joining each word to its group heading on the printed bank.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share-out (15 min)

Five or six students read their finished frame aloud to the room.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7):** a running "strong sentence" board, built live from the share-out, one
column per Level. It is not on any student page, and it stays up for Day 2.

**Rotated prompts (three, as the share moves):** which word did you choose that nobody else has used; which word
was the hardest to stretch into sounds; which word tells a reader something they could not guess about your hat.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "___ hat."
- **Level 2:** "It is ___."
- **Level 3:** "It is ___ and ___, and they say two different things."

**Task for every listener:** put a mark next to any word on your own page that you also hear someone else use.

## DAY 2: CHECK AND IMPROVE (75 MIN)

The frame was finished on Day 1. Today opens directly on checking it; there is no finish-your-writing phase.

```
0                    25                          55                75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1------|----------Phase 2----------|----Phase 3-----|
| Read it to a       | Swap in a more exact word | Final line     |
| partner and check  |                           | check          |
```

### Phase 1: Read it to a partner and check (25 min)

In pairs. Each student reads their frame aloud to a partner. The partner says which part of the hat each word
describes, out loud, without being shown anything. The writer then works down a printed yes-or-no list, marking
the page for each one (Quality Standards §E6: every item is answered by a mark, not by a sentence).

**Levels 1-2 check list:**

> 1. My partner could say what my word describes. (yes / no)
> 2. My word has one letter or letter pair for each sound I hear. (yes / no)
> 3. My word says something true about my own hat. (yes / no)
> 4. (Level 2) My sentence starts with a capital and ends with a period. (yes / no)

**Level 3 check list:** items 1 to 4 above, plus:

> 5. My two words come from two different groups on the bank. (yes / no)
> 6. My partner said two different things back to me, not the same thing twice. (yes / no)

**Answer note:** answers vary. The one to watch is item 1 at Level 3: if the partner says the same thing back
twice ("it is dark, and it is dark"), item 6 is a no, and Phase 2's swap is where it gets fixed.

### Phase 2: Swap in a more exact word (30 min)

Each student changes one word for a word that tells a reader more, using the printed pairs below as a starting
point and the Draft Word Bank for anything not listed. The old word is crossed out, never erased, and the new one
is written above it, so both are visible (Section 0.4a).

> nice -> soft, warm, smooth
> big -> wide, long, round
> old -> worn, faded, damp
> good -> clean, bright, thick
> dark -> black, gray, blue

- **Levels 1-2:** cross out your word and write a more exact one above it, then read the new frame aloud.
- **Level 3:** cross out one of your two words and write a more exact one above it; if both words already came
  from two different groups and both are exact, swap the word your partner found hardest to picture.

**Answer note:** answers vary. A swap is genuine when the new word could not be said about most hats in the room:
"black" is more exact than "dark," and "worn" is more exact than "old," but "blue" for "black" is a change, not an
improvement, and is the one to send back.

**Board-dependent moment:** the "strong sentence" board from Day 1 stays up and gains a second line under each
column: the word that was crossed out and the word that replaced it, added live as students volunteer swaps.

### Phase 3: Final line check (20 min)

Each student reads their improved frame aloud to a different partner from Phase 1. The listener says the one word
they can picture best. Nothing is rewritten in this phase.

**Task for every listener:** say the word you can picture best, then say one thing that word tells you about the
hat.

**What this lesson does not do.** There is no Peer Editing form, no publishing, and no Closing Transfer Check at
this position; every student leaves Day 2 with a finished frame that has been checked once and improved once.
Lesson 4 runs the reader's request, Peer Editing, the final copy, publishing, and the Closing Transfer Check, and
never re-runs today's check list.
