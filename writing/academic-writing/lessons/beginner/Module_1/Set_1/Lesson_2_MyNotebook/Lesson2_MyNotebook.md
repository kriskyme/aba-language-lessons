## Grammar focuses

**Focus B (this lesson's centerpiece, whole class): "and" joins two words that say two different things.** The
rule, stated plainly: the word "and" sits between the two slots of Level 3's frame, and the two words on either
side of it must describe two different things about the object. Two words for the same thing (thin and slim, big
and large) is not two things; it is one thing said twice. This is Level 3's own row of Section 0.4's bank and the
only Focus B the frame regime offers.

**How Focus B pairs with Focus A (named aloud at the start of Phase 1):** yesterday's work was choosing one word
that really fits and spelling it from its sounds; today's is putting two of those words together so they do not
overlap.

**Levels 1-2 receive Focus B as whole-class content, not as a separate session** (Lesson prompt 0.4). Their own
frames keep one slot; what they take from the mini-lesson is the judgment about whether two words say the same
thing, which their own tasks use in Phase 2 and Phase 3.

**Confusable pair (Phase 3): the short e and short i vowels.** red / rid, ten / tin, wet / wit, pen / pin, big /
beg, bed / bid. One sound apart, and the wrong letter makes a real word, so a spelling check alone does not catch
it. New to the Rotation Log (it's / its, quiet / quite, and then / than are already used elsewhere).

## DAY 1: JOINING TWO WORDS WITH "AND" (75 MIN)

### Phase 1: Focus B mini-lesson: "and" between two different words (20 min)

The rule and the counter-model together, in the sample's grammar-box style, printed before any task that uses it:

> **It is ___ and ___.**
> The two words say two different things about the object.
>
> It is black and soft. (a color and a feel: two things)
> It is thin and slim. (a size and a size: one thing, twice)
> It is red and lined. (a color and what is on the cover: two things)

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** the class builds one new two-word sentence about a notebook that
nobody has said yet, choosing the two words from different groups of the Word Bank as the teacher writes them up,
then tries a second pairing that fails the rule and says why it fails. Both stay on the board for Phase 2.

### Phase 2: Better of two, then pair judgment (30 min)

**Activity 1: Circle the better one (15 min).** Six pairs of sentences about a notebook are printed. In each pair,
one sentence tells a reader more than the other. Students circle the better sentence and write the one word that
made it better.

> 1. It is nice. / It is bent.
> 2. It is good. / It is lined.
> 3. It is old. / It is torn.
> 4. It is big. / It is thick.
> 5. It is fine. / It is gray.
> 6. It is nice and good. / It is tan and rough.

**Answer note:** bent, lined, torn, thick, gray, and item 6's pair tan and rough. In items 1, 2, and 5 the weaker
sentence uses a word that could be said about anything; in 3 and 4 both words are real describing words, but the
second is the more exact of the two (torn says how it is old; thick says how it is big), so the choice is not a
scan for the "nice / good" word. Item 6 is also the first item to carry two slots and previews Activity 2.

- **Level 1:** circle the better sentence, then write the word that made it better next to it.
- **Level 2:** as above, and write the better sentence out in the frame.
- **Level 3:** as above, and for item 6 write out the pair that says two different things.

**Activity 2: Pair judgment (15 min).** Eight printed pairs. Students circle the pairs that say two different
things about a notebook and cross out the pairs that say one thing twice.

> 1. black and soft
> 2. thin and slim
> 3. red and lined
> 4. big and large
> 5. new and clean
> 6. tan and rough
> 7. old and torn
> 8. flat and thin

**Answer note:** circle 1, 3, 6, and 7 (a color and a feel; a color and a cover mark; a color and a feel; a
condition and how that condition shows). Cross out 2, 4, and 8 (all size words twice). Item 5 is the hard one and
is meant to be argued: new and clean can be two different things (age and state), so accept it circled if the
student can say the difference out loud, and crossed out if they say clean is just what new means.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the same eight pairs, read aloud by the
teacher one at a time, with the student answering "same" or "different" and marking the page with one mark. The
student then says which two things one circled pair describes.

### Phase 3: Short e and short i (25 min)

**Activity 3: Circle the word that belongs in the sentence (25 min).** Eight sentences, each with two spellings
one vowel apart. Both spellings are real words, so only the meaning of the sentence decides it.

> 1. My notebook is (red / rid).
> 2. I write with a (pen / pin).
> 3. The cover is (wet / wit).
> 4. There are (ten / tin) pages left.
> 5. My notebook is (big / beg).
> 6. I read it in (bed / bid).
> 7. The corner has a (bend / bind) in it.
> 8. The paper is (thin / then).

**Answer note:** red, pen, wet, ten, big, bed, bend, thin. Every rejected option is a real English word, so no
item can be finished by crossing out a nonsense spelling; items 4 and 7 are the two where both options could
start a sensible sentence and only the rest of the line settles it.

**Level 3, heavier tier (inside the same 25 minutes):** choose any two of the eight correct words that could sit
in one frame together and write that sentence out, then say which two things it describes.

**Answer note:** red and thin, red and wet, and thin and wet all work (a color, a size, a state). What fails: two
size words, or a word that describes the pen or the pages rather than the notebook.

## DAY 2: SPELLING FROM SOUND, AND PREWRITING ON A NEW OBJECT (75 MIN)

```
0                     25                            55                75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1-------|-----------Phase 2-----------|-----Phase 3-----|
| Spell from sound    | Prewriting: my hat and the  | Prewriting share |
| with a partner      | Draft Word Bank             | and board        |
```

### Phase 1: Spell from sound with a partner (25 min)

In pairs. Partner A reads three words aloud from the printed Word Bank while Partner B covers the list with one
hand and writes each word from its sounds. Then they check the three together against the list and swap roles.
No frame is written in this phase; these are words only.

**Reader's three words, Round 1:** smooth, bent, gray.
**Reader's three words, Round 2:** plain, torn, slim.

**Answer note:** the reader's own printed list is the key. The three words in each round come from three
different groups on purpose, so the writer cannot guess the next one from the last. smooth and gray carry the
two-letter spellings for one sound (oo, gr) and are the two most likely to need a second try.

- **Levels 1-2:** write each word, then check it against the list.
- **Level 3:** write each word, then mark the two of the six that could go in one frame together.

**Answer note (Level 3):** gray with smooth, gray with torn, and plain with bent all work. Marking slim with bent
or slim with any size word is the error to catch.

**Foundation Support (plugs in alongside Level 1, not in place of it):** the reader says the word and the student
traces it from the printed list, then says which part of a notebook it could describe. The tracing copy is
already printed on the page; nothing is prepared in advance.

### Phase 2: Prewriting on a new object: my hat (30 min)

**Introducing the draft object.** From here to the end of the Set, the writing is about a hat the student owns.
Students circle one from this printed list, so nobody is without one:

> baseball cap, beanie, sun hat, wool hat, bucket hat, rain hat, visor, headband

**Draft Word Bank (the same five groups, refilled for a hat; printed here and reprinted in every later lesson):**

> **Color:** red, black, tan, gray, blue
> **Size and shape:** big, flat, long, round, wide
> **Feel:** soft, warm, thick, smooth, rough
> **Condition:** new, old, worn, clean, damp
> **Cover and marks:** plain, striped, spotted, checked, bright

**Activity 4: Plan the words (30 min).** Three words from the Draft Word Bank are written on the page by the
student, chosen to fit the hat they circled. Then one is circled.

- **Levels 1-2:** write three words from the Draft Word Bank that fit your hat, then circle the one that tells a
  reader the most.
- **Level 3:** write three words that fit your hat, then circle two that say two different things.

**Answer note:** answers vary by hat. Circulation check: the three words come from at least two different groups,
and at Level 3 the circled two are not both from the Size and shape group. A student whose hat is plain and black
has as much to say as one with a striped cap; check they have not written a word that describes a different hat.

**This is rehearsal, not a plan to keep** (Quality Standards §D9). Lesson 3 rebuilds its own plan on its own
page, from its own reprinted Draft Word Bank, so nothing written here has to survive to be useful and no task
here says "next time."

### Phase 3: Prewriting share and board synthesis (20 min)

Three students from each Level read out one of their words and the hat it describes. The teacher builds one
shared list on the board, grouped by the five Word Bank groups, so the room can see which groups the class has
filled and which are still nearly empty.

**Board-dependent moment:** the list exists nowhere on any student page; it is built live from what students
report, and it holds more words than any one student wrote.

**Rotated prompts (three, in order, as the share moves around the room):** which word did you write that nobody
else has said yet; which of your three words describes something a reader could not guess; which group on the
board is still almost empty, and what word could go in it.

**Stems:**
- **Level 1:** "My hat is ___."
- **Level 2:** "I wrote ___, and it says ___ about my hat."
- **Level 3:** "I wrote ___ and ___, and they say two different things: ___ and ___."

**Task for every listener:** write on your own page one word you hear that also fits your hat, in a group you
have not used yet.
