## Scenario: My Notebook, then My Hat

Every student describes something of their own so that a reader who has never seen it can picture it. **Purpose
(Module 1, Describing):** describe an object by choosing the words that really fit it, spelling each one from
sound. Nothing in this Set narrates, explains, or evaluates.

- **Practice object (Lessons 1-2 and the Mentor Ladder): my notebook.** The notebook or workbook the student
  writes in, described from what they know of it. Grammar input, controlled practice, the frame rounds, and every
  Mentor Text use it. The notebook is a subject, not a prop (Quality Standards §D8): no task asks the student to
  look at it, point to a part, hold it up, or have it on the desk.
- **Draft object (introduced at Lesson 2 Day 2's prewriting; Lessons 3-4 write about it): my hat.** One hat the
  student owns, chosen from a printed list of eight the student circles at the start of each drafting lesson, so
  no student is without one: **baseball cap, beanie, sun hat, wool hat, bucket hat, rain hat, visor, headband.**
  Category title and slug for Lessons 3-4: "My Hat." The Draft Word Bank is built for the category, not one item.
- **Closing Transfer Check object (Lesson 4 Day 2, a third object, used nowhere else in the Set):** the student's
  own shoe, described to a partner.
- **No named reader and no second audience.** A named reader with a stated concern is Level 6's required feature
  and two distinct readers is Level 8's (Lesson prompt 0.2); Beginner reaches neither, so the Scenario states
  neither. Its reader is the partner who hears the sentence read aloud at Lesson 4.

**Both objects are writable at every task Level.** Level 1 spells one describing word into the adjective-noun frame
("___ book" / "___ hat"): a notebook and a hat both offer several genuinely different one-word answers that are
decodable from short-vowel and consonant-blend patterns (red, black, tan, big, thin, flat, soft, old, new, wet).
Level 2 spells one word into "It is ___." from the same pool. Level 3 spells two words into "It is ___ and ___."
and the two must be two different observations, which both objects support easily (a colour and a feel, a size and
a condition) without inviting synonyms.

**A note on where Level 1's open slot sits.** The CSV's Level 1 row fixes a memorized adjective-noun frame and its
own worked example spells the noun ("big ___" -> "dog"). This Set puts the open slot on the adjective ("___ book",
"___ hat") and keeps the frame adjective-noun. Reason: the object is fixed by the Scenario, so a noun slot has
exactly one correct answer and Level 1's own required feature (0.2: a genuine choice the student sounds out, not a
foregone answer) could not be met. The frame kind, the output length, and the spell-from-sound demand are all
unchanged.

## Grammar Focus A/B

**Row anchoring.** Lesson prompt 0.4's row-selection rule anchors Focus A/B at the band's own native Paragraph
Composition row, and names only Intermediate, Advanced, and Proficient. **Beginner has no Paragraph Composition
Level at all,** so the rule does not resolve. This plan anchors at **Level 1's row**, the Band's calibration Level
(Conventions §A), with Levels 2-3's rows folded into the same whole-class mini-lessons. The gap itself is logged in
`Index.md`'s Pending work rather than patched silently into the prompt.

- **Focus A (Level 1's row): letter-sound spelling patterns for describing words.** Short-vowel CVC words (red,
  tan, big, wet, hot, old) and common consonant blends and digraphs in describing words (flat, thin, black, soft,
  slim, plain), plus the routine itself: say the word, stretch the sounds, write a letter for each sound, read it
  back. Level 1's row is what makes this the core form: the output *is* a word spelled from sound.
- **Level 2's row, folded into the same mini-lesson (never a separate session):** the fixed frame's single form,
  "It is ___." - one form, one slot, a full sentence with a capital and a period.
- **Focus B (Lesson 2 Day 1, taught as its own point): "and" joining two different describing words** inside the
  two-slot frame "It is ___ and ___." Taken from Level 3's Focus B cell, the only Focus B the frame regime offers
  (the Bank marks Focus B "n/a" at Levels 1-2). Taught with its own rule: the two words either side of "and" say
  two different things about the object, never the same thing twice.
- **Confusable pair (Lesson 2 Day 1): the short e / i vowel pair in describing and naming words** - red / rid,
  ten / tin, wet / wit, bed / bid, pen / pin, beg / big. The forced choice is which spelling matches the sound the
  student hears, which is Focus A's own routine under pressure. New to the Log (it's / its, quiet / quite, and
  then / than are all already used).
- **Essay Focus A/B direction: N/A.** Beginner's task Levels (1, 2, 3) never reach Essay Composition, so Lesson
  prompt 0.4c does not apply, and neither does its Level 5 receptive bridge.

**Flagged deviation, recorded rather than hidden.** Anchoring at Level 1's row means the simple-present -s form
(Level 3's Focus A cell) is **not** taught in this Set. Lesson prompt's Lesson 3 Day 2 Phase 2 tells frame-regime
Levels to "add one -s sentence about what the object does," which assumes that form has been taught; at Beginner it
has not. Lesson 3 Day 2 therefore runs the swap-in-a-more-exact-word half of that phase only, and adds no second
sentence. Logged in `Index.md` Pending work as a Lesson-prompt gap.

## Leveled Mentor Ladder direction (on the practice object, my notebook)

Presented by the teacher on Lesson 1 Day 2, on the board or read aloud, in Level order; never printed in the packet
(Lesson prompt 0.1b, packet prompt 2.6). All three models describe one model student's own notebook, so students
see one object climb three steps. Beginner has **3 Mentor Texts** and no Mentor Essay. Directions only; the texts
themselves are written at generation time.

- **Level 1:** the adjective-noun frame with one describing word sounded out for the model student's own notebook,
  and the sounding-out itself made visible (the sounds named before the letters are written), so the model teaches
  the routine and not a word to copy.
- **Level 2:** the same object in the full-sentence frame "It is ___.", with a **different** observation from Level
  1's, so the step up the ladder is a step and not a restatement - a complete sentence, capital and period visible.
- **Level 3:** the two-slot frame "It is ___ and ___." with two genuinely different observations (a colour and a
  feel, or a size and a condition), plus the counter-model the teacher names aloud: two words for the same thing
  (big and fat) is not two observations.

The ladder is distinct in kind across the three Levels: a word inside a phrase, a word inside a sentence, and two
different observations joined inside one sentence. It stays on the practice object throughout; the hat never
appears in it (Lesson prompt 0.1a: the ladder models form, not content).

## Lesson-by-lesson activity shapes (Quality Standards §D10; Lesson prompt 0.4d)

Beginner is the first Set in this lesson type where **every** task Level is fixed-output, so this table is what
keeps the Set from becoming twelve rounds of the same frame. The frame is produced at most once per Level per day;
Lesson 1 Day 1's guided round is the only task that says "three times" (one word per Word Bank group, in one task).
No shape appears twice in one lesson, and no shape sits in the same slot in consecutive lessons.

| Lesson | Day | Shape 1 | Shape 2 | Shape 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Sort the Word Bank into its groups (§D10 bank) | Choose what fits: circle the words that fit the notebook, cross out the ones that do not (Level 3: circle a pair that says two different things) | **Complete the frame** - the Set's one guided round, one word per Word Bank group |
| 1 | 2 | Fix the wrong word: three printed frame sentences about the notebook, each with a word that does not fit or is misspelled | Build the word from its sounds: printed sound strings written back as whole words (Focus A practice, no frame produced) | Mentor Ladder walkthrough (no student production) |
| 2 | 1 | Better of two: circle the more exact of two printed frame sentences and write the word that made it better | Pair judgment: circle the printed pairs that say two different things, cross out the ones that repeat | Confusable-pair drill: e / i, circle the spelling that matches the sound |
| 2 | 2 | Spell from sound with a partner: a partner reads three Word Bank words aloud, the student writes each without looking, then checks (Level 3 also marks which two would go in one frame) | Plan the words: three Draft Word Bank words that fit the hat, circle the one (Level 3: two) | Prewriting share and board synthesis (no printed production) |
| 3 | 1 | Circle the hat on the printed list, then plan the words (three Draft Word Bank words) | **The draft** - the frame completed once on the hat, the lesson's one round | Mid-draft share-out (read aloud, no new writing) |
| 3 | 2 | Check and improve: read the frame to a partner, who says which part each word describes; swap in a more exact word where one fits | Final line check, read aloud | - |
| 4 | 1 | A reader's request, then Peer Editing (oral partner check, Beginner's placement per 0.6) | **The reader's part** - one more frame sentence about the part the reader asked for; circle the better of the two for the final copy | Revision share |
| 4 | 2 | Final copy | Publish: read aloud to two partners in turn, or copies passed around (room-neutral, Quality Standards §D11; **not** a gallery walk) | **Transfer** - the frame on a third object, the student's own shoe |

Lessons 1-2 carry the item minimums (Lesson prompt 0.4b: at least 3 distinct activities per day, 6-8 items per
fill-in, classify, choose-the-form, or combine activity, at least 6 in the confusable-pair drill); Lessons 3-4 are
drafting and revision days and are exempt.

**Foundation Support (below Level 1, alongside it and never in place of it; Quality Standards §B, Lesson prompt
0.5).** Plugs in at three named points: Lesson 1 Day 1's guided round (match a printed Word Bank word to the
object described, select rather than spell), Lesson 2 Day 2's dictation (trace a pre-written word, then say which
word is which), and Lesson 3 Day 1's draft (teacher-scribed dictation: the student says the word, the teacher
writes it, and the student says afterward which word describes what). Nothing here needs a card set the teacher
prepares.
