Grammar: Telling What Someone Said Without Quoting It

Direct speech

The speaker's exact words, inside quotation marks. The word in front of the quotation marks is the reporting verb: it says who spoke and how.

My brother said, "I burned the rice."
said = the reporting verb. Everything inside the quotation marks is exactly what he says.

Indirect speech

My brother said he had burned the rice.

What changesHis wordsYour words
1. The quotation marks go. The words are yours now."I burned the rice."he had burned the rice
2. Tense shift: the verb steps back one tense, because the reporting verb is in the past.burnedhad burned
3. Pronoun shift: pronouns move to your side of the conversation.Ihe

Three reporting verbs, three patterns

Two of the three take a person straight after them. Say never does.

VerbPatternExample
saysay (that) something, with no person after itShe said the pan was too small.
telltell someone (that) somethingShe told me the pan was too small.
askask someone why, what, or whetherShe asked me why I had used that pan.

The verb you choose to report with tells your reader what to think of the speaker, whether you meant it or not.

If you writeyour reader decides the speaker
saidnothing in particular
admitteddid not want to say it
insistedsaid it again, when nobody had asked
mutteredsaid it quietly, maybe not meaning to be heard
complainedthought it was someone else's fault
promisedcommitted to something

My brother said it was fine. / My brother insisted it was fine. / My brother admitted it was fine.
