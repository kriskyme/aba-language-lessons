Grammar: Starting a Sentence Before the Sentence Starts

You can put a short piece in front of your sentence to tie two actions together or to jump the story forward. The comma after it is not optional.

OpenerExampleWhat it does
-ingReaching for the pan, I knocked the bowl over.ties two actions to the same moment
-edLeft on the heat too long, the sauce turned to paste.puts the cause first
time phraseAfter three tries, I gave up on the sauce.jumps the story forward
time phraseBy the end of the week, I had stopped cooking altogether.covers a long stretch fast

An -ing opener attaches to whatever comes next, so that thing has to be the one doing it.

Wrong: Reaching for the pan, the bowl fell over.    Right: Reaching for the pan, I knocked the bowl over.

Wrong: Having burned the first batch, the second one came out better.    Right: Having burned the first batch, I watched the second one more carefully.

Ask yourself: who or what is doing the opener? That has to be the subject of the main sentence.

One opener slows a moment down or moves time along. Two or three in a row stop sounding chosen and start sounding like a pattern.

A Word That Is Easy to Mix Up

say takes no person after it. tell takes a person after it. A question after a person needs ask.

She said the pan was too small. / She told me the pan was too small. / She asked me why I had used that pan.
