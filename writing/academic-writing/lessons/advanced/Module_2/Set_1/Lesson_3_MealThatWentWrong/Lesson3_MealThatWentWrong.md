## What this lesson carries forward

**Nothing a student wrote earlier** (Quality Standards §D9). Lesson 2's prewriting was rehearsal. Today's quick
plan is built fresh inside this packet, so a student who was absent on Lesson 2 Day 2 can start every task here.

**The one thing that carries is the choice of dish,** and even that is re-circled today from the same printed
list of eight, so nobody is stuck.

**What students write today is the whole piece.** At Levels 4-5 that is one paragraph. At Levels 6-7 it is a
complete essay: opening, the middle that tells what happened, the turn where the piece says what it meant, and
an ending. There is no later lesson that adds a missing part, because this Set is standalone. Lesson 4 revises
and publishes what exists at the end of today.

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching:** reported speech and thought with the

the rule that the opener attaches to the subject that follows. The self-edit checklist names both by what they
do, never by a label.

**The named reader: Mina,** a friend who wants to make the same thing herself and is sure it is simple.

## DAY 1: DRAFTING THE PIECE (75 MIN)

```
0     10                                 60                75  (minutes)
|Ph 1--|-------------Phase 2--------------|----Phase 3-----|
| Quick | Drafting the whole piece,        | Mid-draft       |
| plan  | complete today                   | share-out       |
```

### Phase 1: Quick plan (10 min)

Built inside this packet, with no reference to Lesson 2's products (Quality Standards §D9). The Draft Word Bank
is reprinted immediately before it. Students circle the dish from the printed list first.

> **instant noodles / rice / eggs / soup / pasta / a sandwich / curry / pancakes**

**Level 4.** Three Word Bank words, then three lines: *First ___. Then ___. In the end ___.* and one line:
*because ___.*

**Level 5.** Three Word Bank words, then four or five things that happened in order, with the one that could
show how you felt circled.

**Level 6.** Three Word Bank words, then five decisions on five lines: the kind of opening; the sentence saying
what it showed you; the order of what happened, two or three items; where the turn lands; the closing move.

**Level 7.** The same five lines, with the main-point line signalling a reason without naming it, plus a sixth:
the detail you will put in early and not explain.

**Circulation check (teacher, not printed):** does each plan already carry that Level's required feature, and do
Levels 6-7 have all five lines filled before they start writing prose?

### Phase 2: Drafting the whole piece, complete today (50 min)

Every student finishes today. The teacher circulates on the required feature in progress, not only at the end.

**Level 4 (one paragraph), a four-step path.** 1. Open with when it was and the first thing you did. 2. Add the
next thing, with a time word you choose. 3. Add one more thing, with a different time word. 4. Close with how it
turned out and one sentence beginning *because*. Count your sentences at the end: you should have between three
and six.

**Level 5 (one extended paragraph), list then build.** Work from your ordered list. Build the paragraph around
the detail you circled, and put that detail where a reader will not rush past it. End with why it mattered.
**Check before you stop: no word in your paragraph names a feeling.** Count your sentences: between six and
nine.

**Level 6 (a complete essay).** Write it in the order of your plan, and write all of it today.

1. The opening: your chosen opening sentence, two or three sentences of connecting information, then the
   sentence saying what the experience showed you, **last**.
2. The middle: two or three paragraphs telling what happened in order, each opening with a sentence that names
   what that paragraph is about.
3. The turn: the place where you stop telling what happened and start saying what it meant. It can be the last
   part of the final middle paragraph or a paragraph of its own.
4. One sentence, anywhere after the turn, that answers what Mina is likely to think of you.
5. The ending: say what it showed you again in new words, then close with advice, a prediction, a question, or
   an opinion.

**Level 7 (a complete essay).** The same five steps with four changes: the main point signals a reason without
naming it; the detail you marked goes in early and is never explained; the pacing is deliberate, so at least one
moment is slowed down and at least one stretch of time is crossed in a single sentence; and the ending does not
explain the implication.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7): the strong sentence board.** As students draft, collect sentences
offered from any Level and write them up under headings the class supplies. Build it live through the block; it
stays up for Day 2.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share-out (15 min)

Each student reads one sentence from their draft to a neighbour, and four or five are read to the room.
Level 4 reads its *because* sentence. Level 5 reads the sentence holding the circled detail, and the neighbour
says what they think it shows. Level 6 reads the sentence that answers Mina. Level 7 reads the sentence that
crosses the most time. Add two or three of these to the strong sentence board.

## DAY 2: SELF-EDIT AND IMPROVE (75 MIN)

```
0                    25                      55           75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1-------|--------Phase 2--------|--Phase 3---|
| Self-edit checklist  | Improve the piece     | Share-out  |
| pass                 | from the marks        | and final  |
|                      |                       | line check |
```

The draft is complete from Day 1, so Day 2 opens directly on checking it. There is no "finish your piece" phase
and no "from last time" wording in the packet (Quality Standards §D9). **Phases 1 and 2 are printed as one
section, "Check and Improve Your Writing," with one lettered Task per star holding that Level's check items and
then its improvement steps** (Style Guide §F). Students count sentences only; the word ranges stay with the
teacher.

### Phases 1 and 2 combined into one Task per Level: check, then improve (55 min)

Marks go on the page: circle, underline, box. **Cross out, do not erase,** so the first version stays readable
next to the new one.

**Level 4.** 1. Underline your time words. Do you have at least two, and did you choose them yourself? 2. Box
the sentence that says how it turned out. 3. Circle the word *because*. Is there a real reason after it, not
just a repeat of what happened? 4. Check every sentence has a subject and a verb. 5. Count your sentences:
between three and six. 6. Now improve it: replace one plain verb with a more exact one from the Word Bank, and
add one more thing that happened if you have fewer than four sentences.

**Level 5.** 1. Underline the detail that shows how you felt. 2. Read your paragraph and circle any word that
names a feeling. If you circled any, cross them out and let the detail do the work. 3. Box the sentence that
says why it mattered. 4. Check that your events are in order and that you have not doubled back. 5. Check every
sentence has a subject and a verb, and check the comma after any opener. 6. Count your sentences: between six
and nine. 7. Now improve it: move your underlined detail if a reader would rush past it where it is, and
replace one plain verb with a more exact one.

**Level 6.** 1. Underline the sentence saying what the experience showed you. Is it the last sentence of your
opening? 2. Underline the first sentence of each middle paragraph. Does each one name what its paragraph is
about? 3. Box the turn, where you stop telling what happened and start saying what it meant. 4. Circle the
sentence that answers what Mina is likely to think of you. 5. Check your ending says the main point again in
new words and then does one of the four closing moves. 6. Check your reporting verbs: is each one the verb you
would choose on purpose? 7. Check the comma after every opener, and check no opener is attached to the wrong
subject. 8. Now improve it: rewrite one sentence so the join between two paragraphs is smoother, and replace one
reporting verb with one that does more work.

**Level 7.** 1. Underline your main-point sentence. Does it signal a reason without naming it? 2. Underline the
first sentence of each middle paragraph. 3. Box the turn. 4. Find the detail you put in early and did not
explain. Is it still unexplained? 5. Read your ending. Does it avoid explaining the implication? 6. Find the
place where you slowed a moment down and the place where you crossed a lot of time quickly. Mark both. If you
cannot find one of them, make one. 7. Check the comma after every opener and that no opener is attached to the
wrong subject. 8. Trade with a partner. Ask them to write, in one line, what they think your real reason was.
Compare it with what you planned. 9. Now improve it: change one thing in response to what your partner wrote,
crossing out rather than erasing.

**Answer note:** none of these is a key; every item is a mark the student makes on their own page. The teacher's
check while circulating is that the marks exist and that the improvement step at the end of each Task has
actually changed something visible.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7).** Return to the strong sentence board from Day 1 and add newly improved
lines to it as students finish their improvement step, replacing the earlier version next to it where a student
offers both.

### Phase 3: Share-out and final line check (20 min)

Partners trade and read each other's last sentence aloud, then say one thing that sentence does. Four or five
read to the room. Nothing is collected and nothing is published today.

**What this lesson does not do.** There is no Peer Editing exchange, no publishing, and no Closing Transfer
Check at this position. All three belong to Lesson 4, which is this Set's finale.
