Task H. ★★★ Write your whole piece, in the order of your plan, on your own paper. All of it today.

  1. 1.The opening: your opening sentence, two or three sentences of connecting information, then the sentence saying what it showed you, last.
  2. 2.The middle: two or three paragraphs telling what happened in order, each opening with a sentence that names what that paragraph is about.
  3. 3.The turn: where you stop telling what happened and start saying what it meant.
  4. 4.One sentence, anywhere after the turn, that answers what Mina is likely to think of you.
  5. 5.The ending: say what it showed you again in new words, then close with advice, a prediction, a question, or an opinion.

Task I. ★★★★ Write your whole piece, in the order of your plan, on your own paper. All of it today.

  1. 1.The opening: your opening sentence, two or three sentences of connecting information, then your main-point sentence last.
  2. 2.The middle: two or three paragraphs telling what happened in order, each opening with a sentence that names what that paragraph is about. Put your unexplained detail in early.
  3. 3.The turn: where you stop telling what happened and start saying what it meant.
  4. 4.Pacing: slow at least one moment down, and cross at least one long stretch of time in a single sentence.
  5. 5.The ending: close without explaining what the reader is meant to work out.

Unit 3B: Writing About a Meal That Went Wrong

Going-wrong verbs: burned · boiled over · undercooked · oversalted · spilled · dropped

What went wrong: the timer · the heat · the recipe · the smoke · the pan · the smell
Time words: at first · halfway through · by then · in the end · too late · the next morning · one evening
People and places: my brother · my roommate · my friend · the kitchen · the bin · the shop · the table
Check and Improve Your Writing

Put your marks on the page: circle, underline, box. When you change something, cross it out and write the new version next to it. Do not erase.

Task A. ★

  1. 1.Underline your time words. Do you have at least two, and did you choose them yourself?
  2. 2.Box the sentence that says how it turned out.
  3. 3.Circle the word "because". Is there a real reason after it, not a repeat of what happened?
  4. 4.Check that every sentence has someone doing something.
  5. 5.Count your sentences: between three and six.
  6. 6.Now improve it. Replace one plain verb with a more exact one from the Word Bank, and add one more thing that happened if you have fewer than four sentences.

Task B. ★★

  1. 1.Underline the detail that shows how you felt.
  2. 2.Circle any word that names a feeling. Cross out every one you circled, and let the detail do the work.
  3. 3.Box the sentence that says why it mattered.
  4. 4.Check your events are in order and that you have not doubled back.
  5. 5.Check the comma after any opener.
  6. 6.Count your sentences: between six and nine.
  7. 7.Now improve it. Move your underlined detail if a reader would rush past it where it is, and replace one plain verb with a more exact one.

Task C. ★★★

  1. 1.Underline the sentence saying what the experience showed you. Is it the last sentence of your opening?
  2. 2.Underline the first sentence of each middle paragraph. Does each one name what its paragraph is about?
  3. 3.Box the turn, where you stop telling what happened and start saying what it meant.
  4. 4.Circle the sentence that answers what Mina is likely to think of you.
  5. 5.Check your ending says your main point again in new words and then does one of the four closing moves.
  6. 6.Check your reporting verbs. Is each one the verb you would choose on purpose?
  7. 7.Check the comma after every opener, and that no opener is attached to the wrong subject.
  8. 8.Now improve it. Rewrite one sentence so the join between two paragraphs is smoother, and replace one reporting verb with one that does more work.

Task D. ★★★★

  1. 1.Underline your main-point sentence. Does it signal a reason without naming it?
  2. 2.Underline the first sentence of each middle paragraph.
  3. 3.Box the turn.
  4. 4.Find the detail you put in early and did not explain. Is it still unexplained?
  5. 5.Read your ending. Does it avoid explaining what the reader is meant to work out?
  6. 6.Mark the place where you slowed a moment down and the place where you crossed a lot of time quickly. If one is missing, make it.
  7. 7.Check the comma after every opener, and that no opener is attached to the wrong subject.
  8. 8.Trade with a partner. Ask them to write, in one line, what they think your real reason was. Compare it with what you planned.
  9. 9.Now improve it. Change one thing in response to what your partner wrote, crossing out rather than erasing.
