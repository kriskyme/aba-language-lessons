## What this lesson works from

**The student's own complete piece,** written and improved at Lesson 3. It arrives with the marks and crossings
from that lesson still on it; nothing was collected and nothing was cleaned up in between.

**No new grammar.** Everything used today was taught in Lessons 1-2 and is named by what it does, never by a
label:

- reported speech and thought, with *say / tell / ask* and reporting verbs that carry an attitude;
- non-finite openers with their comma, and the rule that the opener attaches to the subject that follows;
- the student's own time connectors, one outcome, one stated reason (Level 4's own row);
- for Levels 6-7, the shape of a piece that tells a story and then says what it meant.

**This day never re-runs Lesson 3's checklist.** That was the correctness pass. Today is revision **for effect**:
what the piece does to a reader, not whether it is right.

**Output targets for the finished piece (Section 0.2), checked by the teacher on the final copy.** Level 4: 3-6
sentences, 40-90 words. Level 5: 6-9 sentences, 90-150 words. Level 6: about 300-450 words. Level 7: about
450-650 words. The ranges are the teacher's; **students count nothing today.**

## DAY 1: PEER EDITING AND REVISION FOR EFFECT (75 MIN)

```
0            15                                 60         75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1----|--------------Phase 2------------|-Phase 3--|
| Title, then  | Revise for effect, with the     | Revision  |
| the Peer     | before and after record         | share     |
| Editing form |                                 |           |
```

### Phase 1: Title, then the Peer Editing exchange (15 min)

**Title (3 min).** Every student writes a title on their piece. It is the first time the piece has had one, and
the title is the first thing a reader will meet at Phase 2 of Day 2.

**Peer Editing exchange (12 min), the Set's one exchange (Section 0.6).** Pieces are traded with a neighbour.
The reader fills a short printed form on the writer's page. **This is Peer Editing, not a reader's request: it
has the form, the compliment, and the suggestion, and it runs once in this Set, here.**

The form has five items. Items 1 to 3 branch by what the reader is holding, so a Level 4 reader can complete a
form on a Level 7 essay and the other way round:

1. If you are reading one paragraph: number the time words you can find. If you are reading a longer piece:
   number the paragraphs, and tick each one whose first sentence tells you what that paragraph is about.
2. Box the sentence where you find out how it turned out.
3. If you are reading one paragraph: write the word you think shows how the writer felt. If you are reading a
   longer piece: underline the sentence where the writer stops telling the story and starts saying what it
   meant, and write in the margin what you think the writer wanted you to think of them.
4. One specific compliment: copy out one sentence you would keep exactly as it is.
5. One specific suggestion: write one thing you wanted more of, and say where in the piece you wanted it.

**Teacher note, not printed.** Circulate on item 3. On a Level 5 piece, if the reader names a feeling word that
is actually printed in the paragraph, that is the check failing and the writer should hear it. On a Level 7
piece, compare what the reader wrote in the margin against what that writer planned as the motive to be
inferred; the gap between the two is the most useful thing on the page today.

### Phase 2: Revise for effect, with the before and after record (45 min)

Not a correctness pass. Every change is recorded in a printed before-and-after table with three columns: what I
first wrote, what I changed it to, what the change does. **Required at Level 7** (0.4a), expected at Levels 4-6.

**Levels 4-5, three moves.**

1. **Join.** Find two short sentences that happen close together in time. Join them with an opener, and put the
   comma in.
2. **Upgrade.** Find one plain word and replace it with a more exact one from the Word Bank, or with a reporting
   verb that says something about the speaker.
3. **Answer the reader.** Add the sentence your partner asked for in item 5 of their form, in the place they
   asked for it.

**Level 6, the same three moves on the whole piece, plus one.**

4. **Smooth a join.** Rewrite the first sentence of one middle paragraph so it connects to the paragraph before
   it as well as naming its own subject.

**Level 7, the same four, plus one.**

5. **Move the detail.** Shift the detail you planted early, or shift the sentence that gives the outcome away, so
   that the reader reaches the ending later than they do now. Record in the table what the move does to when the
   reader understands.

**Cross out, do not erase.** The original stays readable beside the new version, which is what makes the
revision visible rather than claimed.

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live: the before and after board.** Take two or three volunteered rows
from students' tables and write both versions up. The class says what each change did, in their own words, and
those words go on the board next to the pair. No handout contains it.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

Three or four students read one row from their table aloud, both versions, and the class names what changed.
Level 4 and Level 5 rows are read first so the smallest changes are heard as real changes.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY, PUBLISH, CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0             20                          50              75  (minutes)
|--Phase 1-----|----------Phase 2---------|----Phase 3----|
| Final copy    | Publish and share        | Closing        |
|               | (pass and read)          | Transfer Check |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy (20 min)

A clean copy of the whole piece, with the title at the top, capitals and full stops in place. No proofreading
list, no counting task, nothing to tick.

**The piece is finished here.** Nothing in this lesson or its packet says the student will keep working on it,
because there is no later Module waiting for it: this is a standalone Set and the arc ends today.

### Phase 2: Publish and share (30 min)

**The piece is genuinely finished, and this is the publishing step.**

**Pass and read (18 min).** Each final copy is passed to a neighbour, read, and passed on again, so every student
reads two pieces that are not their own partner's. **Nobody leaves their seat and nothing goes on a wall**
(Quality Standards §D11). After each piece, the reader writes one line on a card, differentiated by the reader's
own Level:

- Level 4: one thing that happened in the story you just read.
- Level 5: the detail that told you how the writer felt.
- Level 6: the sentence where the story turned into what it meant.
- Level 7: what you think the writer wanted you to work out, and the detail that told you.

**Read-alouds (12 min).** Three or four students are cold-called to read their favourite sentence from their own
piece, and say in one sentence why they kept it. Nothing is collected; the copies go home.

### Phase 3: Closing Transfer Check (25 min)

**One check, on something new.** A standalone Set has one Skill Spotlight and one Closing Transfer Check, and
this Check covers the skill the Spotlight named at Lesson 1: telling what happened, in order, and letting what
people said carry how the writer felt about it. **There is no second, separate verb task.** That belongs only to
a Module Pair's last lesson, where the shared piece was written in the other Module's genre; this Set's piece is
already in Module 2's own, so the piece itself has exercised Module 2's verb.

**The subject: one thing that happened with your own water bottle or cup.** A third subject, used nowhere else
in this Set and in no other Band's Set. Nobody brings anything or holds anything up: it is written and said from
memory, like everything else in this Set (Quality Standards §D8).

On cards, in pairs. Each student writes, then reads to their partner, who does the listening task.

- **Level 4.** Three sentences: what happened, in order, with two time words you choose, ending with how it
  turned out and one reason. Partner: say back the order in which the three things happened.
- **Level 5.** Three or four sentences including one detail that shows how you felt, with no word that names a
  feeling. Partner: guess the feeling and name the words that told you.
- **Level 6.** Four sentences: what happened, then one sentence saying why it mattered, written for a partner
  who is likely to think it was a small thing. Partner: say what the writer expected you to think.
- **Level 7.** Four sentences with the outcome held back until the last one, and one earlier detail left
  unexplained. Partner: say what you think the unexplained detail was doing there.

**Transfer share (inside the 25 min).** Two or three pairs are cold-called to read their cards and their
partner's response. **Demonstration only:** no hands, no thumbs, nothing collected.

**Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7).** Capture the transfer instances live as pairs share: a short column of
what happened to whom, in the students' own words, built from the cards rather than prepared.

**The Set is finished.**
