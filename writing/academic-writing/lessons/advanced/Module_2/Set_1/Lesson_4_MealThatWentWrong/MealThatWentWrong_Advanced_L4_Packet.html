Unit 4A: Finishing Your Story About a Meal That Went Wrong

Writing · Narrating Advanced 2.1.4.0

Objective: I can tell what happened, in order, and let what people said carry how I felt about it.

Going-wrong verbs: burned · boiled over · undercooked · oversalted · spilled · dropped
Kitchen actions: forgot · misread · guessed · rushed · swapped · tasted
What went wrong: the timer · the heat · the recipe · the smoke · the pan · the smell
Time words: at first · halfway through · by then · in the end · too late · the next morning · one evening
People and places: my brother · my roommate · my friend · the kitchen · the bin · the shop · the table
Name Your Piece

Task A. Write a title at the top of your piece.

Trade and Check

Trade pieces with the person next to you. Fill this in on their page, not yours.

Task B. Read the whole piece once before you write anything.

  1. 1.If you are reading one paragraph, number the time words you can find. If you are reading a longer piece, number the paragraphs and tick each one whose first sentence tells you what that paragraph is about.
  2. 2.Box the sentence where you find out how it turned out.
  3. 3.If you are reading one paragraph, write the word you think shows how the writer felt. If you are reading a longer piece, underline the sentence where the writer stops telling the story and starts saying what it meant, and write in the margin what you think the writer wanted you to think of them.
  4. 4.Copy out one sentence you would keep exactly as it is.
  5. 5.Write one thing you wanted more of, and say where in the piece you wanted it.
Make It Stronger

Take your own piece back. Write every change in the table as you make it. Cross out on your piece, do not erase, so both versions can be read.

Task C. ★ Make these three changes.

  1. 1.Find two short sentences about things that happened close together. Join them with an opener, and put the comma in.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word with a more exact one from the Word Bank.
  3. 3.Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place they asked for it.

Task D. ★★ Make these three changes.

  1. 1.Find two short sentences about things that happened close together. Join them with an opener, and put the comma in.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word with a more exact one, or swap a reporting verb for one that says something about the speaker.
  3. 3.Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place they asked for it.

Task E. ★★★ Make these four changes.

  1. 1.Join two short sentences about things that happened close together with an opener, and put the comma in.
  2. 2.Swap one reporting verb for one that says more about the speaker.
  3. 3.Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place they asked for it.
  4. 4.Rewrite the first sentence of one middle paragraph so it connects to the paragraph before it as well as naming its own subject.

Task F. ★★★★ Make these five changes.

  1. 1.Join two short sentences about things that happened close together with an opener, and put the comma in.
  2. 2.Swap one reporting verb for one that says more about the speaker.
  3. 3.Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place they asked for it.
  4. 4.Rewrite the first sentence of one middle paragraph so it connects to the paragraph before it as well as naming its own subject.
  5. 5.Move the detail you put in early, or move the sentence that gives the outcome away, so a reader reaches the ending later than they do now.

Task I. If you are called on, read your favourite sentence from your own piece and say in one sentence why you kept it.

Wrap It Up

One thing that happened with your own water bottle or cup. Write it on your card, then read it to your partner and do their listening task.

Task J. ★ Write three sentences: what happened, in order, with two time words you choose, ending with how it turned out and one reason.

Listening: say back the order in which the three things happened.

Task K. ★★ Write three or four sentences including one detail that shows how you felt. Use no word that names a feeling.

Listening: guess the feeling and name the words that told you.

Task L. ★★★ Write four sentences: what happened, then one sentence saying why it mattered, written for a partner who is likely to think it was a small thing.

Listening: say what the writer expected you to think.

Task M. ★★★★ Write four sentences with the outcome held back until the last one, and one earlier detail left unexplained.

Listening: say what you think the unexplained detail was doing there.
