## Plan

**Band Lesson #:** Advanced 1 (matches the existing `Rotation_Log_Advanced.md` row; this document does not
renumber or re-log it).

**Scenario:** Every task Level compares two real apartments for Sam, a friend deciding whether to move in as a
roommate, whose one stated priority is quiet (works from home). Apartment A: smaller, quieter, on a residential
street, thick walls. Apartment B: bigger, cheaper, closer to downtown, noticeably louder. Writable across the
full Band: Levels 4-5 write a single comparative paragraph; Levels 6-7 write a full comparison essay weighing the
two apartments against each other with Sam's stated concern in view throughout.

**Grammar Focus A/B pairing:** Focus A (tense control + hedging/evaluative language: seems, tends to, a bit, at
least, worth it) and Focus B (complex sentences with a concession subordinator: although, even though) are
Section 0.4's Level 5 row — Advanced's own **native** Paragraph Composition floor. This required a v3.1
correction: the row was originally anchored at Level 4 (comparatives + because), which is actually
**Intermediate's** native centerpiece on loan to Advanced as an extension-down accommodation, not Advanced's own
native row — the mistake made this lesson read as Lesson 1 (Intermediate) with the nouns changed. Under the
corrected rule, Level 4 still receives its own row (comparatives + because; simple/compound sentences), taught in
the same whole-class Grammar mini-lesson rather than as the centerpiece, still fully sufficient for Level 4's own
required feature.

**Essay Focus A/B direction:** Anchored at Level 6's row (Advanced's own native Essay Composition floor — no
correction needed here, unlike Focus A/B above). Essay Focus A: the five-paragraph essay shape, five hook types,
thesis construction (direct at Level 6, indirect at Level 7). Essay Focus B: topic-sentence/supporting-sentence
structure, outlining (general at Level 6, specific at Level 7), combining sentences for cohesion. Mentor Essay
model: a comparison essay ("Two Ways to Commute to Work," driving vs. biking), per Section 0.9's
Module-to-real-world-form mapping for a comparison Scenario. Level 5 gets a light, receptive essay-structure
bridge (find/underline one body paragraph's topic sentence on the same shared model essay) without raising
Level 5's own output past a single paragraph.

**Real-world writing form:** Descriptive/comparative paragraph (Levels 4-5); comparison essay (Levels 6-7).

**Leveled Mentor Ladder direction:**
- **Level 4:** a short paragraph naming one comparison and one explicit "because" reason between the two
  apartments.
- **Level 5:** an extended paragraph organizing several concrete comparison details logically, using at least one
  hedge word, with an implied (not stated) preference for one apartment.
- **Level 6:** a five-paragraph comparison essay with a direct thesis, addressed directly to Sam, explicitly
  naming why one feature (quiet) matters to Sam and shifting register at least once.
- **Level 7:** a five-paragraph comparison essay with an indirect thesis, an embedded implication the reader must
  infer, sophisticated cohesion, and a visible self-revision demonstration (a crossed-out sentence replaced
  in place).

**Task-Level basis:** as quoted verbatim in the table above — each task Level's Mentor Text/Essay and drafting
task in the generated lesson maps directly to its own row.
