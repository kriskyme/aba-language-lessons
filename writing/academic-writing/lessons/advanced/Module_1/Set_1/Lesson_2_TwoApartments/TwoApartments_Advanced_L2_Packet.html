Grammar

Admitting a Downside, and Still Making Your Point

WordMeaningExample
althoughadmits something is true, then makes a bigger pointAlthough Apartment B is cheaper, Apartment A is quieter.
even thoughsame idea, a slightly stronger admissionEven though the kitchen is small, I still prefer Apartment A.
buta plain contrast (already familiar)Apartment B is bigger, but Apartment A is quieter.

Joining Two Sentences

ConnectorMeaningExample
andadds informationApartment A is small, and it is quiet.
butadds contrastApartment A is small, but it is quiet.
sogives a resultApartment B got noisy, so I kept looking.

Last lesson you hinted at a feeling with a softening word. Today you'll admit a real downside and still make your point, in one sentence.

Topic Sentences and Supporting Sentences

A topic sentence states a paragraph's main point. Supporting sentences give examples, reasons, facts, or specifics. Look back at "Two Ways to Commute to Work" (Lesson 1) and find its second body paragraph, the one about cost: notice how every sentence after the topic sentence supports it with a specific detail.
