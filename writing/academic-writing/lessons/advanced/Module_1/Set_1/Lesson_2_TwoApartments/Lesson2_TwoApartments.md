## Grammar Focus B (Level 5's row, this lesson's centerpiece for Levels 4-5; lighter component for Levels 6-7)

**Focus B (this lesson):** complex sentences with a contrast/concession subordinator (although, even though),
alongside plain "but." Level 5's paired row.

**Level 4's own required feature (this lesson, same whole-class session):** simple versus compound sentences,
and/but/so.

**Confusable pair (this lesson):** "your" vs. "you're," since the Scenario addresses Sam directly throughout, at
every Level.

## Essay Focus B (Levels 6-7, this lesson)

**Essay Focus B (this lesson):** topic sentence + supporting sentence structure; outlining (**Level 6:** general;
**Level 7:** specific); combining sentences for cohesion.

**Level 7 extension:** sophisticated cohesion; a concession/counter structure ("while X is true, Y matters
more"); the embedded-implication and self-revision requirements, modeled inside the Level 7 Mentor Essay

### Phase 1 (20 min)

**Levels 4-5 (Grammar Focus B: complex sentences with a concession subordinator):**

| Word        | Meaning                                             | Example                                                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------|
| although    | admits something is true, then makes a bigger point | Although Apartment B is cheaper, Apartment A is quieter.      |
| even though | same idea, a slightly stronger admission            | Even though the kitchen is small, I still prefer Apartment A. |
| but         | a plain contrast (already familiar)                 | Apartment B is bigger, but Apartment A is quieter.            |

Tie to Focus A: "Yesterday you hinted at a feeling with a hedge word. Today we'll admit a real downside and still
make our point, in one sentence."

**Continuing the same whole-class Grammar mini-lesson (8 min):** simple versus compound sentences, taught to
everyone right after the concession-subordinator content above, not delivered separately.

| Connector | Meaning          | Example                                   |
| --------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------|
| and       | adds information | Apartment A is small, and it is quiet.    |
| but       | adds contrast    | Apartment A is small, but it is quiet.    |
| so        | gives a result   | Apartment B got noisy, so I kept looking. |

**Levels 6-7 (Essay Focus B: topic sentences and supporting sentences):** a topic sentence states the paragraph's
main point; supporting sentences give examples, reasons, facts, or specifics. Model this with Lesson 1's "Two
Ways to Commute" essay's second body paragraph.

### Phase 2 (30 min)

**Levels 4-5, Activity 7 (identify the sentence type, 8 items) and Activity 8 (combine using although/even
though, 6 items).**

> Activity 7: for each sentence, write "concession" if it admits a downside and makes a bigger point, or
> "contrast" if it is a plain "but" sentence.
>
> 1. Although Apartment B is cheaper, Apartment A is quieter.
> 2. Apartment A is smaller, but it feels calmer.
> 3. Even though the kitchen is tight, the quiet makes up for it.
> 4. Apartment B is louder, but it's closer to the train.
> 5. Although the rent is higher, Apartment A seems worth it.
> 6. Apartment A has a smaller closet, but that doesn't bother me.
> 7. Even though Apartment B is bigger, I still lean toward Apartment A.
> 8. Apartment B is cheaper, but the noise tends to be a real problem.
>
> Activity 8 (combine each pair using although or even though):
>
> 1. Apartment B is cheaper. Apartment A is quieter.
> 2. The kitchen in Apartment A is small. It doesn't bother me much.
> 3. Apartment B is closer to the train. I still prefer Apartment A.
> 4. Apartment A costs more. It's worth it for the quiet.
> 5. Apartment B has a second closet. Apartment A wins on noise.
> 6. Apartment A's street is quieter. Apartment B's kitchen is bigger.

**Level 4's own practice (folded into Phase 2, 12 min): identify simple (S) or compound (CD) and add the missing
comma (8 items).** This is a practice activity, not a second teaching input: Level 4 heard the same Grammar
mini-lesson as everyone else and now practices its own required feature specifically.

**Levels 6-7, specific outlining (15 min) and combining sentences for cohesion (15 min).** Complete a specific
outline for "Two Ways to Commute to Work," expanding Lesson 1's general outline. Then combine short sentence sets
into one cohesive sentence.

### Phase 3 (25 min)

**Levels 4-5, confusable pair and mixed editing:**

**"your" vs. "you're."** you're = you are; your = belonging to you.

> 1. **\_\_\_** going to want to see Apartment A's bedroom first.
> 2. Apartment B fits **\_\_\_** budget better than Apartment A does.
> 3. **\_\_\_** right that the kitchen in Apartment B is bigger.
> 4. I think **\_\_\_** going to like how quiet Apartment A is.
> 5. **\_\_\_** concern about noise is exactly why I toured both places.
> 6. **\_\_\_** welcome to come see either one this weekend.

**Mixed editing, 8 errors:**

> Although Apartment B are cheaper, Apartment A seem quieter to me. It tend to stay calm even on weekends, and
> the walls is thick enough that noise almost never get through. The kitchen were a bit small, but that don't
> bother me. Even though Apartment B has more space, it's noise are hard to ignore at night.

Verified errors (8): "Apartment B are" -> "is"; "Apartment A seem" -> "seems"; "It tend to" -> "It tends to"; "the
walls is" -> "are"; "noise almost never get" -> "gets"; "The kitchen were" -> "was"; "that don't bother" ->
"doesn't bother"; "it's noise are" -> "its noise is."

**Levels 6-7, extension ladder close (Level 7's layer, 5 min):** name Level 7's additions starting Day 2:
sophisticated cohesion, a concession/counter structure ("while X is true, Y matters more," the Level 7 upgrade of
today's "although X, Y"), an indirect thesis and stronger hook, and the self-revision mechanism modeled in
Lesson 1's Level 7 Mentor Essay.

## DAY 2: TASK-LADDER PRACTICE AND PREWRITING (75 MIN)

### Phase 1: Required-feature warm-up, differentiated (25 min)

**Level 4:** write two comparison sentences and two because-sentences about Apartment A vs. B, then join one pair
into a compound sentence.

**Level 5:** write two sentences, each with a concrete detail and a hedge word from Lesson 1's box, then trade
with a partner and ask what it implies about which apartment is preferred.

**Level 6:** draft a rough direct thesis for their own essay, and one sentence using "given that" or "since" to
name why one feature matters to Sam specifically.

**Level 7:** draft a rough indirect thesis, and one sentence using a concession/counter structure ("while X is
true, Y matters more").

### Phase 2: Prewriting, differentiated (30 min)

**Level 4:** a two-column note: "What I'm comparing" / "My real reason," plus one planned compound sentence.

**Level 5:** a list of 3-4 concrete details comparing the two apartments, each paired with a hedge word, then
circle the one that most clearly implies a preference.

**Level 6:** a general outline for their own essay: thesis, then each body paragraph's topic sentence, with a
note on where the register shift to Sam happens.

**Level 7:** a specific outline for their own essay, plus a note on which detail will carry the embedded
implication and what the self-revision target is likely to be.

### Phase 3: Prewriting share and board synthesis (20 min)

A few students from each task Level share one idea. Board-dependent moment (Section 0.7): a shared class idea
board in two columns (Apartment features / Why it matters to a reader like Sam).
