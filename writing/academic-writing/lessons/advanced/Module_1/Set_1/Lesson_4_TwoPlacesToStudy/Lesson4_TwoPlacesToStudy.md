## What this lesson carries forward

**The carried draft.** Each student brings Module 1's part, checked and improved at Lesson 3 Day 2: a paragraph
(Levels 4-5) or the body paragraphs under a working thesis line (Levels 6-7) comparing their home with one
place to study for Dana.
It is the one thing carried between lessons (Quality Standards §D9); everything else this lesson needs is in
its own packet.

**Module 1's tools, used here for effect (Lesson prompt 0.4a):** a concession or compound join (Focus B); a more
exact word from the Draft Word Bank (Focus A's evaluative vocabulary); a sentence the reader asked for; and, at
Level 7, a moved sentence or paragraph. Corrections (a comma, a tense slip) were Lesson 3 Day 2's business and
do not count as moves.

## DAY 1: REVISE FOR EFFECT (75 MIN)

```
0              15                                            60                  75  (minutes)
|---Phase 1----|-------------------Phase 2--------------------|------Phase 3------|
| A reader's   | Revise for effect: three moves (four at 7)   | Revision share    |
| request      | in a before/after table                      | (board)           |
```

### Phase 1: A reader's request (15 min)

Partners trade pieces and read them silently. Each reader writes one line on the writer's page: one thing they
want to know more about, as Dana would ("I want to know more about ___": the noise at night, how far the
library is, what the desk is like). Then the pieces go back. This is not Peer Editing (Lesson prompt 0.6): one
request, no form, no compliment or suggestion. Peer Editing happens once, at Pair position 8.

### Phase 2: Revise for effect with Module 1's tools (45 min)

Every student makes three changes that make the piece stronger, each recorded in the packet's before/after
table (what I wrote / what I changed it to / what the change does). Visible revision: cross out on the draft,
write the new version beside it.

- **Levels 4-6, three moves:** (1) **Join:** two short sentences joined with a concession (although, even
  though, while) or, at Level 4, with and, but, or so; or a plain "but" turned into "although." (2) **Upgrade:**
  one plain word replaced with a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank (quiet becomes calm or private; far
  becomes hard to reach). (3) **Add:** the sentence the reader asked for, placed where it belongs, not at the
  end. At Level 6 the added sentence may also be the one that turns to Dana, if the request was Dana-shaped.
- **Level 7, a fourth move (a different shape):** (4) **Move:** one sentence or one paragraph relocated so that
  the detail carrying the implication lands late, where a reader feels it most; the table's "what it does"
  column names the implication the move protects.

**Teacher circulation:** three visible changes on every Level 4-6 page (four at Level 7) with a filled table row
for each; a change that only corrects is not a move, and the student is sent back to the move list. The essay
Levels' moves may sit in any body paragraph; the working thesis line is not a move.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a before/after board in two columns. Two or three volunteers
read one row of their table aloud (a Level 7 move first, since a relocation is hardest to hear; then a Level 5
or 6 join or upgrade; then a Level 4 addition). The teacher writes each "before" on the left and each "after"
on the right, and the class names in one line what the change did.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY AND CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0                    30                      60           75  (minutes)
|-------Phase 1-------|-------Phase 2--------|--Phase 3---|
| Final copy          | Closing Transfer     | Transfer   |
| (not publishing)    | Check (cards)        | share      |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy, not publishing (30 min)

Students write a clean copy of Module 1's part on the packet's lines, every Day 1 change in place, capitals
and periods intact: the paragraph at Levels 4-5; the working thesis line and the body paragraphs at Levels 6-7. No proofread checklist and no counting task at this position (the piece was checked once,
at Lesson 3 Day 2); the teacher's circulation check is that each table row shows up in the copy. The word
ranges (Level 4: 40-90; Level 5: 90-150; Level 6 bodies: 200-300; Level 7 bodies: 300-450) remain the teacher's
check and are not printed. A student who cannot finish the copy in thirty minutes finishes it as Module 2's
Lesson 5 opens, and the teacher notes it.

The part is complete and self-revised; the piece is not. It is not published, posted, read to the class, or
exchanged for peer editing at this position: it moves to Module 2's Set, which writes the introduction and
conclusion around these bodies (Levels 6-7) or a second, narrative paragraph (Levels 4-5) with Module 2's own
tools, assembles the whole, runs Peer Editing on both Modules' focuses, and publishes it (Conventions §C). The copy goes into the student's
folder unmarked, with no hand-off note; Module 2's Lesson 5 re-reads it, and the teacher's recap there names
what this Module added. The packet's one line under Final Copy, "You'll keep working on this piece soon," is
the whole hand-off. Nothing is collected for grading.

### Phase 2: Closing Transfer Check (30 min)

Every student applies Module 1's spotlighted skill (describing something of their own by comparing it to
something else and giving a real reason) to a new object, not the two places. The object is written in: **the
student's own desk or table at home, compared with a partner's.** In pairs, students each write on a card:

- **Level 4:** one sentence comparing your desk with your partner's, with a reason word (because, since, as,
  given that) and a real reason ("My desk is smaller than Ana's, because it has to fit under the window").
- **Level 5:** the same comparison sentence, then a second sentence with one concrete detail about your own desk
  that lets your partner guess how you feel about it, with no feeling word. The partner reads it and says the
  guess out loud; a wrong guess sends the writer back for a sharper detail.
- **Level 6:** a three-sentence mini-body: a topic sentence stating one point about your desk compared with
  your partner's, one supporting sentence, and one sentence that says why that point would matter to your
  partner, addressed to them by name.
- **Level 7:** the same three sentences, one of them a concession (although, even though, while), with the
  detail chosen so that the partner infers your attitude toward your own desk without being told; the partner
  says what they inferred.

Levels 4-5 pairs are encouraged to join the comparison and its reason, or the two Level 5 sentences, into one
compound or concession sentence where it fits naturally. Demonstration only: no thumbs, no hands, nothing
collected.

### Phase 3: Transfer share (15 min)

The teacher cold-calls two or three pairs to read their cards aloud. **Board-dependent moment, co-constructed
live:** capture two or three of the read-aloud instances on the board under Day 1's before/after board, one per
Level where possible, so the class sees the same skill on a desk that it saw on two places to study.
