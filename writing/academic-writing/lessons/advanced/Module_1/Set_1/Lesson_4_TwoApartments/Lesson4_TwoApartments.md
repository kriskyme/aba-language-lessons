## DAY 1: SELF-REVISION AND DRAFT COMPLETION (75 MIN)

**Self-edit checklist pass (all task Levels, Level-specific items marked):**

1. (Level 4) Circle your own comparative word, and underline your "because" reason. Does the reason make sense as
   a real reason?
2. (Level 5) Circle one hedge word you used. Does it suggest a feeling without saying it outright?
3. Put a box around one concession or compound sentence. Is the comma in the right place?
4. (Levels 6-7) Can a partner, without being told, identify your hook, your thesis, and each body paragraph's
   topic sentence? If not, note where they got lost.
5. (Level 6) Find the sentence that talks directly to Sam about Sam's concern. Does the register genuinely shift
   there?
6. (Level 7) What are you implying but never stating outright? Check it against your own self-revision note: does
   it match, or did the revision aim somewhere else?

**Self-revision time:** students revise based on their own checklist pass and complete the draft to full length.
**No Peer Editing exchange at this position** - that happens once, at Module 2's Lesson 4, once Module 2's own
tools are also available (see the note above). **Level 7 students must produce one of the concrete self-revision
artifacts from Section 0.4a**; this is not optional at Level 7. Levels 4-6 are encouraged to do the same if time
allows.

Quick revision share: a before/after board built from two or three volunteered examples, most naturally from
Level 7.

## DAY 2: DRAFT HAND-OFF AND CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

Final polish/proofread pass against the Day 2 (Lesson 3) checklist, completing the draft to full length. **Hand-off
note, not publishing:** students write a short note (a sentence or two) naming what Module 1's own focus (tense
control, hedging, concession sentences, and - for Levels 6-7 - the essay's hook/thesis/body structure) added to
the piece, and that it now moves to Module 2's Set for revision, peer editing, and publishing. **Do not publish or
share the piece at this position.**

**Closing Transfer Check:** in pairs, every student applies the spotlighted skill to something new: "In two or
three sentences, compare two different things, hint at how you feel about one of them without saying it
outright, and give a real reason for the comparison, written for a specific reader with a specific concern."
Level 6-7 pairs are additionally asked to state, out loud, what a one-sentence direct or indirect thesis for that
new comparison would be. The teacher cold-calls 2-3 pairs to share; this is not a self-report. Board-dependent
moment (Section 0.7): capture instances live on the board.
