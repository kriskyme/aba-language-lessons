Unit 3A: Describing Two Places to Study

Writing · Describing Advanced 1.1.3.2

Objective: I can describe something of my own by comparing it to something else and giving a real reason for the comparison.

Word Bank
Sound and light: quiet · noisy · calm · busy · bright · dim
Space: roomy · cramped · open · crowded · private · shared
Comfort: warm · cool · soft · hard · cozy · plain
Access: near · far · free · open late · easy to reach · hard to book
Feel: focused · relaxed · distracting · welcoming · formal · familiar

Use these words if you need them. You may also use your own.

Get Ready to Write

Task A. Pick one of these places to compare with your home and circle it: the library · a cafe · a classroom after school · a park · a friend's place.

Task B. ★ Plan in two lines.

  1. 1.Write three Word Bank words that fit your home and three that fit the place you circled, one from each of three different groups:
  2. 2.Write one line naming the comparison you will make and the reason for it:

Task C. ★★ Plan the order.

  1. 1.Number the groups in the order you will describe them: sound and light, space, comfort, access, feel.
  2. 2.Write one detail that would let Dana guess which place you prefer, without your saying so:

Task D. ★★★ Write a general outline of your body paragraphs.

  1. 1.A direct working thesis naming two points:
  2. 2.One topic sentence per body paragraph.
  3. 3.The sentence that will say why one point matters to Dana:

Task E. ★★★★ Write a specific outline of your body paragraphs.

  1. 1.An indirect working thesis:
  2. 2.Three topic sentences, each with two details in order under it.
  3. 3.Mark the detail whose emphasis carries the implication, and name the implication for yourself in one line:
Write It

Task F. ★ Write one paragraph comparing your home with the place you circled, one sentence per step, 3-6 sentences.

  1. 1.Name the two places in one sentence.
  2. 2.One sentence about your home as a place to study.
  3. 3.One comparison sentence with a comparative (quieter than, more crowded than, better than).
  4. 4.The reason, with a reason word (because, since, as, given that), joined to the comparison or on its own.
  5. 5.One sentence Dana could use to choose, joined with and, but, or so.

Task G. ★★ Write one paragraph of 6-9 sentences.

  1. 1.Open with one sentence naming both places.
  2. 2.Describe them in your planned order, one or two sentences per group, in one tense.
  3. 3.Use at least one softening word where you are not sure, or where a thing is mixed.
  4. 4.Place one detail that implies which place you prefer, with no feeling word.
  5. 5.Close with one sentence Dana could act on.

Task H. ★★★ Write your body paragraphs, under a direct working thesis.

  1. 1.Copy your working thesis onto the first line. It stays one line, not a paragraph.
  2. 2.Write two or three body paragraphs, each opening with its topic sentence and supporting it with details, reasons, or contrasts.
  3. 3.In one body paragraph, one sentence that says why a feature matters to Dana.
  4. 4.One place where you turn to Dana directly ("You said you cannot work with...").
  5. 5.No beginning and no ending yet: those are written in a later unit around these paragraphs.

Task I. ★★★★ Write your body paragraphs, under an indirect working thesis.

  1. 1.Copy your working thesis onto the first line.
  2. 2.Write three body paragraphs whose details are chosen and placed so that the implication is felt, never stated.
  3. 3.Connect with what's more, admittedly, this suggests, on the other hand, not only "and."
  4. 4.Somewhere in the draft, cross out one sentence and write a better one in its place.
  5. 5.No beginning and no ending yet: those are written in a later unit around these paragraphs.
Share a Line

Task J. ★ Read your comparison sentence to the class.

Task K. ★★ Read the detail that shows which place you prefer. The class says what they think you prefer.

Task L. ★★★ Read the sentence that tells Dana why a feature matters.

Task M. ★★★★ Read the sentence you crossed out, then the one you wrote in its place.

Check and Improve Your Writing

Mark your draft as each line says. When you change something, cross it out and write the new version next to it. Do not erase.

Task A. ★ Check your paragraph, then make it better.

  1. 1.Does every sentence have a subject and a verb, in one tense?
  2. 2.Is there exactly one comparison? Circle the comparative.
  3. 3.Is there one reason with a reason word? Put a box around it.
  4. 4.Is there one sentence joined with and, but, or so, with a comma before it? Underline it.
  5. 5.Do capitals and periods sit where they belong?
  6. 6.Replace one plain word with a more exact one from the Word Bank.
  7. 7.If any check said no, add what is missing.
  8. 8.Add one more real detail about either place.

Task B. ★★ Check your paragraph, then make it better.

  1. 1.Is the whole paragraph in one tense? Circle any verb that slips.
  2. 2.Are the details in the order you planned? Number them in the margin.
  3. 3.Is there at least one softening word? Underline it.
  4. 4.Is there exactly one detail that implies your preference, with no feeling word? Put a box around it.
  5. 5.Is there a concession sentence with its comma?
  6. 6.If a feeling word slipped in, replace it with a detail.
  7. 7.Add one softening word where a claim is too flat.
  8. 8.Move one detail so the order runs the way you planned.

Task C. ★★★ Check your body paragraphs, then make them better.

  1. 1.Is the working thesis on the first line direct, naming two points? Underline it.
  2. 2.Does every body paragraph open with a topic sentence? Circle each one.
  3. 3.Does every sentence after it support that point? Underline any sentence that does not.
  4. 4.Is there one sentence that says why a feature matters to Dana? Put a box around it.
  5. 5.Is there one place where you turn to Dana directly? Mark it with a star.
  6. 6.Add or sharpen the Dana sentence; add the turn to Dana if it is missing.
  7. 7.Rewrite one topic sentence so it states the paragraph's one point; move any sentence that belongs in another paragraph.
  8. 8.Give a thin paragraph one more supporting sentence.

Task D. ★★★★ Check your body paragraphs, then make them better.

  1. 1.Is the working thesis indirect? Underline it.
  2. 2.Which detail carries the implication? Put a box around it. If any sentence states the implication outright, mark it for cutting.
  3. 3.Is there a crossed-out line replaced in place? If not, that is your first change.
  4. 4.Do the paragraphs connect with connecting phrases rather than "and"? Circle each one.
  5. 5.Cut the sentence that states the implication, and give the detail that carries it more room or a later place.
  6. 6.Cross out one line and write a better one in its place, if you have not already.
  7. 7.Combine two short sentences with a connecting phrase.
  8. 8.Reword the working thesis if the paragraphs have outgrown it.
Share a Line

Sit with a partner and read one line each.

Task E. ★ Read your reason sentence to your partner.

Task F. ★★ Read the detail that implies your preference. Your partner says what they infer.

Task G. ★★★ Read the sentence where you turn to Dana.

Task H. ★★★★ Read your replacement line. Your partner says what the paragraph now implies.
