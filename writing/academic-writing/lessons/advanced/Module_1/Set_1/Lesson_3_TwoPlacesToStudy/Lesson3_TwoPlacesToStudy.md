## What this lesson carries forward

**Scenario.** Every student compares their own home with one place to study, circled from the printed list at
the start of Day 1 (the library, a cafe, a classroom after school, a park, a friend's place), for Dana, a
classmate who needs quiet to study for exams and is choosing where to study this month. Levels 6-7 address Dana
directly. **This Module's part (Conventions §C):** Levels 4-5 write their one descriptive paragraph; Levels 6-7
write the body paragraphs under a working thesis line, and Module 2 adds the introduction and conclusion. Nothing is visited, brought, or drawn; the places are written from what the student knows. Lesson 2's
prewriting was rehearsal; this lesson builds its own plan.

**Grammar already taught (Lessons 1-2), used here without re-teaching.** Focus A: one tense across the piece;
hedging and evaluative words (seems, tends to, a bit, fairly, mostly, at least, worth it); comparatives and
reason words (because, since, as, given that). Focus B: concession (although, even though, while) and compound
sentences (and, but, so), with their commas. Essay Focus A/B (Levels 6-7): the five-paragraph shape, hook types,
the working thesis (direct and indirect), topic and supporting sentences, outlines.

### Phase 1: Quick plan (10 min)

Every student picks the other place from the printed list and circles it. Then a minimal plan built inside this
packet, with no reference to Lesson 2's prewriting or Lesson 1's ladder (Quality Standards §D9). The Draft Word
Bank is printed before it.

- **Level 4:** three Draft Word Bank words that fit each place, one from each of three different groups; then
  one line naming the comparison you will make and the reason for it.
- **Level 5 (a different shape):** the order you will describe things in (number the groups), and one detail
  that would let Dana guess which place you prefer without your saying so.
- **Level 6:** a general outline of the body paragraphs: a direct working thesis naming two points, one topic
  sentence per body paragraph, and the sentence that will say why one point matters to Dana.
- **Level 7 (a different shape):** a specific outline of the body paragraphs: an indirect working thesis, three
  topic sentences with two details each in order, the detail whose emphasis carries the implication, and the
  implication named in one line for yourself only.

**Teacher circulation check (not printed):** each plan already carries its Level's required feature.

### Phase 2: Drafting this Module's part (50 min)

Every Level completes its part today: the whole paragraph at Levels 4-5, the body paragraphs at Levels 6-7. Sentence and paragraph counts are in the packet; the word ranges above
are the teacher's circulation check and never reach the packet.

**Level 4, a five-step path (steps, not a frame; each sentence is the student's own):** (1) name the two places
in one sentence; (2) one sentence about your home as a place to study; (3) one comparison sentence with a
comparative; (4) the reason, with a reason word (because, since, as, given that), joined to the comparison or
standing alone; (5) one sentence Dana could use to choose, joined with and, but, or so. Aim for 3-6 sentences.

**Level 5, an ordered path:** (1) an opening sentence naming both places; (2) details in your planned order,
one or two sentences per group, one tense throughout; (3) at least one hedge where you are not sure or where a
thing is mixed; (4) one detail, placed where it lands best, that implies which place you prefer, with no
feeling word; (5) a closing sentence Dana could act on. Aim for 6-9 sentences.

**Level 6, the body paragraphs from the general outline:** copy the working thesis onto the first line of the
draft (it stays a line, not a paragraph); then two or three body paragraphs, each opening with its topic
sentence and supporting it with details, reasons, or contrasts; one sentence in a body paragraph that says why a
feature matters to Dana; one register shift to address Dana's concern directly ("You said you cannot work
with..."). No introduction and no conclusion: Module 2 writes them around these bodies. A body paragraph may be
short today.

**Level 7, the body paragraphs from the specific outline:** the indirect working thesis on the first line; three
body paragraphs whose selection and emphasis of detail build the implication without stating it; sophisticated
cohesion between and inside paragraphs (what's more, admittedly, this suggests, on the other hand); and,
somewhere in the draft, one sentence crossed out and replaced in place, the mark of unprompted self-revision. No
introduction and no conclusion.

**Teacher circulation:** the required feature is checked in progress, not only at the end (a comparison and a
reason on the page by sentence four at Level 4; the implied detail placed at Level 5; the Dana sentence and the
register shift at Level 6; a crossed-out line at Level 7). Level 6-7 students who reach the last body paragraph
with under ten minutes left write it in three sentences and expand it on Day 2's improvement pass; the bodies
are complete, if lean, by the end of the block.

**Foundation Support (alongside Level 4, not in place of it):** a student who cannot yet write an original
comparison sentence chooses two Draft Word Bank words, one for each place, and writes "My home is ___, but the
___ is ___," then adds "because" and a reason said aloud to the teacher first.

### Phase 3: Mid-draft share (15 min)

Each student prepares one line to read: **Level 4,** the comparison sentence; **Level 5,** the detail meant to
imply the preference (the class says what they infer); **Level 6,** the sentence that tells Dana why a feature
matters; **Level 7,** the crossed-out line and its replacement. A few students from each Level read aloud.
**Board-dependent moment, co-constructed live:** a running "strong sentence" board, one line per Level added as
it is read; the teacher writes the student's words.

## DAY 2: CHECK AND IMPROVE (75 MIN)

```
0                         25                              55                    75  (minutes)

| Check: marks on the     | Improve from the marks        | Share a line        |
| page, one list per Level|                               |                     |
```

The draft is complete from Day 1; Day 2 opens directly on checking it. No "finish your piece" phase and no
"from last time" wording in the packet. The packet prints one "Check and Improve Your Writing" Task per star,
the check questions first and the improvement steps after, under one section title.

### Phase 1: Self-edit checklist pass (25 min)

Marks on the page: circle, underline, box. One list per Level (Lesson prompt 0.6, composition and essay
regimes); the packet prints each as questions inside that Level's Task.

- **Level 4:** Does every sentence have a subject and a verb, in one tense? Is there exactly one comparison
  (circle the comparative)? Is there one reason with a reason word (box it)? Is there one sentence joined with
  and, but, or so, with a comma before it (underline it)? Do capitals and periods sit where they belong?
- **Level 5:** Is the whole paragraph in one tense (circle any verb that slips)? Are the details in the order
  you planned (number them in the margin)? Is there at least one hedge (underline it)? Is there exactly one
  detail that implies your preference, and does it use no feeling word (box it)? Is there a concession sentence
  with its comma? Do capitals and periods sit where they belong?
- **Level 6:** Is the working thesis on the first line direct, naming two points (underline it)? Does every body
  paragraph open with a topic sentence (circle each), and does every sentence after it support that point
  (underline any that does not)? Is there one sentence that says why a feature matters to Dana (box it)? Is
  there one register shift that addresses Dana directly (mark it with a star)? Are complex sentences and their
  commas under control?
- **Level 7:** Is the working thesis indirect (underline it)? Which detail carries the implication, and is the
  implication nowhere stated (box the detail; if you find a sentence that states it, mark it for cutting)? Is
  there a crossed-out line replaced in place (if not, that is Phase 2's first job)? Do the paragraphs connect
  with cohesive devices rather than "and" (circle each device)? Does the emphasis fall where you planned it,
  with register control sustained?

### Phase 2: Improve the piece from the marks (30 min)

Visible revision throughout: cross out, do not erase.

- **Level 4:** a more exact word from the Draft Word Bank for one plain one; the missing feature added if a
  check said no; one more concrete detail about either place; the compound sentence's comma put in place.
- **Level 5:** a feeling word, if one slipped in, replaced by a detail; a tense slip corrected; one hedge added
  where a claim is too flat; one detail moved so the order runs the way it was planned.
- **Level 6:** the Dana sentence added or sharpened; the register shift added if missing (one sentence that
  turns to Dana directly); a topic sentence rewritten so it states the paragraph's one point; a sentence that
  supports a different point moved to the paragraph it belongs in; a thin body paragraph given one more
  supporting sentence.
- **Level 7:** the implication-stating sentence cut if one exists, and the detail that carries it given more
  emphasis (position or length); the self-revision made visible if it was not (cross out one line and replace
  it in place); two short sentences combined with a cohesive device; the working thesis reworded if the bodies
  have outgrown it.

### Phase 3: Share a line (20 min)

In pairs, each student reads one line to a partner: **Level 4,** the reason sentence; **Level 5,** the detail
that implies the preference, and the partner says what they infer; **Level 6,** the register-shift sentence;
**Level 7,** the replacement line, and the partner says what the paragraph now implies. **Board-dependent
moment, co-constructed live:** return to Day 1's "strong sentence" board and add newly improved lines beside
the originals.
