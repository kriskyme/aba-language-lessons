Unit 4A: Finishing the Piece About My Bag

Writing · Narrating Proficient 2.1.4.0

Objective: I can tell what happened with something of my own, in order, so that the order itself carries what mattered.

Draft Word Bank
Time and sequence: that winter · the week after · by then · until then · ever since · the first month
Reaching back: already · up to that point · the year before · earlier that autumn · before that · since the spring
What happened: split · snapped · gave way · soaked through · held · survived
How it turned out: worth it · a nuisance · no trouble · the last straw · fine in the end · still going
Parts to name exactly: the strap · the base · the zip · the lining · the buckle · the front pocket

Use these words when a task asks for one. You may also use your own.

Put It Together

Task A. Give your piece a title and put its parts in order.

  1. 1.Write a title on the line: the bag's name as you would give it to someone else.
  2. 2.If you have two paragraphs, number them 1 for the description and 2 for the story.
  3. 3.If you have body paragraphs, number them 1 for the introduction, 2 for the body paragraphs in the order they already stand, and 3 for the conclusion. Cross out the working thesis line at the top of the bodies: the introduction carries that sentence now, and it should appear once.
Trade and Check

Task B. Trade whole pieces with a partner, read once through, then fill this in on the writer's page.

  1. 1.In the first part, underline the detail that tells you most about the bag, and circle the part of the bag it names.
  2. 2.If it is a two-paragraph piece: number the events of the second paragraph in the order they happened, and box the sentence that says why it mattered. If it is an essay: box the hook, underline the thesis, and box the closing move.
  3. 3.If it is a ★★★ or ★★★★ piece: write what you think the writer is not saying outright. For a ★★★★ piece, also write the answer each of the two groups reaches, and whether the ending settles between them:
  4. 4.Copy out one sentence you would keep exactly as it is:
  5. 5.Write one thing you want to know more about, or one place where you could not tell what happened first or what the ending was doing:
Change It for Effect

Task C. ★ Make three changes to your piece, and write each one in the table.

  1. 1.Take one sentence in the second paragraph and rewrite it either with a part moved to the front or as a cleft. Check the inversion rule if you fronted a negative or restrictive word.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word with a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank, or with the name of the part it is really about.
  3. 3.Make the change your partner asked for.

  1. 1.Move a part to the front, or make a cleft, in the introduction or the conclusion, so the change falls where the reader arrives or leaves.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word in your closing move, so the suggestion names something the reader can actually do.
  3. 3.Make the change your partner asked for. If it belongs in a body paragraph, put it there and leave the conclusion alone.

Task E. ★★★ Make four changes to your piece, and write each one in the table.

  1. 1.Move a part to the front, or make a cleft, in the introduction, so the hook's detail arrives where you want it.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word with one that carries the implication rather than one that only sounds better.
  3. 3.Make the change your partner asked for, and check it does not state the thing you are implying.
  4. 4.Move one sentence so that what the reader works out arrives later than it did. In the table, say what they now work out, and when.

Task F. ★★★★ Make five changes to your piece, and write each one in the table.

  1. 1.Move a part to the front, or make a cleft, in the Marcus group, so the two groups' sentence shapes pull further apart.
  2. 2.Replace one plain word in the introduction, so the thing your hook names belongs more clearly to both readers.
  3. 3.Make the change your partner asked for.
  4. 4.Move one sentence so the complication lands later than it did.
  5. 5.Rewrite one connecting sentence so both readers' questions stay open through it. Write it before and after, and say in the third column which reader was being shut out.

Your Final Copy

Task A. Copy your whole piece cleanly under your title.

  1. 1.Put the parts in the order you numbered them.
  2. 2.Your revisions go in as you copy.
  3. 3.Capitals and full stops where they belong.
Read Two Finished Pieces

Task B. Pass your copy to a neighbor, read the one that reaches you, and pass it on again, so that you read two pieces that are not your partner's. Stay in your seat.

Task C. ★ For each piece you read, write one line on your card: the one detail that made you see that bag.

Task D. ★★ For each piece you read, write on your card the one detail that made you see that bag, and what the ending asked you to do.

Task E. ★★★ For each piece you read, write on your card the one detail that made you see that bag, and one thing the writer did not say outright.

Task F. ★★★★ For each piece you read, write on your card the one detail that made you see that bag, and whether the piece ended up recommending one thing or two.

Now Try Both Skills on Your Wallet

Task G. ★ Write four sentences about your own wallet or card holder on one side of a card.

  1. 1.Two describing it. One of them lets your partner guess how you feel about carrying it, with no feeling word.
  2. 2.Two telling one thing that happened with it, in order.
  3. 3.Swap cards. Write what your partner guessed:

Task H. ★★ Write three sentences about your own wallet or card holder on one side of a card.

  1. 1.One describing it for someone about to buy one.
  2. 2.Two telling one thing that happened with it, with one clear shift from telling what happened to saying why it mattered.
  3. 3.Swap cards. Write what your partner said the reason was:

Task I. ★★★ Write three sentences about your own wallet or card holder on one side of a card.

  1. 1.Hold the outcome back to the last sentence.
  2. 2.Put one earlier detail in that hints at why you kept it, without explaining it.
  3. 3.Swap cards. Write what your partner worked out:

Task J. ★★★★ Write three sentences about your own wallet or card holder on one side of a card.

  1. 1.Narrate them so that a careful reader can tell the account is quietly on your side.
  2. 2.Change the distance you stand at once, without saying that you are doing it.
  3. 3.Swap cards. Write what your partner said the account was leaving out:
The Last Time You Were Late to Something That Mattered

Task K. ★ On the other side of the card, write three to five sentences.

  1. 1.Tell it in order, and say why it mattered.
  2. 2.One detail lets a partner guess how you felt. No feeling word appears anywhere.

Task L. ★★ On the other side of the card, write three to five sentences.

  1. 1.Tell it in order, and say why it mattered.
  2. 2.Shift once from telling what happened to saying what you make of it now.
  3. 3.Write it for a reader you know is likely to think it was your own fault.

Task M. ★★★ On the other side of the card, write three to five sentences.

  1. 1.Hold the outcome back until the final sentence.
  2. 2.Put one earlier, unexplained detail in that hints at your real motive.

Task N. ★★★★ On the other side of the card, write three to five sentences.

  1. 1.Narrate it so that a careful reader can tell the account quietly favors you: a detail left out, your own reaction described more sympathetically than anyone else's.
  2. 2.Change the pacing at least twice.
  3. 3.Never say outright that the account is one-sided.
