## What this lesson does, and what it deliberately does not

This is the last lesson of Module 1's Set, but it is **not the end of the piece**. The Pair's piece is built in
parts (Conventions §C): Module 1's part is finished here and kept, and Module 2 writes the introduction and the
conclusion around it at Pair position 7, then assembles and publishes at position 8.

So this lesson:

- **revises Module 1's part for effect**, using Module 1's own tools, with the before and after both visible;
- **makes a final copy** of that part, which goes into the student's folder **unmarked**;
- **runs the Closing Transfer Check** on Module 1's skill, on a third object.

And this lesson deliberately does **not**:

- re-run the correctness checklist. That happened once, at Lesson 3 Day 2. Revision here is for effect: a join,
  an upgrade, an order, a reader's request answered, never a second hunt for commas.
- run Peer Editing. In a Module Pair that happens once, at position 8, over both Modules' work (0.6). Today's
  partner reading is one request, with no form, no compliment, and no suggestion.
- publish or share the piece. It is not finished. Nothing is read out, passed around, or displayed.
- attach a hand-off note. The final copy carries no note, no stem, and no feature-marking task. Module 2's
  Lesson 5 re-reads the draft itself.

**Draft Word Bank for the bag** (reprinted in both units of the packet; the upgrade move draws on it):

- Shape and size: slouched, boxy, deep, flat, bulging, narrow
- Fit and feel: padded, stiff, soft, heavy, digging, balanced
- Condition: frayed, scuffed, faded, stretched, stained, split
- Use: commuting, the gym, groceries, travel, a laptop, overnight
- Parts to name exactly: the strap, the base, the zip, the lining, the buckle, the front pocket

## DAY 1: REVISE FOR EFFECT (75 MIN)

```
0              15                                    60                      75  (minutes)
|---Phase 1----|---------------Phase 2---------------|-------Phase 3--------|
| A reader's   | Revise for effect with Module 1's   | Revision share       |
| request      | tools                               |                      |
```

### Phase 1: A reader's request (15 min)

Trade pieces with a partner and read. On the writer's page, write **one** thing you want to know more about. One
sentence, naming a part of the bag or a moment in the piece. Not a correction, not a compliment, not a list.

Then take your own piece back and read the request before you start.

_Teacher note, not printed:_ this is not Peer Editing (0.6). If a partner starts marking commas, take the pen
back and ask them for the one thing they wanted to know.

### Phase 2: Revise for effect with Module 1's tools (45 min)

Every change goes in the before-and-after table on the page, so the revision is visible rather than claimed
(0.4a). Cross out on the draft, do not erase.

**The table, printed once per Level with the right number of rows:**

| What I first wrote | What I changed it to | What the change did |
|---|---|---|

**Level 5, Task A: three moves.**

1. **Join.** Find two short sentences next to each other. Join them using a reduced clause, so one of them
   becomes a participle. Write both versions in the table.
2. **Upgrade.** Find one plain word and replace it with a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank, or with the
   name of the part it is really about.
3. **The request.** Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place in the order where it belongs.

**Level 6, Task B: three moves.**

1. **Join.** As above, and choose two sentences inside the same body paragraph so the join tightens that
   paragraph rather than bridging two.
2. **Upgrade.** Replace one plain word in the sentence about Priya, so the sentence names what she will be
   doing rather than what the bag is like.
3. **The request.** Add the sentence your partner asked for. If it belongs to a point your thesis does not
   name, change the thesis instead of leaving the sentence stranded.

**Level 7, Task C: four moves.**

1. **Join.** As above.
2. **Upgrade.** Replace one plain word with a more exact one, choosing a word that carries the implication
   rather than one that only sounds better.
3. **The request.** Add the sentence your partner asked for, and check that it does not accidentally state the
   thing you are implying.
4. **Move.** Move one sentence so that the detail carrying the implication lands later than it did. Say in the
   table what the reader now works out, and when.

**Level 8, Task D: five moves.**

1. **Join.** As above, in the Priya group.
2. **Upgrade.** Replace one plain word in the Marcus group with a more exact one, so the two groups' vocabulary
   pulls further apart.
3. **The request.** Add the sentence your partner asked for.
4. **Move.** Move one sentence so that the complication lands later than it did.
5. **Reshape.** Take one paragraph of the Marcus group and change its shape: put its shortest sentence last, or
   invert its order, so the two groups are built visibly differently. Write the paragraph's first and last
   sentence in the table, before and after.

**Answer note:** no key; every row is on the student's own piece. Circulation priorities, in order: a table row
whose "what the change did" column repeats the change instead of naming its effect; a Level 7 move that pushed
the implication so late it disappeared; a Level 8 reshape that changed the words rather than the shape; any
student correcting punctuation instead of revising, which is Lesson 3's business and not today's.

### Phase 3: Revision share (15 min)

Two or three volunteers read one row of their table aloud: the before, the after, and nothing else. The class
names what the change did. **Board-dependent moment:** build a before-and-after board from what is read out,
with the effect named in the class's own words ("it puts the worn strap last," "it stops us being told"). Not a
prepared list.

## DAY 2: FINAL COPY AND CLOSING TRANSFER CHECK (75 MIN)

```
0                          30                              60                75  (minutes)
|----------Phase 1---------|-----------Phase 2-------------|----Phase 3-----|
| Final copy               | Closing Transfer Check        | Transfer share |
```

### Phase 1: Final copy (30 min)

Write a clean copy of Module 1's part, with the revisions in place, capitals and full stops where they belong.
Levels 6 to 8 copy the working thesis line at the top, then the body paragraphs. Level 5 copies the paragraph.

The copy goes into your folder. There is no proofreading list and nothing to count.

_Teacher note, not printed:_ the copy is kept unmarked. No note is attached, no feature is labeled, and nothing
is collected or displayed. Module 2's Lesson 5 opens by having students re-read this page cold, and a page
covered in labels would do that work for them.

### Phase 2: Closing Transfer Check (30 min)

The skill this Module has been practicing, applied to something new: **your keys.** Not the bag, and not
anything written earlier in this Set. In pairs, on a card.

- **Level 5, Task A:** write three to five sentences describing your keys in an order you choose. One detail
  lets your partner guess how you feel about carrying them, and no feeling word appears. Read the card to your
  partner. Your partner says what they guessed.
- **Level 6, Task B:** write three sentences that work as one body paragraph: a topic sentence stating one point
  about your keys, then two sentences supporting it. One of them says why that point would matter to someone
  who is about to be given a copy. Read it to your partner. Your partner says what the point was.
- **Level 7, Task C:** write three sentences about your keys that let your partner work out something you never
  state. Cross out one word as you go and write a more exact one beside it, leaving both legible. Your partner
  writes on the card what they worked out; you say whether that was it.
- **Level 8, Task D:** write four sentences about your keys. The first two set up one impression. The last two
  complicate it without contradicting it, and sound different from the first two. Your partner reads the card
  and says where the change happens.

**Answer note:** no key; this is a demonstration, not a graded task. What the teacher is watching for, one item
per Level: a Level 5 card with a feeling word in it; a Level 6 card that is three details with no point; a Level
7 card where the partner reads the implication straight off the page because it was written down; a Level 8 card
whose last two sentences contradict the first two rather than complicating them.

### Phase 3: Transfer share (15 min)

Two or three pairs are cold-called to read their cards from their seats. Capture the instances on the board as
they are read: the guessed feeling, the point, the inference, the place where the change happened. Demonstration,
not self-report. Nothing is collected.

Module 1's part is finished. Module 2's Set picks it up at Pair position 5.
