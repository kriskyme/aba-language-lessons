Use these words when a task asks for one. You may also use your own.

A Reader's Request

Task A. Trade pieces with a partner and read.

  1. 1.On the writer's page, write one thing you want to know more about. One sentence, naming a part of the bag or a moment in the piece.
  2. 2.Take your own piece back and read the request before you start the next task.
Change It for Effect

Task B. ★ Make three changes to your paragraph, and write each one in the table.

  1. 1.Join. Find two short sentences next to each other and join them so one becomes a shortened clause.
  2. 2.Upgrade. Replace one plain word with a more exact one from the Draft Word Bank, or with the name of the part it is really about.
  3. 3.The request. Add the sentence your partner asked for, in the place in the order where it belongs.

Your Final Copy

Task A. Write a clean copy of your piece with your changes in place.

  1. 1.Capitals and full stops where they belong.
  2. 2.If you are writing body paragraphs, copy the working thesis line at the top, then the paragraphs.
  3. 3.Put the copy in your folder.
Now Try It on Your Keys

Task B. ★ Write three to five sentences about your keys on a card.

  1. 1.Put the details in an order you choose.
  2. 2.One detail lets your partner guess how you feel about carrying them. No feeling word appears.
  3. 3.Read the card to your partner. Write what they guessed:

Task C. ★★ Write three sentences about your keys on a card, working as one body paragraph.

  1. 1.A topic sentence stating one point about your keys, then two sentences supporting it.
  2. 2.One of them says why that point would matter to someone about to be given a copy.
  3. 3.Read it to your partner. Write what they said the point was:

Task D. ★★★ Write three sentences about your keys on a card that let your partner work out something you never state.

  1. 1.Cross out one word as you go and write a more exact one beside it, leaving both legible.
  2. 2.Your partner writes on the card what they worked out. Say whether that was it.

Task E. ★★★★ Write four sentences about your keys on a card.

  1. 1.The first two set up one impression.
  2. 2.The last two complicate it without contradicting it, and sound different from the first two.
  3. 3.Your partner reads the card and says where the change happens. Write what they said:
